# Implementation Plan: Small Farm Victoria

## Experimental Learning Phase

**Plan Type**: Pre-Production Skill Building (Experimental)
**Time Horizon**: 24 weeks (6 months)
**Budget Envelope**: $2,000 - $5,000 (drip-fed as validated)
**Time Commitment**: 15 hours/week
**Team**: Family operation
**Success Criteria**: Confident in growing skills + validated crop selection before any commercial investment

---

## Executive Summary

This plan focuses exclusively on **learning to grow** before any commercial activity. You'll systematically build growing skills through structured experiments in your 50sqm home space, using a fail-fast approach where each phase unlocks the next investment. The goal is to answer: "Can we reliably grow quality produce?" before spending money on markets, land, or infrastructure.

**Philosophy**: Treat your home space as a laboratory. Every crop is an experiment. Every failure is data. Only proceed to production when growing is no longer the uncertainty.

### 🌱 Phase 1: Microgreens Mastery (Week 3-8)
**Goal**: Achieve consistent, quality microgreens harvests
**Budget**: $300 - $600 (unlocked after Phase 0 success)
**Time**: 15 hrs/week
**Exit Criteria**: 10 successful harvests across 5+ varieties with consistent quality

| Step | Action | Output | Cost | Time |
|------|--------|--------|------|------|
| 1.1 | Expand to 5 varieties: sunflower, pea shoots, radish, broccoli, basil | Variety range established | $50-100 (seeds) | 2h |
| 1.2 | Add second grow light for increased capacity | 8-10 tray capacity | $50-100 | 1h |
| 1.3 | Implement 2-week succession planting schedule | Continuous harvest pipeline | $0 | 2h planning |
| 1.4 | First harvest and taste test with family | Quality feedback | $0 | 1h |
| 1.5 | Track yield per tray: weight, quality grade (A/B/C), time to harvest | Yield data for 10+ harvests | $5 (kitchen scale) | Ongoing |
| 1.6 | Experiment with different growing mediums | Identify best medium for each variety | $30-50 | 3h |
| 1.7 | Learn to identify and address: mold, damping off, leggy growth | Problem-solving skills | $0 | Ongoing |
| 1.8 | Start herb seedlings: basil, coriander, parsley, mint | Herbs growing alongside microgreens | $20-40 | 2h |

**🧪 Experiments to Run**:
1. **Growing medium comparison**: Coco coir vs soil vs hemp mats - track germination, growth rate, yield
2. **Variety profitability test**: Which varieties grow fastest, yield most, taste best?
3. **Stacking density test**: How many trays can you manage in your space effectively?
4. **Blackout period test**: 2 vs 3 vs 4 days blackout for sunflower - which produces best stems?
5. **Harvest timing test**: Harvest at 7, 10, 14 days - compare yield, taste, shelf life

**📊 Weekly Metrics to Track**:
| Metric | Target | Why It Matters |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Trays planted | 6-10/week | Production capacity |
| Germination rate | >80% | Consistency indicator |
| Harvest success rate | >90% | Quality control |
| Time spent | 15h/week | Sustainability check |
| Enjoyment (1-10) | >6 | Critical for long-term |

**🚦 Go/No-Go Decision (End of Week 8)**:
- ✅ **GO** if: 10+ successful harvests, >80% germination, enjoying the work
- 🔄 **EXTEND** if: Improving but not consistent - add 2-4 weeks practice
- ❌ **REASSESS** if: Consistent failures despite troubleshooting, or dreading the work

**Common Problems & Solutions**:
| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Mold on trays | Overwatering, poor airflow | Reduce water, add fan |
| Leggy/pale growth | Insufficient light | Lower lights, increase hours |
| Uneven germination | Inconsistent moisture | Pre-soak seeds, mist evenly |
| Yellowing | Nutrient deficiency or overwatering | Check medium, reduce water |
| Seeds not sprouting | Old seeds or wrong temp | Fresh seeds, check temperature |

### 🥬 Phase 3: Production Simulation (Week 17-24)
**Goal**: Simulate commercial production without selling - validate capacity and quality
**Budget**: $500 - $1,500 (unlocked after Phase 2 success)
**Time**: 15 hrs/week (test 20-25 hrs for 2 weeks to simulate production pace)
**Exit Criteria**: 4 consecutive weeks of "market-ready" harvests; confident in scaling decision

| Step | Action | Output | Cost | Time |
|------|--------|--------|------|------|
| 3.1 | Define "market quality" standards with photos | Quality benchmark documented | $0 | 2h |
| 3.2 | Set weekly production targets (e.g., 20 microgreen trays, 5kg herbs, 10 lettuce heads) | Production schedule | $0 | 1h |
| 3.3 | Run 4-week "mock market" - harvest as if selling, give to family/friends/neighbors | Production rhythm tested | $100-200 (inputs) | 4 weeks |
| 3.4 | Package produce as you would for market | Presentation skills | $50-100 (packaging) | 2h |
| 3.5 | Track time meticulously: growing, harvesting, packaging, cleanup | True time cost data | $0 | Ongoing |
| 3.6 | Conduct taste tests with recipients - gather feedback | Quality validation | $0 | 1h |
| 3.7 | Simulate "crop failure" recovery - what if 50% of a planting fails? | Resilience test | $0 | 1h |
| 3.8 | 2-week "intensity test": increase to 20-25 hrs/week | Sustainability check | $0 | 2 weeks |
| 3.9 | Calculate actual cost per unit from all experiments | Real unit economics | $0 | 3h |
| 3.10 | Family decision meeting: Go to production or not? | Aligned decision | $0 | 2h |

**🧪 Experiments to Run**:
1. **Shelf life test**: How long does your produce stay fresh? (Critical for markets)
2. **Scale stress test**: Double your production for 2 weeks - what breaks?
3. **Quality consistency test**: Can you hit quality standards 4 weeks in a row?
4. **Time-per-unit calculation**: Exactly how long does each product take to produce?
5. **Recovery test**: Intentionally "lose" a batch - how quickly can you recover?

**📊 Production Simulation Metrics**:
| Metric | Target | Why It Matters |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Quality pass rate | >90% of harvest meets market standard | Saleable produce |
| Production consistency | Hit targets 4 weeks in a row | Reliable supply |
| Time efficiency | Improving week-over-week | Scalability |
| Cost per unit | Decreasing or stable | Profitability indicator |
| Enjoyment at higher hours | Still >6/10 | Long-term sustainability |

**🚦 Final Go/No-Go Decision (End of Week 24)**:
- ✅ **PROCEED TO COMMERCIAL** if:
  - >90% harvests meet quality standards
  - Time commitment feels sustainable
  - Family is aligned and enthusiastic
  - Cost per unit supports profitable pricing
  - You're excited about scaling up
- 🔄 **EXTEND EXPERIMENTAL** if:
  - Skills improving but not consistent
  - Need more practice with specific crops
  - Seasonal timing requires waiting
- ⏸️ **PAUSE/HOBBY** if:
  - Consistent quality issues despite effort
  - Time commitment feels unsustainable
  - Family enthusiasm has waned
  - Better to keep as rewarding hobby, not business
- ❌ **STOP** if:
  - Fundamental inability to grow quality produce
  - Zero enjoyment in the work
  - Clear this isn't the right path

### Cost Minimization Strategies
1. **Drip-feed investment**: Only release budget for next phase after success criteria met
2. **Start with basics**: Resist urge to buy "professional" equipment until skills proven
3. **Free knowledge first**: YouTube, library books, Reddit before paid courses
4. **Reuse and repurpose**: Food containers as trays, household items as tools
5. **Bulk buying**: Seeds and medium in larger quantities once varieties confirmed

### Where NOT to Cut Costs
- **Quality seeds**: Cheap seeds = poor germination = wasted time
- **Adequate lighting**: Insufficient light = leggy, poor quality crops
- **Growing medium**: Poor medium = disease, poor results
- **Record-keeping tools**: A notebook costs $5 but saves thousands in repeated mistakes

## ⚠️ Risk Register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Early Warning |
|------|------------|--------|------------|---------------|
| Complete crop failure | Medium | Low (learning phase) | Treat as data, diagnose, retry | 3+ failures same cause |
| Mold/disease outbreak | High | Medium | Improve airflow, reduce water, sanitize | Fuzzy growth on trays |
| Time commitment unsustainable | Medium | High | Track honestly, adjust expectations | Dreading farm time |
| Family enthusiasm wanes | Medium | High | Regular check-ins, share successes | Declining participation |
| Overinvestment before validation | Low | High | Strict phase gates, drip-feed budget | Urge to "buy more stuff" |
| Seasonal timing issues | Medium | Low | Plan around seasons, use indoor growing | Outdoor failures in wrong season |
| Pests in outdoor area | High | Medium | Prevention, ID skills, organic solutions | First pest sighting |

## 🎯 Decision Framework

### After Each Phase, Ask:

**Technical Readiness**
1. Did we hit the success criteria for this phase?
2. What's our germination/success rate trend?
3. Can we identify and solve problems independently?

**Personal/Family Readiness**
4. Are we enjoying this? (Honest answer)
5. Is 15 hrs/week sustainable? Could we do more?
6. Is the family still aligned?

**Commercial Readiness** (Phase 3 only)
7. Is our quality market-ready?
8. Can we produce consistently?
9. Do the economics work at our cost per unit?

### The "Hell Yes or No" Test

At the end of Phase 3, the question is simple:
> "Based on everything we've learned, are we **excited** to invest $5,000-10,000 and 15-20 hrs/week to start selling at farmers markets?"

- **Hell Yes** → Proceed to commercial phase (revisit main action-plan.md)
- **Hesitant Yes** → Extend experimental phase, identify what's holding you back
- **No** → Valuable learning. Keep as hobby, or explore different business direction
