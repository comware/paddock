## Overview

Clover microgreens (typically red clover or crimson clover) produce delicate, mild-flavored shoots that are popular for their gentle taste and good nutritional profile. The small seeds produce fine, delicate plants with characteristic clover-shaped cotyledons.

Clover is often associated with sprouting (where it's extremely popular), but grown as a microgreen in soil it develops more flavor and nutrition than jar-sprouted clover. The plants are delicate but grow quickly and reliably.

Red clover in particular contains isoflavones and is marketed for health benefits.

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Red clover or crimson clover seeds
- Small, round, uniform seeds
- Untreated seeds (important for edible use)

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- 2-3 years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Optional but can speed germination

**Process:**
1. Soak 4-8 hours if desired
2. Drain and rinse
3. Plant promptly

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Fine coco coir or seed-starting mix

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-35g per 10x20 tray (small seeds)

Use shaker for even distribution. Don't cover seeds.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

Keep moist, 18-22°C.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When germination complete

Moderate light, 12-14 hours daily.

### 7. Watering

Gentle misting or bottom watering. Keep consistently moist.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 8-12 days, height 5-8cm, leaves developed

**Yield:** 200-350g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

Very delicate - handle gently. Refrigerate, 5-7 days shelf life.
