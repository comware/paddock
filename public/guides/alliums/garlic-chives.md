# Garlic Chive Microgreens

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Days to Harvest** | 21-28 days |
| **Blackout Period** | 7-10 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | Yes (12-24 hours) |
| **Seed Density** | 30-50g per 10x20 tray |
| **Expected Yield** | 3-5:1 ratio |
| **Flavor Profile** | Mild garlic with chive undertones, flat leaves |

## Overview

Garlic chives (Chinese chives, Allium tuberosum) are flat-leaved relatives of regular chives with a mild garlic flavor. As microgreens, they produce flat shoots with gentle garlic-chive flavor that's popular in Asian cuisine.

Growing requirements are similar to regular chives - very slow germination and long cycle. The flat leaves distinguish them visually from round-leaved regular chives.

The garlic-chive flavor combination is unique and valued in Chinese, Korean, and other Asian cuisines.

## Best Uses

- Asian cuisine (Chinese, Korean, Japanese)
- Garlic flavor without intensity
- Dumpling and noodle dishes
- Stir-fry finishing
- Premium garnish

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Garlic chive seeds (Allium tuberosum)
- Similar to regular chive seeds
- Fresh seeds important

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Use within 1 year

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes

Soak 12-24 hours, drain thoroughly.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Fine coco coir or soil

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 30-50g per 10x20 tray

Spread evenly, don't cover, mist gently.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 7-10 days

Same patience required as regular chives.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When shoots visible

Moderate light, 12-14 hours.

### 7. Watering

Careful moisture management over long cycle.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 21-28 days, height 8-12cm, flat leaves

**Yield:** 100-200g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

Delicate. Refrigerate, 7-10 days shelf life.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Bare patches, many seeds still hard and unsprouted after the blackout should have ended.

**Causes:**
- Under-soaking - this crop needs yes (12-24 hours), and a short soak leaves the seed coat intact
- Over-soaking, which drowns the seed and starts rot before it can sprout
- Old seed, or seed stored warm and humid
- Medium dried out during blackout

**Solution:**
- Soak for the full stated time - yes (12-24 hours) - in cool water, then drain and rinse thoroughly
- Sow at 30-50g per 10x20 tray so seeds sit in contact with medium, not stacked on each other
- Keep the medium consistently damp through blackout, and hold 18-22°C
- Test a small batch before committing a full tray

**Prevention:** Buy seed dated for the current season and store it cool, dark and dry.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 7-10 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**
- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 30-50g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 7-10 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 7-10 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 21-28 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Seed Coats Stuck on the Tips

**Symptoms:** Black seed coats riding up on the tips of the shoots.

**Causes:**
- Not weighted during blackout
- Blackout ended too early, before the shoot could shed the coat

**Solution:**
- Weight the tray during blackout so shoots have to push against something
- Keep the cover on for the full blackout - alliums are slow and cutting it short leaves coats attached
- Mist and rub gently to remove any remaining coats before harvest

**Prevention:** Alliums are the slowest crops in this library. Almost every allium problem traces back to rushing them.

## Pro Tips

1. **Asian Market** - Target Chinese, Korean, and Japanese restaurants specifically.
