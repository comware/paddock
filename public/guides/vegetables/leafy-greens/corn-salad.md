## Overview

Corn salad — better known by its French name, mâche, and also called lamb's lettuce — is a small, soft-leafed rosette that is a genuine cold-season crop in a way none of the other greens in this batch really are. It has the lowest soil temperature range here by a clear margin, will germinate and grow through cold that would stall lettuce or spinach, and tolerates a real frost with barely a mark on the leaves. It's the crop to reach for when the rest of the garden has shut down for winter.

The flip side of that cold-hardiness is heat intolerance: mâche dislikes warm weather intensely, bolting or simply refusing to grow well once temperatures climb, which makes it the mirror image of a crop like mustard greens. It's also slow and a little uneven to germinate compared to most greens here, so patience at the sowing stage is part of growing it well.

## When to Sow

Soil temperature range is 5-20°C, the coolest of any leafy green in this guide. As a southern Australian guide, sow through autumn and into winter (Mar-Jul) for the main crop, and again in very early spring (Aug) if the weather is still cool — treat spring and summer sowings as a mistake waiting to happen.

Sow too early into the residual warmth of late summer or early autumn and germination is poor and slow, since mâche seed can be reluctant to germinate in anything but cool soil. Sow too late into a warming spring and the plant bolts almost immediately rather than forming the low rosette it's grown for.

## Sowing

Corn salad is direct-sown; it's slow-growing and low-value enough per plant that transplanting isn't worth the effort, and it establishes fine directly in cool soil. Sow shallow at 5 mm — expect germination to take noticeably longer than the brassica greens in this guide, sometimes two to three weeks in cold soil, which catches out growers expecting a quick brassica-speed emergence.

Space plants 10 cm apart in rows 20 cm apart. It's a small, low-growing plant, so crowding mostly affects individual rosette size rather than causing disease problems, and mâche is often grown at close spacing deliberately as a dense mat that's cut all at once.

## Growing On

Corn salad needs very little once established — steady moisture through winter is usually all it takes, since cool-season rainfall in most of southern Australia covers a lot of its needs without supplementary watering. It isn't a heavy feeder and grows slowly and steadily rather than in the quick flushes typical of a summer green, so there's not much to actively manage beyond keeping weeds down around the low rosettes, where they can outcompete a slow-establishing crop.

## Harvest

Corn salad takes 40-50 days to reach a useful size, slower than most of the other greens here given its cool-weather, unhurried growth habit. It's commonly harvested as whole small rosettes cut at the base, rather than picked leaf by leaf, since the leaves are small and the whole plant is usually the practical unit to work with. Some growers do pick outer leaves from larger rosettes for a longer harvest window, in which case leave the centre intact to keep it producing. Because it's grown through the cold months it's suited to, mâche doesn't face the same urgent "harvest before it bolts" pressure that a lot of the warmer-season greens do — it will hold reasonably well in the ground as long as the cold weather continues.

## Succession

With a 21-day succession interval, 3-4 successions spaced three weeks apart through the autumn-to-winter window will keep a rolling supply of small rosettes through the part of the year when good, fresh salad greens are otherwise hard to come by. There's little point trying to push successions into spring or summer, since the crop's whole appeal is being the thing that grows when everything else has stopped.

## Common Problems

**Slow, patchy germination.** This is mostly just how mâche behaves in cool soil rather than a fault — germination can take two to three weeks. Keep the seed bed consistently moist during this stretch and be patient rather than assuming the sowing has failed and resowing too soon.

**Rot in wet winter soil.** Corn salad tolerates cold well but not waterlogging, and a heavy, poorly drained bed through a wet winter can see rosettes rot at the base. Improve drainage before sowing, and avoid low-lying parts of the garden that pool water after rain.

**Bolting in an unexpected warm spell.** Even a brief run of unseasonably warm weather during a late-sown or late-standing crop can trigger bolting, since mâche has very little heat tolerance. Harvest promptly if a warm spell is forecast, and don't push sowings into the warmer shoulders of the season expecting it to cope the way a hardier green might.

## In the Bed

Corn salad belongs to its own small family (commonly placed in Valerianaceae, sometimes folded into Caprifoliaceae depending on the classification used), unrelated to the brassica greens, the Asteraceae greens, or chard and spinach, which makes it a genuinely clean rotation partner alongside any of them. It's small, low, and undemanding, so it slots easily into odd corners or as a winter cover for a bed that would otherwise sit bare, and being finished by the time warm weather arrives, it frees the ground right when spring crops need it.
