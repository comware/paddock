# Hemp Microgreens

| **Expected Yield** | 5-8:1 ratio |

## Overview

Hemp microgreens are nutritious, protein-rich shoots from the Cannabis sativa plant - the same species as marijuana but bred for fiber/seed with negligible THC. Hemp seeds are legal to purchase and grow in most regions (check local regulations), and the microgreens contain no psychoactive compounds.

Growing hemp is straightforward - large seeds germinate reliably and produce sturdy shoots. The main consideration is sourcing seeds from reputable suppliers and understanding local regulations.

Hemp microgreens are marketed for their protein content, omega fatty acids, and complete amino acid profile.

## Best Uses

- Protein-rich microgreen
- Vegan protein source marketing
- Substantial salad ingredient

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Hemp seeds for sprouting/microgreens
- Unhulled seeds (hulled won't sprout)
- From reputable supplier
- Check local regulations before purchasing

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Use within 1 year for best germination

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes

**Process:**
1. Rinse thoroughly
2. Soak 8-12 hours
3. Seeds will swell
4. Drain completely

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Coco coir or soil

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 150-200g per 10x20 tray

Single layer, press gently. Large seeds handle easily.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-5 days

Keep at 18-22°C, moist. Watch for mold on seed hulls.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When stems 4-5cm tall

Moderate light, 12-14 hours daily.

### 7. Watering

Bottom watering preferred. Keep moist but not waterlogged.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 10-14 days, height 10-15cm, cotyledons green and open

**Yield:** 750g-1.5kg from 10x20 tray (good yield)

### 9. Post-Harvest

Remove any remaining seed hulls. Refrigerate, 7-10 days shelf life.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Bare patches, many seeds still hard and unsprouted after the blackout should have ended.

**Causes:**
- Under-soaking - this crop needs yes (8-12 hours), and a short soak leaves the seed coat intact
- Over-soaking, which drowns the seed and starts rot before it can sprout
- Old seed, or seed stored warm and humid
- Medium dried out during blackout

**Solution:**
- Soak for the full stated time - yes (8-12 hours) - in cool water, then drain and rinse thoroughly
- Sow at 150-200g per 10x20 tray so seeds sit in contact with medium, not stacked on each other
- Keep the medium consistently damp through blackout, and hold 18-22°C
- Test a small batch before committing a full tray

**Prevention:** Buy seed dated for the current season and store it cool, dark and dry.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 4-5 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**
- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 150-200g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 4-5 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 4-5 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 10-14 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Seeds Rotting Instead of Sprouting

**Symptoms:** Sour smell, mushy seeds, slimy patches in the tray within the first two days.

**Causes:**
- Over-soaked - large seeds drown if left in water too long
- Not rinsed and drained after soaking
- Sitting in standing water in the tray

**Solution:**
- Soak only for the stated time, then drain and rinse until the water runs clear
- Tip out any water left standing under the tray
- Get air moving as soon as the cover comes off
- Discard the tray if it smells sour - a rotting tray does not recover

**Prevention:** Set a timer for the soak. Large seeds are far less forgiving of a long soak than a short one.
