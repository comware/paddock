# Oat Microgreens (Oat Grass)

| **Blackout Period** | 3-4 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | Yes (8-12 hours) |

## Overview

Oat grass (oat microgreens) is essentially the oat equivalent of wheatgrass - young grass shoots with mild, sweet flavor. Compared to wheatgrass, oat grass is milder and slightly sweeter, making it more palatable for those who find wheatgrass too intense.

Growing oat grass is straightforward and economical. Oat groats (hulled oats) or unhulled oat seeds both work, though unhulled seeds may have slightly better germination.

Oat grass is popular in juice bars and for those seeking a milder alternative to wheatgrass.

## Best Uses

- Wheatgrass alternative (milder)
- Juice bar ingredient
- Smoothie addition
- Health food market
- Pet grass (cats and dogs)

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Oat groats (hulled) or whole oat seeds (unhulled)
- Not quick oats or rolled oats (won't sprout)
- Feed oats work if untreated

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- 2-3 years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes

**Process:**
1. Rinse thoroughly
2. Soak 8-12 hours
3. Seeds will swell
4. Drain completely

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Coco coir, soil, or hemp mat

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 150-200g per 10x20 tray

Spread evenly, press into medium.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

Standard conditions, keep moist.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When shoots 5-7cm tall

Moderate light for green color development.

### 7. Watering

Keep moist. Grass likes consistent moisture.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 10-14 days, height 12-18cm, bright green blades

**Yield:** 800g-1.5kg from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

Cut at soil level. Use quickly or juice immediately. 3-5 days refrigerated.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Bare patches, many seeds still hard and unsprouted after the blackout should have ended.

**Causes:**
- Under-soaking - this crop needs yes (8-12 hours), and a short soak leaves the seed coat intact
- Over-soaking, which drowns the seed and starts rot before it can sprout
- Old seed, or seed stored warm and humid
- Medium dried out during blackout

**Solution:**
- Soak for the full stated time - yes (8-12 hours) - in cool water, then drain and rinse thoroughly
- Sow at 150-200g per 10x20 tray so seeds sit in contact with medium, not stacked on each other
- Keep the medium consistently damp through blackout, and hold 18-22°C
- Test a small batch before committing a full tray

**Prevention:** Buy seed dated for the current season and store it cool, dark and dry.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 3-4 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**

![Root hairs, fine and even on every stem, beside patchy cobwebby mould](/guides/diagrams/root-hairs-vs-mould.svg)

- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 150-200g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 3-4 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 3-4 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 10-14 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Harvesting Roots and Medium With the Cut

**Symptoms:** Cut grass tangled with root mat and bits of medium.

**Causes:**
- Cut too low, into the root mat rather than above it

**Solution:**
- Cut about a centimetre above the medium - grass regrows from the base, so a high cut is fine
- Use sharp scissors and cut in small handfuls rather than dragging through the tray
- A dense mat is normal for a grass and is not a fault

**Prevention:** Oat grass will often give a second cut if the first is taken high enough.

## Pro Tips

1. **Milder Than Wheat** - Good alternative for wheatgrass-sensitive customers.

2. **Not Rolled Oats** - Must use whole oats, not processed.

3. **Pet Grass** - Popular for cats and dogs - secondary market.

4. **Juice Quickly** - Best used fresh or juiced immediately.

5. **Affordable** - Oat seeds are very economical.
