## Overview

Sesame microgreens are a specialty crop that captures the subtle nutty flavor of sesame in microgreen form. The tiny seeds produce delicate shoots that hint at the familiar sesame taste.

Growing sesame is challenging - the seeds are small, germination can be uneven, and the plants are delicate. Sesame also prefers warm conditions and doesn't tolerate cool temperatures well.

For growers serving Asian cuisine markets, sesame microgreens offer authentic flavor that complements sesame-heavy dishes.

## Best Uses

- Asian cuisine
- Sesame-flavored dishes
- Sushi accompaniment
- Middle Eastern cuisine
- Specialty flavor applications

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Raw, unhulled sesame seeds
- Not toasted (won't germinate)
- Black or white sesame both work

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Use within 1-2 years

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes

Soak 4-8 hours, drain thoroughly.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Fine seed-starting mix

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-40g per 10x20 tray

Spread evenly, do not cover.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-5 days

Keep WARM (22-28°C). Sesame likes heat.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When germination visible

Moderate light, warm conditions.

### 7. Watering

Keep moist but not wet. Delicate seedlings.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 12-18 days, height 4-6cm, leaves developed

**Yield:** 100-200g from 10x20 tray (low)

### 9. Post-Harvest

Very delicate. Refrigerate, 5-7 days.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Bare patches, many seeds still hard and unsprouted after the blackout should have ended.

**Causes:**
- Under-soaking - this crop needs yes (4-8 hours), and a short soak leaves the seed coat intact
- Over-soaking, which drowns the seed and starts rot before it can sprout
- Old seed, or seed stored warm and humid
- Medium dried out during blackout

**Solution:**
- Soak for the full stated time - yes (4-8 hours) - in cool water, then drain and rinse thoroughly
- Sow at 25-40g per 10x20 tray so seeds sit in contact with medium, not stacked on each other
- Keep the medium consistently damp through blackout, and hold 22-28°C (warm!)
- Test a small batch before committing a full tray

**Prevention:** Buy seed dated for the current season and store it cool, dark and dry.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 4-5 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**

![Root hairs, fine and even on every stem, beside patchy cobwebby mould](/guides/diagrams/root-hairs-vs-mould.svg)

- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 25-40g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 4-5 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 4-5 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 12-18 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Bitterness in an Over-Mature Tray

**Symptoms:** Sharp or bitter flavour, tough stems.

**Causes:**
- Left past its harvest window - sesame turns bitter as it matures
- Grown too cool, so the crop sat in the tray longer than it should

**Solution:**
- Harvest at the stated window rather than waiting for more size
- Keep it genuinely warm - this crop wants the warm end of the range and stalls below it
- Taste a few shoots daily as harvest approaches

**Prevention:** Sesame has a narrower good-flavour window than most crops here. Harvest on time rather than on size.

## Pro Tips

1. **Warmth Essential** - Sesame is tropical. Keep warm throughout.

2. **Raw Seeds Only** - Toasted sesame won't germinate.

3. **Black Sesame** - Black variety may show color in stems.

4. **Asian Market** - Natural fit with sesame-heavy Asian cuisines.

5. **Challenging Crop** - Not for beginners. Master basics first.
