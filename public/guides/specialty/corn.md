# Field Corn Microgreens

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Days to Harvest** | 10-14 days |
| **Blackout Period** | 4-5 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | Yes (12-24 hours) |
| **Seed Density** | 200-350g per 10x20 tray |
| **Expected Yield** | 6-10:1 ratio |
| **Flavor Profile** | Mild corn sweetness, larger shoots than popcorn |

## Overview

Field corn (dent corn, flint corn) produces larger, more substantial shoots than popcorn while maintaining mild corn flavor. Decorative varieties like Indian corn or blue corn can produce colorful shoots with purple or red tints.

Growing is identical to popcorn - straightforward with high yields. The main advantage of field corn varieties is the potential for color (from pigmented varieties) and larger shoot size.

Field corn is even more economical than popcorn as seeds are available in bulk from feed stores.

## Best Uses

- Colored microgreens (from pigmented varieties)
- Substantial corn shoots
- Very economical production
- Bulk applications
- Southwestern cuisine

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Field corn, dent corn, or decorative corn
- Blue corn or Indian corn for color
- Untreated seed (important!)
- Feed corn works but ensure no chemical treatment

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Years of viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes - larger kernels need longer

**Process:**
1. Rinse thoroughly
2. Soak 12-24 hours
3. Kernels should feel soft
4. Drain completely

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Coco coir, soil, or hemp mat

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 200-350g per 10x20 tray

Single dense layer, press in firmly.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-5 days

Cover, keep warm (20-25°C), moist.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When shoots 5-8cm tall

Light brings out color in pigmented varieties.

### 7. Watering

Keep moist. Large seeds hold moisture - don't overwater.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 10-14 days, height 15-20cm, before toughening

**Yield:** 1.5-3kg from 10x20 tray (excellent)

### 9. Post-Harvest

Cut at soil level. Refrigerate, 3-5 days shelf life.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Bare patches, many seeds still hard and unsprouted after the blackout should have ended.

**Causes:**
- Under-soaking - this crop needs yes (12-24 hours), and a short soak leaves the seed coat intact
- Over-soaking, which drowns the seed and starts rot before it can sprout
- Old seed, or seed stored warm and humid
- Medium dried out during blackout

**Solution:**
- Soak for the full stated time - yes (12-24 hours) - in cool water, then drain and rinse thoroughly
- Sow at 200-350g per 10x20 tray so seeds sit in contact with medium, not stacked on each other
- Keep the medium consistently damp through blackout, and hold 20-28°C
- Test a small batch before committing a full tray

**Prevention:** Buy seed dated for the current season and store it cool, dark and dry.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 4-5 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**
- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 200-350g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 4-5 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 4-5 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 10-14 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Hulls Riding Up on the Shoots

**Symptoms:** Seed hulls stuck on the tips of the shoots instead of left behind in the medium.

**Causes:**
- Seed not weighted during blackout, so shoots lifted the hull with them
- Sown too shallow, with nothing for the seed to push against

**Solution:**
- Weight the tray during blackout - a second tray with a few kilos on it is standard for corn
- Sow into a well-levelled medium and press the seed in
- Mist heavily and rub hulls off by hand before harvest if any remain

**Prevention:** Corn is grown under weight for exactly this reason. It is not optional for a clean tray.

## Pro Tips

1. **Blue Corn Color** - Blue and red corn varieties can produce tinted shoots.

2. **Untreated Seed** - Ensure seeds aren't treated with fungicides.

3. **Very Economical** - Feed store prices for bulk corn are minimal.

4. **Larger Than Popcorn** - More substantial shoots for some applications.

5. **Light for Color** - Pigmented varieties need light to develop color.
