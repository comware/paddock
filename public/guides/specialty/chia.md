# Chia Microgreens

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Days to Harvest** | 10-14 days |
| **Blackout Period** | 3-4 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | No (mucilaginous!) |
| **Seed Density** | 25-40g per 10x20 tray |
| **Expected Yield** | 5-7:1 ratio |
| **Flavor Profile** | Mild, slightly grassy, with superfood status |

## Overview

Chia microgreens share the same mucilaginous challenge as flax - the seeds form a thick gel coating when wet that can prevent germination. This is the same property that makes chia pudding popular, but it makes microgreen growing tricky.

Chia's "superfood" marketing status makes it attractive to health-conscious customers. The microgreens are mild-flavored with good nutrition, and the name recognition helps with sales.

Growing chia requires the same careful moisture management as flax.

## Best Uses

- Superfood marketing
- Health food market
- Smoothie addition
- Mild microgreen mix component
- Marketing-driven sales

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Chia seeds (Salvia hispanica)
- Tiny, oval, speckled gray/black seeds
- Food-grade is fine

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- 2-4 years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** NO - Do NOT pre-soak!

Pre-soaking creates impossible-to-handle gel mass.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Well-draining coco coir or terracotta saucer

**Note:** Some growers use unglazed terracotta trays which absorb excess moisture.

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-40g per 10x20 tray

**Critical technique:**
1. Spread dry seeds thinly and evenly
2. Mist VERY lightly
3. Gel will form immediately
4. Don't add more water until needed

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

Good airflow, moderate humidity, patience.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When shoots emerge

Moderate light, 12-14 hours.

### 7. Watering

Bottom watering only once established. Minimal water.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 10-14 days, height 4-6cm, small leaves developed

**Yield:** 100-250g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

Rinse if needed. Refrigerate, 5-7 days.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Uneven stand, bare patches, seeds visible on the surface still unsprouted.

**Causes:**
- Surface dried out during blackout - this seed is sown dry and has no soaked reserve to fall back on
- Seed sown too thickly, so the top layer never reaches the medium
- Medium too cold to start
- Old seed

**Solution:**
- Mist once or twice a day through blackout so the top few millimetres never dry
- Sow at 25-40g per 10x20 tray, spread evenly rather than heaped
- Keep the tray and hold 18-22°C
- Press seed gently into the medium so every seed makes contact

**Prevention:** Weigh the seed rather than eyeballing it - dense patches are the usual cause of an uneven stand.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 3-4 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**
- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 25-40g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 3-4 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 3-4 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 10-14 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Gel Coat Mistaken for Mould

**Symptoms:** A clear, jelly-like film over the seed within minutes of the first watering.

**Causes:**
- This is mucilage, and it is what chia seed does when wet - it is not a fault and not mould

**Solution:**
- Leave it alone. The gel holds moisture against the seed and is how chia germinates.
- **Never pre-soak chia.** Soaked chia becomes a solid gel mass that cannot be spread or drained.
- Sow dry onto a damp medium and mist from above - do not bottom-water into the gel
- If you genuinely suspect mould, look for a musty smell and cobwebby texture; mucilage is odourless and smooth

**Prevention:** Expect the gel. It is the single most common reason a first chia tray gets thrown away unnecessarily.

## Pro Tips

1. **Never Soak** - Same rule as flax. No pre-soaking.

2. **Superfood Status** - Name recognition helps sales.

3. **Terracotta Trick** - Unglazed terracotta absorbs excess moisture.

4. **Thin Seeding** - Less is more with mucilaginous seeds.

5. **Patience** - Germination through gel is slow.
