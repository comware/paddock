## Overview

Quinoa microgreens leverage the powerful "superfood" marketing status of quinoa while being relatively easy to grow. The small seeds produce delicate shoots with mild flavor and the name recognition that drives health-conscious consumers.

Growing quinoa is straightforward - the main consideration is rinsing seeds well to remove saponins (bitter coating) before planting. The plants grow quickly and reliably.

The combination of easy growing and strong marketing appeal makes quinoa an attractive crop for health food markets.

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Quinoa seeds (white, red, or black)
- Unwashed preferred (can rinse yourself)
- Food-grade quinoa works

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- 2-3 years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes - also removes saponins

**Process:**
1. Rinse thoroughly multiple times
2. Water should run clear (removing saponins)
3. Soak 4-8 hours
4. Rinse again, drain completely

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Fine coco coir or seed-starting mix

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-40g per 10x20 tray

Spread evenly, press lightly. Small seeds.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

Standard conditions, 18-22°C, moist.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When germination complete

Moderate light, 12-14 hours. Red quinoa may show color.

### 7. Watering

Keep moist. Gentle watering for small seeds.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 10-14 days, height 5-8cm, leaves developed

**Yield:** 150-300g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

Handle gently - delicate. Refrigerate, 5-7 days.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Bare patches, many seeds still hard and unsprouted after the blackout should have ended.

**Causes:**
- Under-soaking - this crop needs yes (4-8 hours), and a short soak leaves the seed coat intact
- Over-soaking, which drowns the seed and starts rot before it can sprout
- Old seed, or seed stored warm and humid
- Medium dried out during blackout

**Solution:**
- Soak for the full stated time - yes (4-8 hours) - in cool water, then drain and rinse thoroughly
- Sow at 25-40g per 10x20 tray so seeds sit in contact with medium, not stacked on each other
- Keep the medium consistently damp through blackout, and hold 18-22°C
- Test a small batch before committing a full tray

**Prevention:** Buy seed dated for the current season and store it cool, dark and dry.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 3-4 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**
- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 25-40g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 3-4 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 3-4 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 10-14 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Bitter, Soapy Flavour

**Symptoms:** A soapy or bitter aftertaste in an otherwise healthy-looking tray.

**Causes:**
- Saponin, the natural bitter coating on quinoa seed, has not been washed off

**Solution:**
- Rinse the seed thoroughly before sowing - run water over it until the water no longer foams
- Rinse again after soaking
- Harvest on time; flavour sharpens in an over-mature tray

**Prevention:** Rinse until the water runs clear and stops foaming. Foam is the visible test for saponin.
