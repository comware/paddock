# Anise Microgreens

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Days to Harvest** | 14-21 days |
| **Blackout Period** | 4-5 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | Yes (8-12 hours) |
| **Seed Density** | 25-40g per 10x20 tray |
| **Expected Yield** | 4-6:1 ratio |
| **Flavor Profile** | Sweet licorice/anise flavor, distinctive and aromatic |

## Overview

Anise microgreens deliver concentrated sweet licorice flavor from the same plant that produces anise seeds used in baking and liqueurs. The microgreens have feathery leaves and the characteristic anise aroma that's either loved or disliked.

Growing anise is moderately challenging - similar to fennel and dill in requirements. The flavor is stronger and sweeter than fennel, making it more specialized in its applications.

For customers who specifically want licorice notes, anise microgreens are the purest expression of that flavor.

## Best Uses

- Licorice flavor applications
- Dessert garnish
- Mediterranean cuisine
- Liqueur cocktails
- Asian cuisine (Vietnamese especially)
- Specialty flavor seekers

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Anise seeds (Pimpinella anisum)
- Small, ridged, gray-brown seeds
- Fresh seeds for best germination

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Use within 2 years

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes

Soak 8-12 hours, drain.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Fine coco coir or soil

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-40g per 10x20 tray

Spread evenly, press gently.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-5 days

Standard conditions.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When germination visible

Moderate light, 12-14 hours.

### 7. Watering

Keep moist. Gentle watering for delicate plants.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 14-21 days, height 5-8cm, feathery leaves

**Yield:** 100-200g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

Delicate handling. Refrigerate, 5-7 days.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Bare patches, many seeds still hard and unsprouted after the blackout should have ended.

**Causes:**
- Under-soaking - this crop needs yes (8-12 hours), and a short soak leaves the seed coat intact
- Over-soaking, which drowns the seed and starts rot before it can sprout
- Old seed, or seed stored warm and humid
- Medium dried out during blackout

**Solution:**
- Soak for the full stated time - yes (8-12 hours) - in cool water, then drain and rinse thoroughly
- Sow at 25-40g per 10x20 tray so seeds sit in contact with medium, not stacked on each other
- Keep the medium consistently damp through blackout, and hold 18-22°C
- Test a small batch before committing a full tray

**Prevention:** Buy seed dated for the current season and store it cool, dark and dry.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 4-5 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**
- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 25-40g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 4-5 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 4-5 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 14-21 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Stalling Part-Way Through

**Symptoms:** Growth that starts well then sits still for days.

**Causes:**
- This crop is genuinely slow, and a stall is often normal rather than a fault
- Too cold, which stretches an already long cycle
- Medium dried out mid-cycle

**Solution:**
- Check the expected harvest window before deciding something is wrong
- Hold the warmer end of the germination range
- Keep moisture even - a slow crop has more days in which to dry out
- Resist resowing early; slow does not mean failed

**Prevention:** Judge this crop against its own timeline, not against a fast brassica in the next tray.

## Pro Tips
