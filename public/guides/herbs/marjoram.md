## Overview

Marjoram microgreens offer the sweet, delicate flavor of marjoram - like oregano's gentler cousin. The tiny seeds produce small, tender plants with the distinctive marjoram aroma that's prized in Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cuisines.

Growing marjoram microgreens is challenging - very tiny seeds, slow germination, and low yields. The plants are delicate and require careful handling. This makes marjoram a specialty item for experienced growers.

For chefs who specifically want marjoram flavor, the microgreens offer a fresh expression of this classic herb.

## Best Uses

- Mediterranean cuisine
- Middle Eastern dishes
- Meat dishes (especially lamb)
- Italian cuisine
- Delicate herb flavor
- Fine dining

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Marjoram seeds (Origanum majorana)
- Very tiny seeds
- Fresh seeds important

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Use within 1-2 years

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Optional - seeds are very small

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Very fine seed-starting mix

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 15-25g per 10x20 tray (tiny seeds!)

Use shaker or mix with sand for distribution. Do not cover.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 5-7 days

Keep at 20-25°C (warmer helps), mist very gently.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When germination visible

Moderate light, 12-14 hours.

### 7. Watering

Very gentle misting. Tiny seeds wash away easily.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 18-25 days, height 3-5cm, leaves developed

**Yield:** 50-100g from 10x20 tray (very low)

### 9. Post-Harvest

Extremely delicate. Refrigerate, 5-7 days.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Uneven stand, bare patches, seeds visible on the surface still unsprouted.

**Causes:**
- Surface dried out during blackout - this seed is sown dry and has no soaked reserve to fall back on
- Seed sown too thickly, so the top layer never reaches the medium
- Medium too cold to start
- Old seed

**Solution:**
- Mist once or twice a day through blackout so the top few millimetres never dry
- Sow at 15-25g per 10x20 tray, spread evenly rather than heaped
- Keep the tray and hold 20-25°C
- Press seed gently into the medium so every seed makes contact

**Prevention:** Weigh the seed rather than eyeballing it - dense patches are the usual cause of an uneven stand.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 5-7 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**
- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 15-25g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 5-7 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 5-7 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 18-25 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Stalling Part-Way Through

**Symptoms:** Growth that starts well then sits still for days.

**Causes:**
- This crop is genuinely slow, and a stall is often normal rather than a fault
- Too cold, which stretches an already long cycle
- Medium dried out mid-cycle

**Solution:**
- Check the expected harvest window before deciding something is wrong
- Hold the warmer end of the germination range
- Keep moisture even - a slow crop has more days in which to dry out
- Resist resowing early; slow does not mean failed

**Prevention:** Judge this crop against its own timeline, not against a fast brassica in the next tray.

## Pro Tips

1. **Tiny Seeds** - Like dust. Mix with sand for even spreading.

2. **Warmer Germination** - Needs warmth to germinate (unlike many herbs).

3. **Very Low Yield** - Premium pricing essential.

4. **Oregano Alternative** - Gentler, sweeter than oregano.

5. **Specialty Only** - Not for high-volume production.
