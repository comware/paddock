## Overview

Mint microgreens are one of the most challenging crops due to extremely slow germination, dust-like seeds, and very low yields. True mint (Mentha species) seeds are notoriously difficult - most commercial mint is grown from cuttings rather than seed for good reason.

When successful, mint microgreens deliver authentic mint flavor in miniature form. The challenge is whether the effort is worthwhile given the difficulty and low yields.

Most growers find that sourcing fresh mint from herb suppliers is more practical than growing mint microgreens. This guide exists for completeness, but expect significant challenges.

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- True mint seeds (peppermint or spearmint)
- Extremely tiny, dust-like seeds
- Fresh seeds critical

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Use within 1 year (poor viability)

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** No - seeds are too tiny to handle when wet

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Very fine seed-starting mix, perfectly smooth

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 5-10g per 10x20 tray (minimal!)

**Critical technique:**
1. Mix seeds with fine sand for distribution
2. Use shaker or sieve
3. Do NOT cover - needs light
4. Mist extremely gently

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 7-10 days (minimal darkness - mint needs light)

Use very loose cover or skip blackout entirely.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** Immediately or when any germination seen

Moderate light, 12-14 hours. Light helps germination.

### 7. Watering

Extremely gentle misting only. Seeds wash away with any force.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 21-28 days, height 3-5cm, mint leaves visible

**Yield:** 30-75g from 10x20 tray (very low)

### 9. Post-Harvest

Very delicate. Refrigerate, 5-7 days shelf life.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: No Germination

**Symptoms:** Nothing happens

**Causes:** Old seeds, covered too deeply, too dark

**Solution:** Fresh seeds, surface sow, provide light

**Prevention:** Accept that mint from seed is very difficult

### Problem 2: Seeds Wash Away

**Symptoms:** Seeds cluster, bare patches

**Causes:** Watering too forcefully

**Solution:** Mist only, extremely gently

**Prevention:** Bottom water once established, use spray bottle on finest setting

## Pro Tips

1. **Consider Alternatives** - Fresh mint from herb suppliers may be more practical.

2. **Extremely Challenging** - This is expert-level growing. Expect failures.

3. **Light for Germination** - Unlike most microgreens, mint needs light to germinate.

4. **Low Yield Reality** - Very low yields mean very premium pricing needed.

5. **Fresh Seeds Only** - Old mint seeds simply won't germinate.

## Market Notes

**Honest Assessment:**
Mint microgreens are technically possible but often not practical. The extremely low yields, long cycle, and high failure rate make this a specialty-within-a-specialty. Consider whether sourcing conventional mint might better serve your business needs.
