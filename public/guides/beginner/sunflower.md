# Sunflower Microgreens

## Quick Facts

|-----------|-------|

| **Blackout Period** | 3-4 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | Yes (8-12 hours) |

| **Expected Yield** | 6-8:1 ratio |
| **Flavor Profile** | Nutty, crunchy, mild sunflower seed taste |

## Overview

Sunflower microgreens are one of the most popular and beginner-friendly varieties to grow. They produce tall, sturdy shoots with a satisfying crunch and a flavor reminiscent of raw sunflower seeds with a slightly nutty, earthy taste. The large seeds are easy to handle, germinate reliably, and grow quickly.

These microgreens are nutritionally dense, packed with protein, healthy fats, vitamins E and B, and essential minerals like zinc and selenium. Their substantial size and satisfying texture make them a favorite for salads, sandwiches, and as a garnish.

Sunflower microgreens are forgiving of minor mistakes, making them the ideal starting point for new growers. Success with sunflower builds confidence for tackling more challenging varieties.

## Best Uses

- Fresh in salads as a hearty, crunchy base
- Sandwiches and wraps for texture
- Smoothie bowls and Buddha bowls
- Garnish for soups and main dishes
- Juicing (high yield, mild flavor)

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Black oil sunflower seeds (not striped confectionery type)
- Seeds should be plump, uniform in size, and free of debris
- Purchase from reputable microgreen seed suppliers
- Avoid bird seed - often treated or low germination

**Storage:**
- Keep in a cool, dry place (15-20°C)
- Store in airtight container away from light
- Properly stored seeds remain viable for 2-3 years
- Check germination rate if seeds are older than 1 year

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes - sunflower seeds have hard shells that benefit from soaking.

**Process:**
1. Measure 80-120g of seeds for a 10x20 tray
2. Place in a bowl or jar with 3x volume of water
3. Soak for 8-12 hours (overnight works well)
4. Water should be room temperature (18-22°C)
5. Seeds will plump up and may split slightly - this is normal
6. Drain and rinse before planting
7. Do not soak longer than 12 hours (risk of fermentation)

**Tip:** If seeds float, they may be empty - skim these off.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium options:**
- Coco coir (recommended for beginners)
- Soil-based potting mix
- Hemp or jute mats for cleaner harvest

**Setup:**
1. Use a 10x20 tray with drainage holes (growing tray)
2. Place inside a solid tray (water tray) for bottom watering
3. Add 2-3cm of moistened growing medium
4. Level the surface - avoid compacting
5. Medium should be moist but not waterlogged
6. Pre-moisten before seeding

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 80-120g per 10x20 tray (dense but single layer)

**Technique:**
1. Spread pre-soaked seeds evenly across the surface
2. Seeds should be touching but not piled on top of each other
3. Gently press seeds into the medium for good contact
4. Do NOT cover with additional medium
5. Mist the surface lightly

**Common mistake:** Over-seeding leads to poor air circulation and mold.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

**Method:**
1. Cover with another tray (inverted or humidity dome)
2. Place a weight on top (1-2kg) - this strengthens stems
3. Keep in a dark location or cover stack with cloth
4. Temperature: 18-24°C ideal
5. Check daily and mist if surface is drying

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Seeds swell, radicles (roots) emerge
- Day 2-3: White shoots appear, lifting the cover
- Day 3-4: Yellow/pale shoots 3-5cm tall, pushing hard against weight

**Remove from blackout when:** Shoots are 3-5cm tall and pushing against the cover tray.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** After 3-4 days in blackout, when shoots are 3-5cm tall.

**Light requirements:**
- Natural light: Bright indirect or direct sunlight (4-6 hours)
- Artificial: Full-spectrum LED, 12-16 hours/day
- Position 15-30cm from light source

**What happens:**
- Yellow shoots turn green within 24-48 hours (photosynthesis begins)
- Cotyledons (seed leaves) fully open
- Growth rate accelerates
- Seed hulls shed naturally (some may need help)

**Tip:** Rotate trays daily for even growth if using side-lit setup.

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering preferred

**Technique:**
1. Add water to the bottom tray (1-2cm)
2. Allow medium to absorb for 15-30 minutes
3. Drain excess - roots should not sit in standing water
4. Water when medium surface feels dry to touch

**Frequency:**
- Blackout phase: Mist once daily or as needed
- Light phase: Bottom water once or twice daily
- Adjust based on temperature and humidity

**Signs of problems:**
- Wilting = underwatered (water immediately)
- Yellowing at base = overwatered (reduce frequency)
- Mold = too wet, poor airflow

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 10-14 days from seeding (7-10 days in light)
- Height: 10-15cm
- Cotyledons (seed leaves) fully expanded
- True leaves just starting to emerge (optional - more tender before)
- Most seed hulls have fallen off

**Technique:**
1. Harvest in the morning for best crunch and shelf life
2. Use sharp scissors or knife
3. Cut just above the soil/medium line
4. Cut in sections or harvest entire tray
5. Avoid pulling - this disturbs roots and adds debris

**Yield:** Expect 400-600g from a 10x20 tray (6-8x seed weight)

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Remove any remaining seed hulls (pick by hand or gentle shake)
2. Rinse in cold water only if necessary
3. Dry thoroughly - excess moisture reduces shelf life
4. Use a salad spinner or spread on clean towels

**Storage:**
- Place in container lined with paper towel
- Store in refrigerator (2-4°C)
- Shelf life: 7-10 days when properly stored
- Do not wash until ready to use

**Tip:** Harvest only what you need; greens stay fresher on the tray.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor Germination

**Symptoms:** Patchy growth, many ungerminated seeds

**Causes:**
- Old or improperly stored seeds
- Inadequate pre-soaking
- Temperature too cold or too hot
- Medium too dry or too wet

**Solution:**
- Ensure fresh seeds from reliable source
- Soak for full 8-12 hours
- Maintain 20-24°C during germination
- Keep medium consistently moist but not soggy

**Prevention:** Test germination rate before planting full trays.

### Problem 2: Mold/Fungus

**Symptoms:** White fuzzy growth, slimy stems, musty smell

**Causes:**
- Overwatering
- Poor air circulation
- Overcrowded seeding
- High humidity with stagnant air

**Solution:**
- Improve airflow immediately (fan on low)
- Reduce watering frequency
- Remove affected areas if localized
- Hydrogen peroxide spray (3%) can help

**Prevention:**
- Do not overwater
- Ensure adequate spacing
- Use clean trays and fresh medium
- Consider small fan for air movement

### Problem 3: Leggy/Weak Stems

**Symptoms:** Tall, thin, pale stems that fall over

**Causes:**
- Insufficient light
- Light too far away
- Too warm during growth
- Not enough weight during blackout

**Solution:**
- Move closer to light source
- Increase light duration
- Lower growing temperature
- Use adequate weight (1-2kg) during blackout

**Prevention:** Proper blackout weight and strong light from day 1.

### Problem 4: Hulls Not Shedding

**Symptoms:** Seed coats stuck on cotyledons

**Causes:**
- Insufficient humidity during growth
- Seeds not fully soaked
- Low-quality seeds with tough hulls

**Solution:**
- Mist heavily and gently brush or shake
- Pick off by hand for market-quality harvest
- Some hulls shed naturally as greens dry

**Prevention:**
- Ensure thorough pre-soaking
- Maintain humidity during early growth
- Use quality seeds

### Problem 5: Yellowing Greens

**Symptoms:** Yellow or pale cotyledons in light phase

**Causes:**
- Insufficient light
- Overwatering (root damage)
- Nutrient deficiency (rare in microgreens)
- Disease

**Solution:**
- Increase light intensity or duration
- Check watering - allow medium to dry slightly
- Ensure good drainage

**Prevention:** Strong light and proper watering from the start.

## Pro Tips

1. **Weight is Key** - Using 1-2kg weight during blackout produces thicker, stronger stems. This stress makes the seedlings push harder, resulting in sturdier greens with better texture.

2. **Staggered Planting** - Plant a new tray every 3-4 days for continuous harvest. This ensures you always have fresh sunflower microgreens at peak quality.

3. **Hull Removal Hack** - Mist greens and run your hand gently across the tops. Most loose hulls will stick to your hand or fall off. A quick shake of harvested greens also helps.

4. **Early Harvest Option** - Harvest when cotyledons just open for more tender, delicate greens. Wait for true leaves for more robust flavor and texture.

5. **Save the Best Seeds** - If you find a seed source that performs exceptionally, buy in bulk. Germination rates can vary significantly between batches.

## Scheduling Recommendations

For continuous supply:
- **Small scale (personal use):** Plant 1 tray every 5-7 days
- **Market/restaurant supply:** Plant 2-3 trays every 3-4 days
- **Peak freshness:** Harvest 1-2 days before selling/serving
