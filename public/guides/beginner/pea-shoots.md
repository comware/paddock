## Overview

Pea shoots are a grower favorite, producing beautiful curling tendrils with a fresh, sweet pea flavor. They're one of the most forgiving microgreens for beginners, with large seeds that are easy to handle and plants that tolerate a range of growing conditions.

The shoots are highly versatile in the kitchen - eaten raw in salads, added to stir-fries, or used as an elegant garnish. Their sweet flavor and attractive tendrils make them popular with chefs and home cooks alike.

Pea shoots contain high levels of vitamin C, beta-carotene, folate, and fiber. They're also one of the highest-yielding microgreens by weight, making them economical to grow.

## Best Uses

- Fresh salads (standalone or mixed)
- Stir-fries (add at the last moment)
- Sandwich and wrap filling
- Garnish for soups and Asian dishes
- Juicing blends

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Whole dried peas (green or yellow)
- Specifically labeled for sprouting/microgreens
- Avoid split peas (will not germinate)
- Organic preferred to avoid treatment chemicals

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location (15-20°C)
- Airtight container away from light
- Keeps 2-3 years when properly stored

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes - peas have hard seed coats.

**Process:**
1. Measure 100-150g of seeds per 10x20 tray
2. Rinse seeds to remove dust
3. Soak in 3x volume of room temperature water
4. Duration: 8-12 hours (overnight)
5. Seeds will swell to nearly double size
6. Drain and rinse thoroughly before planting

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium options:**
- Soil or coco coir (best yields)
- Hemp mats work but may need extra watering

**Setup:**
1. Fill tray with 2-3cm of moistened medium
2. Level surface without compacting
3. Pre-moisten thoroughly

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 100-150g per 10x20 tray

**Technique:**
1. Spread soaked seeds evenly in single layer
2. Seeds can touch but not pile up
3. Press gently into medium
4. Do NOT cover with additional medium
5. Mist surface lightly

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

**Method:**
1. Cover with inverted tray or dome
2. Add light weight (0.5-1kg) - less than sunflower
3. Keep in dark at 18-22°C
4. Mist daily to maintain moisture

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Roots emerge, seeds swell more
- Day 2-3: White shoots appear
- Day 3-4: Shoots 5-7cm, beginning to curl

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When shoots are 5-7cm and pushing cover

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate to bright light
- 12-16 hours per day
- Direct sunlight or full-spectrum LED

**What happens:**
- Shoots green up within 24-48 hours
- Tendrils begin to form and curl
- Growth accelerates significantly

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering preferred

**Frequency:**
- Blackout: Mist once daily
- Light phase: Bottom water 1-2x daily
- Peas drink more than most microgreens

**Tip:** Never let medium dry out completely - peas wilt quickly.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 10-14 days from seeding
- Height: 10-15cm with developed tendrils
- Before first true leaves fully develop
- Tendrils just starting to reach for support

**Technique:**
1. Cut just above soil line with sharp scissors
2. Harvest entire tray or in sections
3. Can do a second cut (regrowth) but quality is lower

**Yield:** 500-800g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Minimal washing needed if grown cleanly
2. Dry gently - pea shoots bruise easily
3. Handle with care - tendrils are delicate

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate in breathable container
- Line with paper towel
- Shelf life: 5-7 days
- Eat soon for best flavor and crunch

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Root Rot

**Symptoms:** Brown, mushy roots; wilting despite wet medium

**Causes:** Overwatering, poor drainage, seeds sitting in water

**Solution:** Improve drainage, reduce watering, ensure tray drains completely

**Prevention:** Use well-draining medium, never let roots sit in standing water

### Problem 2: Mold

**Symptoms:** White fuzzy growth between seeds/stems

**Causes:** Too wet, poor airflow, overcrowded seeds

**Solution:** Increase airflow, reduce watering, remove affected areas

**Prevention:** Proper spacing, good air circulation, avoid overwatering

### Problem 3: Sparse Germination

**Symptoms:** Many ungerminated seeds

**Causes:** Old seeds, insufficient soaking, too cold

**Solution:** Use fresh seeds, soak full 8-12 hours, maintain 18-22°C

**Prevention:** Test germination rate, ensure proper pre-soaking

## Pro Tips

1. **Don't Skimp on Soaking** - Full 8-12 hour soak makes a big difference in germination rate and speed.

2. **Second Harvest Possible** - Pea shoots can regrow for a second (smaller) harvest if cut above the lowest node.

3. **Tendril Timing** - Harvest when tendrils are curling but before they grab onto things. This is peak tenderness.

4. **Cool Growing** - Peas prefer cooler temps than most microgreens. Hot conditions (>25°C) reduce quality.

5. **Great Mixer** - Pea shoots blend well with almost any other microgreen variety for mixed salads.

## Scheduling Recommendations

- **Continuous harvest:** Plant every 5-7 days
- **Pea shoots grow slightly slower in winter** - adjust schedule accordingly
- **Summer tip:** Grow in coolest part of your space

## Companion Planting

Grows well with:
- **Sunflower** - Similar schedule
- **Buckwheat** - Similar requirements
- **Radish** - Faster, provides variety
