# Buckwheat Microgreens

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Days to Harvest** | 8-12 days |
| **Blackout Period** | 3-4 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | Yes (4-8 hours) |
| **Seed Density** | 80-100g per 10x20 tray |
| **Expected Yield** | 6-8:1 ratio |
| **Flavor Profile** | Mild, slightly tangy with tender heart-shaped leaves |

## Overview

Buckwheat microgreens produce beautiful heart-shaped cotyledons in a vibrant shade of green, sometimes with pink or red stems. Despite its name, buckwheat is not related to wheat and is naturally gluten-free, making it suitable for those with wheat sensitivities.

The flavor is mild with a slight tanginess - less assertive than sunflower or radish, making it an excellent base for salad mixes. The attractive leaf shape makes it popular for garnishing.

Buckwheat is one of the most affordable microgreens to grow due to low seed cost. It's also very forgiving, tolerating a range of conditions and bouncing back from minor neglect.

## Best Uses

- Salad base or mix component
- Garnish (beautiful leaf shape)
- Smoothies and juicing
- Light wraps and sandwiches
- Gluten-free dishes

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Whole buckwheat groats (hulled)
- Triangular seeds, tan/brown color
- Unhulled (black) buckwheat also works but has hull mess

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Can go rancid faster than other seeds (fatty)
- Use within 1-2 years for best results

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes - improves germination significantly

**Process:**
1. Measure 80-100g of seeds
2. Rinse to remove any debris
3. Soak in room temperature water 4-8 hours
4. Don't over-soak (produces slime)
5. Rinse thoroughly after soaking - seeds get slimy
6. Drain well before planting

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Coco coir or soil preferred (mats work but less ideal)

**Setup:** 2-3cm moistened medium, leveled surface

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 80-100g per 10x20 tray

**Technique:**
1. Spread evenly - seeds will stick together due to residual slime
2. Use fingers to separate clumps
3. Single layer, minimal overlap
4. Press gently into medium
5. Mist surface

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

**Method:**
1. Cover with tray, light weight optional
2. Keep in dark at 18-22°C
3. Mist daily - buckwheat likes moisture

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Seeds swell, roots emerge
- Day 2-3: Pale stems push up
- Day 3-4: Heart-shaped cotyledons forming

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When cotyledons are forming, stems 3-4cm

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate light is sufficient
- 12-14 hours per day
- Less light-demanding than some varieties

**What happens:**
- Leaves green up beautifully
- Heart shape becomes defined
- Some varieties develop pink/red stems

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering preferred (top misting disturbs delicate leaves)

**Frequency:**
- Keep consistently moist
- Buckwheat doesn't tolerate drying out well
- 1-2x daily depending on conditions

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 8-12 days from seeding
- Height: 8-12cm
- Cotyledons fully opened and developed
- Before true leaves emerge

**Technique:**
1. Cut above medium level
2. Heart-shaped leaves are delicate - handle gently
3. Harvest in morning for best texture

**Yield:** 400-600g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Very gentle handling required - bruises easily
2. Minimize washing - pat dry if needed
3. Best used fresh

**Storage:**
- Shorter shelf life than radish/sunflower (4-5 days)
- Keep refrigerated
- Use quickly for best quality

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Slimy/Sticky Seeds

**Symptoms:** Seeds clump together, slimy residue

**Causes:** Natural - buckwheat produces mucilage when wet

**Solution:** Rinse thoroughly after soaking, spread quickly before it sets

**Prevention:** Don't over-soak, rinse well, work quickly

### Problem 2: Mold Between Dense Seeds

**Symptoms:** White fuzz between germinating seeds

**Causes:** Poor airflow, too wet, seeds too dense

**Solution:** Improve airflow, reduce watering, thin if needed

**Prevention:** Proper spacing, good ventilation, don't overwater

### Problem 3: Slow Growth

**Symptoms:** Seeds germinate but growth stalls

**Causes:** Too cold, underwatered, poor quality seeds

**Solution:** Increase temperature, check moisture, try fresh seeds

**Prevention:** Maintain 18-22°C, consistent moisture, quality seeds

## Pro Tips

1. **Quick Rinse is Key** - Rinse soaked seeds well to remove slime. This makes even spreading much easier.

2. **Gentle Touch** - Heart-shaped leaves bruise easily. Handle minimally during harvest and packaging.

3. **Short Shelf Life** - Plan sales/use carefully. Best within 3-4 days of harvest.

4. **Beautiful Garnish** - The heart shapes are visually striking. Market this for garnish use.

5. **Affordable Grower** - Very low seed cost makes this economical even with shorter shelf life.

## Scheduling Recommendations

- **Continuous supply:** Plant every 4-5 days
- **Account for shorter shelf life** in planning
- **Good winter crop** - tolerates lower light

## Companion Planting

Grows well with:
- **Sunflower** - Similar schedule, different texture
- **Pea shoots** - Complementary flavors
- **Radish** - Add for color contrast
