## Overview

Red amaranth microgreens are grown primarily for their stunning color - deep magenta to red stems and leaves that create dramatic visual impact. The flavor is mild and earthy, similar to spinach but less pronounced. The color is what sells this variety.

Amaranth seeds are tiny (dust-like) and require careful handling. The plants are relatively slow growing and produce lower yields than brassicas, but the color commands premium pricing.

Red amaranth is popular in high-end restaurants, food photography, and anywhere dramatic color is valued.

## Best Uses

- Visual garnish (primary use)
- Color accent in salads
- Food photography
- Upscale plating
- Smoothies (adds color without strong flavor)

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Red amaranth variety (Red Garnet, Red Army, etc.)
- Tiny, black, shiny seeds (smaller than poppy seeds)
- Fresh seeds - amaranth viability drops quickly

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Use within 1 year for best results

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** No - seeds are too tiny

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Very fine coco coir or seed-starting mix

**Setup:**
1. 1cm of very fine, smooth medium
2. Pre-moisten well
3. Level surface critical

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 10-15g per 10x20 tray (extremely tiny seeds)

**Technique:**
1. Mix seeds with fine sand for easier distribution
2. Use shaker or sieve to spread
3. Do NOT cover - amaranth needs light to germinate
4. Mist very gently - seeds wash away easily

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days (minimal blackout)

**Method:**
1. Cover loosely - some light beneficial
2. Keep at 20-25°C (warmer than lettuce)
3. Mist very gently daily

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When germination begins

**Light requirements:**
- Bright light essential for color development
- 14-16 hours per day
- More light = deeper color

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Very gentle misting or bottom watering

**Frequency:**
- Keep moist but not wet
- Shallow roots are delicate
- Careful not to dislodge tiny seedlings

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 10-14 days from seeding
- Height: 4-6cm
- Deep red/magenta color developed
- Before leaves become tough

**Yield:** 50-100g from 10x20 tray (low yield)

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Very delicate - handle minimally
2. Color may stain hands slightly
3. Don't wash unless necessary

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate at 2-4°C
- Shelf life: 5-7 days
- Color holds well

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor Color Development

**Symptoms:** Green instead of red, pale color

**Causes:** Insufficient light

**Solution:** Move closer to lights, increase duration

**Prevention:** Bright light from day 1 of light phase

### Problem 2: Seeds Wash Away

**Symptoms:** Clumping, bare patches

**Causes:** Watering too heavily

**Solution:** Gentler watering, let establish before heavy watering

**Prevention:** Very gentle misting, bottom water when established

### Problem 3: Slow/Poor Germination

**Symptoms:** Sparse growth

**Causes:** Old seeds, covered too deeply, too cold

**Solution:** Fresh seeds, surface sow, warmer temps

**Prevention:** Seeds under 1 year old, never cover

## Pro Tips

1. **Color is Everything** - The value is in the color. Bright light is non-negotiable.

2. **Tiny Seeds = Care** - Handle seeds carefully. Mix with sand for easier spreading.

3. **Premium Pricing** - Low yield justifies premium prices. Market the visual impact.

4. **Photography Star** - Food photographers love red amaranth. Build those relationships.

5. **Mild Flavor** - Don't oversell the flavor - it's mild. Sell the visual impact.
