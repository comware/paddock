# Orach (Mountain Spinach) Microgreens

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Days to Harvest** | 12-16 days |
| **Blackout Period** | 4-5 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | Optional (4-8 hours) |
| **Seed Density** | 25-35g per 10x20 tray |
| **Expected Yield** | 5-7:1 ratio |
| **Flavor Profile** | Mild, spinach-like with striking red/purple varieties |

## Overview

Orach (also called mountain spinach or French spinach) produces microgreens with a mild, spinach-like flavor. The red and purple varieties are particularly striking, producing deep magenta to purple leaves that add dramatic color to dishes.

Unlike amaranth, orach is easier to grow with larger seeds and more vigorous growth. It's a good alternative to red amaranth for growers who want color but find amaranth too challenging.

Orach tolerates heat better than spinach, making it a useful summer alternative for spinach-like flavor.

## Best Uses

- Colorful salad component (red/purple varieties)
- Spinach alternative (especially in summer)
- Visual garnish
- Mild-flavored colored green

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Red orach or purple orach for color
- Green orach for spinach-like flavor without color
- Larger seeds than amaranth, papery covering

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- 2-3 years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Optional - 4-8 hour soak can help germination

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Coco coir or soil - not as demanding as amaranth

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-35g per 10x20 tray

Spread evenly, press gently, mist surface.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-5 days

Standard protocol - cover, 18-22°C, mist daily.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When stems 2-3cm

Bright light for best color development in red/purple varieties.

### 7. Watering

Bottom watering preferred. Keep moist.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 12-16 days, height 5-8cm, good color developed

**Yield:** 150-250g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

Handle gently to preserve leaves. Refrigerate, 5-7 days shelf life.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Uneven stand, bare patches, seeds visible on the surface still unsprouted.

**Causes:**
- Surface dried out during blackout - this seed is sown dry and has no soaked reserve to fall back on
- Seed sown too thickly, so the top layer never reaches the medium
- Medium too cold to start
- Old seed

**Solution:**
- Mist once or twice a day through blackout so the top few millimetres never dry
- Sow at 25-35g per 10x20 tray, spread evenly rather than heaped
- Keep the tray and hold 18-22°C
- Press seed gently into the medium so every seed makes contact

**Prevention:** Weigh the seed rather than eyeballing it - dense patches are the usual cause of an uneven stand.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 4-5 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**
- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 25-35g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 4-5 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 4-5 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 12-16 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

### Problem 4: Seed Washed Out of Position

**Symptoms:** Seed pooled at one end, bare patches where it was sown evenly.

**Causes:**
- Very fine seed moves when watered from above before it has rooted

**Solution:**
- Bottom-water from sowing rather than misting overhead
- If misting, use the finest setting
- Sow onto a pre-moistened medium so seed sticks where it lands

**Prevention:** Water from below until seedlings have rooted.

## Pro Tips

1. **Heat Tolerant** - Better summer performer than spinach.
2. **Easier Than Amaranth** - Similar color potential with easier growing.
3. **Purple Power** - Purple orach provides dramatic color impact.
4. **Mild Flavor** - Don't oversell flavor - it's subtle. Focus on color and nutrition.
