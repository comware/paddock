# Callistemon (Bottlebrush) Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy-Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed |
| **Time to Root** | 6-10 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 70-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Callistemons (bottlebrushes) are iconic Australian natives known for their distinctive cylindrical flower spikes that attract birds. Most species and cultivars propagate well from semi-hardwood cuttings, making it practical to multiply favorite varieties.

Seed propagation is also possible for species but produces variable offspring. Named cultivars like 'Captain Cook', 'Little John', and 'Dawson River Weeper' must be propagated vegetatively to maintain their characteristics.

Note: Some bottlebrushes have been reclassified to genus Melaleuca, but the propagation technique remains the same.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating bird-attracting gardens
- Multiplying compact cultivars
- Screening and hedging
- Replacing declining plants
- Sharing with other gardeners

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Reliable method with good success rates.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)
- Wood condition: Current growth, slightly firmed

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth, semi-hardened
   - 10-15cm lengths
   - Non-flowering shoots preferred
   - Healthy, typical foliage

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node at base
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep foliage at top
   - May wound base lightly

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Medium to strong formula
   - Recommended for consistent results

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 4-5cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Warm conditions (20-25°C)
   - Moderate humidity
   - Bright light
   - Bottom heat helpful

6. **Ongoing care** (6-10 weeks)
   - Keep moist but well-drained
   - Ventilate regularly
   - New growth indicates rooting

7. **Pot up when rooted**
   - Well-drained potting mix
   - Native plant mix fine but not essential
   - Grow on in full sun

### Seed (Species Only)

**When to use:** For species when specific variety not needed.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Process:**
1. Collect seed from mature capsules
2. Capsules often remain on branches for years
3. Sow on surface, press in lightly
4. Keep moist and warm
5. Germination 2-4 weeks
6. Seedlings variable - select best

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Base turns brown/black, cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, cold conditions.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, increase warmth.

**Prevention:** Well-drained medium, don't overwater.

### Problem 2: Slow or No Rooting

**Symptoms:** After 8 weeks, no root development.

**Causes:** Too cold, wood too mature, no hormone.

**Solution:** Increase warmth, be patient.

**Prevention:** Optimal timing, bottom heat, use hormone.

### Problem 3: Compact Varieties Lose Form

**Symptoms:** Dwarf cultivar produces tall growth.

**Causes:** Reverted growth from base, or seedling variation.

**Solution:** Remove reverted shoots. Ensure propagating from true material.

**Prevention:** Take cuttings from typical growth, not vigorous shoots.

## Pro Tips

1. **Post-flowering material** - The best cutting material often comes after the flowering flush, when new growth has firmed.

2. **Avoid flowering shoots** - Cuttings with flowers or buds root poorly. Choose vegetative growth.

3. **Weeping varieties** - Weeping callistemons ('Dawson River Weeper', 'Macarthur') propagate as easily as upright forms.

4. **Not phosphorus-sensitive** - Unlike Proteaceae natives, callistemons tolerate normal fertilizers. They're less fussy overall.

5. **Quick to flower** - Well-grown cuttings often flower within 12-18 months.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy callistemons produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Full sun, well-drained.
- **Pruning:** Tip pruning after flowering promotes bushy growth.
- **Feeding:** Annual balanced fertilizer fine.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, semi-hardened
- Healthy, typical foliage
- Non-flowering shoots
- From reliable flowering plants
