# Melaleuca (Paperbark) Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy-Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed |
| **Time to Root** | 6-10 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 70-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Melaleucas (paperbarks and honey myrtles) are diverse Australian natives ranging from large trees to compact shrubs. Most garden varieties propagate well from semi-hardwood cuttings, particularly the smaller shrubby species and cultivars.

The genus now includes many plants formerly classified as Callistemon (bottlebrushes), and the propagation technique is the same for both groups. Popular cultivars like 'Revolution Gold' and 'Claret Tops' root reliably with standard technique.

Seed propagation is possible for species but produces variable offspring. Named cultivars must be propagated vegetatively.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating screening and hedges
- Multiplying compact cultivars
- Bird-attracting gardens
- Wetland plantings (appropriate species)
- Replacing declining plants

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Reliable method for most garden melaleucas.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)
- Wood condition: Current growth, slightly firmed

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth, semi-hardened
   - 10-15cm lengths
   - Non-flowering shoots preferred
   - Healthy, typical foliage

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node at base
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep foliage at top
   - May wound base lightly

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Medium to strong formula
   - Recommended for consistent results

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 4-5cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Warm conditions (20-25°C)
   - Moderate humidity
   - Bright light
   - Good ventilation

6. **Ongoing care** (6-10 weeks)
   - Keep moist but well-drained
   - Ventilate regularly
   - New growth indicates rooting

7. **Pot up when rooted**
   - Well-drained potting mix
   - Handle roots carefully
   - Full sun position

### Seed (Species Only)

**When to use:** For species when specific variety not needed.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Process:**
1. Collect tiny seed from mature capsules
2. Surface sow (fine seed)
3. Press in, don't cover
4. Keep moist, warm
5. Germination 2-4 weeks
6. Variable offspring

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Base turns soft and brown.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, reduce watering.

**Prevention:** Well-drained medium, don't overwater.

### Problem 2: Slow Rooting

**Symptoms:** After 8 weeks, minimal roots.

**Causes:** Too cold, wood too mature.

**Solution:** Increase warmth, be patient.

**Prevention:** Bottom heat, optimal timing.

### Problem 3: Colored Foliage Varieties Revert

**Symptoms:** Golden or purple foliage turns green.

**Causes:** Insufficient light, or cutting from reverted growth.

**Solution:** Move to full sun. Verify cutting source.

**Prevention:** Full sun for colored varieties. Take cuttings from typical-colored growth.

## Pro Tips

1. **Melaleuca = Callistemon** - Many bottlebrushes are now classified as melaleucas. The propagation technique is identical.

2. **Size matters** - Large tree melaleucas (M. quinquenervia, M. armillaris) are harder from cuttings than compact shrub types.

3. **Wetland tolerance** - Once established, some melaleucas tolerate wet feet. But cuttings need good drainage.

4. **Colored foliage** - 'Revolution Gold' and similar colored varieties need full sun to maintain their color.

5. **Quick establishment** - From cutting to flowering can be 12-18 months for compact varieties.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy melaleucas produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Full sun for most (part shade for rainforest types).
- **Pruning:** Tip pruning after flowering encourages bushy growth.
- **Feeding:** Annual balanced fertilizer.
- **Watering:** Varies by species - many drought-tolerant once established.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, semi-hardened
- Healthy, typical foliage
- Non-flowering shoots
- Typical color for variety
