# Leptospermum (Tea Tree) Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy-Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed |
| **Time to Root** | 6-10 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 70-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Leptospermums (tea trees or manuka) are versatile Australian and New Zealand natives with masses of small flowers in spring and summer. Most species and cultivars propagate well from semi-hardwood cuttings, making it practical to multiply favorite flowering varieties.

Popular cultivars like 'Burgundy Queen', 'Pink Cascade', and 'Cardwell' root reliably with standard technique. The fine-textured foliage makes propagation straightforward, and rooted cuttings establish quickly.

Seed propagation is possible for species but produces variable results, especially for flower color. Named cultivars must be propagated vegetatively.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating mass displays of flowering shrubs
- Multiplying selected flower colors
- Hedging and screening
- Container specimens
- Replacing old or leggy plants

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Reliable method with good success rates.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (after flowering)
- Wood condition: Current growth, firmed but still green

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth, semi-hardened
   - 8-12cm lengths
   - Non-flowering shoots preferred
   - Healthy, typical growth

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node at base
   - Strip lower leaves
   - Keep foliage at top
   - Fine-textured - handle carefully

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Medium-strength formula
   - Recommended for consistent results

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 3-4cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Warm conditions (20-24°C)
   - Moderate humidity
   - Bright light
   - Good ventilation

6. **Ongoing care** (6-10 weeks)
   - Keep moist but well-drained
   - Ventilate regularly
   - Watch for new growth

7. **Pot up when rooted**
   - Well-drained potting mix
   - Handle roots carefully
   - Grow on in full sun

### Seed (Species Only)

**When to use:** For species when specific variety not needed.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Process:**
1. Collect tiny seeds from mature capsules
2. Surface sow (dust-fine seed)
3. Press in gently, don't cover
4. Keep moist
5. Germination 2-4 weeks
6. Prick out when large enough to handle

### Environment Setup

**Propagation unit:**
Covered tray or propagator. Well-drained medium. Moderate humidity with good ventilation.

**Key factor:**
Leptospermums are relatively undemanding. Good drainage and moderate humidity suit them well.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Fine Foliage Dries Out

**Symptoms:** Tips brown, leaves dry and crisp.

**Causes:** Humidity too low, underwatering.

**Solution:** Increase humidity, mist more frequently.

**Prevention:** Maintain cover, don't let medium dry out.

### Problem 2: Slow Rooting

**Symptoms:** After 8 weeks, minimal root development.

**Causes:** Too cold, or wood too mature.

**Solution:** Increase warmth, be patient.

**Prevention:** Bottom heat, optimal timing.

### Problem 3: Flower Color Different Than Parent

**Symptoms:** Rooted plant flowers different color than parent.

**Causes:** Material taken from reverted branch, or seedling variation (unlikely from cuttings).

**Solution:** Verify cutting source next time.

**Prevention:** Take cuttings from typical flowering branches.

## Pro Tips

1. **After flowering best** - The new growth after spring flowering is ideal cutting material - it's had time to firm while still being relatively young.

2. **Fine foliage advantage** - The small leaves mean less transpiration stress, making leptospermum cuttings relatively easy to keep healthy.

3. **Color variation** - Species show wide flower color variation. Propagate from known flowering plants to ensure desired color.

4. **NZ vs Australian species** - NZ leptospermums (manuka) and Australian tea trees propagate similarly, with slight variation in rooting time.

5. **Quick to flower** - Well-grown cuttings often flower the first spring after propagation.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy leptospermums produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Full sun, well-drained.
- **Pruning:** Light pruning after flowering maintains shape and provides cuttings.
- **Feeding:** Annual balanced fertilizer.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, post-flowering
- Healthy, typical foliage
- Non-flowering shoots
- From verified flower colors
