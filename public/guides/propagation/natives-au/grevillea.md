# Grevillea Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed (species only), grafting |
| **Time to Root** | 6-12 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 50-80% typical |

## Overview

Grevilleas are among Australia's most popular native plants, with spectacular spider-like flowers and excellent bird-attracting properties. Most grevilleas propagate reasonably well from semi-hardwood cuttings, making it practical to multiply favorite varieties.

Success rates vary between species and hybrids. Smaller-leafed grevilleas like G. rosmarinifolia and hybrids like 'Robyn Gordon' generally root more easily than large-leafed species. Strong rooting hormone and excellent drainage are essential.

Seed propagation is possible for species grevilleas but produces variable offspring. Hybrids must be propagated vegetatively to maintain their characteristics.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Multiplying bird-attracting screening plants
- Creating grevillea feature gardens
- Preserving favorite hybrid varieties
- Replacing declining plants
- Sharing with other gardeners

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Standard method for grevilleas with moderate success rates.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)
- Wood condition: Current season's growth, well-firmed

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth, semi-hardened
   - 8-12cm lengths
   - Non-flowering shoots preferred
   - Healthy, typical foliage

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node at base
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep foliage at top
   - Wound base (scrape one side)

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Strong formula (IBA 3000-5000ppm)
   - Essential for grevilleas
   - Apply to wounded base

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Very well-drained mix (perlite-based)
   - Insert 3-4cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Bottom heat helpful (20-25°C)
   - Moderate humidity
   - Bright light
   - Good ventilation

6. **Ongoing care** (6-12 weeks)
   - Keep moist but never wet
   - Good drainage essential
   - Watch for phosphorus toxicity symptoms
   - Don't disturb to check roots

7. **Pot up when rooted**
   - Native plant potting mix (low phosphorus)
   - Handle roots carefully
   - Grow on in protected position

### Seed (Species Only)

**When to use:** For species grevilleas when hybrids not needed.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Process:**
1. Collect seed from mature follicles
2. No pre-treatment usually needed
3. Sow on surface or barely covered
4. Keep moist, warm
5. Germination 3-8 weeks (variable)
6. Seedlings variable - select best

### Grafting (Difficult Species)

**When to use:** For species that root poorly on own roots.

**Process:**
1. Use vigorous rootstock (e.g., G. robusta seedlings)
2. Side-veneer or approach graft
3. Specialist technique
4. Used commercially for difficult hybrids

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (20-25°C base) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate (not excessive) |
| **Light** | Bright |
| **Medium** | Excellent drainage essential |
| **Bottom heat** | Helpful |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation unit:**
Very free-draining medium. Bottom heat helpful. Moderate humidity - not tropical. Good light. Ventilation important.

**Critical factor:**
Drainage. Grevilleas are adapted to well-drained Australian soils. Wet conditions cause root rot rapidly.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Base turns brown/black, cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage.

**Solution:** Improve drainage dramatically. Reduce watering.

**Prevention:** Very free-draining medium, don't overwater, good ventilation.

### Problem 2: Yellowing Leaves After Potting

**Symptoms:** New growth yellows between veins after potting up.

**Causes:** Phosphorus toxicity from standard potting mix.

**Solution:** Move to native plant mix. May recover.

**Prevention:** Always use low-phosphorus native plant mix for grevilleas.

### Problem 3: Variable Success Between Species

**Symptoms:** Some grevillea types root well, others fail repeatedly.

**Causes:** Natural variation in rooting ability.

**Solution:** Accept lower rates for difficult species. Try grafting.

**Prevention:** Research species-specific difficulty. Take more cuttings of difficult types.

## Pro Tips

1. **Phosphorus sensitivity** - Grevilleas are highly sensitive to phosphorus. Use only native plant fertilizers and potting mixes.

2. **Smaller-leaved = easier** - Fine-leaved grevilleas (rosmarinifolia types) generally root more readily than large-leaved species.

3. **Popular hybrids vary** - 'Robyn Gordon' and 'Superb' are relatively easy. 'Honey Gem' and silky oak types are more challenging.

4. **Drainage, drainage, drainage** - More grevilleas fail from wet feet than any other cause. Pure perlite is not too extreme.

5. **Native plant mix essential** - Standard potting mixes kill grevilleas via phosphorus toxicity. This applies at all stages from rooted cutting to mature plant.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy grevilleas produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Full sun, well-drained position.
- **Pruning:** Tip pruning encourages bushy growth and cutting material.
- **Feeding:** Native plant fertilizer only (low phosphorus).
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established; water young plants.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, semi-hardened
- Healthy, typical foliage
- Non-flowering shoots preferred
- From vigorous, healthy plants
