| Attribute | Value |

## Overview

Eucalypts are iconic Australian trees, but they're challenging to propagate from cuttings. Unlike most ornamentals, eucalypts root poorly from mature wood, and success rates are often low and unpredictable.

Seed propagation is the standard method for most eucalypt species and works well. Seeds germinate readily and grow rapidly. The limitation is that seed produces variable offspring, which matters when specific forms (mallee-form or dwarf cultivars) are desired.

For named cultivars, juvenile cuttings (from young seedlings or regrowth after hard pruning) offer better success than mature wood, though the technique remains challenging.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Growing eucalypts from locally-collected seed
- Establishing windbreaks and screening
- Native tree restoration
- Feature specimens
- Multiplying selected forms (challenging)

## Propagation Methods

### Seed (Recommended)

**Overview:** Most reliable method for eucalypts. Standard approach for species.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Obtain seed**
   - Collect mature capsules from known trees
   - Purchase from native seed suppliers
   - Fresh seed germinates best

2. **Prepare seed**
   - Most eucalypt seed needs no pre-treatment
   - Some species benefit from smoke treatment
   - Very fine seed - handle carefully

3. **Sow seed**
   - Free-draining seed-raising mix
   - Surface sow or barely cover
   - Press in gently
   - Fine seed - don't bury

4. **Provide conditions**
   - Warm (20-25°C)
   - Keep moist but not wet
   - Bright light
   - Good ventilation

5. **Germination** (2-4 weeks typical)
   - Most species germinate readily
   - Thin if overcrowded
   - Transplant when large enough to handle

6. **Grow on**
   - Pot up carefully
   - Eucalypts grow rapidly
   - Don't let become pot-bound
   - Plant out while still young (pot-bound eucalypts establish poorly)

### Juvenile Cuttings (Challenging)

**When to use:** For specific cultivars or forms.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: December to February (summer)
- Material: Young seedling growth OR regrowth after coppicing

**Process:**
1. Use juvenile foliage only (different leaf shape from adult)
2. Take 8-10cm tip cuttings
3. Remove lower leaves
4. Apply strong rooting hormone (IBA 4000-5000ppm)
5. Insert in free-draining mix
6. High humidity, bottom heat
7. Results highly variable (20-50%)
8. Some species refuse entirely

### Grafting (Specialist Method)

**When to use:** For difficult cultivars on vigorous rootstock.

**Process:**
1. Use seedling rootstock of compatible species
2. Side-veneer or cleft graft
3. Specialist technique
4. Used commercially for named cultivars

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (22-28°C for cuttings) |
| **Humidity** | High (for cuttings) |
| **Light** | Bright |
| **Medium** | Excellent drainage |
| **Bottom heat** | Essential for cuttings |

### Environment Setup

**For seed:**
Standard seed-raising conditions. Free-draining mix, warmth, moisture.

**For cuttings:**
Specialized high-humidity environment. Bottom heat essential. Very free-draining medium.

**Critical factor:**
Eucalypt cuttings are notoriously difficult. Don't expect high success rates even with optimal technique.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Don't Root

**Symptoms:** Multiple batches fail completely.

**Causes:** Eucalypts are simply difficult from cuttings.

**Solution:** Switch to seed propagation. Accept cuttings as challenging.

**Prevention:** Use only juvenile foliage, strong hormone, bottom heat.

### Problem 2: Seedlings Dampen Off

**Symptoms:** Seedlings collapse at soil level.

**Causes:** Fungal disease from overwatering or poor drainage.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, reduce watering, improve ventilation.

**Prevention:** Free-draining mix, don't overwater, good air circulation.

### Problem 3: Pot-Bound Seedlings Don't Establish

**Symptoms:** Planted eucalypts fail to establish or blow over.

**Causes:** Circling roots from being too long in pot.

**Solution:** Difficult to fix. Try root pruning before planting.

**Prevention:** Plant out while young - don't let eucalypts become pot-bound.

## Pro Tips

1. **Seed is the way** - Accept that eucalypts propagate best from seed. Cuttings are for specialists.

2. **Juvenile vs adult foliage** - If attempting cuttings, juvenile foliage (round, opposite leaves) roots better than adult foliage (long, alternate leaves).

3. **Don't pot-bind** - Eucalypts have aggressive root systems. Keep them moving to larger pots or plant out early.

4. **Local seed** - Seed from local provenances often establishes better than seed from distant sources.

5. **Fast growers** - From seed, many eucalypts reach 2+ meters in first year. Plan for their speed.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

For cutting production (challenging):

- **Coppicing:** Hard pruning produces juvenile regrowth suitable for cuttings.
- **Lignotuber species:** Some eucalypts resprout readily from the base.
- **Young stock plants:** Juvenile plants produce better cutting material.

### Selecting Material

For cuttings:

- Juvenile foliage only
- Current season's soft growth
- Non-flowering tips
- From vigorous regrowth
