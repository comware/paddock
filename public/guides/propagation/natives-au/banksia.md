# Banksia Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Best Method** | Seed |
| **Alternative Methods** | Cuttings (some species), grafting |
| **Time to Root** | 8-16 weeks (cuttings) |
| **Best Season** | Autumn (cuttings), spring (seed) |
| **Success Rate** | 30-60% (cuttings), 50-80% (seed) |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Essential for cuttings |

## Overview

Banksias are iconic Australian natives with spectacular flower spikes, but they're among the more challenging natives to propagate from cuttings. Many species root poorly, and success rates are often low and variable.

Seed propagation is generally more reliable for species banksias and is the recommended method for most home gardeners. Many banksia seeds require smoke treatment or heat to break dormancy, simulating bushfire conditions.

For cultivars and hybrids that must be propagated vegetatively, semi-hardwood cuttings with strong hormone, excellent drainage, and patience offer the best approach. Grafting onto vigorous rootstocks is used commercially for difficult varieties.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Growing species banksias from seed
- Preserving selected forms or cultivars
- Creating wildlife habitat gardens
- Feature specimens and cut flowers
- Replacing lost plants

## Propagation Methods

### Seed (Recommended for Species)

**Overview:** Most reliable method for species banksias.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Collect seed**
   - Harvest mature follicles (the "cones")
   - Place in paper bag in warm spot
   - Seeds release as follicles open
   - Some species need heat to open

2. **Treat seed if needed**
   - Many species need smoke treatment
   - Use smoke water or smoked vermiculite
   - Some species germinate without treatment
   - Research your specific species

3. **Sow seeds**
   - Free-draining native seed mix
   - Sow on surface or barely cover
   - Space well - seedlings resent disturbance

4. **Provide conditions**
   - Warm (20-25°C)
   - Keep moist but well-drained
   - Bright light

5. **Germination** (variable)
   - 3-8 weeks typical
   - Some species slower
   - Smoke treatment dramatically improves rates for many species

6. **Grow on carefully**
   - Pot up carefully - banksias hate root disturbance
   - Native plant mix (low phosphorus)
   - Don't overpot

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Challenging)

**When to use:** For cultivars or when specific genetics required.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)

**Process:**
1. Select current season's growth, well-firmed
2. Take 8-12cm lengths, wound base
3. Apply strong rooting hormone (IBA 4000-5000ppm)
4. Insert in very free-draining mix
5. Bottom heat (22-25°C), moderate humidity
6. Rooting 8-16 weeks (highly variable)
7. Success rates 30-60% depending on species
8. Some species refuse to root

### Grafting (Commercial Method)

**When to use:** For difficult-to-root cultivars.

**Process:**
1. Use vigorous species as rootstock (e.g., B. integrifolia)
2. Side-veneer or approach graft
3. Specialist technique
4. Allows difficult cultivars to be grown on vigorous roots

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (22-25°C) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate only |
| **Light** | Bright |
| **Medium** | Excellent drainage essential |
| **Bottom heat** | Very helpful for cuttings |

### Environment Setup

**For seed:**
Free-draining native seed mix. Warm conditions. Smoke treatment if required.

**For cuttings:**
Extremely free-draining medium. Bottom heat. Moderate humidity - excessive humidity promotes rot.

**Critical factor:**
Drainage and phosphorus. Banksias die in wet conditions and are extremely sensitive to phosphorus.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor Seed Germination

**Symptoms:** Seeds fail to germinate or germination very patchy.

**Causes:** Dormancy not broken (no smoke treatment), old seed.

**Solution:** Apply smoke treatment. Try fresh seed.

**Prevention:** Research species requirements. Use smoke water or smoked vermiculite.

### Problem 2: Cuttings Fail Repeatedly

**Symptoms:** Multiple batches of cuttings rot or simply don't root.

**Causes:** Some banksia species are extremely difficult from cuttings.

**Solution:** Try seed instead. Consider grafting. Accept limitations.

**Prevention:** Research your species - some root reasonably well (B. spinulosa, B. ericifolia), others are notoriously difficult.

### Problem 3: Seedlings Die After Transplanting

**Symptoms:** Healthy seedlings die after potting up.

**Causes:** Root disturbance (banksias hate it) or phosphorus toxicity.

**Solution:** Usually too late. Improve technique next time.

**Prevention:** Transplant with minimal root disturbance. Use only native plant mix.

## Pro Tips

1. **Smoke treatment is key** - For most banksias, smoke water or smoked vermiculite transforms germination rates from poor to excellent. It simulates post-bushfire conditions.

2. **Species variation is huge** - Some banksias (B. spinulosa, B. ericifolia) propagate reasonably. Others (B. coccinea, B. speciosa) are notoriously difficult. Research before attempting.

3. **Root disturbance = death** - Banksias have proteoid roots that hate disturbance. Transplant only when essential, and never bare-root.

4. **Phosphorus kills banksias** - As Proteaceae family members, banksias are extremely phosphorus-sensitive. Native plant mixes only, forever.

5. **Heat opens cones** - To release seed from many banksia cones, place in a warm oven (100°C) or paper bag in hot sun. Follicles open as they dry.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy banksias produce the best propagation material:

- **Position:** Full sun, excellent drainage.
- **Pruning:** Light pruning after flowering.
- **Feeding:** Native plant fertilizer only, sparingly.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, semi-hardened
- Healthy typical foliage
- Non-flowering shoots
- From vigorous plants
