# Westringia Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Softwood cuttings |
| **Time to Root** | 4-8 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 80-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Westringias (Westringia fruticosa and related species) are among the easiest Australian natives to propagate from cuttings. Often called "coastal rosemary" for their resemblance to that herb, westringias root quickly and reliably, making them ideal for native plant beginners.

Semi-hardwood cuttings taken in late summer to autumn root in 4-8 weeks with high success rates. The fine-textured foliage and compact growth make westringias popular for hedging, and propagation allows you to create uniform plantings from a single parent.

Unlike many Proteaceae natives, westringias aren't phosphorus-sensitive, though they still appreciate free-draining conditions.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating uniform native hedges
- Multiplying low-maintenance garden plants
- Coastal and drought-tolerant gardens
- Replacing old or leggy plants
- Sharing with other gardeners

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Reliable method with very high success rates.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)
- Wood condition: Current growth, slightly firmed

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth, semi-hardened
   - 8-12cm lengths
   - Tip cuttings work well
   - Healthy, typical growth

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node at base
   - Strip lower 3-4cm of leaves
   - Keep foliage at top

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Medium-strength formula
   - Recommended for faster rooting
   - Not essential for most westringias

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 3-4cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Warm conditions
   - Moderate humidity
   - Bright light
   - Cover or propagator helpful

6. **Ongoing care** (4-8 weeks)
   - Keep moist but well-drained
   - Watch for new growth
   - Ventilate regularly

7. **Pot up when rooted**
   - Any well-drained potting mix
   - Handle roots gently
   - Grow on in full sun

### Softwood Cuttings (Spring Method)

**When to use:** Earlier in season.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Process:**
1. Take 8-10cm tip cuttings of soft new growth
2. Remove lower leaves
3. Apply rooting hormone
4. Insert in propagation mix
5. High humidity
6. Roots in 3-6 weeks
7. Slightly more prone to wilting

| **Humidity** | Moderate |
| **Light** | Bright |
| **Medium** | Well-drained |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation unit:**
Simple covered tray works well. Well-drained mix. Moderate humidity. Westringias are undemanding.

**Key factor:**
Westringias are forgiving. Unlike many native plants, they tolerate a range of conditions and don't require specialized treatment.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Wilt Despite Cover

**Symptoms:** Tips droop, leaves look stressed.

**Causes:** Too hot, insufficient humidity, or too much sun.

**Solution:** Move to cooler position, increase humidity, shade.

**Prevention:** Avoid hot direct sun, maintain humidity.

### Problem 2: Slow Rooting

**Symptoms:** After 6 weeks, minimal root development.

**Causes:** Too cold, or material too woody.

**Solution:** Increase warmth. Be patient.

**Prevention:** Optimal timing, semi-hardwood (not fully mature).

### Problem 3: Leggy Growth After Potting

**Symptoms:** Plants grow tall and sparse.

**Causes:** Insufficient light or no pinching.

**Solution:** Pinch tips to encourage branching. Full sun position.

**Prevention:** Full sun from early stage, pinch tips regularly.

## Pro Tips

1. **Easiest native** - If you're new to native plant propagation, westringia is the perfect starting point. Success builds confidence.

2. **Pinch early, pinch often** - Westringias benefit from tip pinching to promote bushy, hedge-worthy growth. Start early.

3. **Not phosphorus-sensitive** - Unlike grevilleas and banksias, westringias tolerate normal fertilizers. They're more forgiving all-round.

4. **Variety matters** - Grey-leaved varieties ('Smokey', 'Wynyabbie Gem') root as easily as green types.

5. **Quick to establish** - From cutting to planting-out in 6-8 months. Established plants within a year.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy westringias produce abundant cutting material:

- **Position:** Full sun, any well-drained soil.
- **Pruning:** Regular trimming provides cutting material and maintains shape.
- **Feeding:** Annual balanced fertilizer fine.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, semi-hardened
- Healthy, typical foliage
- Compact, well-shaped growth
- No pest or disease problems
