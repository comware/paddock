# Kangaroo Paw Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy (division), Intermediate (seed) |
| **Best Method** | Division |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed, tissue culture |
| **Time to Root** | Immediate (division) |
| **Best Season** | Late winter to spring |
| **Success Rate** | 90-95% (division), 60-80% (seed) |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Kangaroo paws (Anigozanthos species and hybrids) are iconic Western Australian natives with spectacular flowering stalks. They propagate easily by division, making it simple to multiply established clumps.

Division is the standard method for home gardeners and ensures offspring identical to the parent. The rhizomatous growth habit means established plants naturally form clumps that can be separated.

Seed propagation is possible for species but produces variable offspring. The many modern hybrids (developed for cut flower production and gardens) are sterile or don't come true from seed and must be divided.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Multiplying favorite flower colors
- Rejuvenating declining clumps
- Creating mass plantings
- Sharing with other gardeners
- Container growing

## Propagation Methods

### Division (Recommended)

**Overview:** Simple and reliable method for all kangaroo paws.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: July to September (late winter to early spring)
- Just before new growth begins

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Prepare the plant**
   - Water well day before dividing
   - Cut back old foliage to 10-15cm
   - Dig up entire clump (or section)

2. **Divide the clump**
   - Shake off excess soil
   - Identify natural divisions
   - Pull or cut into sections
   - Each division needs roots and growing points

3. **Prepare divisions**
   - Trim damaged roots
   - Remove dead or yellowing foliage
   - Dust any wounds with fungicide (optional)

4. **Replant immediately**
   - Well-drained position
   - Same depth as before
   - Water in well
   - Mulch lightly (keep mulch away from crown)

5. **Aftercare**
   - Keep moist until established
   - New growth indicates success
   - Feed once actively growing

### Seed (Species Only)

**When to use:** For species kangaroo paws when specific variety not needed.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Process:**
1. Collect seed from dried flower stalks
2. Some species need smoke treatment
3. Surface sow on sandy mix
4. Keep moist, warm (20-25°C)
5. Germination 3-8 weeks (variable)
6. Seedlings variable
7. Slow to flowering size

### Tissue Culture (Commercial)

**When to use:** Commercial production of hybrid cultivars.

**Process:**
- Laboratory technique
- Used for mass production of named cultivars
- Not practical for home propagation

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Soil** | Excellent drainage essential |
| **Position** | Full sun |
| **Water** | Regular until established |
| **Mulch** | Light, away from crown |

### Environment Setup

**For division:**
Plant directly into final position or pots. Well-drained soil or potting mix. Full sun.

**For seed:**
Sandy seed-raising mix. Warm conditions. Smoke treatment beneficial for some species.

**Critical factor:**
Drainage. Kangaroo paws are prone to root rot in wet conditions. Well-drained soil is non-negotiable.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Divisions Rot After Planting

**Symptoms:** Crown turns soft and black, plant dies.

**Causes:** Poor drainage, overwatering, mulch against crown.

**Solution:** Usually too late. Improve drainage for next attempt.

**Prevention:** Excellent drainage, don't overwater, keep mulch away from crown.

### Problem 2: Ink Spot Disease

**Symptoms:** Black spots on leaves, spreading blackening.

**Causes:** Fungal disease, especially in humid conditions.

**Solution:** Remove affected foliage. Improve air circulation. Fungicide if severe.

**Prevention:** Good air circulation, avoid overhead watering, remove old foliage.

### Problem 3: Poor Flowering After Division

**Symptoms:** Healthy plant but few or no flowers.

**Causes:** Division too small, insufficient light, or plants too young.

**Solution:** Wait another season. Ensure full sun. Feed appropriately.

**Prevention:** Make reasonably sized divisions. Full sun position.

## Pro Tips

1. **Drainage is everything** - More kangaroo paws die from wet feet than any other cause. Sandy, free-draining conditions are essential.

2. **Cut back after flowering** - Removing spent flower stalks and old foliage improves plant appearance and health.

3. **Ink spot management** - This common fungal disease is best managed by good hygiene. Remove affected foliage promptly.

4. **Hybrid sterility** - Most modern kangaroo paw hybrids are sterile or don't come true from seed. Division is the only way to propagate them.

5. **Western Australian natives** - Kangaroo paws come from WA's sandy, low-nutrient soils. They can suffer from too much fertility.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy kangaroo paws produce the best divisions:

- **Position:** Full sun, excellent drainage.
- **Feeding:** Light native plant fertilizer annually.
- **Watering:** Regular during growing season, less when dormant.
- **Grooming:** Remove spent flowers and dead foliage.

### Division Size

For best results:

- Each division should have multiple growing points
- Good root system attached
- Some healthy foliage
- Don't divide too small
