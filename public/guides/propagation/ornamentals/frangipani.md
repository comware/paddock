# Frangipani Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed (species only) |
| **Time to Root** | 4-8 weeks (after callusing) |
| **Best Season** | Late winter to spring |
| **Success Rate** | 85-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Helpful but not essential |

## Overview

Frangipanis (Plumeria species) are among the easiest ornamental trees to propagate from cuttings. Their thick, succulent stems contain substantial energy reserves that power root development, and their tolerance of dry conditions during establishment makes them forgiving of imperfect technique.

The key unique requirement is callusing - frangipani cuttings must dry for several weeks before planting to allow the cut end to heal. This prevents rot and is essential for success. Once callused and planted, rooting is reliable even without hormone.

The fragrant tropical flowers that make frangipanis beloved appear within 2-3 years of propagation, and cuttings produce exact flower-color matches to the parent.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Multiplying fragrant flowering trees
- Creating tropical garden displays
- Container growing in cooler areas
- Sharing specific color forms
- Replacing frost-damaged trees

## Propagation Methods

### Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Traditional method with very high success. The callusing step is unique and essential.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: July to September (late winter to early spring)
- Take during dormancy before new growth begins

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Tip cuttings 30-50cm long
   - Healthy, firm wood
   - From previous season's growth
   - Thicker stems often better

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut cleanly with sharp saw or secateurs
   - Angled cut at base optional (helps drainage)
   - Remove any leaves (usually absent in winter)

3. **CRITICAL: Callusing period**
   - Stand cuttings upright in dry, shaded spot
   - Leave for 2-4 weeks until cut end dries and seals
   - White sap must stop flowing and surface must harden
   - Do NOT skip this step - it prevents rot

4. **Apply rooting hormone** (optional)
   - Medium strength if using
   - Not essential but may speed rooting

5. **Plant callused cuttings**
   - Well-drained potting mix or sandy soil
   - Insert 8-12cm deep
   - Firm gently
   - Water once then keep barely moist

6. **Provide conditions**
   - Warm position, protected from frost
   - Full sun once planted
   - Minimal water until roots develop
   - Do not overwater - they rot easily

7. **Monitor rooting** (4-8 weeks)
   - New leaf growth indicates roots forming
   - Don't dig up to check
   - Increase water gradually as growth appears

8. **Grow on**
   - Leave in pot until well-established
   - Plant out after first full growing season

### Seed (For Species Only)

**When to use:** For Plumeria rubra species (not named varieties).

**Process:**
1. Collect seed pods when brown and dry
2. Remove papery wing from seeds
3. Sow immediately (viability drops)
4. Plant 1cm deep, wing end up
5. Keep warm and barely moist
6. Germination 1-2 weeks
7. Variable flower colors and forms

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best | After callusing, plant as weather warms |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Good | Can take and callus late cuttings |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Possible | Early autumn, limited time to establish |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Take cuttings | Dormant wood, callus indoors |

### Optimal Timing

- **Taking cuttings:** July to August
- **Callusing:** 2-4 weeks indoors
- **Planting:** September to October
- **Key factor:** Time cutting-taking so planting coincides with warming spring weather

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (22-30°C) |
| **Humidity** | Low to moderate |
| **Light** | Full sun once planted |
| **Medium** | Very well-drained |
| **Water** | Minimal until rooted |

### Environment Setup

**Callusing:**
Dry, shaded, frost-free location. Stand upright. Air circulation. No water.

**After planting:**
Full sun, warm position. Very free-draining medium. Minimal water. Frost protection.

**Critical factor:**
Unlike most cuttings, frangipanis want DRY conditions during establishment. Overwatering causes rot.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cutting Rots After Planting

**Symptoms:** Base turns black and mushy, cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Insufficient callusing, overwatering, poor drainage.

**Solution:** Usually too late. Take fresh cuttings and callus properly.

**Prevention:** Full 2-4 week callusing, very free-draining medium, minimal water.

### Problem 2: Cutting Shrivels Without Rooting

**Symptoms:** Cutting becomes soft and wrinkled.

**Causes:** Cutting taken when too immature, or conditions too hot without any moisture.

**Solution:** If firm at base, try light watering. May still root if core viable.

**Prevention:** Take mature, firm cuttings. Provide some moisture after planting (just not excessive).

### Problem 3: No Growth After Planting

**Symptoms:** Cutting remains dormant with no new leaves.

**Causes:** Too cold, or very large cutting taking longer to root.

**Solution:** Be patient. Ensure warm conditions. Large cuttings can take longer.

**Prevention:** Warm position, appropriate size cuttings, patience.

## Pro Tips

1. **Callusing is non-negotiable** - The single most important step. Fresh-cut frangipanis planted immediately will rot. The 2-4 week drying period seals the wound and is essential.

2. **Bigger often works** - Unlike many plants, large frangipani cuttings (50cm+) root reliably. The energy reserves in thick stems power establishment.

3. **Water = danger** - More frangipanis fail from overwatering than anything else. Keep barely moist until active growth shows roots have formed.

4. **Winter deciduous** - Frangipanis drop leaves in winter. This is normal, not death. Take cuttings during dormancy.

5. **Color runs true** - Cuttings produce exact flower-color matches. Important given the huge variety of frangipani colors available.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Trees

Healthy frangipanis produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Full sun, warm, frost-free.
- **Pruning:** Pruning provides cutting material and encourages branching.
- **Feeding:** Annual balanced fertilizer.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established; reduce in winter.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Healthy, firm previous season's growth
- Pencil-thick or larger
- From known flowering plants (color verified)
- No signs of rust or scale
