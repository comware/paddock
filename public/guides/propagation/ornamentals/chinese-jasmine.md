# Chinese Jasmine Propagation

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Layering, softwood cuttings |
| **Time to Root** | 3-6 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 85-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Optional |

## Before You Propagate This One

*Jasminum polyanthum* — Chinese jasmine, also sold as pink jasmine — is an **environmental weed in eastern Australia**, including Victoria. It invades bushland, rainforest margins and creek lines, twines up and smothers what is already growing there, and is very hard to remove once established.

What makes it a weed is exactly what makes it easy to propagate. It layers wherever a stem touches soil and it suckers from the roots, so it does the job in this guide by itself, without being asked, in any direction it can reach.

None of that means you cannot grow it. It is a long-established garden plant and it is sold in nurseries across the country. It does mean two things are worth deciding before you take a cutting:

- **Keep it off the ground.** Grow it on a structure, and check along the base a couple of times a year for stems that have rooted where they touched.
- **Never dump prunings in bushland, on a creek bank, or over the back fence.** Green waste bin or hot compost. A discarded stem is a new plant.

If your place backs onto bush or a waterway, [star jasmine](/guides/propagation/ornamentals/jasmine) (*Trachelospermum jasminoides*) gives you much the same evergreen screen and scent with far less of this problem.

## Overview

Chinese jasmine roots about as readily as anything in this library. Semi-hardwood cuttings taken in late summer strike quickly, hormone is optional rather than needed, and layering is close to automatic.

The practical skill here is not getting it to root. It is deciding how many you actually want, and keeping the ones you did not plan on from establishing themselves.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Replacing an existing plant that has become woody and bare at the base
- Filling a run of trellis or wire from one plant instead of buying several
- Pots, where the roots are contained by definition
- Gifts and swaps — with the weed status passed on alongside

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Fast, high-percentage, and easy to control the number of plants you end up with.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)
- Wood condition: This season's growth, firmed but still flexible

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's twining growth
   - 10-15cm lengths with 3-4 nodes
   - Avoid the thin whippy tips and the old woody base

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut just below a node
   - Strip the lower two nodes clean
   - Keep 3-4 leaves at the top

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Optional — it roots without
   - A light dusting evens up the timing across a batch

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Ordinary propagation mix
   - Insert to the first stripped node
   - Several to a pot is fine

5. **Provide environment**
   - Warm, bright, out of direct sun
   - Moderate humidity; a plastic bag over the pot is plenty
   - Bottom heat unnecessary

6. **Ongoing care** (3-6 weeks)
   - Keep moist
   - Ventilate every few days
   - Roots usually within a month

7. **Pot up when rooted**
   - Standard potting mix
   - Pinch the tip to force branching, or it makes one long runner

### Layering (Easiest, and Watch It)

**When to use:** When you want one or two plants and have a stem that reaches the ground.

**Process:**
1. Peg a stem into soil or a sunken pot
2. Cover the buried section
3. Roots form in 4-8 weeks, often faster
4. Sever and lift

Worth knowing: this is what the plant does unassisted. If you have grown Chinese jasmine for a few years, you may already have layered plants around the base you did not put there. Check before you propagate — you may not need to.

### Softwood Cuttings (Spring)

**When to take material:** October to December.

**Process:** 8-10cm soft tips, strip lower leaves, high humidity, roots in 2-4 weeks. Faster than semi-hardwood, wilts more readily.

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Good | Softwood, after flowering |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Good | Keep out of direct heat |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Best | Semi-hardwood, prime time |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Poor | Slow, and rot is likelier than roots |

### Optimal Timing

- **Semi-hardwood cuttings:** February to April
- **Softwood cuttings:** October to December
- **Layering:** Any time the soil is warm
- **Key factor:** Take cuttings after flowering finishes, not during

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (18-25°C) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate |
| **Light** | Bright, indirect |
| **Medium** | Ordinary propagation mix |
| **Bottom heat** | Not necessary |

### Environment Setup

Undemanding. A pot on a bright windowsill with a bag over it will do. The main failure is heat — direct summer sun through glass cooks cuttings faster than they can root.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: More Plants Than You Wanted

**Symptoms:** Rooted stems around the base of the parent, or in neighbouring pots.

**Causes:** Natural layering. This is the plant working normally.

**Solution:** Lift and pot them if wanted, or cut and remove them. Do not leave them to establish.

**Prevention:** Keep stems off soil, check the base twice a year.

### Problem 2: Cuttings Rot Rather Than Root

**Symptoms:** Base goes soft and brown.

**Causes:** Too wet, too cold, or taken in winter.

**Solution:** Discard and retake in the proper window.

**Prevention:** Late summer to autumn, moist not wet, ventilate.

### Problem 3: One Long Runner and No Bush

**Symptoms:** The rooted cutting grows a single stem several metres long.

**Causes:** Twining habit, unchecked.

**Solution:** Pinch the tip. Repeat.

**Prevention:** Pinch as soon as it is potted, and tie in early so it branches where you want it.

## Pro Tips

1. **Take fewer than you think** — Nine out of ten will strike. Ten cuttings is ten plants, and you probably wanted three.

2. **Prunings are not compost-heap material** — Every node is a potential plant. Hot compost or the green bin, never a pile at the back of the block.

3. **Cut after flowering** — Material taken mid-flower puts its energy into the blooms and roots slower.

4. **Pots make the containment decision for you** — If you are unsure about it in the ground, grow it in a large pot on a frame.

5. **Tell whoever you give it to** — A plant passed over the fence without its weed status is how it reached the bush in the first place.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

- **Position:** Full sun to part shade, on a structure, off the ground.
- **Pruning:** Hard prune straight after flowering — this is also when you get cutting material.
- **Feeding:** Rarely needed. It is vigorous without help.
- **Watering:** Regular while establishing, tough afterwards.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, firmed but flexible
- Short internodes rather than long whippy runners
- Healthy green leaves, no yellowing
- No flower buds
