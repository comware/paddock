# Peace Lily Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |

| **Alternative Methods** | None practical |
| **Time to Root** | Already rooted (division) |

| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Peace lilies (Spathiphyllum) propagate exclusively by division. Unlike many houseplants, they cannot be grown from leaf or stem cuttings. The good news: division is simple, nearly foolproof, and creates instant established plants.

Peace lilies naturally form clumps with multiple crowns, each capable of independent growth. Division simply separates these natural groupings, each with its own roots and leaves already in place.

This makes propagation straightforward - you're not asking the plant to create new roots, just separating existing complete plants that have grown together.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Managing overgrown clumps
- Filling new containers
- Sharing with friends
- Refreshing pot-bound plants

## Propagation Methods

### Division (The Only Method)

**Overview:** Simple, nearly 100% success, instant results.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)
- When repotting
- When plant is crowded

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Water day before**
   - Moist roots are easier to work with
   - Reduces transplant stress

2. **Remove from pot**
   - Tip pot and slide out plant
   - Support the foliage

3. **Assess the clump**
   - Identify separate crowns (growing points)
   - Each division needs leaves and roots

4. **Separate divisions**
   - Gently tease apart with fingers
   - Or use clean knife if tangled
   - Aim for 3-5 leaves per division minimum

5. **Trim if needed**
   - Remove any dead or damaged leaves
   - Trim very long roots slightly

6. **Pot divisions**
   - Fresh potting mix
   - Same depth as before
   - Water thoroughly

7. **Aftercare**
   - Keep moist for first week
   - Bright indirect light
   - Some wilting normal initially

| **Humidity** | Moderate to high |

### Environment Setup

**For divisions:**
Standard pot with drainage, regular potting mix, normal indoor conditions. Peace lilies are tolerant.

**Critical factor:**
Divisions already have roots. Success is nearly guaranteed if each division has adequate roots and leaves.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Wilting After Division

**Symptoms:** Leaves droop despite moist soil.

**Causes:** Root disturbance, transplant shock.

**Solution:** Normal and temporary. Keep moist, maintain humidity, be patient.

**Prevention:** Water well before dividing, handle roots gently.

### Problem 2: Yellow Leaves After Division

**Symptoms:** Some leaves yellow and die.

**Causes:** Normal stress response. Old leaves may be sacrificed.

**Solution:** Remove yellowed leaves. New growth will follow.

**Prevention:** Some yellowing is normal - minimize by gentle handling.

### Problem 3: Division Too Small

**Symptoms:** Very small divisions struggle.

**Causes:** Insufficient leaves/roots to support recovery.

**Solution:** Keep small divisions in high humidity. They may survive but grow slowly.

**Prevention:** Aim for 3-5 leaves minimum per division.

## Pro Tips

1. **Combine with repotting** - Divide when you'd normally repot. Minimizes handling.

2. **Bigger divisions = faster recovery** - Small divisions survive but take longer to flourish.

3. **Humidity helps recovery** - Temporary plastic bag cover or bathroom location aids establishment.

4. **Division only method** - Don't waste time trying leaf or stem cuttings. They won't work.

5. **Spring timing ideal** - Active growth helps recovery. Avoid winter divisions.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy peace lilies produce dividable clumps:

- **Position:** Bright indirect to low light tolerated.
- **Watering:** Keep consistently moist.
- **Feeding:** Monthly during growing season.
- **When to divide:** Every 2-3 years or when pot-bound.

### Selecting Divisions

Each division should include:

- At least 3-5 healthy leaves
- Adequate root system
- Growing point (crown)
- No signs of rot or disease
