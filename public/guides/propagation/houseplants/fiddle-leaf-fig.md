# Fiddle Leaf Fig Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Stem cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Air layering |
| **Time to Root** | 6-12 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 50-70% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Fiddle leaf figs (Ficus lyrata) are more challenging to propagate than most houseplants. They're slower to root and more prone to failure than their pothos or philodendron companions. However, with patience and proper technique, propagation is achievable.

The key challenges are slower rooting times and sensitivity to conditions. Fiddle leaf figs demand consistency - stable temperatures, appropriate moisture, and patience measured in months rather than weeks.

Air layering offers higher success rates but requires more setup. Stem cuttings are simpler but need careful attention to prevent rot before roots develop.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating new plants from prunings
- Backup for valued specimens
- Sharing with patient friends
- Branching encouragement (pruning promotes bushiness)

## Propagation Methods

### Stem Cuttings (Most Common)

**Overview:** Simpler setup, moderate success rate.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: September to December (spring to early summer)
- During active growth only

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select cutting material**
   - 15-20cm stem sections
   - Include 2-3 leaves
   - Healthy, mature growth (not newest)

2. **Take cuttings**
   - Cut with clean, sharp blade
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Let milky sap dry 1-2 hours

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Dip cut end in hormone powder
   - Recommended for fiddle leaf figs
   - Improves success rate

4. **Plant in soil**
   - Well-draining propagation mix
   - Insert 5-8cm deep
   - Water lightly

5. **Create humidity**
   - Cover with plastic bag or propagator
   - Maintain high humidity
   - Don't let leaves touch plastic

6. **Provide ideal conditions**
   - Warm: 22-28°C
   - Bright indirect light
   - Keep soil barely moist
   - Patience: 6-12 weeks to root

7. **Confirm rooting**
   - Gentle tug resistance
   - New leaf emergence
   - Pot up when roots established

### Air Layering (Higher Success)

**When to use:** For valuable plants or when cutting success is poor.

**Process:**
1. Select point on healthy stem
2. Make shallow cuts through bark
3. Apply rooting hormone to wound
4. Wrap with moist sphagnum moss
5. Cover tightly with plastic wrap
6. Keep moss moist (check weekly)
7. Roots visible through plastic in 6-12 weeks
8. Cut below rooted section
9. Plant immediately

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (22-28°C) - critical |
| **Humidity** | High (70%+) |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Well-draining, airy |
| **Water** | Barely moist, not wet |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation container:**
Pot with drainage, clear plastic cover or bag for humidity. Bottom heat helpful but not essential.

**Critical factor:**
Warmth and humidity. Fiddle leaf figs need consistent warm, humid conditions. Cold or dry = rot before rooting.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cutting Rots Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Stem turns black and mushy.

**Causes:** Too wet, too cold, bacterial infection.

**Solution:** Usually too late. Start fresh.

**Prevention:** Well-draining medium, barely moist (not wet), warm conditions, let sap dry before planting.

### Problem 2: Leaves Drop During Rooting

**Symptoms:** Leaves fall off cutting.

**Causes:** Stress, low humidity, too much water loss.

**Solution:** Increase humidity. One leaf remaining can still succeed.

**Prevention:** High humidity environment, reduce leaf size by cutting leaves in half.

### Problem 3: No Rooting After 8+ Weeks

**Symptoms:** Cutting alive but no roots.

**Causes:** Too cold, insufficient light, poor material.

**Solution:** Check temperature (must be 22°C+), increase light, be patient up to 12 weeks.

**Prevention:** Optimal timing, warm conditions, healthy cutting material.

## Pro Tips

1. **Air layering is more reliable** - Higher success rate than cuttings. Worth the extra effort for valuable plants.

2. **Let sap dry** - Fiddle leaf figs exude milky sap. Let cut end dry 1-2 hours before planting to reduce rot risk.

3. **Reduce leaf size** - Cut large leaves in half to reduce water loss while rooting.

4. **Bottom heat helps** - Heat mat under propagation container speeds rooting significantly.

5. **Expect slow progress** - 6-12 weeks to root is normal. Patience required.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy fiddle leaf figs produce better cutting material:

- **Position:** Bright indirect light, some direct morning sun.
- **Watering:** Allow top few cm to dry between waterings.
- **Feeding:** Monthly during growing season.
- **Consistency:** They hate change - stable conditions essential.

### Selecting Material

Choose cuttings that display:

- Semi-mature stems (not newest growth)
- Healthy, undamaged leaves
- Good green color
- No brown spots or disease
