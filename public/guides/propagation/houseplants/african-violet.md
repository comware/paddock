# African Violet Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Leaf cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Division, sucker removal |
| **Time to Root** | 3-6 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Any time (indoor plants) |
| **Success Rate** | 85-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

African violets (Saintpaulia) are classic houseplants that propagate easily from leaf cuttings. A single healthy leaf can produce multiple new plantlets, making this a rewarding propagation experience.

The fuzzy leaves that make African violets charming are surprisingly good at producing new plants. Leaves root readily in water or soil, and the plantlets that emerge are exact replicas of the parent.

African violets can be propagated year-round since they're indoor plants, though active growth periods produce faster results.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Multiplying favourite varieties
- Sharing with friends
- Creating collections
- Replacing aging plants
- Competition and exhibition plants

## Propagation Methods

### Leaf Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Simple, reliable, produces multiple plantlets.

**When to take material:**
- Any time for indoor plants
- Best during active growth periods

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select healthy leaves**
   - Middle row leaves (not oldest or youngest)
   - Healthy, unblemished
   - Remove entire leaf with stem

2. **Prepare the cutting**
   - Cut stem (petiole) to 3-4cm length
   - Angle cut at 45 degrees (optional but traditional)

3. **Choose rooting method**
   - Water: Easy, visual progress
   - Soil: Direct, less transplant shock

4. **Water rooting method**
   - Place stem through plastic wrap or foil over water
   - Only stem touches water, not leaf
   - Change water weekly
   - Roots in 2-4 weeks
   - Plantlets emerge 4-8 weeks later

5. **Soil rooting method**
   - Insert stem 1-2cm into moist African violet mix
   - Keep consistently moist
   - Cover with plastic for humidity
   - Plantlets emerge in 6-10 weeks

6. **Separate plantlets**
   - When plantlets have 3-4 leaves
   - Gently separate from mother leaf
   - Pot individually in small pots
   - Keep moist until established

### Division (For Multi-Crowned Plants)

**When to use:** When plant has multiple crowns.

**Process:**
1. Remove from pot
2. Identify separate crowns with roots
3. Gently separate
4. Pot individually
5. Keep moist until established

### Sucker Removal

**When to use:** When suckers appear on established plants.

**Process:**
1. Remove suckers with sharp blade
2. Root as for leaf cuttings
3. Or plant directly if roots attached

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (20-26°C) |
| **Humidity** | High during rooting |
| **Light** | Bright indirect or fluorescent |
| **Medium** | Light, well-draining |
| **Water** | Consistently moist |

### Environment Setup

**For leaf cuttings:**
Covered container or pot in plastic bag for humidity. Bright indirect light or under grow lights. African violet potting mix or light peat/perlite blend.

**Critical factor:**
Consistent moisture and warmth. Don't let cuttings dry out, but avoid waterlogging.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Leaf Rots

**Symptoms:** Leaf turns mushy at base.

**Causes:** Too wet, water on leaf, cold conditions.

**Solution:** Start fresh with new leaf.

**Prevention:** Keep leaf dry (only stem in water/soil), good drainage, warmth.

### Problem 2: No Plantlets After Months

**Symptoms:** Roots present but no baby plants.

**Causes:** Insufficient light, cold, wrong variety (rare doubles sometimes struggle).

**Solution:** Increase light and warmth. Be patient - some take longer.

**Prevention:** Bright indirect light, consistent warmth, patience.

### Problem 3: Plantlets Are Weak

**Symptoms:** Tiny, struggling babies.

**Causes:** Low light, insufficient nutrition.

**Solution:** Improve conditions. Very light feeding once plantlets appear.

**Prevention:** Good light levels, healthy mother leaf provides energy.

## Pro Tips

1. **Middle row leaves work best** - Not the oldest outer leaves or youngest center leaves. Mature but vigorous.

2. **One leaf = multiple plants** - Each leaf cutting can produce 2-5+ plantlets. Great return on investment.

3. **Don't rush separation** - Wait until plantlets have 3-4 leaves before separating. Larger plantlets establish better.

4. **Mother leaf can be re-used** - After removing plantlets, the mother leaf can sometimes produce another round.

5. **Water rooting shows progress** - Great for beginners who want to see what's happening. Transfer carefully to soil.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy African violets produce excellent leaves:

- **Position:** Bright indirect light or under grow lights.
- **Watering:** Keep evenly moist; avoid wetting leaves.
- **Feeding:** Regular African violet fertiliser.
- **Grooming:** Remove spent flowers and damaged leaves.

### Selecting Material

Choose leaves that display:

- Healthy appearance
- Good color
- No pests or disease
- Middle row position
