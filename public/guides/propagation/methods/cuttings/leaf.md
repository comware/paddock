# Leaf Cuttings

## Overview

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Success Rate** | 70-95% typical |
| **Time to Root** | 3-8 weeks typical |
| **Best Season** | Spring to autumn |
| **Equipment Needed** | Sharp knife, shallow trays, rooting medium, humidity dome |

## What Are Leaf Cuttings?

Leaf cuttings use a whole leaf, part of a leaf, or a leaf with its stalk (petiole) to produce entirely new plants. Unlike stem cuttings where roots grow from an existing stem, leaf cuttings regenerate new stems, roots, and eventually a complete plant from leaf tissue alone.

This remarkable ability is limited to certain plant families, primarily succulents, begonias, African violets, and some houseplants. These species have cells that can dedifferentiate (become unspecialised) and then redifferentiate into roots, stems, and new leaves - a process called totipotency.

Leaf cuttings are particularly satisfying for beginners because they're simple, require minimal equipment, and often produce multiple plants from a single leaf. Watching a new plant emerge from what was just a leaf is one of gardening's small miracles.

## When to Use This Method

**Best for:**
- Succulents (echeveria, sedum, crassula, graptopetalum)
- African violets (Saintpaulia)
- Begonias (especially rex types)
- Streptocarpus (Cape primrose)
- Peperomias
- Sansevieria (snake plant)
- Some peppers (Peperomia, not food peppers)

**Not recommended for:**
- Most flowering plants
- Woody plants and shrubs
- Plants that don't have the genetic ability to regenerate
- Variegated plants if you want to maintain variegation (often reverts)

## Leaf Cutting Types

Different plants require different leaf cutting techniques:

### Type 1: Whole Leaf with Petiole

**Method:** Leaf removed with its stalk intact; stalk inserted into medium.

**Best for:**
- African violets
- Peperomias
- Florist's gloxinia

### Type 2: Whole Leaf Without Petiole

**Method:** Leaf laid flat on medium; new plants emerge from base or veins.

**Best for:**
- Most succulents
- Sansevieria (whole or sections)
- Some begonias

### Type 3: Leaf Section Cuttings

**Method:** Leaf cut into sections; each section produces a new plant.

**Best for:**
- Begonia rex (wedge cuttings)
- Sansevieria (horizontal sections)
- Streptocarpus (lengthwise sections)

### Type 4: Leaf Vein Cuttings

**Method:** Cuts made across main veins; new plants form at wound points.

**Best for:**
- Begonia rex
- Begonia masoniana

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1: Selecting Leaves

Choose healthy, mature leaves for best results.

**Selection criteria:**
- Fully developed, mature leaves (not too young or old)
- No pest damage or disease
- Typical colour and form for the species
- Firm and turgid (well-hydrated)

**What to avoid:**
- Very young, still-developing leaves
- Old, yellowing leaves
- Damaged or blemished leaves
- Leaves from stressed plants

**Key points:**
- Middle-aged leaves have best regeneration capacity
- One healthy leaf can produce multiple plants
- Water parent plant 24 hours before

**Common mistakes:**
- Using old or damaged leaves
- Taking from dehydrated plants

### Step 2: Taking the Cutting

Technique varies by cutting type.

**For whole leaf with petiole (African violet, peperomia):**
1. Select healthy leaf with strong stalk
2. Cut or snap petiole from stem
3. Trim petiole to 2-4cm length
4. Keep leaf intact

**For whole leaf without petiole (succulents):**
1. Gently twist or cut leaf from stem
2. Ensure clean break at base
3. Include the entire base where it attached to stem
4. Don't damage the attachment point

**For leaf sections (begonia, sansevieria):**
1. Remove healthy leaf
2. Cut into sections with sharp blade
3. Mark which end was toward the stem (for sansevieria)
4. Each section should be 5-8cm

**Key points:**
- Clean, sharp cuts prevent rot
- For succulents, complete base is essential
- Mark top/bottom on sectioned leaves

**Common mistakes:**
- Tearing instead of clean cuts
- Incomplete succulent leaf bases

### Step 3: Callusing (Succulents Only)

Succulent leaves must callus before planting.

**Process:**
1. Lay leaves in dry, shaded location
2. Allow cut surface to dry and heal
3. Wait 2-7 days depending on humidity
4. Surface should feel dry and slightly tough

**Why callusing matters:**
- Prevents rot when planted
- Seals wound against pathogens
- Indicates leaf is ready to root

**Conditions for callusing:**
- Dry air (not humid)
- Shade (not dark, not bright sun)
- Room temperature
- Good air circulation

**Key points:**
- This step is NOT needed for non-succulents
- Humid climates need longer callusing
- Leaf should not shrivel excessively

**Common mistakes:**
- Skipping callusing entirely
- Callusing too long (leaf desiccates)
- Callusing in high humidity

### Step 4: Preparing Medium

Different plants need different media.

**For succulents:**
- 70% coarse sand or perlite, 30% potting mix
- Or pure pumice/perlite
- Must drain instantly
- Barely moist

**For African violets:**
- 50% perlite, 50% peat or coco coir
- Or commercial African violet mix
- Light and moisture-retentive
- Evenly moist

**For begonias:**
- 50% perlite, 50% peat
- Good moisture retention
- Acidic pH preferred
- Evenly moist

**Key points:**
- Drainage critical for succulents
- Moisture retention needed for tropical types
- Pre-moisten before planting

**Common mistakes:**
- Using regular potting mix for succulents (too wet)
- Dry medium for tropical leaf cuttings

### Step 5: Planting Technique

Method depends on cutting type.

**Whole leaf with petiole:**
1. Make hole in medium at 45° angle
2. Insert petiole 1-2cm deep
3. Leaf blade rests on surface or slightly above
4. Firm gently around petiole

**Whole succulent leaf:**
1. Lay flat on surface of medium
2. Base can touch medium or hover just above
3. Don't bury the leaf
4. Some growers don't use medium at all initially

**Leaf sections (sansevieria):**
1. Insert bottom end (was toward base of leaf) 2-3cm into medium
2. Wrong way up = won't root
3. Space 3-5cm apart

**Vein cuttings (begonia rex):**
1. Lay whole leaf flat, underside down
2. Pin down to ensure contact with medium
3. Make small cuts across major veins
4. Keep pinned and moist

**Key points:**
- Succulents need less contact with medium
- Tropical types need consistent moisture
- Sections must be right way up

**Common mistakes:**
- Burying succulent leaves (causes rot)
- Dry medium for tropical cuttings
- Sections planted upside down

### Step 6: Creating Environment

Environment varies dramatically by plant type.

**For succulents:**
- NO humidity dome (causes rot)
- Bright, indirect light
- Minimal water initially
- Good air circulation

**For African violets and begonias:**
- Humidity dome essential
- Bright, indirect light
- Keep consistently moist
- Warm conditions (20-24°C)

**For sansevieria:**
- Can go either way
- Prefers dry side
- Low light tolerant
- Warm temperatures help

**Key points:**
- Succulent and tropical needs are opposite
- Succulents rot in humidity
- Tropical types dry out without humidity

**Common mistakes:**
- Putting succulent leaves under humidity dome
- Letting tropical cuttings dry out

### Step 7: Ongoing Care

Monitor and maintain appropriate conditions.

**Succulent leaf cuttings:**
- Mist lightly once or twice weekly only
- No watering until roots visible
- Remove any rotting leaves immediately
- Wait for visible roots before watering

**Tropical leaf cuttings:**
- Maintain humidity
- Mist regularly
- Keep medium moist (not wet)
- Vent dome periodically

**Timeline awareness:**
- Succulents: 2-4 weeks to see roots/plantlet
- African violets: 4-8 weeks to see plantlet
- Begonias: 4-12 weeks for new plants

**Key points:**
- Patience required - process is slow
- Check for rot regularly
- Roots often appear before shoots

**Common mistakes:**
- Overwatering succulents
- Letting tropical cuttings dry out
- Giving up too early

### Step 8: Recognising Success

Signs that propagation is working.

**Succulents:**
- Small pink/white roots emerge from leaf base
- Tiny rosette or plantlet appears
- Mother leaf may wrinkle as energy transfers to baby

**African violets:**
- Roots visible if growing in water
- Small leaves emerge at soil level
- Takes 6-12 weeks for visible plantlet

**Begonias:**
- Small plantlets at base or wound points
- Multiple plants possible from one leaf
- 6-16 weeks for sizeable plantlets

**Key points:**
- Succulents: many leaves produce multiple babies
- Plantlet growth is initially very slow
- Mother leaf provides energy to babies

**Common mistakes:**
- Removing mother leaf too early
- Disturbing developing plantlets

### Step 9: Potting Up Babies

Separate and pot individual new plants.

**When ready:**
- Succulent: Plantlet has own roots, 2-3cm size
- African violet: Several leaves, established root system
- Begonia: Plantlet is 3-5cm, own roots visible

**Separation process:**
1. Wait until baby is viable size
2. Gently separate from mother leaf
3. Allow any damaged roots to callus (succulents)
4. Pot into appropriate mix

**First care:**
- Small pot (oversized pots cause rot)
- Appropriate soil for species
- Careful watering - baby roots are delicate
- Bright indirect light

**Key points:**
- Don't rush - babies are fragile
- Mother leaf often dies after babies established
- Some mother leaves produce multiple generations

**Common mistakes:**
- Separating too early
- Overwatering tiny babies
- Using pots too large

Shallow tray with coarse sand/perlite mix. No cover. Bright windowsill or under grow light. Mist weekly at most.

Shallow tray with moist peat/perlite. Cover with plastic. Bright indirect light. High humidity essential.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Rot Before Rooting (Succulents)

**Symptoms:** Leaf becomes translucent, mushy, brown; never produces roots.

**Causes:**
- No callusing before planting
- Medium too wet
- Too much humidity
- Damaged leaf base

**Solutions:**
- Discard rotted leaves
- Improve drainage dramatically
- Remove humidity if present
- Start fresh with proper callusing

**Prevention:**
- Always callus succulent leaves
- Minimal water until roots visible
- Never use humidity domes for succulents

### Problem 2: Leaf Dries Before Rooting (Tropical)

**Symptoms:** Leaf becomes crispy, shrivelled, or papery before producing plantlet.

**Causes:**
- Humidity too low
- Medium dried out
- Too much air circulation
- Damaged petiole

**Solutions:**
- Usually too late if severely dried
- Try increasing humidity with remaining
- Keep medium consistently moist

**Prevention:**
- Use humidity dome
- Mist regularly
- Check moisture daily

### Problem 3: Slow or No Plantlet Development

**Symptoms:** Roots form but no new plant emerges, or process is very slow.

**Causes:**
- Temperature too cool
- Low light
- Old or unhealthy leaf
- Insufficient time (some are just slow)

**Solutions:**
- Add bottom heat
- Improve lighting
- Be patient - some take months
- Ensure leaf is still alive

**Prevention:**
- Use healthy, mature leaves
- Provide warmth (20-24°C)
- Adequate light

## Pro Tips

1. **Succulent leaves pointing up slightly root better** - Position leaves so the base barely touches or hovers just above the medium. Water collecting at base causes rot; slight elevation allows roots to grow downward naturally.

2. **Begonia vein cuts work best with multiple small cuts** - Rather than a few large cuts across veins, make many small incisions. Each cut point can produce a plantlet, maximising yield from one leaf.

3. **African violet petioles at 45° produce more babies** - Inserting the petiole at an angle rather than straight down increases the number of plantlets that form at the soil surface.

4. **Succulent mother leaves transfer colour** - The mother leaf often fades or shrivels as it transfers energy to babies. This is normal - don't remove until completely dried, as it's still feeding the plantlet.

5. **Variegated sansevieria from leaf cuttings revert to green** - To maintain variegation, propagate by division instead. Leaf cuttings produce genetically normal (green) plants.

## Variations & Adaptations

### Water Rooting

Some species (African violets, pothos) root readily in plain water:
1. Suspend leaf with petiole in water
2. Use jar or container with support
3. Keep water level consistent
4. Change water weekly
5. Pot up when roots 2-3cm long

### Sand-Only Method for Succulents

Many propagators skip medium entirely:
1. Lay leaves on dry surface (tray, paper)
2. No water at all until roots visible
3. Then transfer to very dry medium
4. Extremely low rot risk
