# Softwood Cuttings

## Overview

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Success Rate** | 70-95% typical |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks typical |
| **Best Season** | Spring to early summer |
| **Equipment Needed** | Secateurs, rooting medium, humidity dome, heat mat (optional) |

## What Are Softwood Cuttings?

Softwood cuttings are taken from the current season's new, green, flexible growth before it begins to harden and turn woody. This is the most common and often most successful form of stem cutting propagation for many plants.

The key characteristic of softwood is its flexibility - stems should bend easily without snapping. This young growth contains high levels of natural growth hormones and actively dividing cells, making it ideal for rapid root development. However, the same qualities that make softwood root quickly also make it vulnerable to wilting and rot, requiring careful humidity management.

Softwood cuttings are particularly effective for herbaceous plants, many shrubs, and houseplants. They're the go-to method for herbs like basil, mint, and rosemary, as well as garden favourites like hydrangeas, fuchsias, and geraniums.

## When to Use This Method

**Best for:**
- Herbs (basil, mint, oregano, sage, rosemary)
- Herbaceous perennials (salvias, geraniums, penstemons)
- Many houseplants (pothos, philodendron, tradescantia)
- Deciduous shrubs in active growth (hydrangea, fuchsia, weigela)
- Plants that root quickly and easily

**Not recommended for:**
- Dormant deciduous plants (use hardwood cuttings instead)
- Plants with very woody growth only
- Species that root poorly from stem cuttings
- Conifers (most need semi-hardwood or hardwood)

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1: Timing Your Cuttings

Take softwood cuttings in **spring to early summer** (September to December in Australia) when plants are in active growth.

**Best time of day:** Early morning when plants are fully hydrated.

**Ideal conditions:**
- Parent plant is healthy and well-watered
- New growth is 8-15cm long
- Stems bend without snapping
- No flower buds (or remove them)

**Key points:**
- Water parent plant the day before
- Avoid taking cuttings during heat waves
- Cloudy days are better than hot sunny ones

**Common mistakes:**
- Taking cuttings when plant is stressed or dry
- Waiting until growth becomes too woody

### Step 2: Selecting and Cutting Material

Choose healthy, vigorous shoots from the current season's growth.

**Selection criteria:**
- Non-flowering shoots (or pinch off buds)
- Healthy, pest-free foliage
- Typical growth (not unusually weak or strong)
- No signs of disease

**Making the cut:**
1. Cut stems 8-12cm long
2. Make the cut just below a node (where leaf joins stem)
3. Cut at a 45° angle for larger surface area
4. Place immediately in damp paper towel or water

**Key points:**
- Work quickly - softwood wilts fast
- Take more cuttings than you need (expect some losses)
- Keep cuttings cool and moist while working

**Common mistakes:**
- Letting cuttings dry out before planting
- Crushing stems with blunt tools

### Step 3: Preparing Cuttings

Process cuttings promptly in a shaded, cool area.

**Preparation steps:**
1. Remove lower leaves (keep top 2-4 leaves)
2. If remaining leaves are large, cut them in half horizontally
3. Remove any flower buds or flowers
4. Trim base cut if it has dried or crushed

**Why reduce leaf area:**
- Reduces water loss through transpiration
- Balances water demand with no root system
- Prevents leaves touching medium (rot risk)

**Key points:**
- Handle stems gently
- Make clean cuts on leaves (not tears)
- Keep removed leaves for composting

**Common mistakes:**
- Leaving too many or too large leaves
- Removing all leaves (need some for photosynthesis)

### Step 4: Applying Rooting Hormone (Optional but Recommended)

Rooting hormone increases success rate and speeds root development.

**Application method:**
1. Pour small amount of hormone onto paper or dish
2. Dip cut end 2-3cm into hormone
3. Tap off excess gently
4. Don't return unused hormone to container

**Choosing hormone strength:**
- Softwood: Use lowest strength (purple/No.1)
- Easy-rooting plants may not need any
- Gel adheres better than powder to soft stems

**Key points:**
- Fresh hormone works better (check expiry)
- Don't over-apply - thin coating is sufficient
- Plant immediately after applying

**Common mistakes:**
- Using hormone that's too strong (can inhibit rooting)
- Double-dipping (contaminates container)

### Step 5: Planting Cuttings

Insert cuttings into pre-moistened rooting medium.

**Process:**
1. Make hole with pencil or dibber (don't push cutting straight in)
2. Insert cutting about 3-5cm deep (1-2 nodes below surface)
3. Gently firm medium around stem
4. Space cuttings so leaves don't touch each other or dome

**Medium preparation:**
- Mix medium and moisten before planting
- Should be damp but not waterlogged
- If squeezed, minimal water should drip

**Key points:**
- Pre-made holes prevent hormone being scraped off
- Firm enough to support, not tight
- One cutting per cell, or space appropriately

**Common mistakes:**
- Planting too deep or too shallow
- Cuttings touching each other (disease spread)
- Medium too wet or too dry

### Step 6: Creating Humidity Environment

Softwood cuttings must have high humidity until roots develop.

**Setup options:**

**Option A - Propagation tray with dome:**
1. Place tray in solid base tray
2. Cover with clear dome
3. Close or partly close vents
4. Position in bright indirect light

**Option B - Plastic bag method:**
1. Place pot in large clear plastic bag
2. Insert sticks to keep bag off leaves
3. Seal loosely with rubber band
4. Open daily for air exchange

**Key points:**
- Some condensation is normal
- Excessive dripping = too much humidity
- Vent daily to prevent fungal problems

**Common mistakes:**
- Direct sun on covered cuttings (cooks them)
- Forgetting to vent (causes rot)

### Step 7: Positioning and Environment

Place cuttings in optimal location for rooting.

**Light:** Bright, indirect light. No direct sun.

**Temperature:**
- Air: 18-24°C
- Medium (with heat mat): 20-24°C

**Location options:**
- Bright windowsill (not south-facing/sunny)
- Under grow lights
- In shadehouse or greenhouse (shaded)

**Key points:**
- Consistent conditions are better than variable
- Avoid drafts and temperature swings
- Bottom heat significantly speeds rooting

**Common mistakes:**
- Direct afternoon sun (overheats)
- Cold windowsill (slows rooting)
- Dark location (weak growth)

### Step 8: Ongoing Care

Monitor and maintain cuttings during rooting period.

**Daily tasks:**
- Check moisture (mist if surface drying)
- Vent humidity dome briefly
- Remove any fallen or rotting material
- Watch for problems

**Weekly tasks:**
- Check for root development (gently)
- Remove any failed cuttings
- Ensure dome/bag is clean

**Watering:**
- Mist to maintain humidity
- Bottom water if medium is drying
- Never waterlog

**Key points:**
- Consistency is more important than perfection
- Less intervention is often better
- Patience - roots take 2-4 weeks

**Common mistakes:**
- Overwatering
- Disturbing cuttings too frequently
- Giving up too early

### Step 9: Checking for Roots

Test for rooting without damaging developing roots.

**Methods to check:**
1. **Gentle tug test:** Light resistance = roots forming
2. **Look through drainage holes:** Visible white roots
3. **New growth:** Often indicates roots established
4. **Stability:** Cutting doesn't wobble in medium

**When to check:**
- After 2 weeks for easy species
- After 3-4 weeks for slower species
- When you see new top growth

**Key points:**
- Don't pull hard (damages roots)
- Visible roots through container = definitely ready
- Some species root faster than others

**Common mistakes:**
- Checking too frequently
- Pulling too hard and breaking roots

### Step 10: Hardening Off and Potting Up

Transition rooted cuttings to normal growing conditions.

**Hardening off process:**
1. Week 1: Remove dome for increasing periods (1-4 hours)
2. Week 2: Dome off most of day
3. Week 3: Dome off completely, introduce brighter light

**Potting up:**
1. Wait until roots are well-developed (filling container)
2. Use pot one size larger
3. Use standard potting mix (not propagation mix)
4. Water well after potting
5. Keep shaded for a few days

**Key points:**
- Don't rush this stage
- Watch for wilting during transition
- If wilting, return to humidity briefly

**Common mistakes:**
- Hardening off too quickly
- Potting up before roots established
- Using oversized pots

## Environmental Requirements

| Factor | Optimal Range | Acceptable Range |
|--------|---------------|------------------|
| **Temperature** | 20-24°C | 18-27°C |
| **Humidity** | 85-95% | 70-95% |
| **Light** | Bright indirect | Moderate to bright (no direct sun) |
| **Air circulation** | Minimal with daily venting | Avoid stagnant air |

### Creating the Right Environment

**Option 1: Heated propagator**
Purpose-built propagators with thermostat control provide ideal conditions. Set bottom heat to 20-22°C, keep vents partly closed initially, and position in bright indirect light.

**Option 2: DIY humidity dome**
Use clear plastic containers, large plastic bags, or purchased domes over trays. Support with sticks if using bags to keep plastic off foliage. Vent daily.

**Option 3: Heat mat + dome**
Combine seedling heat mat (with thermostat) under trays with standard propagation dome. Most cost-effective serious setup.

|-------|----------|----------------|
| Initial stress | 1-3 days | Some wilting normal, should recover |
| Root initiation | 5-10 days | No visible change, but roots starting |
| Root development | 1-3 weeks | Resistance on tug test, possible new growth |
| Ready to pot up | 3-6 weeks | Strong roots visible, stable plant |
| **Total** | **3-6 weeks** | |

### Factors That Affect Speed

**Faster rooting:**
- Bottom heat (20-24°C soil temperature)
- Rooting hormone used
- Spring timing (peak growth hormones)
- Easy-rooting species
- Healthy, vigorous parent material

**Slower rooting:**
- Cool conditions (below 18°C)
- Late season cuttings
- Difficult species
- Weak or stressed parent plants
- Flowering material (energy diverted)

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Wilting That Doesn't Recover

**Symptoms:** Leaves droop and don't perk up after 2-3 days, may become crispy.

**Causes:**
- Humidity too low
- Too much heat or direct sun
- Cutting dried out before planting
- Root damage (if already rooting)

**Solutions:**
- Increase humidity (check dome seal)
- Move to cooler location
- Mist more frequently
- If severe, cutting may not recover

**Prevention:**
- Process cuttings quickly after cutting
- Keep in water or damp towel while working
- Ensure humidity dome is properly sealed

### Problem 2: Stem Rot at Base

**Symptoms:** Stem turns black/brown at soil level, becomes mushy, cutting collapses.

**Causes:**
- Medium too wet
- Poor drainage
- Damaged or crushed stems
- Fungal infection

**Solutions:**
- Remove affected cuttings immediately
- Reduce watering
- Improve drainage
- Take fresh cuttings with clean tools

**Prevention:**
- Use well-draining medium
- Don't overwater
- Make clean cuts
- Sterilise tools between plants

### Problem 3: Mould on Medium or Cuttings

**Symptoms:** White, grey, or green fuzzy growth on surface or leaves.

**Causes:**
- Poor air circulation
- Excessive humidity
- Dead material left in tray
- Contaminated medium

**Solutions:**
- Increase ventilation
- Remove affected material
- Sprinkle cinnamon (antifungal)
- May need to restart if severe

**Prevention:**
- Vent daily
- Remove fallen leaves promptly
- Use sterile medium
- Don't overcrowd cuttings

## Pro Tips

1. **Water the parent plant well 24 hours before** - Hydrated plants produce cuttings that root better. Stressed, dry plants have lower success rates.

2. **Work in batches in the shade** - Set up a shaded area with damp paper towels. Cut several stems, wrap in damp towel, then process all at once. Prevents individual cuttings drying out.

3. **The bend test is reliable** - Softwood bends without snapping. If it snaps cleanly, it's becoming semi-hardwood. If it's very limp, it may be too young.

4. **Take more cuttings than you need** - Expect 20-30% losses on new species. Ten cuttings is better than three, and you can always give away extras.

5. **Bottom heat is your best friend** - A thermostat-controlled heat mat will improve success rates on most species by 20-30%. Worth the investment.

## Variations & Adaptations

### Tip Cuttings vs Stem Sections

**Tip cuttings** (include growing tip): Preferred for most plants. Natural growth hormones concentrated at tip.

**Stem sections** (middle of stem): Can work but often slower. Useful when material is limited.

### Water Rooting

Some easy species (pothos, tradescantia, mint) root readily in plain water. Simply place cuttings in jar of water on windowsill. Change water weekly. Pot up when roots are 3-5cm long.
