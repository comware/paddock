# Division

## Overview

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Success Rate** | 90-100% typical |
| **Time to Root** | Instant (already rooted) |
| **Best Season** | Spring or autumn |
| **Equipment Needed** | Spade, fork, sharp knife (some plants), buckets |

## What Is Division?

Division is the simplest form of vegetative propagation - physically separating a clump-forming plant into multiple pieces, each with its own roots and shoots. Unlike cuttings that need to develop roots, divided sections are already complete plants that just need to establish in their new location.

This method works for plants that naturally grow in expanding clumps, producing multiple crowns, offsets, or suckers from a central point. Many perennials, ornamental grasses, bulbs, and groundcovers propagate easily this way.

Division is not only the fastest way to multiply these plants but also a beneficial practice for plant health. Many clumping plants become congested over time, with the centre dying out while edges remain vigorous. Division rejuvenates these plants while producing new ones.

## When to Use This Method

**Best for:**
- Clumping perennials (hostas, daylilies, agapanthus)
- Ornamental grasses
- Spreading groundcovers (mondo grass, ajuga)
- Rhizomatous plants (iris, canna)
- Succulents with offsets (aloe, agave)
- Many herbs (chives, mint, lemongrass)
- Some bulbs and tubers

**Not recommended for:**
- Plants with single crowns or taproots
- Woody shrubs (use cuttings)
- Trees
- Very young or recently planted specimens

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1: Timing Your Division

Choose the right season for the plant type.

**Spring division (September-November in Australia):**
- Best for summer/autumn flowering perennials
- Ornamental grasses
- Most tropical plants
- Plants that flower before mid-summer

**Autumn division (March-May in Australia):**
- Best for spring/early summer flowering perennials
- Bulbs (after foliage dies)
- Plants that need winter establishment
- Most cool-climate perennials

**Avoid dividing:**
- During flowering
- During extreme heat or cold
- When very dry or waterlogged
- When plants are stressed

**Key points:**
- Cool, overcast days are ideal
- Water plant well 24-48 hours before
- Have new locations prepared before lifting

**Common mistakes:**
- Dividing during hot, sunny weather
- Working with dry plants

### Step 2: Preparing the Plant

Set up the plant for easy division.

**Preparation steps:**
1. Water thoroughly 1-2 days before
2. Cut back tall foliage by half (optional but helpful)
3. Mark new planting locations
4. Prepare holes and soil amendments
5. Have buckets of water ready for divisions

**Why cut back foliage:**
- Reduces water loss while roots establish
- Easier to handle and see crown structure
- Redirects energy to root recovery
- Not essential but often helpful

**Key points:**
- Cooler, overcast conditions are best
- Early morning or late afternoon
- Keep divisions shaded and moist throughout

**Common mistakes:**
- Dividing on hot sunny afternoons
- Not preparing new locations first

### Step 3: Lifting the Plant

Remove the plant from the ground with minimal damage.

**Lifting process:**
1. Insert fork in circle around plant, 15-20cm from centre
2. Loosen soil from all sides
3. Lever plant upward gently
4. Lift when loosened, supporting root mass
5. Move to work area

**For very large clumps:**
- May need to cut around edges first with spade
- Divide in ground before full lifting
- Or lift in sections

**Key points:**
- Fork is gentler than spade
- Protect roots from sun and wind
- Work efficiently but not rushed

**Common mistakes:**
- Cutting through centre with spade (damages crowns)
- Leaving lifted plants in sun

### Step 4: Assessing the Clump

Examine the plant structure to plan divisions.

**What to look for:**
- Natural division points (gaps, separate rosettes)
- Active growth points (crowns with leaves/shoots)
- Dead or woody centre (discard this)
- Root mass distribution

**Division size planning:**
- Large divisions = faster recovery, fewer plants
- Small divisions = more plants, slower establishment
- Each piece needs: at least one growth point + adequate roots

**Key points:**
- Discard old, woody, or dead sections
- Outer portions are usually most vigorous
- Work in shade or bring to covered area

**Common mistakes:**
- Keeping the tired centre
- Making divisions too small

### Step 5: Dividing the Clump

Separate into viable sections.

**Methods for different plant types:**

**Easy to pull apart (hostas, daylilies):**
1. Gently pull sections apart with hands
2. Work from outer edge inward
3. Tease roots apart if tangled
4. Sections come apart at natural breaks

**Need cutting (agapanthus, ornamental grasses):**
1. Use sharp spade or knife
2. Cut vertically through crown
3. Make clean cuts, don't tear
4. Aim for 3-5 growing points per division

**Rhizomes (iris, canna):**
1. Snap or cut at natural joints
2. Each section needs at least one fan/growing point
3. Trim roots to 10-15cm
4. Trim leaves to 10-15cm (iris)

**Bulbs and offsets:**
1. Gently separate offset bulbs from parent
2. Each offset is a new plant
3. Discard very small offsets (replant to grow on)

**Key points:**
- Clean cuts heal better than tears
- Bigger divisions establish faster
- Minimum 3-5 shoots per division for most perennials

**Common mistakes:**
- Divisions too small (struggle to establish)
- Ragged cuts (rot risk)
- Including dead material

### Step 6: Preparing Divisions for Planting

Clean up and trim before replanting.

**Preparation steps:**
1. Trim damaged or dead roots
2. Reduce excessive foliage
3. Remove dead leaves and debris
4. Keep in water or moist environment until planting

**Optional treatments:**
- Dust cut surfaces with fungicide (for disease-prone plants)
- Dip roots in seaweed solution (stimulates establishment)

**Key points:**
- Don't let roots dry out
- Plant same day if possible
- Discard any diseased material

**Common mistakes:**
- Leaving divisions sitting in sun
- Planting damaged or dead sections

### Step 7: Replanting

Get divisions back in the ground promptly.

**Planting process:**
1. Dig hole larger than root mass
2. Add compost if soil is poor
3. Position at same depth as before (or slightly shallower for iris)
4. Spread roots naturally
5. Backfill and firm gently
6. Water deeply immediately

**Spacing:**
- Same as original spacing recommendation for species
- Closer for quick cover, wider for specimen plants
- Consider mature size

**Special cases:**
- Iris: Rhizome partially exposed to sun
- Bulbs: Appropriate depth for species
- Groundcovers: Multiple pieces for faster fill

**Key points:**
- Plant at correct depth
- Water immediately and generously
- Firm soil to eliminate air pockets

**Common mistakes:**
- Planting too deep (crowns rot)
- Not watering in properly
- Expecting immediate flowering

### Step 8: Aftercare

Support divisions while they establish.

**First few weeks:**
- Water regularly (don't let dry out)
- Shade from harsh sun if needed
- Remove any dead foliage
- Mulch to retain moisture (not against stems)

**First season:**
- Regular watering until established
- Light feeding after 4-6 weeks
- Don't expect peak flowering first year
- Watch for stress signs

**Signs of success:**
- New leaf growth
- Plant remaining turgid
- No wilting after initial transplant stress

**Key points:**
- Consistent watering crucial for establishment
- Don't over-fertilise stressed plants
- Patience - may take a season to flourish

**Common mistakes:**
- Letting divisions dry out after planting
- Over-fertilising (causes weak growth)
- Expecting instant results

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Divisions Wilting After Planting

**Symptoms:** Leaves drooping, even with moist soil.

**Causes:**
- Root damage during division
- Hot, sunny conditions
- Divisions too small
- Not enough water

**Solutions:**
- Provide temporary shade
- Water more frequently
- Mist foliage in cool hours
- Trim more foliage to reduce demand

**Prevention:**
- Divide on cool, overcast days
- Larger divisions are more resilient
- Keep roots moist throughout
- Water well immediately after planting

### Problem 2: Crown Rot After Division

**Symptoms:** Base of plant becomes mushy, plant collapses.

**Causes:**
- Planted too deep
- Damaged crown tissue
- Waterlogged soil
- Diseased parent material

**Solutions:**
- Remove and replant shallower (if caught early)
- Improve drainage
- Usually fatal - remove affected plants

**Prevention:**
- Plant at correct depth (crowns at or just below surface)
- Clean cuts with sharp tools
- Avoid waterlogged sites
- Don't divide diseased plants

### Problem 3: Poor Recovery/Growth

**Symptoms:** Plant survives but doesn't thrive, little new growth.

**Causes:**
- Division too small
- Poor soil conditions
- Wrong season for that species
- Root damage

**Solutions:**
- Extra care: water, shade, protection
- Light feeding after established
- May need full season to recover
- Consider site suitability

**Prevention:**
- Make adequately sized divisions
- Prepare soil well
- Divide at appropriate season
- Handle roots carefully

## Pro Tips

1. **Divide when plants tell you** - Reduced flowering, dead centres, and crowded clumps indicate division is needed. For most perennials, this is every 3-5 years.

2. **The outer pieces are the best** - The young, vigorous growth is always at the edges of clumps. Discard the old, woody centre and use the fresh outer sections.

3. **Pre-dig your holes** - Have all new locations prepared before you lift the plant. Divisions should spend minimum time out of ground.

4. **Use two forks for tough clumps** - Insert two garden forks back-to-back into the clump and lever apart. This separates with less root damage than cutting.

5. **Divisions make great gifts and trades** - Share extras with neighbours and gardening friends. Most divided perennials produce more sections than you need.

## Division by Plant Type

### Clumping Perennials
(Hostas, daylilies, ornamental grasses)
- Lift entire clump
- Pull or cut apart
- Replant divisions immediately

### Rhizomatous Plants
(Iris, canna, lily of the valley)
- Lift or expose rhizomes
- Cut into sections with growing points
- Replant at appropriate depth

### Suckering Plants
(Raspberry, mint, running bamboo)
- Dig up suckers with roots
- Sever connection to parent
- Replant immediately

### Bulbs and Offsets
(Agave, aloe, daffodils)
- Separate offsets from parent
- May need knife for tough connections
- Plant at appropriate depth
