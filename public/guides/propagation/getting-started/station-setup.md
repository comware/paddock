# Setting Up Your Propagation Station

A dedicated propagation area makes consistent results easier and keeps your materials organised.

---

## Location Requirements

### Light Conditions

**Ideal:** Bright, indirect light

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **North-facing window** (Southern Hemisphere) | Consistent bright light, no scorching | May be too dim in winter |
| **East-facing window** | Gentle morning sun | Afternoon may be dim |
| **Under grow lights** | Consistent, controllable | Extra cost, setup needed |
| **Greenhouse/shadehouse** | Ideal environment | Not everyone has access |

**Avoid:**
- Direct afternoon sun (overheats cuttings under domes)
- Dark corners (slow rooting, weak growth)
- Fluctuating light conditions

### Temperature

**Optimal range:** 20-24°C for most species

**Consider:**
- Consistent warmth is better than fluctuating
- Heat rises - top shelves are warmer
- Avoid cold drafts from doors/windows
- Bottom heat can compensate for cooler room temps

### Access & Workflow

**Location should allow:**
- Daily monitoring without disruption
- Easy watering/misting access
- Good visibility to spot problems early
- Space for tools and materials nearby

## Basic Setup Options

### Option 1: Windowsill Station

**Best for:** Beginners, small batches, limited space

**Setup:**
- Waterproof tray on sill
- Heat mat underneath (if windowsill is cold)
- Propagation trays with domes
- Spray bottle within reach

**Capacity:** 1-2 standard trays (20-50 cuttings)

**Cost:** $50-100

### Option 2: Shelving Unit

**Best for:** Serious hobbyists, multiple batches

**Setup:**
- Wire or solid shelving (avoid wood - moisture damage)
- Multiple levels for different stages
- Grow lights if away from windows
- Heat mats on propagation shelves

**Capacity:** 4-8 trays across levels (100-300 cuttings)

**Cost:** $150-300

### Option 3: Heated Propagator

**Best for:** Year-round propagation, difficult species

**Setup:**
- Large heated propagator with thermostat
- Placed in bright location
- Dedicated power supply
- Ventilation management

**Capacity:** Varies by unit size (50-200 cuttings)

**Cost:** $100-300 for unit alone

### Option 4: Outdoor Shadehouse

**Best for:** Large scale, hardier species

**Setup:**
- 50-70% shade cloth structure
- Misting system (manual or automatic)
- Benches at working height
- Protection from rain (optional roof)

**Capacity:** Hundreds to thousands of cuttings

**Cost:** $500-2000+

## Essential Station Components

### Work Surface

**Requirements:**
- Waterproof or water-resistant
- Easy to clean
- Adequate size for working
- At comfortable height

**Options:**
- Stainless steel bench
- Plastic-topped table
- Large waterproof tray on any surface
- Purpose-built potting bench

### Water Access

**Needs:**
- Clean water source nearby
- Watering can for larger volumes
- Spray bottle for misting
- Drainage for excess

**Pro tip:** Keep a filled watering can at room temperature. Cold tap water can shock cuttings.

### Storage for Supplies

**Organise:**
- Rooting mediums (sealed to prevent drying/contamination)
- Pots and trays (stacked by size)
- Labels and markers
- Tools (hung or in container)
- Rooting hormones (cool, dark spot)

### Waste Management

**Plan for:**
- Spent medium disposal
- Failed cuttings removal
- Plant debris cleanup
- Easy sweeping/wiping

## Lighting Systems

### Natural Light Only

**Works if you have:**
- Large, bright windows
- Consistent daylight hours
- Ability to move trays seasonally

**Limitations:**
- Variable with weather/seasons
- May need supplementing in winter
- Not ideal for all species

### Supplemental Grow Lights

**When to add:**
- Dim indoor spaces
- Winter propagation
- Shelving away from windows
- Consistent results required

### Grow Light Types

| Type | Pros | Cons | Cost |
|------|------|------|------|
| **LED panels** | Energy efficient, low heat, long-lasting | Higher upfront cost | $50-200 |
| **T5 fluorescents** | Even coverage, good for seedlings | Bulbs need replacement | $40-100 |
| **LED strip lights** | Flexible, easy to install | May need multiple strips | $30-80 |

**Settings:**
- Height: 15-30cm above cuttings
- Duration: 12-16 hours daily
- Timer: Essential for consistency

## Temperature Management

### Heat Mats

**Standard setup:**
- Mat under propagation tray
- Thermostat for control (essential!)
- Target: 20-24°C soil temperature
- Waterproof mat essential

**Sizing:** Match mat to tray size or slightly larger

### Thermostats

**Why essential:**
- Heat mats without thermostats can overheat
- Allows precise temperature setting
- Prevents cooking roots
- Energy savings

**Types:**
- Plug-in with probe
- Digital with display
- Programmable (day/night settings)

### Heating Tips

- Place probe in rooting medium, not air
- Monitor ambient room temperature too
- Cold rooms may need more heat input
- Check mat is working periodically

---

## Humidity Management

### Passive Humidity (Domes)

**Standard approach:**
- Clear plastic dome over tray
- Vents in dome for adjustment
- Works for most situations

**Managing condensation:**
- Some condensation is normal
- Excess pooling = too much humidity
- Vent daily to refresh air
- Wipe excessive droplets

### Active Misting

**Manual misting:**
- Spray bottle for small setups
- Pump sprayer for larger areas
- 2-3 times daily in dry conditions

**Automated misting:**
- Timer-controlled misters
- Ideal for large setups
- Requires plumbing/power
- Watch for overwatering

### Humidity Troubleshooting

| Issue | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Too dry (cuttings wilting) | Mist more, reduce venting |
| Too wet (mould, rot) | Increase venting, mist less |
| Uneven humidity | Check dome seal, redistribute trays |

## Organisation Systems

### Staging Areas

**Recommended zones:**

1. **Preparation area** - cutting, treating, labelling
2. **Active propagation** - trays with domes, heat mats
3. **Hardening off** - domes removed, transitioning
4. **Growing on** - potted cuttings before final planting

### Labelling System

**Every tray needs:**
- Plant name (species and variety)
- Date cuttings taken
- Number of cuttings
- Method/hormone used

**Label materials:**
- Waterproof markers
- Plastic labels (reusable)
- Grease pencils
- Label maker (long-lasting)

### Record Keeping

**Track:**
- What you propagated
- Success/failure rates
- Timing observations
- Conditions and methods

**Options:**
- Dedicated notebook
- Paddock app
- Spreadsheet
- Photo documentation

## Maintenance Routines

### Daily Tasks

- Check moisture levels
- Monitor for problems (rot, mould, pests)
- Vent humidity domes
- Note any changes

### Weekly Tasks

- Clean up dead/failed material
- Inspect for pests
- Check equipment function
- Update records

### Monthly Tasks

- Clean trays and domes
- Sanitise tools
- Check rooting hormone freshness
- Restock supplies

### Seasonal Tasks

- Deep clean all equipment
- Assess station layout
- Plan propagation schedule
- Update lighting for season

---

## Scaling Up

### Signs You Need More Space

- Constantly running out of tray space
- Multiple batches competing for resources
- Hardening off cuttings taking up propagation space
- Can't keep up with demand

### Expansion Options

1. **Add shelving** - vertical space is efficient
2. **Second heat mat** - doubles heated capacity
3. **Outdoor shadehouse** - for hardy species
4. **Dedicated cold frame** - for hardwood cuttings

### Workflow Improvements

- Label templates for speed
- Pre-mixed rooting medium storage
- Tool station at work height
- Better water access

## Safety Considerations

### Electrical Safety

- Use RCD-protected outlets near water
- Keep electrical connections dry
- Check cords for damage regularly
- Don't overload circuits

### Ergonomics

- Work at comfortable height
- Good lighting for detailed work
- Keep frequently used items within reach
- Take breaks during long sessions

### Hygiene

- Clean tools between plants
- Dispose of diseased material properly
- Wash hands after handling plants/soil
- Keep work area tidy to spot problems
