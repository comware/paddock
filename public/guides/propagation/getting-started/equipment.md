# Essential Equipment

Everything you need to start propagating plants, from budget essentials to professional upgrades.

---

## Cutting Tools

Good cutting tools are your most important investment. Clean cuts heal faster and reduce disease risk.

### Secateurs (Pruning Shears)

**What to look for:**
- Bypass style (scissor action) for clean cuts
- Sharp, quality steel blades
- Comfortable grip for extended use
- Easy to disassemble for sharpening

**Recommended brands (Australia):**
- Felco (professional grade, Swiss-made)
- Bahco (excellent mid-range)
- Fiskars (budget-friendly, widely available)

**Price range:** $25-150

### Grafting Knife

**For grafting work:**
- Single-bevel blade for precise cuts
- Folding style is safest
- Keep razor-sharp
- Dedicated use only (don't use for other tasks)

**Price range:** $20-80

### Scissors or Snips

**For delicate work:**
- Small, sharp scissors for soft cuttings
- Bonsai scissors work well
- Useful for leaf cuttings and cleanup

## Containers

### Propagation Trays

**Standard size:** 280mm x 540mm (fits most heat mats)

**Features to look for:**
- Drainage holes in cell trays
- Solid tray underneath to catch water
- Clear dome for humidity
- Vents in dome for airflow control

**Types:**

| Type | Best For | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| **Cell trays (40-72 cells)** | Multiple small cuttings | Easy to transplant |
| **Deep pots (10cm)** | Larger cuttings, root cuttings | More root space |
| **Shallow trays** | Leaf cuttings, small cuttings | Good for succulents |
| **Tubes (50mm)** | Native plants, tree seedlings | Deep root development |

### DIY Options

**Budget alternatives:**
- Clear plastic takeaway containers (with drainage holes added)
- Large plastic bottles cut in half
- Yoghurt containers for individual cuttings
- Zip-lock bags for humidity

## Rooting Media

### Primary Options

**Perlite**
- Volcanic glass, excellent drainage
- Sterile and reusable
- Mix 50/50 with coco coir
- $10-15 per 10L bag

**Coco Coir**
- Sustainable coconut husk fibre
- Good moisture retention
- Usually sold as compressed blocks
- Rehydrate before use
- $8-15 per block (makes ~60L)

**Vermiculite**
- Expanded mica, holds moisture well
- Good for moisture-loving plants
- Often mixed with other media
- $12-18 per 10L bag

**Propagation Sand**
- Coarse, washed sand
- Excellent drainage
- Best for succulents and Australian natives
- Available at landscape suppliers

### Recommended Starter Mix

For most cuttings, mix:
- 50% perlite
- 50% coco coir

This provides good drainage, moisture retention, and is sterile.

## Temperature Control

### Heat Mats

**Essential for winter propagation and tropical plants.**

**What to look for:**
- Waterproof construction
- Size matches your trays
- Thermostat control (some sold separately)
- UL/CE certified for safety

**Recommended products:**
- Rogue Seedling Heat Mat (Australian)
- VIVOSUN Heat Mat (international)

**Price range:** $30-80 for mat, $30-50 for thermostat

### Heated Propagators

**All-in-one units with built-in heating.**

**Features:**
- Base with heating element
- Thermostat control
- Clear dome with vents
- Various sizes available

**Price range:** $60-200

### DIY Heating Options

- Top of refrigerator (gentle, consistent warmth)
- Near hot water cylinder cupboard
- Sunny windowsill (monitor for overheating)
- Above hydronic heating pipes

## Humidity Control

### Propagation Domes

**Clear plastic covers that maintain humidity.**

**Features to look for:**
- Adjustable vents
- Tall enough for cuttings (15-20cm minimum)
- Fits standard trays
- Clear for light transmission

### Misting Equipment

**For maintaining humidity:**

| Method | Effort | Best For |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Spray bottle** | High (manual) | Small batches, beginners |
| **Hand mister** | Medium | Regular misting |
| **Automated misting** | Low | Serious propagators |

**Spray bottle tips:**
- Use fine mist setting
- Spray in morning to allow drying
- Room temperature water
- Clean regularly to prevent bacterial growth

## Rooting Hormones

### Types Available

**Powder (most common):**
- Clonex Rooting Hormone Powder
- Yates Clonex Purple (IBA 3g/kg - softwood)
- Yates Clonex Red (IBA 8g/kg - semi-hardwood/hardwood)

**Gel:**
- Clonex Rooting Gel
- Longer contact time with cutting
- Easy to see coverage

**Price range:** $15-30

### Strength Guide

| Cutting Type | Hormone Strength |
|--------------|------------------|
| Softwood, easy-rooting | Lowest strength or none |
| Semi-hardwood | Medium strength |
| Hardwood, difficult species | Highest strength |

### Storage

- Keep in cool, dark place
- Seal tightly after use
- Don't return unused hormone to container
- Replace annually for best potency

## Labels and Recording

### Plant Labels

**Essential for tracking:**
- Plastic labels (reusable with pencil)
- Wooden stakes
- Aluminium tags (long-lasting)

**What to record:**
- Species/variety name
- Date cutting taken
- Hormone used (if any)
- Parent plant location

### Recording System

**Track your results:**
- Notebook dedicated to propagation
- Spreadsheet (digital backup)
- App like Paddock for tracking batches

**Record:**
- Date started
- Number of cuttings
- Success rate
- Any issues encountered
- What worked well

## Tool Maintenance

### Cleaning

**After each use:**
1. Wipe blades with clean cloth
2. Remove sap with rubbing alcohol
3. Oil lightly to prevent rust

**Between plants (disease prevention):**
- Dip or wipe with 70% isopropyl alcohol
- Or use 10% bleach solution (rinse after)
- Essential when working with diseased material

### Sharpening

**Secateurs:**
- Sharpen the bevelled edge only
- Use diamond file or whetstone
- Maintain original angle
- Sharpen before they get dull

**Frequency:** Every few months of regular use

### Storage

- Keep tools dry
- Hang or store in dry location
- Oil metal parts seasonally
- Replace worn springs and blades as needed
