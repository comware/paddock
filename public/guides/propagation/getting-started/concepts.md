# Core Concepts

Understanding these fundamental terms will help you succeed with plant propagation.

---

## Vegetative Propagation

**What it is:** Creating new plants from parts of existing plants (stems, leaves, roots) rather than from seeds.

**Why it matters:**
- Produces exact genetic copies (clones) of the parent plant
- Preserves desirable traits like flavour, disease resistance, or growth habit
- Often faster than growing from seed
- Only way to reproduce some hybrids that don't come true from seed

**Main methods:**
- **Cuttings:** Using pieces of stem, leaf, or root
- **Division:** Separating a clump into multiple plants
- **Layering:** Rooting stems while still attached to parent
- **Grafting:** Joining parts of two plants together

---

## Rooting Hormone

**What it is:** A synthetic or natural compound that stimulates root growth on cuttings.

**Why it matters:**
- Dramatically improves success rates on many species
- Speeds up root development
- Helps difficult-to-root plants establish
- Not essential for easy-rooting species

**Types available:**

| Form | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|------|------|------|----------|
| **Powder** | Long shelf life, easy to use | Can clump if damp | Most cuttings |
| **Gel** | Sticks well, easy to see coverage | Shorter shelf life | Softwood cuttings |
| **Liquid** | Precise concentration control | Requires mixing | Commercial growers |

**How to use:**
1. Dip cutting end in hormone (2-3cm depth)
2. Tap off excess powder/gel
3. Insert into rooting medium immediately
4. Don't double-dip to avoid contamination

**Natural alternatives:**
- Willow water (contains salicylic acid)
- Honey (antibacterial properties)
- Cinnamon powder (antifungal)

## Node

**What it is:** The point on a stem where leaves attach and where growth buds are located.

**Why it matters:**
- This is where roots will emerge from cuttings
- Cuttings must include at least one node (usually 2-3)
- Buds at nodes produce new growth
- Internodes (sections between nodes) rarely root well

**Identifying nodes:**
- Look for leaf scars or current leaf attachment points
- Often slightly swollen compared to internode
- May have visible dormant buds
- On some plants, marked by a ring or colour change

**Optimal cutting length:**
- Include 2-4 nodes per cutting
- Make bottom cut just below a node
- Make top cut just above a node
- This maximises rooting potential and new growth

---

## Callus

**What it is:** A mass of undifferentiated cells that forms over a wound before roots develop.

**Why it matters:**
- First sign that a cutting is responding
- Roots typically emerge from callus tissue
- Some species need to callus before planting (especially succulents)
- Lack of callus may indicate the cutting won't root

**Callus formation timeline:**

| Cutting Type | Callus Appears | Notes |
|--------------|----------------|-------|
| Softwood | 5-10 days | May go straight to roots |
| Semi-hardwood | 1-3 weeks | Visible white tissue |
| Hardwood | 3-6 weeks | Slower but normal |
| Succulents | 3-7 days | Let callus form before planting |

**What to look for:**
- White or cream-coloured tissue at cut end
- Slightly swollen area around base
- Tissue looks alive, not brown or black

## Bottom Heat

**What it is:** Warming the rooting medium from below, typically to 20-24°C (68-75°F).

**Why it matters:**
- Root development is faster in warm soil
- Most species root significantly better with bottom heat
- Keeps roots warm while tops stay cooler
- Can extend the propagation season into cooler months

**How to provide:**

**Option 1: Heated propagator**
- Purpose-built units with thermostat
- Most reliable temperature control
- Ideal for serious propagation

**Option 2: Heat mat**
- Affordable seedling mats
- Place under trays
- Use with thermostat for best results

**Option 3: DIY methods**
- Top of refrigerator (gentle warmth)
- Near hot water cylinder
- Above hydronic heating pipes

**Temperature guide:**
- Cool-climate plants: 18-21°C
- Warm-climate plants: 21-24°C
- Tropical plants: 24-27°C

## Humidity Dome

**What it is:** A clear cover that maintains high humidity around cuttings while they develop roots.

**Why it matters:**
- Cuttings have no roots to absorb water
- High humidity reduces water loss through leaves
- Prevents wilting during critical rooting period
- Creates a mini greenhouse environment

**Options:**

| Method | Humidity Level | Best For |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| **Clear plastic dome** | 85-95% | Most cuttings |
| **Plastic bag** | 80-90% | Single pots, small batches |
| **Glass cloche** | 80-90% | Individual plants |
| **Propagator with vents** | 70-90% | Adjustable humidity |

**Important considerations:**
- Vent daily to prevent fungal issues
- Remove condensation to allow air circulation
- Gradually reduce humidity as roots form
- Remove dome once roots are established (usually 2-4 weeks after rooting)

---

## Rooting Medium

**What it is:** The material cuttings are planted in to develop roots.

**Why it matters:**
- Must hold moisture while allowing drainage
- Provides support for cuttings
- Should be sterile to prevent disease
- Different from potting mix - lower nutrients

**Common options:**

| Medium | Drainage | Moisture | Best For |
|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Perlite/pumice** | Excellent | Moderate | Most cuttings |
| **Vermiculite** | Good | High | Moisture-loving plants |
| **Coco coir** | Good | High | General purpose |
| **Perlite + coco (50/50)** | Excellent | Moderate-high | Recommended blend |
| **Propagation sand** | Excellent | Low | Succulents, Australian natives |
| **Water** | N/A | 100% | Easy-rooting plants only |

**Don't use:**
- Garden soil (disease risk, poor drainage)
- Rich potting mix (can rot cuttings)
- Compost (too nutrient-rich, may harbour pathogens)

## Hardening Off

**What it is:** Gradually acclimatising rooted cuttings to normal growing conditions.

**Why it matters:**
- New roots are delicate and easily damaged
- Sudden change from humid to dry air causes stress
- Sun can scorch leaves grown under cover
- Wind and temperature changes need introduction

**The process:**
1. **Week 1:** Remove humidity dome for increasing periods daily
2. **Week 2:** Move to brighter light (still indirect)
3. **Week 3:** Introduce some direct morning sun
4. **Week 4:** Full outdoor conditions (if appropriate)

**Signs of successful hardening:**
- New leaf growth
- Sturdy stems
- Plant maintains turgor (not wilting)
- Healthy root system visible through drainage holes

---

## Mother Plant

**What it is:** A plant maintained specifically as a source of propagation material.

**Why it matters:**
- Ensures consistent, healthy cutting material
- Can be managed for maximum cutting production
- Known genetics and health status
- Allows systematic propagation

**Selecting mother plants:**
- Choose healthy, vigorous specimens
- Look for desirable traits to reproduce
- Avoid plants with any disease symptoms
- Young-to-middle-aged plants are ideal

**Mother plant care:**
- Regular feeding for strong growth
- Prune to encourage branching
- Keep pest and disease free
- Replace every 3-5 years as vigour declines

---

## Strike Rate

**What it is:** The percentage of cuttings that successfully root.

**How to calculate:**
```
Strike Rate = (Rooted Cuttings / Total Cuttings) x 100
```

**Example:** 15 rooted from 20 cuttings = 75% strike rate

**Typical rates by difficulty:**

| Difficulty | Expected Rate |
|------------|---------------|
| Easy (pothos, mint) | 90-100% |
| Moderate (rosemary, lavender) | 70-90% |
| Challenging (camellias, magnolias) | 50-70% |
| Difficult (some natives, grafted varieties) | 30-50% |

**Improving strike rates:**
- Use healthy, vigorous parent material
- Take cuttings at the right time of year
- Provide optimal temperature and humidity
- Use rooting hormone when recommended
- Keep medium consistently moist but not waterlogged
