## Rot & Fungal Issues

### Stem Rot (Basal Rot)

**Symptoms:**
- Stem turns black or brown at base
- Soft, mushy tissue
- May have foul smell
- Cutting collapses

**Causes:**
- Medium too wet
- Poor drainage
- Damaged tissue when cutting
- Unclean cutting tools
- Too deep planting

**Solutions:**
1. Remove affected cuttings immediately
2. Check drainage - holes clear?
3. Reduce watering
4. Take fresh cuttings with clean tools

**Prevention:**
- Use well-draining medium
- Don't overwater
- Make clean cuts with sterile tools
- Allow succulents to callus before planting
- Don't bury cuttings too deeply

---

### Fungal Growth on Medium

**Symptoms:**
- White, grey, or green fuzzy growth
- Musty smell
- May spread across surface
- Usually starts in corners or edges

**Causes:**
- Too much humidity
- Poor air circulation
- Old or contaminated medium
- Dead plant material left in tray

**Solutions:**
1. Increase ventilation immediately
2. Remove visible mould carefully
3. Sprinkle cinnamon on surface (antifungal)
4. Consider hydrogen peroxide spray (3%, diluted)
5. If severe, restart with fresh medium

**Prevention:**
- Vent domes daily
- Remove dead material promptly
- Use sterile medium
- Adequate spacing between cuttings
- Don't leave pooled water

---

### Damping Off

**Symptoms:**
- Seedlings/cuttings suddenly collapse
- Thin, water-soaked stem at soil line
- Affects multiple plants in succession
- Rapid progression

**Causes:**
- Fungal pathogens (Pythium, Rhizoctonia)
- Overwatering
- Poor drainage
- Contaminated medium or tools

**Solutions:**
1. Remove affected plants and surrounding medium
2. Improve airflow dramatically
3. Reduce watering
4. Apply fungicide if available
5. May need to restart batch

**Prevention:**
- Use sterile medium
- Clean containers
- Avoid overwatering
- Good air circulation
- Bottom watering preferred

## Wilting Problems

### Temporary Wilting (First 24-48 Hours)

**Symptoms:**
- Leaves droop after taking cuttings
- Usually recovers within 2 days
- Cutting still looks alive

**Cause:** Normal stress response - no roots to absorb water yet.

**Action:** Increase humidity, mist regularly, be patient.

---

### Persistent Wilting

**Symptoms:**
- Wilting doesn't improve after 2-3 days
- Progressive decline
- Leaves may crisp at edges

**Causes:**
- Humidity too low
- Temperature too high
- Root damage (if potted cutting)
- Cutting dried out before planting

**Solutions:**
1. Check humidity dome is sealed properly
2. Move to cooler location
3. Mist more frequently
4. Ensure cutting wasn't delayed between taking and planting

**Prevention:**
- Process cuttings quickly
- Maintain high humidity
- Avoid hot locations
- Keep cuttings in damp paper towel while working

---

### Wilting in Established Cuttings

**Symptoms:**
- Previously healthy cutting starts wilting
- May coincide with roots appearing
- Often happens during hardening off

**Causes:**
- Root damage during handling
- Too rapid transition from humidity
- Watering issue (too much or too little)

**Solutions:**
1. Return to high humidity temporarily
2. Check root health (gently!)
3. Adjust watering
4. Slow down hardening off process

## Leaf Problems

### Yellowing Lower Leaves

**Symptoms:**
- Bottom leaves turn yellow first
- Upper leaves still green
- Gradual progression upward

**Causes:**
- Natural resource redistribution (normal in some cases)
- Overwatering
- Nutrient deficiency (less common in propagation)

**Action:**
- If only 1-2 leaves and plant otherwise healthy, remove and monitor
- If progressive, check watering regime
- Reduce watering if medium is staying wet

---

### Brown Leaf Edges

**Symptoms:**
- Leaf margins turn brown/crispy
- Often starts at tips
- Leaves may curl

**Causes:**
- Low humidity
- Fertiliser burn (shouldn't fertilise cuttings)
- Accumulated salts in medium
- Chemical exposure

**Solutions:**
1. Increase humidity
2. Don't fertilise cuttings until established
3. Use clean water (avoid softened water)

---

### Leaf Drop

**Symptoms:**
- Leaves fall off cutting
- May be lower leaves only or all leaves

**Causes:**
- Stress response (sometimes normal)
- Overwatering
- Disease
- Environmental shock

**When normal:** Some species drop leaves during rooting and regrow them.

**When concerning:** Rapid leaf drop with stem discolouration = likely rot.

## Slow or No Rooting

### No Roots After 4+ Weeks

**Possible causes:**

**1. Temperature too low**
- Solution: Add heat mat, move to warmer location
- Most species need 20-24°C soil temperature

**2. Wrong time of year**
- Solution: Wait for appropriate season
- Each species has optimal timing

**3. Wrong cutting type**
- Hardwood cuttings need different treatment than softwood
- Check species-specific guide

**4. Cutting from poor material**
- Weak or stressed parent plants produce poor cuttings
- Old wood may root slowly

**5. Species is naturally slow**
- Some plants take 2-3 months
- Research typical timeframe for your species

**Diagnosis steps:**
1. Is the cutting still alive? (Green, turgid)
2. Check temperature with thermometer
3. Verify cutting type matches season
4. Research species-typical rooting time

---

### Callus But No Roots

**Symptoms:**
- White tissue forming at base (callus)
- No actual roots emerging
- Cutting otherwise healthy

**Causes:**
- Waiting for conditions to be right
- May need more time
- Sometimes callus forms but roots don't follow

**Action:**
- Be patient - roots often follow callus
- Ensure temperature is adequate
- Try rooting hormone if not already used
- If no progress after 2+ more weeks, may not be viable

## Disease Issues

### Spreading Problems

**Symptoms:**
- Healthy cuttings next to sick ones start dying
- Problem moves through tray systematically
- Similar symptoms appearing in sequence

**Cause:** Infectious disease spreading through contact or water splash.

**Action:**
1. Remove all affected cuttings immediately
2. Isolate healthy cuttings if possible
3. Clean and sterilise trays
4. Consider starting fresh batch

---

### Bacterial Soft Rot

**Symptoms:**
- Rapid mushiness
- Strong unpleasant odour
- Spreads quickly
- Brown/black watery tissue

**Cause:** Bacterial infection, often enters through wounds.

**Action:**
- Discard affected cuttings
- Clean tools with 10% bleach solution
- Start fresh if widespread

---

### Virus Symptoms

**Symptoms:**
- Unusual patterns on leaves (mottling, streaking)
- Distorted growth
- Stunted development
- Pattern doesn't match nutrient deficiency

**Cause:** Viral infection (inherited from parent plant).

**Action:**
- Discard affected cuttings
- Don't propagate from plants showing these symptoms
- Viruses cannot be cured

---

## Environmental Problems

### Heat Stress

**Symptoms:**
- Wilting during hottest part of day
- Scorched leaves
- Cooking smell (severe cases)
- Cuttings under dome especially affected

**Causes:**
- Direct sun on dome (greenhouse effect)
- Heat mat too hot (no thermostat)
- Hot weather without ventilation

**Solutions:**
1. Move out of direct sun immediately
2. Vent domes in hot weather
3. Use thermostat on heat mat
4. Consider shade cloth in summer

---

### Cold Damage

**Symptoms:**
- Black or water-soaked tissue
- Wilting that doesn't recover
- Occurs after cold night

**Causes:**
- Exposure to frost or near-freezing temps
- Cold drafts from windows/doors
- Tropical species in cold environment

**Solutions:**
1. Move to warmer location
2. Add heat mat
3. Protect from drafts
4. Tropical species need consistent warmth

---

### Light Problems

**Too much light:**
- Bleached or scorched leaves
- Wilting in afternoon
- Brown crispy patches

**Too little light:**
- Pale, stretched growth
- Slow rooting
- Weak stems

**Solution:** Bright indirect light for most cuttings. Adjust position as needed.

## Pest Issues

### Fungus Gnats

**Symptoms:**
- Small black flies hovering around plants
- Tiny larvae in medium surface
- May damage roots

**Control:**
- Allow surface to dry slightly between watering
- Yellow sticky traps catch adults
- Beneficial nematodes for larvae
- Sand layer on surface deters egg-laying

### Aphids

**Symptoms:**
- Clusters on new growth
- Sticky residue
- Distorted leaves

**Control:**
- Spray with water to dislodge
- Insecticidal soap
- Remove badly affected material

### Spider Mites

**Symptoms:**
- Fine webbing on leaves
- Stippled, bronzed leaves
- Tiny moving dots (mites)

**Control:**
- Increase humidity (mites hate humidity)
- Spray with water
- Insecticidal soap
- Remove badly affected cuttings

## When to Give Up

Sometimes cuttings fail. Know when to move on:

### Definitely Failed

- Completely black/brown throughout
- Dried to crisp
- Pulled out of medium = no resistance and brown base
- Strong rotting smell

### Worth Waiting

- Green and turgid but no roots yet
- Lost leaves but stem is alive
- Slow but stable

### Making the Call

If after 2x the normal rooting time there's no progress and the cutting looks stressed, it's unlikely to succeed. Take new cuttings and try again, adjusting conditions based on what you learned.

## Prevention Checklist

Before starting each batch, verify:

- [ ] Tools cleaned and sharp
- [ ] Medium fresh and sterile
- [ ] Containers clean
- [ ] Parent plant healthy
- [ ] Correct season for this species
- [ ] Temperature adequate
- [ ] Humidity setup ready
- [ ] Labels prepared

## Quick Fixes Summary

| Problem | Quick Fix |
|---------|-----------|
| Rotting | Reduce water, improve drainage |
| Wilting | Increase humidity, reduce heat |
| Mould | Increase ventilation |
| No roots | Add heat, be patient |
| Yellowing | Check watering regime |
| Scorching | Move out of direct sun |
| Pests | Isolate, treat appropriately |

When in doubt, check the basics: **temperature, humidity, moisture level, and light**. Most problems trace back to one of these four factors.
