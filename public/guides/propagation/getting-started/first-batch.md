# Your First Propagation Batch

A step-by-step guide to your first successful propagation using softwood cuttings - the easiest and most forgiving method for beginners.

---

## Choosing Your First Plant

### Best Beginner Plants

Start with one of these nearly foolproof options:

| Plant | Success Rate | Time to Root | Why It's Great for Beginners |
|-------|--------------|--------------|------------------------------|
| **Pothos** | 95%+ | 2-3 weeks | Roots in water or soil, almost impossible to fail |
| **Mint** | 90%+ | 1-2 weeks | Vigorous, roots from any stem piece |
| **Basil** | 90%+ | 2-3 weeks | Fast growing, clear visual feedback |
| **Coleus** | 90%+ | 2-3 weeks | Easy, dramatic foliage |
| **Tradescantia** | 95%+ | 1-2 weeks | Roots at every node, very forgiving |
| **Geranium** | 85%+ | 3-4 weeks | Reliable, good teaching plant |

### Avoid for First Attempt

- Lavender (needs perfect drainage, slow)
- Australian natives (specific requirements)
- Anything labelled "difficult to propagate"
- Plants with milky sap (can irritate skin)

## What You'll Need

### Essential Items

- [ ] Healthy parent plant
- [ ] Clean, sharp scissors or secateurs
- [ ] Small pots or cell tray
- [ ] Rooting medium (perlite + coco coir mix)
- [ ] Clear plastic bag or humidity dome
- [ ] Spray bottle with water
- [ ] Labels and marker

### Optional but Helpful

- [ ] Rooting hormone powder
- [ ] Heat mat
- [ ] Rubbing alcohol for tool cleaning

## Step-by-Step Process

### Day Before: Preparation

**1. Water your parent plant**
- Cuttings from well-hydrated plants root better
- Morning water is ideal
- Don't waterlog - normal watering

**2. Prepare your workspace**
- Clean surface
- Gather all materials
- Pre-moisten rooting medium

**3. Clean your tools**
- Wipe secateurs with rubbing alcohol
- Sharpen if needed

---

### Day 1: Taking Cuttings

**Step 1: Select cutting material**

Look for:
- Healthy, pest-free growth
- Current season's growth (green, flexible)
- Stems with 3-4 leaf nodes
- Non-flowering stems preferred

**Step 2: Make the cut**

1. Choose a stem 8-12cm long
2. Cut just below a node (where leaf attaches)
3. Make a clean, angled cut (45°)
4. Take 5-6 cuttings (expect some to fail)

**Step 3: Prepare cuttings**

1. Remove lower leaves (keep top 2-4 leaves)
2. If leaves are large, cut them in half (reduces water loss)
3. Keep cuttings in damp paper towel while working

**Step 4: Apply rooting hormone (optional)**

1. Pour small amount of hormone onto paper
2. Dip cut end 2-3cm deep
3. Tap off excess
4. Don't return used hormone to container

**Step 5: Plant cuttings**

1. Make hole in moist rooting medium with pencil
2. Insert cutting 3-5cm deep
3. Gently firm medium around stem
4. Space cuttings so leaves don't touch

**Step 6: Create humidity**

1. Water gently to settle medium
2. Place clear bag or dome over cuttings
3. Ensure some airflow (dome vents open slightly)
4. Label with plant name and date

**Step 7: Position**

1. Place in bright, indirect light
2. No direct sunlight (will overheat)
3. Warm spot (20-24°C ideal)
4. Out of drafts

---

### Week 1: Establishment

**Daily tasks:**
- Check moisture - mist if medium is drying
- Remove any condensation from inside dome
- Watch for wilting (normal for first 24-48 hours)
- Look for any rotting leaves - remove immediately

**What to expect:**
- Some wilting in first 1-2 days is normal
- Leaves should perk up by day 3-4
- No visible root growth yet (happening underground)
- Cuttings should look healthy, not deteriorating

**Warning signs:**
- Blackening stems = remove immediately (rot)
- Mould on medium = increase ventilation
- Severe wilting after 3 days = check humidity/heat

---

### Week 2: Root Development

**Care routine:**
- Continue misting as needed
- Vent dome more frequently (2-3 times daily)
- Watch for new growth (good sign!)

**How to check for roots (gently!):**
- Look for roots through drainage holes
- Very light tug - resistance means roots forming
- Don't pull hard or disturb unnecessarily

**What to expect:**
- First tiny roots visible in easy species
- Leaves should remain turgid
- Possibly some new leaf growth

---

### Week 3-4: Rooting Confirmation

**Signs of success:**
- Resistance when gently tugged
- New leaf growth appearing
- Plant looks healthy and stable
- Roots visible through pot base

**If roots are established:**
1. Start removing humidity dome for longer periods
2. Begin hardening off process
3. Water when medium surface dries

**If no roots yet:**
- Be patient - some species take longer
- Check cutting is still alive (green, turgid)
- Maintain humidity and wait another week

---

## After Rooting: Hardening Off

### Week 1 Post-Rooting

**Gradual transition:**
- Day 1-2: Remove dome for 1 hour
- Day 3-4: Remove dome for 2-3 hours
- Day 5-7: Remove dome for half the day

**Monitor closely for:**
- Wilting when dome is off
- If wilting occurs, replace dome and slow down

### Week 2 Post-Rooting

**Increase exposure:**
- Dome off all day, only overnight if needed
- Introduce to brighter light gradually
- Begin normal watering routine

### Potting Up

**When ready:**
- Roots filling current container
- Strong new growth
- Plant stable without humidity support

**How to pot up:**
1. Use pot one size larger
2. Use regular potting mix (not propagation mix)
3. Handle roots gently
4. Water thoroughly after potting
5. Keep in shade for a few days

## Common First-Time Mistakes

### Over-Watering

**Symptom:** Stems go black and mushy at base
**Fix:** Medium should be moist, not wet. If you squeeze it, no water should drip.

### Too Much Direct Sun

**Symptom:** Leaves scorch, cuttings wilt severely
**Fix:** Bright indirect light only. No afternoon sun through windows.

### Checking Too Often

**Symptom:** Disturbed cuttings, slow rooting
**Fix:** Once settled, don't disturb. Check with eyes, not hands.

### Giving Up Too Early

**Symptom:** Discarding cuttings that haven't rooted yet
**Fix:** If cuttings are still green and turgid, they're still trying. Wait.

### Skipping Humidity

**Symptom:** Cuttings wilt and dry out
**Fix:** Humidity dome is essential for most cuttings. Use it.

## Next Steps After Success

### Expand Your Skills

1. **Try more of the same plant** - build confidence
2. **Try another easy species** - different plant, same technique
3. **Try semi-hardwood cuttings** - same process, woodier material
4. **Attempt something slightly harder** - rosemary, lavender

### Build Your Collection

With your new skills, you can:
- Multiply plants you love
- Share with friends and family
- Fill garden gaps inexpensively
- Preserve favourite varieties

### Learn More Methods

Once confident with softwood cuttings:
- Division (even easier, instant results)
- Simple layering (hands-off approach)
- Hardwood cuttings (winter project)

## Troubleshooting Your First Batch

| Issue | Likely Cause | Solution |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| All cuttings wilted in 24h | Too hot, not enough humidity | Move to cooler spot, increase humidity |
| Stems rotting at base | Too wet, no drainage | Use drier medium, ensure drainage |
| No roots after 4 weeks | Too cold, wrong plant type | Add heat mat, check species requirements |
| Mould on medium | Poor airflow | Increase ventilation, reduce misting |
| Leaves dropping | Normal for some species | Remove fallen leaves, watch for new growth |
