# Aloe Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Offsets (pups) |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks (pups already rooted) |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 95-100% (pups) |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Aloes propagate readily from offsets (pups) that form around the base of mature plants. This natural reproductive method makes aloe propagation simple - the plants do most of the work themselves.

Unlike many succulents, aloes don't propagate well from leaf cuttings. Their thick, moisture-filled leaves tend to rot rather than root. Stick with pups for reliable results.

The medicinal Aloe vera and ornamental aloes (A. arborescens, A. plicatilis, etc.) all propagate well from pups. Once a plant is mature, it typically produces several pups annually.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Sharing medicinal Aloe vera
- Building aloe collections
- Container gardening
- Landscaping in frost-free areas
- Gifts for new gardeners

## Propagation Methods

### Offsets/Pups (Recommended)

**Overview:** Nearly foolproof method using naturally-produced babies.

**When to separate:**
- Best months: September to January (spring to midsummer)
- When pups are 10-15cm tall

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Identify suitable pups**
   - Growing around mother plant's base
   - At least 10cm tall
   - Developing own root system
   - Several leaves visible

2. **Prepare for separation**
   - Water mother plant day before
   - Have potting mix ready
   - Clean, sharp knife if needed

3. **Remove pups**
   - Gently dig around pup
   - Find where it connects to mother
   - Pull apart or cut connecting root
   - Preserve as many pup roots as possible

4. **Let callus** (important)
   - Set pup aside in shade
   - Allow cut surface to dry 1-3 days
   - Prevents rot when planted

5. **Plant pup**
   - Well-draining succulent mix
   - Plant at same depth it was growing
   - Don't bury too deep
   - Water lightly

6. **Establish**
   - Bright indirect light initially
   - Water sparingly first 2 weeks
   - Increase light and water gradually
   - Treat as mature plant once growing

### Seed (Slow Alternative)

**When to use:** For rare species or when pups unavailable.

**Process:**
1. Obtain fresh seed
2. Surface sow on sandy mix
3. Keep warm and lightly moist
4. Germination 2-4 weeks
5. Very slow growth
6. Years to mature size

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Pup Rots After Planting

**Symptoms:** Base turns soft, plant collapses.

**Causes:** Insufficient callusing, overwatering, no drainage.

**Solution:** Usually too late. Let next pup callus longer.

**Prevention:** 1-3 days callusing, minimal water initially, well-drained mix.

### Problem 2: Pup Doesn't Establish

**Symptoms:** No new growth, leaves slowly shrivel.

**Causes:** Too small when separated, root damage, too much sun.

**Solution:** Reduce light, maintain minimal moisture.

**Prevention:** Wait for larger pups, handle roots carefully, shade initially.

### Problem 3: Mother Plant Not Producing Pups

**Symptoms:** Mature plant but no offsets appearing.

**Causes:** Too young, wrong growing conditions, pot-bound.

**Solution:** Improve conditions, repot, or wait.

**Prevention:** Good light, appropriate watering, occasional repotting.

## Pro Tips

1. **Wait for size** - Larger pups (10-15cm) establish better than tiny ones. Patience pays.

2. **Leave some roots** - Try to keep pup roots intact when separating. They help establishment even if damaged.

3. **Callusing prevents rot** - The 1-3 day drying period is important. Fresh-cut aloes often rot.

4. **Leaves don't work** - Unlike echeverias, aloe leaves usually rot instead of rooting. Stick with pups.

5. **One mother, many babies** - A healthy Aloe vera can produce dozens of pups over its lifetime.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy aloes produce more pups:

- **Position:** Bright light, some direct sun.
- **Watering:** Allow to dry between waterings.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding during growing season.
- **Repotting:** When crowded - promotes pup production.

### Selecting Pups

Choose pups that display:

- At least 10cm height
- Several developed leaves
- Beginning to form own roots
- Healthy color and form
