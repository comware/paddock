# Haworthia Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Offsets (pups) |
| **Alternative Methods** | Leaf cuttings, seed |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 90-95% (pups), 60-80% (leaves) |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Haworthias are compact, shade-tolerant succulents that propagate readily from offsets. Most species produce pups freely, making propagation simple and reliable.

Unlike their aloe relatives, many haworthias can also be propagated from leaf cuttings, though this is slower. Offset division remains the easiest and most reliable method.

Their small size makes haworthias perfect for windowsills and indoor collections. Propagation allows you to fill containers, share with friends, and maintain backup plants.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Building windowsill collections
- Creating succulent arrangements
- Sharing with other collectors
- Replacing damaged plants
- Container gardening

## Propagation Methods

### Offsets/Pups (Recommended)

**Overview:** Simple method using naturally-produced babies.

**When to separate:**
- Best months: September to January (spring to midsummer)
- When pups have developed their own leaves

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Identify ready pups**
   - Growing around mother plant
   - Multiple leaves developed
   - Often have own roots forming

2. **Remove from pot**
   - Gently unpot the cluster
   - Shake off loose soil
   - Identify where pups attach

3. **Separate pups**
   - Gently pull or twist apart
   - Cut if firmly attached
   - Preserve roots where possible

4. **Let callus**
   - Set aside in shade
   - 1-3 days for cut to dry
   - Prevents rot

5. **Plant pups**
   - Well-draining succulent mix
   - Small pots suit small plants
   - Plant at original depth

6. **Establish**
   - Bright indirect light
   - Light watering
   - Treat as mature plant once settled

### Leaf Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** When pups unavailable.

**Process:**
1. Remove outer leaf with gentle twist
2. Ensure clean break at base
3. Let callus 3-5 days
4. Lay on or insert in soil
5. Mist occasionally
6. Plantlet forms in 4-8 weeks
7. Slower than pups but works

### Seed (For Specialists)

**When to use:** For rare species or hybrids.

**Process:**
1. Fresh seed needed
2. Surface sow on fine mix
3. Keep moist and warm
4. Germination 1-4 weeks
5. Extremely slow growth
6. Years to mature size

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Moderate-warm (18-26°C) |
| **Humidity** | Low to moderate |
| **Light** | Bright indirect (not direct sun) |
| **Medium** | Well-drained |
| **Water** | Light, infrequent |

### Environment Setup

**For pups:**
Small pots with drainage. Bright but not sunny position. Standard succulent conditions.

**Critical factor:**
Haworthias prefer bright shade to direct sun. Don't place newly propagated plants in full sun.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Pup Rots After Separation

**Symptoms:** Base turns mushy.

**Causes:** No callusing, overwatering, damaged tissue.

**Solution:** Start fresh. Let next pup callus properly.

**Prevention:** Allow cut to dry 1-3 days before planting.

### Problem 2: Leaf Cutting Fails

**Symptoms:** Leaf dries without producing plantlet.

**Causes:** Incomplete leaf base, too dry, wrong species.

**Solution:** Try again with different leaf.

**Prevention:** Complete leaf removal with base intact. Some species don't leaf-propagate well.

### Problem 3: Sunburn on New Plants

**Symptoms:** Brown or white patches on leaves.

**Causes:** Too much direct sun.

**Solution:** Move to shadier position. Damage won't heal but plant survives.

**Prevention:** Bright indirect light, not direct sun.

## Pro Tips

1. **Shade lovers** - Unlike many succulents, haworthias prefer bright shade. Direct sun causes sunburn.

2. **Wait for roots** - Pups with visible roots establish faster than rootless ones. Worth waiting.

3. **Leaf propagation works** - Many (not all) haworthias propagate from leaves. Worth trying if pups unavailable.

4. **Small pots suit them** - Haworthias have small root systems. Don't overpot.

5. **Window plant specials** - Their shade tolerance makes haworthias ideal windowsill plants.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy haworthias produce more pups:

- **Position:** Bright indirect light. East or south windows.
- **Watering:** Allow to dry between waterings.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding in growing season.
- **Repotting:** When crowded with pups.

### Selecting Pups

Choose pups that display:

- Multiple developed leaves
- Some root formation
- Healthy coloring
- Large enough to handle easily
