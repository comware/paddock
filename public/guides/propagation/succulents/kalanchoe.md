# Kalanchoe Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Stem cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Leaf cuttings, plantlets (some species) |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 85-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Kalanchoes are easy-to-propagate succulents with diverse forms, from the popular flowering K. blossfeldiana to the bizarre plantlet-producing "mother of thousands" (K. daigremontiana). Most propagate readily from stem or leaf cuttings.

Some species produce plantlets along leaf margins - tiny complete plants that drop and root on their own. These species practically propagate themselves.

The colorful flowers of K. blossfeldiana make it a popular gift plant, and propagation allows you to maintain plants beyond their initial flowering display.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Extending life of gift plants
- Multiplying flowering varieties
- Creating succulent collections
- Sharing with friends
- Container gardens

## Propagation Methods

### Stem Cuttings (Recommended for Most)

**Overview:** Reliable method for all kalanchoes.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: September to February (spring to summer)
- During active growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Healthy stem tips 8-12cm long
   - Non-flowering stems preferred
   - Firm, healthy growth

2. **Take cuttings**
   - Cut cleanly below a node
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep 3-4 pairs at top

3. **Callus cuttings**
   - Set aside in shade
   - Allow 2-4 days for cut to dry
   - Important for rot prevention

4. **Plant callused cuttings**
   - Well-draining succulent mix
   - Insert 2-3cm deep
   - Don't water immediately

5. **Root development**
   - Wait 1 week before first light watering
   - Bright indirect light
   - Keep barely moist
   - Roots in 2-4 weeks

6. **Establish**
   - New growth indicates rooting
   - Increase water gradually
   - Treat as mature plant

### Leaf Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** When stems unavailable.

**Process:**
1. Remove healthy leaf with clean break
2. Let callus 2-4 days
3. Lay on or insert in soil
4. Mist lightly
5. Plantlets form in 4-8 weeks

### Plantlets (Specific Species)

**When to use:** For species that produce margin plantlets.

**Process:**
1. Wait until plantlets have roots
2. Gently detach from leaf margin
3. Plant on soil surface
4. Water lightly
5. Nearly 100% success
6. These species practically propagate themselves!

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot

**Symptoms:** Base turns mushy.

**Causes:** Insufficient callusing, overwatering.

**Solution:** Start fresh with new cutting.

**Prevention:** Longer callusing time (3-5 days), less water.

### Problem 2: Flowering Cuttings Fail

**Symptoms:** Cuttings with flowers don't root well.

**Causes:** Energy going to flowers instead of roots.

**Solution:** Remove flower stalks, or use non-flowering material.

**Prevention:** Choose vegetative stems for cuttings.

### Problem 3: Mother of Thousands Taking Over

**Symptoms:** Plantlets everywhere!

**Causes:** These species are extremely prolific. Plantlets drop and root wherever they land.

**Solution:** Remove unwanted plantlets regularly.

**Prevention:** Grow in isolation if spread is unwanted.

## Pro Tips

1. **Remove flowers** - Cuttings with flower buds should have them removed. Flowers use energy needed for rooting.

2. **Callusing matters** - The 2-4 day drying period is important. Fresh-cut kalanchoes often rot.

3. **Plantlet species** - K. daigremontiana and K. pinnata produce plantlets that root on contact with soil. Propagation is automatic.

4. **Reflowering K. blossfeldiana** - Needs short days (long dark periods) to set flower buds. Propagated plants will rebloom with proper conditions.

5. **Leggy plants** - Stretched kalanchoes can be beheaded and re-rooted. The stump often produces new growth too.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy kalanchoes produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Bright light with some direct sun.
- **Watering:** Allow to dry between waterings.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding in growing season.
- **Pruning:** Encourages bushy growth and provides cuttings.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Firm, plump leaves
- Non-flowering shoots preferred
- Good color
- No signs of disease
