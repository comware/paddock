# Agave Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy (pups), Slow (seed) |
| **Best Method** | Offsets (pups) |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed, bulbils (some species) |
| **Time to Root** | 2-6 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 95-100% (pups) |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Agaves propagate readily from offsets (pups) that form around the base of mature plants. Like their aloe relatives, they don't propagate from leaf cuttings, but the pups they produce are easy to separate and establish.

The slow-growing nature of agaves means patience is required - both for pup production and for new plants to reach mature size. However, the process itself is straightforward.

Some agave species produce bulbils (small plantlets) on their flower stalks after blooming, providing another propagation opportunity. Since agaves are monocarpic (dying after flowering), these bulbils help the colony continue.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Building agave collections
- Architectural specimens
- Xeriscaping
- Container growing
- Succession planting (preparing for monocarpic death)

## Propagation Methods

### Offsets/Pups (Recommended)

**Overview:** Simple, reliable method using naturally-produced babies.

**When to separate:**
- Best months: September to January (spring to midsummer)
- When pups are 10-15cm or larger

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Identify suitable pups**
   - Growing around mother plant base
   - At least 10-15cm diameter
   - Some may have roots already

2. **Safety first**
   - Agave spines are sharp!
   - Wear thick gloves
   - Eye protection recommended

3. **Separate pups**
   - Dig around to expose connection
   - Cut stolon with sharp knife
   - Preserve pup roots if present
   - Handle carefully (spines!)

4. **Let callus**
   - Set aside in dry shade
   - Allow 1-2 weeks for large cut to dry
   - Larger cuts need longer drying
   - Essential for rot prevention

5. **Plant pup**
   - Well-draining gritty mix
   - Plant at same depth
   - Don't water immediately
   - Wait 1-2 weeks before first water

6. **Establish**
   - Minimal water until rooted
   - Protect from extreme sun initially
   - Treat as mature plant once growing

### Bulbils (Some Species)

**When to use:** When flower stalk produces plantlets.

**Process:**
1. Wait until bulbils have small roots
2. Remove from flower stalk
3. Let callus 1 week
4. Plant in gritty mix
5. Water sparingly
6. Smaller plants but nearly 100% success

### Seed (Very Slow)

**When to use:** For species that don't produce pups readily.

**Process:**
1. Collect fresh seed
2. Surface sow on gritty mix
3. Keep warm and lightly moist
4. Germination 2-4 weeks
5. Extremely slow growth
6. Years to decent size

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (22-30°C) |
| **Humidity** | Low |
| **Light** | Bright, some direct sun |
| **Medium** | Very well-drained, gritty |
| **Water** | Minimal |

### Environment Setup

**For pups:**
Dry, warm conditions. Very gritty, well-drained medium. No humidity. Full sun once established.

**Critical factor:**
Extended callusing period. Large agave cuts need 1-2 weeks to dry properly.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Pup Rots After Planting

**Symptoms:** Base turns soft and rotten.

**Causes:** Insufficient callusing, overwatering, wet medium.

**Solution:** Usually too late. Start fresh.

**Prevention:** Longer callusing (1-2 weeks for large cuts), minimal water, very gritty mix.

### Problem 2: Mother Plant Dies After Flowering

**Symptoms:** After spectacular flower stalk, plant dies.

**Causes:** Normal - agaves are monocarpic.

**Solution:** Not needed - it's natural.

**Prevention:** Can't prevent. Collect bulbils and pups as succession.

### Problem 3: Spine Injuries

**Symptoms:** Puncture wounds, scratches, eye injuries.

**Causes:** Agave spines are dangerous.

**Solution:** First aid as appropriate.

**Prevention:** Thick gloves, long sleeves, eye protection. Never put face near plant level.

## Pro Tips

1. **Safety gear essential** - Agave spines cause serious injuries. Thick gloves and eye protection are mandatory.

2. **Extended callusing** - Large agave cuts need longer drying time than small succulents. 1-2 weeks is not excessive.

3. **Monocarpic awareness** - Most agaves flower once after many years, then die. Collect pups as succession plants.

4. **Gritty medium** - Agaves need exceptionally well-drained medium. Add extra grit or pumice to standard succulent mix.

5. **Patience required** - Agaves grow slowly. A pup may take years to reach mature size.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy agaves produce pups over time:

- **Position:** Full sun, excellent drainage.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant; infrequent deep watering.
- **Feeding:** Minimal - native to poor soils.
- **Protection:** May need frost protection depending on species.

### Selecting Pups

Choose pups that display:

- At least 10-15cm size
- Developing own leaves
- Some root formation if visible
- Healthy coloring
