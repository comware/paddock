# Sempervivum (Hens and Chicks) Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Very Easy |
| **Best Method** | Offsets (chicks) |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed |
| **Time to Root** | 1-3 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 98-100% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Sempervivums are the ultimate self-propagating succulents. Known as "hens and chicks," they produce abundant offsets (chicks) connected to the mother plant (hen) by stolons. These offsets root on contact with soil, making propagation almost automatic.

The main task in sempervivum propagation is simply separating the naturally-rooted chicks and relocating them. It's nearly impossible to fail.

Sempervivums are hardy to severe frost, making them exceptional for outdoor rock gardens and green roofs in cold climates where other succulents can't survive.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Rock garden mass plantings
- Green roof applications
- Container arrangements
- Sharing with friends
- Cold-climate succulent gardens

## Propagation Methods

### Offsets/Chicks (The Only Method You Need)

**Overview:** Nature does the work. You just separate and relocate.

**When to separate:**
- Best months: September to April (spring to autumn)
- Works almost any time

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Identify ready chicks**
   - Growing around the hen on stolons
   - Often already rooting where they touch soil
   - Any size works, larger is faster

2. **Separate chicks**
   - Simply cut or break the stolon
   - Or gently pull apart
   - No precision needed
   - Roots or no roots - both work

3. **Plant immediately**
   - No callusing needed for sempervivums
   - Press into soil surface
   - Or plant in containers
   - Water lightly

4. **Done**
   - That's it
   - They root where they sit
   - Almost impossible to fail

### Seed (Rarely Needed)

**When to use:** For specific species or hybridizing.

**Process:**
1. Collect seed from mature flower stalks
2. Surface sow on gritty mix
3. Keep moist, cool
4. Germination 2-4 weeks
5. Very slow growth
6. Takes years to reach offset-producing size

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: This Plant Has No Problems

**Symptoms:** Everything works fine.

**Causes:** Sempervivums are incredibly easy.

**Solution:** Enjoy your success.

**Prevention:** None needed.

### Problem 2: Mother Plant Dies After Flowering

**Symptoms:** The "hen" flowers then dies.

**Causes:** Sempervivums are monocarpic - the flowering rosette dies after blooming. This is normal.

**Solution:** Not needed - this is natural. The numerous chicks carry on.

**Prevention:** Can't prevent - it's their life cycle.

### Problem 3: Chicks Are Too Small

**Symptoms:** Tiny offsets seem too small to separate.

**Causes:** They're young, or crowded.

**Solution:** Wait longer, or separate anyway - small ones still succeed.

**Prevention:** None needed.

## Pro Tips

1. **They propagate themselves** - Chicks root wherever they touch soil. The main task is redistribution, not propagation.

2. **No callusing needed** - Unlike most succulents, sempervivums can be separated and planted immediately.

3. **Monocarpic life cycle** - After flowering, that rosette dies. But the many chicks ensure the colony continues.

4. **Extreme frost tolerance** - Sempervivums survive to -20°C or below. Perfect for cold-climate succulent gardens.

5. **Stolon length varies** - Some varieties produce chicks on long stolons; others cluster tightly. All propagate the same way.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Sempervivums are nearly indestructible:

- **Position:** Full sun to part shade.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant; occasional water fine.
- **Feeding:** Optional; they thrive on neglect.
- **Cold tolerance:** Hardy to severe frost.

### Selecting Offsets

Any offset works:

- Larger ones establish faster
- Smaller ones still succeed
- Rooted or unrooted - both work
- Just separate and plant
