# String of Pearls Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Stem cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Layering |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 85-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

String of Pearls (Senecio rowleyanus, now Curio rowleyanus) is a distinctive trailing succulent with bead-like leaves that propagate easily from stem cuttings. The method is simple: lay stems on soil, and they root where they touch.

This trailing habit makes propagation intuitive - in nature, stems that contact soil root and form new plants. In cultivation, we simply replicate this process deliberately.

The unusual appearance makes String of Pearls a popular houseplant, and propagation allows you to fill out sparse pots, share with friends, and maintain backup plants.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Filling sparse hanging baskets
- Creating fuller displays
- Sharing with friends
- Replacing sections lost to rot
- New containers

## Propagation Methods

### Stem Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Lay stems on soil and let them root.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: September to February (spring to summer)
- During active growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select stems**
   - Healthy trailing stems
   - 10-15cm lengths work well
   - Avoid stems with shriveled pearls

2. **Take cuttings**
   - Cut with clean scissors
   - Multiple cuttings from one strand fine
   - Remove 2-3 pearls from cut end (optional)

3. **Optional callusing**
   - Set aside 1-2 days
   - Or plant immediately in dry conditions
   - String of Pearls is forgiving

4. **Lay on soil surface**
   - Place cuttings on top of succulent mix
   - Don't bury - just lay on surface
   - Can pin with hairpins if needed

5. **Encourage rooting**
   - Keep slightly moist
   - Bright indirect light
   - Mist lightly
   - Roots form at nodes where stems contact soil

6. **Monitor development**
   - Roots visible in 2-4 weeks
   - New growth appears
   - Increase light gradually

### Layering (In-Pot Method)

**When to use:** While still attached to mother plant.

**Process:**
1. Coil trailing stems on soil surface
2. Pin in contact with soil
3. Keep moist
4. Roots form where touching
5. Cut from mother when established
6. Nearly 100% success

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (20-26°C) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Well-drained |
| **Water** | Light, consistent moisture |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation container:**
Shallow pot or tray with succulent mix. Lay cuttings on surface. Mist regularly to maintain light moisture.

**Critical factor:**
Soil contact. Roots form where stems touch moist soil. Ensure contact but don't bury.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Stems Rot Instead of Root

**Symptoms:** Stems turn mushy and black.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, cold conditions.

**Solution:** Reduce water, improve drainage, increase warmth.

**Prevention:** Light moisture, not wet. Well-drained medium.

### Problem 2: Pearls Shrivel Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Beads shrivel and dry.

**Causes:** Too dry, or cutting too long before planting.

**Solution:** Increase misting. Use shorter cuttings.

**Prevention:** Plant within 1-2 days of cutting. Maintain light moisture.

### Problem 3: Sparse, Straggly Growth

**Symptoms:** Long stems with few pearls.

**Causes:** Insufficient light.

**Solution:** Move to brighter position.

**Prevention:** Bright indirect light for compact growth.

## Pro Tips

1. **Surface contact is key** - Don't bury stems. Just lay on soil surface where roots will form at nodes.

2. **Slightly moister than typical** - String of Pearls tolerates more moisture during rooting than many succulents. Light misting helps.

3. **Coiling works well** - Coil long strands on soil surface for faster coverage.

4. **Pinning helps** - Use hairpins or bent wire to keep stems in contact with soil.

5. **Layering while attached** - The easiest method is coiling attached stems on soil. They root while still connected to the mother plant.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy plants produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Bright indirect light. Some morning sun okay.
- **Watering:** Allow to dry somewhat between waterings.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding in growing season.
- **Hanging:** Allows natural trailing habit.

### Selecting Material

Choose stems that display:

- Plump, rounded pearls
- Healthy green color
- Active growth
- No signs of rot or shriveling
