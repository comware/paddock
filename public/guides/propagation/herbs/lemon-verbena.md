# Lemon Verbena Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Softwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Semi-hardwood cuttings, layering |
| **Time to Root** | 3-6 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late spring to midsummer |
| **Success Rate** | 60-80% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Lemon verbena (Aloysia citrodora) has the most intensely lemon-scented leaves of any herb - a single leaf can perfume a room. Propagating this treasure from cuttings is moderately challenging but very worthwhile.

The main difficulty is timing. Lemon verbena is deciduous and frost-tender, entering dormancy late and breaking dormancy late. The window for successful propagation is relatively narrow: late spring through midsummer when growth is active but not too soft.

Cuttings from semi-hardwood (slightly firmer growth) tend to be more successful than very soft tip cuttings, which are prone to wilting. Rooting hormone helps significantly with this species.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Preserving this valuable fragrant herb
- Sharing with other gardeners
- Container growing for frost protection
- Establishing backup plants
- Creating lemon-scented gardens

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings (Primary Method)

**Overview:** Best results with softwood that has just begun to firm.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: November to January (late spring to midsummer)
- Time of day: Early morning
- Wood condition: New growth, slight firmness at base

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - New shoots, 10-15cm
   - Not the very softest tips
   - Growth that has begun to firm at base
   - Non-flowering shoots

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut just below a node
   - Remove lower 2-3 pairs of leaves
   - Keep 2-3 pairs at top
   - Reduce remaining leaves by half if large

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Medium-strength hormone
   - Powder or gel
   - Tap off excess
   - Important for lemon verbena

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix (perlite works well)
   - Insert 3-4cm deep
   - Firm gently
   - Water lightly

5. **Provide environment**
   - High humidity essential
   - Warm (20-25°C)
   - Bright indirect light
   - Cover with plastic or propagator

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Mist regularly or maintain humidity
   - Ventilate briefly daily
   - Keep moist but not waterlogged
   - Remove any fallen leaves

7. **Rooting and potting**
   - Roots develop in 3-6 weeks
   - Wait until well-rooted
   - Pot into good potting mix
   - Grow on in warm spot

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** Slightly later in season when growth has firmed more.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: January to February
- Wood condition: Firmer than softwood, still flexible

**Process:**
1. Take 12-15cm cuttings
2. Remove lower leaves
3. Apply rooting hormone
4. Insert in well-drained mix
5. Keep warm and humid
6. Roots in 4-8 weeks

### Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** Simple method for a few plants.

**Process:**
1. In spring, bend low branch to ground
2. Wound underside slightly
3. Pin and bury wounded section
4. Keep moist
5. Roots develop over growing season
6. Sever and pot up in autumn

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Good | November onwards once growth established |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Best | Prime propagation window |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Poor | Too late, growth slowing |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Not possible | Dormant, no suitable material |

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** November to January
- **Critical factor:** Wait for active growth - lemon verbena breaks dormancy late
- **Avoid:** Early spring (not growing yet), autumn (growth hardening)

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (20-25°C) |
| **Humidity** | High (80%+) |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Very well-drained |
| **Bottom heat** | Helpful but not essential |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation unit:**
Covered seed tray or propagator. High humidity essential. Bottom heat improves results. Good ventilation to prevent disease.

**Key consideration:**
Lemon verbena cuttings wilt easily. Work quickly when taking cuttings and get them into humid conditions fast.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Wilt Immediately

**Symptoms:** Leaves droop within hours of taking cutting.

**Causes:** Not enough humidity, too much light, cutting too soft.

**Solution:** Increase humidity immediately, move to deeper shade.

**Prevention:** Work quickly, mist immediately, use slightly firmer wood, cover promptly.

### Problem 2: Black Stems from Base

**Symptoms:** Cutting blackens from bottom up.

**Causes:** Too wet, fungal infection, poor drainage.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, reduce water, remove affected cuttings.

**Prevention:** Very well-drained medium, avoid overwatering, good ventilation.

### Problem 3: Slow or No Rooting

**Symptoms:** After 6+ weeks, no roots despite healthy-looking cutting.

**Causes:** Temperature too cool, no hormone used, wrong wood maturity.

**Solution:** Increase warmth, try again with hormone and correct wood.

**Prevention:** Ensure warmth (20°C+), always use hormone, choose semi-firm wood.

## Pro Tips

1. **Work fast** - Lemon verbena wilts very quickly once cut. Have everything ready before you take cuttings, and get them into humid conditions within minutes.

2. **Not too soft, not too hard** - The perfect cutting is slightly firmer than pure softwood. If it bends without breaking at all, it's too soft. If it snaps cleanly, it's too hard.

3. **Bottom heat works** - A heat mat under your propagator significantly improves rooting success with this species.

4. **Overwintering young plants** - Newly rooted lemon verbena is very frost-tender. Keep first-year plants in containers and bring under cover for winter.

5. **The dropped leaf trigger** - If your lemon verbena drops its leaves in autumn/winter, don't panic - it's deciduous. It will releaf in spring.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy lemon verbena produces the best cuttings:

- **Position:** Full sun, warm spot, protection from frost.
- **Pruning:** Light trim in spring to encourage bushy growth.
- **Feeding:** Moderate feeding during growth.
- **Winter care:** In frost areas, grow in containers and protect over winter.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, slightly firmed
- Healthy, strongly scented leaves
- Non-flowering shoots
- Vigorous, typical growth
