# Sage Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy-Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Softwood or semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Layering, division, seed (common sage) |
| **Time to Root** | 3-6 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late spring to early autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 70-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Common sage (Salvia officinalis) and its ornamental varieties propagate readily from cuttings. The technique sits between easy thyme and slightly trickier rosemary - most attempts succeed, but attention to a few key factors improves results.

Softwood cuttings from new spring growth root fastest, while semi-hardwood cuttings from late summer are more durable. Both approaches work well. The main consideration is avoiding flowering material - sage flowers profusely and flowering shoots root poorly.

Named sage varieties (purple sage, golden sage, tricolour sage) must be propagated vegetatively. Common grey-green sage can be grown from seed but is still faster from cuttings.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Expanding herb garden collections
- Creating sage variety displays
- Sharing ornamental varieties
- Replacing winter losses
- Container growing

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings (Spring/Early Summer)

**Overview:** Fastest rooting using new spring growth.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October to December (spring to early summer)
- Time of day: Morning
- Wood condition: Soft, new growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - New shoot tips, 8-10cm
   - Non-flowering growth
   - Before stems become firm
   - Healthy, typical growth

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep 2-3 pairs at top
   - Work quickly to prevent wilting

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Dip in mild-medium strength hormone
   - Tap off excess
   - Recommended for sage

4. **Insert and care**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 3cm deep
   - Cover to maintain humidity
   - Keep moist but not wet
   - Ventilate daily

5. **Rooting**
   - 3-4 weeks typically
   - Pot up when well-rooted

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Late Summer)

**When to use:** When spring is missed, or for more robust cuttings.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to March (late summer)
- Wood condition: Firm but still flexible

**Process:**
1. Cut 10-12cm lengths of current season's growth
2. Wood firm at base, slightly softer at tip
3. Strip lower leaves, apply hormone
4. Insert in well-drained mix
5. Cover for humidity
6. Roots in 4-6 weeks

### Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** Low-effort method for a few plants.

**Process:**
1. In spring, wound a low stem where it can reach soil
2. Pin wounded section to ground
3. Cover with soil, keep moist
4. Roots in 2-4 months
5. Sever and transplant in autumn

### Division (Established Plants)

**When to use:** For spreading, multi-stemmed plants.

**Process:**
1. Lift entire plant in spring or autumn
2. Divide into sections with roots
3. Replant immediately
4. Water well

### Seed (Common Sage Only)

**When to use:** Common grey sage. Named varieties don't come true.

**Process:**
1. Sow in spring, barely cover
2. Germination 2-3 weeks
3. Thin or transplant
4. Slow initially, then vigorous

Covered tray or pot with clear plastic. Well-drained propagation mix. Daily venting to prevent fungal issues.

**Outdoor propagation:**
Possible in warm weather in sheltered spot. Light shade initially.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings from Flowering Shoots Fail

**Symptoms:** Cuttings wilt, fail to root, or root slowly.

**Causes:** Flowering shoots direct energy to reproduction, not rooting.

**Solution:** Use only non-flowering material.

**Prevention:** Take cuttings before flowering begins, or cut plants back after flowering to produce fresh growth.

### Problem 2: Stem Rot at Base

**Symptoms:** Base turns black, cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, poor air circulation.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, ventilate more, reduce water.

**Prevention:** Well-drained medium, daily venting, water sparingly.

### Problem 3: Leaves Develop Grey Mould

**Symptoms:** Grey fuzzy growth on leaves, spreading.

**Causes:** Too humid, poor air circulation, dead leaves not removed.

**Solution:** Improve ventilation, remove affected material, reduce humidity.

**Prevention:** Good air flow, remove fallen leaves, don't overcrowd.

## Pro Tips

1. **Beat the flowers** - Sage flowers heavily. Take cuttings before flowering begins (October-November) or cut plants back after flowering to produce fresh growth for cuttings.

2. **Ornamental varieties need care** - Purple, golden, and tricolour sages are slower to establish than common grey sage. Take a few extra cuttings as insurance.

3. **The heel advantage** - Cuttings torn from the main stem with a "heel" of older wood often root better than straight-cut cuttings.

4. **Winter vulnerability** - Young sage plants are susceptible to winter wet. Keep first-year plants protected or in pots with shelter.

5. **Replacement planning** - Sage plants become woody and less productive after 4-5 years. Propagate replacements while parent plants are still vigorous.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy sage produces the best cuttings:

- **Pruning:** Cut back after flowering to produce fresh growth. Light annual spring trim.
- **Position:** Full sun, excellent drainage.
- **Feeding:** Minimal - lean conditions intensify flavour.
- **Age:** Replace plants every 4-5 years as they become woody.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Non-flowering shoots
- Current season's growth
- Healthy, aromatic foliage
- Typical colour and form for variety
