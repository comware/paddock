# Stevia Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Softwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Division, seed (variable) |
| **Time to Root** | 2-3 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 70-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Stevia (Stevia rebaudiana), the natural sweetener plant, propagates readily from cuttings. This is fortunate, because seed-grown plants vary considerably in sweetness and flavour - vegetative propagation from a known sweet parent ensures quality.

Softwood cuttings taken during the growing season root well with basic care. The technique is similar to propagating basil: take soft tip cuttings, keep them humid, and roots develop within a few weeks.

Division of established clumps works in spring but produces fewer plants than cuttings. Seed is possible but results are unpredictable, and germination can be inconsistent.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Multiplying plants with proven sweetness
- Sharing favourite stevia plants
- Replacing winter losses
- Container growing
- Creating backup plants

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Standard technique using soft tip growth. Reliable with basic care.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October to January (spring to midsummer)
- Time of day: Morning
- Wood condition: Soft, current-season growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Soft tip growth, 8-10cm
   - Before flowering if possible
   - Healthy, vigorous shoots
   - From plants with proven sweetness

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep 3-4 leaves at top
   - Work quickly to prevent wilting

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Mild to medium strength
   - Dip base
   - Tap off excess

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 2-3cm deep
   - Firm gently
   - Water lightly

5. **Provide environment**
   - High humidity (cover or mist)
   - Warm (20-25°C)
   - Bright indirect light
   - Ventilate daily

6. **Rooting**
   - Roots develop in 2-3 weeks
   - New growth indicates success
   - Pot up when well-rooted

### Division (Alternative - Spring)

**When to use:** For overwintered plants with multiple crowns.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: September to October (spring)

**Process:**
1. Lift entire clump as growth begins
2. Separate into sections with roots
3. Replant immediately
4. Keep watered until established

### Seed (Variable Results)

**When to use:** Only when cuttings unavailable or for experimenting.

**Process:**
1. Sow in spring on surface of seed mix
2. Light improves germination
3. Germination erratic: 7-21 days
4. Sweetness varies between seedlings
5. Test sweetness before selecting plants to keep

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best | Prime cutting and division time |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Good | Cuttings root well, avoid flowering material |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Possible | Before plant declines |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Not possible | Dormant or dead in most areas |

### Optimal Timing

- **Cuttings:** October to December (before flowering)
- **Division:** September to October (as growth resumes)
- **Note:** Stevia is frost-tender and may die back or die entirely in winter

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (20-25°C) |
| **Humidity** | High initially |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Well-drained |
| **Bottom heat** | Helpful |

### Environment Setup

**Basic setup:**
Covered seed tray or propagator. Moist propagation mix. Daily venting. Warmth speeds rooting.

**Key factor:**
Stevia is frost-tender. All propagation must happen during warm months.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Wilt Despite Humidity

**Symptoms:** Leaves droop even with cover/misting.

**Causes:** Too much sun, not enough humidity, cutting too large.

**Solution:** Deep shade, increase misting, try smaller cuttings.

**Prevention:** Work quickly, shade well, high humidity from start.

### Problem 2: Seedlings Vary in Sweetness

**Symptoms:** Some plants sweet, others bitter or bland.

**Causes:** Genetic variation from seed-grown plants.

**Solution:** Taste-test plants and discard unsatisfactory ones.

**Prevention:** Propagate vegetatively from proven sweet plants.

### Problem 3: Plant Dies in Winter

**Symptoms:** Above-ground parts die; no regrowth in spring.

**Causes:** Frost killed the plant.

**Solution:** Too late for that plant. Protect others.

**Prevention:** In frost areas, treat stevia as annual, bring pots under cover, or take cuttings for next year.

## Pro Tips

1. **Taste before you propagate** - Stevia sweetness varies. Only propagate from plants you've confirmed are sweet.

2. **The insurance cutting** - Before winter, take cuttings and root them indoors. If your main plant dies in frost, you have replacements.

3. **Pinch for bushiness** - Pinching stem tips encourages branching, producing more material for cuttings and more leaf for harvest.

4. **Avoid flowering material** - Once stevia flowers, leaf sweetness and cutting success both decline. Take cuttings before flowering.

5. **Container backup** - Keep at least one plant in a pot that can be moved to protection. Outdoor plants in frost areas are at risk.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy stevia produces the best cuttings:

- **Position:** Full sun, warm spot, frost-free.
- **Pinching:** Regular pinching encourages bushy growth with good cutting material.
- **Watering:** Consistent moisture during growth.
- **Feeding:** Moderate feeding.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Soft, current-season growth
- Non-flowering shoots
- Sweet taste (test it!)
- Healthy, vigorous growth
