# Oregano Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Division or softwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Layering, seed |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 85-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Optional |

## Overview

Oregano (Origanum vulgare and related species) is a willing propagator that offers multiple easy methods. Its spreading, rhizomatous habit means established plants can be divided readily, while softwood cuttings root quickly with minimal fuss.

The ease of oregano propagation makes it an excellent herb for beginners. Success rates are high with any method, and plants establish quickly. Division is so simple it barely counts as propagation - you're essentially just splitting a plant and replanting the pieces.

Greek oregano (O. vulgare subsp. hirtum) is the culinary standard and propagates easily. Named varieties and hybrids must be propagated vegetatively to maintain their flavour profiles.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Expanding herb garden coverage
- Sharing with other gardeners
- Creating Mediterranean herb borders
- Container planting
- Groundcover in dry gardens

## Propagation Methods

### Division (Easiest Method)

**Overview:** Simply split established clumps into smaller pieces. Nearly always works.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: September to October (early spring) or March to April (autumn)
- Plant condition: Actively growing or just starting growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Lift the clump**
   - Water day before
   - Dig around and under entire clump
   - Lift out carefully

2. **Divide into sections**
   - Pull apart by hand where possible
   - Use knife for stubborn sections
   - Each piece needs roots and shoots
   - Divisions of any size work

3. **Replant immediately**
   - Same depth as before
   - Water well
   - Keep moist until established
   - New growth in 2-4 weeks

### Softwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** When you want many plants or can't disturb the parent.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October to January (spring to midsummer)
- Time of day: Morning
- Wood condition: Soft, current-season growth

**Process:**
1. Take 8-10cm tip cuttings
2. Strip lower leaves
3. Insert in propagation mix
4. Keep moist, moderate humidity
5. Roots in 2-4 weeks
6. Pot up when established

### Layering (Simple)

**When to use:** Effortless way to create a few new plants.

**Process:**
1. Pin stems to ground where they touch
2. Cover contact point with soil
3. Keep moist
4. Roots develop in 4-8 weeks
5. Sever and transplant

### Seed

**When to use:** For species oregano. Named varieties don't come true.

**Process:**
1. Sow in spring, surface sow (needs light)
2. Germination 7-14 days
3. Slow initially
4. Less predictable flavour than vegetative propagation

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|

| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Good | Cuttings root quickly |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Good | Division works well |

### Optimal Timing

- **Division:** September to October or March to April
- **Cuttings:** October to January
- **Layering:** Any time during active growth

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Moderate to warm (15-25°C) |
| **Humidity** | Low to moderate |
| **Light** | Bright |
| **Medium** | Well-drained |
| **Drainage** | Good |

### Environment Setup

**For cuttings:**
Simple propagation tray, light cover, well-drained mix. Don't keep too wet.

**For divisions:**
Plant directly in final position or pot up. Keep watered until established.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Wilt and Collapse

**Symptoms:** Leaves droop, cutting fails within days.

**Causes:** Too much sun, not enough moisture, cutting too large.

**Solution:** Move to shade, increase humidity, try again with smaller cuttings.

**Prevention:** Shade initially, don't let dry out, remove excess leaves.

### Problem 2: Division Fails to Establish

**Symptoms:** Divided section wilts and dies.

**Causes:** Not enough roots, planted too deep, too dry.

**Solution:** Usually too late. Keep remaining divisions watered.

**Prevention:** Ensure adequate roots on each division, water well, don't bury crown.

### Problem 3: Weak, Leggy Growth

**Symptoms:** Long, thin stems instead of bushy growth.

**Causes:** Not enough light, too much nitrogen.

**Solution:** Move to sunnier spot, pinch back, reduce feeding.

**Prevention:** Full sun position, lean soil.

## Pro Tips

1. **Division is the winner** - Oregano division is so easy and reliable that there's rarely reason to use other methods unless you need many plants quickly.

2. **Root running** - Oregano spreads by underground runners. You can dig up these runners with roots attached and transplant them directly.

3. **Flavour first** - When propagating oregano for cooking, taste the parent plant first. Oregano flavour varies significantly. Only propagate plants with the flavour you want.

4. **The haircut treatment** - After flowering, cut oregano back hard. This produces a flush of new growth ideal for cuttings.

5. **Container caution** - Oregano spreads vigorously. Container growing or a contained bed prevents it from taking over.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy oregano produces the best propagation material:

- **Cutting back:** Cut back after flowering for fresh growth and to prevent seeding.
- **Position:** Full sun, well-drained soil.
- **Division frequency:** Divide every 3-4 years to maintain vigour.
- **Feeding:** Minimal - rich soil reduces essential oil content.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Strong, aromatic flavour (taste it!)
- Healthy, pest-free growth
- Typical form for the variety
- Active growth (not flowering)
