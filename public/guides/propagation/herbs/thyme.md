# Thyme Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Softwood cuttings or layering |
| **Alternative Methods** | Division, seed |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to early autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 80-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Optional (helps but not essential) |

## Overview

Thyme (Thymus species) is one of the easier Mediterranean herbs to propagate. Its small size, willing nature, and tendency to layer naturally make it accessible for beginners. Unlike lavender or rosemary, thyme rarely presents challenges - most attempts succeed.

The multiple propagation options give flexibility: softwood cuttings in spring and summer, division of established clumps, or simply encouraging natural layering. This redundancy means you can always find a method that works with available material.

Named thyme varieties (lemon thyme, orange thyme, woolly thyme, etc.) must be propagated vegetatively to maintain their characteristics. Common thyme can be grown from seed but is still faster from cuttings.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Filling gaps in herb gardens
- Creating thyme lawns or paths
- Sharing favourite varieties
- Container planting
- Rock garden planting

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings (Primary Method)

**Overview:** Quick and easy using soft, current-season growth.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October to February (spring to mid-summer)
- Time of day: Morning
- Wood condition: Soft, current-season growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Soft, new shoot tips
   - 5-8cm long
   - Non-flowering shoots preferred
   - Healthy, typical growth

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Strip leaves from lower 2-3cm
   - Cut base just below a node
   - Keep remaining leaves

3. **Rooting hormone** (optional)
   - Dip in mild hormone powder
   - Tap off excess
   - Improves speed but not essential

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 2cm deep
   - Firm gently
   - Water lightly

5. **Provide environment**
   - Bright, indirect light
   - Moderate humidity
   - Cover or mist lightly
   - Keep barely moist

6. **Rooting**
   - Roots develop in 2-4 weeks
   - New growth indicates success
   - Pot up when well-rooted

### Layering (Easiest Method)

**Overview:** Thyme layers naturally - this just formalises the process.

**When to use:** Any time during growing season. Virtually guaranteed success.

**Process:**
1. Identify stems touching or near ground
2. Pin them in contact with soil using wire or stone
3. Cover contact point with 1-2cm of soil or compost
4. Keep moist
5. Roots develop in 4-8 weeks
6. Sever from parent, transplant

### Division (Established Clumps)

**When to use:** When you have mature, spreading plants.

**Process:**
1. Lift entire clump in spring or autumn
2. Pull or cut into sections
3. Each section needs roots and shoots
4. Replant immediately
5. Water well, keep moist until established

### Seed (Common Thyme)

**When to use:** For common thyme (T. vulgaris). Named varieties don't come true.

**Process:**
1. Sow in spring on surface
2. Don't cover - needs light
3. Germination 14-28 days
4. Slow-growing seedlings
5. Takes 6-12 months to reach useful size

- **Cuttings:** October to February
- **Division:** September to October or March to April
- **Layering:** Any time during growth, separate in autumn

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot

**Symptoms:** Stems blacken from base, leaves fall.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, too humid.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, reduce water, increase ventilation.

**Prevention:** Well-drained medium, don't overwater, good air circulation.

### Problem 2: Leggy, Weak Growth

**Symptoms:** Rooted cuttings produce long, thin stems.

**Causes:** Not enough light, too warm, overcrowded.

**Solution:** Move to brighter position, pinch tips to encourage branching.

**Prevention:** Bright position from start, cooler night temperatures.

### Problem 3: Slow Establishment After Division

**Symptoms:** Divided plants survive but don't grow.

**Causes:** Roots damaged, planted too deep, too dry.

**Solution:** Water consistently, wait - thyme is resilient.

**Prevention:** Handle roots gently, plant at original depth, water well.

## Pro Tips

1. **Pinch for bushiness** - Once rooted cuttings are established, pinch out the growing tips. This encourages branching and creates bushier, more productive plants.

2. **Natural layering is free plants** - Mature thyme plants often root where stems touch the ground. Simply separate these natural layers and transplant.

3. **Don't wet the foliage** - When watering thyme (plants or cuttings), water the soil, not the leaves. Wet foliage in poor air circulation causes fungal problems.

4. **Quick turnover** - Thyme establishes so quickly that you can take cuttings, root them, and have usable plants within 2-3 months.

5. **Variety choice matters** - Common thyme (T. vulgaris) is easiest. Creeping thymes root as they grow naturally. Lemon thyme is slightly more finicky.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy thyme produces the best propagation material:

- **Pruning:** Trim regularly to maintain compact growth and prevent woodiness.
- **Position:** Full sun, excellent drainage, lean soil.
- **Replacement:** Plants become woody after 4-5 years. Propagate replacements while plants are young.
- **Winter:** Protect from wet winter conditions more than cold.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Soft, current-season growth
- Healthy, aromatic foliage
- Non-flowering shoots if possible
- Typical leaf colour and form for variety
