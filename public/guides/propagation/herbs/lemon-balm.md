# Lemon Balm Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Division |
| **Alternative Methods** | Softwood cuttings, seed |
| **Time to Root** | 2-3 weeks (cuttings), immediate (division) |
| **Best Season** | Spring or autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 90-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) is one of the easiest herbs to propagate - in fact, the challenge is usually preventing it from spreading too vigorously. This eager grower roots from almost any method and establishes quickly.

Division is the simplest approach: established clumps can be pulled apart and replanted with near-certain success. Cuttings root readily in water or soil. Even seed germinates easily, though seedlings take longer to reach usable size.

The forgiving nature of lemon balm makes it an excellent practice plant for beginners learning propagation techniques.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Expanding herb plantings quickly
- Sharing with other gardeners
- Container growing
- Creating lemon-scented groundcover
- Herbal tea gardens

## Propagation Methods

### Division (Easiest - Recommended)

**Overview:** Pull apart established clumps. Nearly 100% success.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: September to October (spring) or March to April (autumn)
- Plant condition: Active growth or preparing for dormancy

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Lift the clump**
   - Water day before
   - Dig around entire clump
   - Lift with roots intact

2. **Separate into divisions**
   - Pull apart by hand - usually separates easily
   - Each piece needs roots and shoots
   - Even small sections work

3. **Replant immediately**
   - Same depth as before
   - Water well
   - Keep moist for a week or two

4. **Establish quickly**
   - New growth within 2 weeks
   - Ready to harvest in 4-6 weeks

### Softwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** When you need many plants or don't want to disturb parent.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October to January (spring to midsummer)

**Process:**
1. Take 10cm tip cuttings
2. Remove lower leaves
3. Place in water (roots in 2-3 weeks) or insert in moist potting mix
4. Keep moist, bright location
5. Pot up when rooted

### Seed (Alternative)

**When to use:** For large quantities, or when no parent plant available.

**Process:**
1. Sow in spring, surface sow
2. Light helps germination
3. Germination 10-14 days
4. Easy but slower than division

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Plant Spreads Too Aggressively

**Symptoms:** Lemon balm takes over garden beds.

**Causes:** Natural vigour - it's meant to spread!

**Solution:** Root barriers, container growing, or regular removal of spreading runners.

**Prevention:** Plant in containers or contained beds. Remove flowers before seeding.

### Problem 2: Weak, Leggy Growth

**Symptoms:** Long, thin stems with sparse leaves.

**Causes:** Not enough light, needs cutting back.

**Solution:** Move to sunnier spot, cut back hard to encourage bushy regrowth.

**Prevention:** Full sun to part shade, regular harvesting encourages dense growth.

### Problem 3: Leaves Lose Lemon Scent

**Symptoms:** Foliage less fragrant than expected.

**Causes:** Too much shade, too much nitrogen, flowering.

**Solution:** Improve light, reduce feeding, cut back before flowering.

**Prevention:** Good light, moderate fertility, regular harvesting.

## Pro Tips

1. **Contain the enthusiasm** - Lemon balm spreads vigorously by runners and self-seeding. Plant in containers or use root barriers unless you want it everywhere.

2. **Water propagation works perfectly** - A cutting in a jar of water on the windowsill will root in 2-3 weeks. Simple and visible.

3. **Harvest to propagate** - Regular harvesting produces the bushy, non-flowering growth ideal for cuttings.

4. **The golden rule** - If you want more lemon balm, just divide what you have. It's that simple.

5. **Variegated forms** - Golden and variegated lemon balm must be propagated vegetatively. They're slightly less vigorous than green forms but still easy.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy lemon balm produces abundant propagation material:

- **Cutting back:** Regular harvest or cutting back produces bushy growth.
- **Position:** Full sun to part shade.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding only - too much nitrogen reduces oil content.
- **Flowers:** Remove flowers to prevent self-seeding and encourage leaf production.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Strong lemon scent (smell it!)
- Healthy, unblemished leaves
- Vigorous growth
- Typical form for variety
