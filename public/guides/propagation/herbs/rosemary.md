# Rosemary Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy-Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Layering, seed (slow) |
| **Time to Root** | 3-6 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late spring to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 70-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus, formerly Rosmarinus officinalis) is one of the most rewarding culinary herbs to propagate. While it's slightly slower to root than mint or basil, success rates are good and the resulting plants are long-lived perennials that will provide harvests for years.

Semi-hardwood cuttings taken from late spring through autumn root reliably with basic equipment. The key is choosing the right wood - not the soft new tips, but slightly firmer growth that's still flexible.

Rosemary can also be grown from seed, but germination is slow and erratic. Cuttings produce plants identical to the parent and reach usable size much faster.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Expanding herb gardens
- Creating rosemary hedges
- Sharing favourite varieties
- Replacing winter losses
- Container growing for patios

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** The standard method, using growth from the current season once it has begun to firm.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: November to March (late spring to early autumn)
- Time of day: Morning
- Wood condition: Current season's growth, firm but flexible

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Non-flowering shoots if possible
   - Current season's growth that bends without snapping
   - Avoid very soft tips or old woody stems
   - Healthy, vigorous growth

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut 8-12cm lengths
   - Strip leaves from lower 2/3
   - Cut base just below a node
   - Retain leaves on upper third

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Dip in medium-strength hormone powder
   - Tap off excess
   - Recommended but not essential

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained mix (perlite, or 50/50 sand/perlite)
   - Insert 3-4cm deep
   - Firm gently
   - Water lightly

5. **Provide environment**
   - Bright, indirect light
   - Cover to maintain humidity (plastic bag or propagator)
   - Ventilate daily
   - Keep moist but not waterlogged

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Check moisture regularly
   - Remove any failed cuttings
   - Rooting in 3-6 weeks
   - Tug gently to test for roots

7. **Pot up**
   - When roots visible or tug test positive
   - Well-drained potting mix
   - Grow on before planting out

### Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** Simple method for a few plants without any equipment.

**Process:**
1. Select low, flexible branch
2. Bend to ground, wound underside slightly
3. Bury wounded section 5cm deep
4. Pin in place with wire or stone
5. Keep moist
6. Roots develop in 2-4 months
7. Sever from parent, transplant

### Seed (Slow)

**When to use:** Only when cuttings unavailable or for experimenting.

**Process:**
1. Sow in spring on surface of seed-raising mix
2. Light needed for germination
3. Germination erratic: 2-4 weeks or longer
4. Slow-growing seedlings
5. Takes 12-18 months to reach useful size

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** November to March (warm months)
- **Avoid:** Winter and very early spring
- **Signs of readiness:** Wood firm but still flexible, not brittle

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (18-24°C) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate to high initially |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Very well-drained |
| **Drainage** | Critical - rosemary hates wet feet |

### Environment Setup

**Basic setup:**
Covered seed tray or pot with clear plastic bag. Well-drained medium essential. Daily venting.

**Outdoor propagation:**
Possible in warm weather in a sheltered, shaded spot. Keep moist but not wet.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot at Base

**Symptoms:** Stem turns black from base upward, leaves drop.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, contaminated medium.

**Solution:** Improve drainage dramatically, let dry between waterings.

**Prevention:** Very well-drained medium, don't overwater, clean containers.

### Problem 2: Cuttings Dry Out and Die

**Symptoms:** Leaves brown and crisp, cutting desiccates.

**Causes:** Not enough humidity, too much sun, medium dried out.

**Solution:** Increase humidity, move to shade, maintain moisture.

**Prevention:** Cover to retain humidity, check moisture daily, avoid direct sun.

### Problem 3: Slow or No Rooting

**Symptoms:** Cutting survives but no roots after 6+ weeks.

**Causes:** Wrong wood type, too cold, no hormone.

**Solution:** Be patient, try new cuttings with correct wood type.

**Prevention:** Use semi-hardwood (firm but flexible), keep warm, use rooting hormone.

## Pro Tips

1. **Strip leaves cleanly** - Pull leaves downward off the stem for a clean break. Cutting leaves leaves stubs that can rot.

2. **Flowering shoots can work** - If you must use flowering material, remove the flowers. The cutting will still root, just slightly less reliably.

3. **Variety matters** - Prostrate and trailing rosemaries root more readily than upright types. Start with easier varieties to build confidence.

4. **The Mediterranean rule** - Rosemary comes from dry Mediterranean climates. It tolerates drought but hates wet roots. When in doubt, water less.

5. **Winter protection** - Newly rooted rosemary is tender. Keep first-year plants protected from hard frosts.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy rosemary produces the best cuttings:

- **Pruning:** Regular harvest/pruning encourages bushy, non-flowering growth ideal for cuttings.
- **Position:** Full sun, excellent drainage. Can grow in poor soil.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding only. Rich soil produces soft, rot-prone growth.
- **Age:** Older plants become woody. Take cuttings from younger growth near branch tips.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth (not old wood)
- Firm but flexible stems
- Healthy, aromatic foliage
- No flowers if possible
