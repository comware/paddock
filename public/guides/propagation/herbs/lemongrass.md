| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |

| **Alternative Methods** | Rooting stalks in water |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring |
| **Success Rate** | 90-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Lemongrass is a clumping grass, so it does not root from cuttings the way a shrub does — it is divided, like any perennial grass, or it is regrown from the base of a stalk.

The second method is the one that surprises people. A stalk bought from a greengrocer, with its base intact, will put out roots in a glass of water on a windowsill in a few weeks. Yates recommends exactly this: wash the base, cut the leaves back by two-thirds, stand the stalks base-down in water, and pot them once the roots are 30-50mm long. It costs a couple of dollars and needs no parent plant at all.

Division is faster and gives a bigger plant sooner, but it needs an established clump. Most growers start with the glass of water and divide from then on.

The real constraint is not propagation, it is cold. Lemongrass is tropical. In a cool-temperate garden with winter frosts it will be knocked back hard or killed outright, so it is usually grown in a pot that can be moved somewhere sheltered, and divided in spring as it comes back into growth.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Turning one clump into several without buying anything
- Keeping a plant going in a pot that gets moved out of the frost
- Starting from a supermarket stalk, with no parent plant
- Rejuvenating a clump that has gone woody and hollow in the centre

## Propagation Methods

### Division (Recommended)

**Overview:** The standard method for an established clump, and the fastest route to a usable plant.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: September to November (spring), as new growth starts
- Plant condition: Established clump, at least a year old

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Prepare the parent**
   - Water the clump well the day before
   - Cut the foliage back to about 30cm so you can see what you are doing

2. **Lift the clump**
   - Loosen all round with a fork, then lift the whole root ball
   - Or slice a section straight out of the side with a spade, leaving the rest in place

3. **Divide**
   - Pull or cut into sections
   - Each division needs several stalks and a decent share of roots
   - Discard the dead woody centre of an old clump

4. **Cut back**
   - Prune each division to around 10cm above the base
   - This is not optional — a division carrying full-length leaves loses more water than its disturbed roots can supply

5. **Replant**
   - Into free-draining potting mix or well-prepared ground
   - Same depth as before, firm in

6. **Aftercare** (2-4 weeks)
   - Keep moist and out of harsh sun until new growth appears
   - New leaves from the centre mean it has taken

### Rooting Stalks in Water (No Parent Plant Needed)

**When to use:** Starting from nothing, or from a bunch bought for cooking.

**Process:**
1. Choose the freshest stalks with the base intact — a firm pale bulb, not a dry sawn-off end
2. Wash the base clean
3. Cut the leaves back by about two-thirds
4. Stand base-down in a glass with 3-4cm of water
5. Bright light, change the water every couple of days
6. Roots appear in 2-4 weeks
7. Pot up once roots are 30-50mm long

Success depends almost entirely on the stalk. A base that has been trimmed flat and gone dry will never root; one with a slightly bulbous, live-looking base nearly always will.

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best | Division as growth restarts |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Good | Water rooting is fast in the warmth |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Possible | Root indoors; too late to establish outside |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Avoid | Dormant or damaged; disturbing it now can kill it |

### Optimal Timing

- **Division:** September to November
- **Water rooting:** Any warm month; spring and summer are quickest
- **Key factor:** Warmth. Nothing much happens below about 18°C.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Stalks Rot in the Glass

**Symptoms:** Base goes slimy and the water clouds.

**Causes:** Water not changed, or too much of the stalk submerged.

**Solution:** Discard, restart with fresh stalks.

**Prevention:** 3-4cm of water only, changed every two or three days.

### Problem 2: Division Collapses After Replanting

**Symptoms:** Leaves brown off within days.

**Causes:** Too much foliage left on a division with disturbed roots.

**Solution:** Cut it back hard anyway — it may still shoot from the base.

**Prevention:** Cut to 10cm at the time of division. Every time.

### Problem 3: Clump Dead in the Middle

**Symptoms:** Live growth in a ring, woody hollow centre.

**Causes:** Age. Clumping grasses do this.

**Solution:** Lift, divide, discard the centre, replant the outer sections.

**Prevention:** Divide every two or three years rather than waiting for it.

## Pro Tips

1. **Buy the bunch with roots showing** — Greengrocer stalks vary enormously. One with a live-looking base is worth picking through the bunch for.

2. **Cut back hard at division** — The single most common reason a division fails is that it was left with too many leaves.

3. **Grow it in a pot in a frost district** — Lemongrass is tropical. A pot that can move to a wall or a shed is the difference between dividing it next spring and buying it again.

4. **Divide before it hollows out** — Every two or three years keeps the clump productive and gives you plants.

5. **The outer stalks are the good ones** — For both cooking and propagating, take from the outside of the clump and leave the centre growing.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

- **Position:** Full sun, warm, sheltered from frost.
- **Pruning:** Cut back old foliage in early spring; harvest outer stalks through the season.
- **Feeding:** It is a grass — nitrogen suits it. Feed through the growing season.
- **Watering:** Regular and generous in summer; keep drier through winter.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Firm, plump bases rather than dry trimmed ends
- Several stalks per division, with roots attached
- Fresh green growth, no yellowing
- No sign of rot at the base
