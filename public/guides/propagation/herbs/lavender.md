# Lavender Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Softwood cuttings, layering, seed (species only) |
| **Time to Root** | 3-6 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late spring to early autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 60-80% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Lavender (Lavandula species) propagation follows similar principles to rosemary - semi-hardwood cuttings taken during the growing season root reliably with basic care. Success rates are moderate, meaning you should take more cuttings than needed to ensure sufficient rooted plants.

The timing and wood selection are critical. Cuttings from material that's too soft rot easily; cuttings from wood that's too hard root slowly or not at all. The sweet spot is semi-hardwood: this season's growth that has begun to firm at the base but is still flexible.

English lavender (L. angustifolia) and its cultivars root more readily than Spanish (L. stoechas) or French (L. dentata) types. All named varieties must be propagated vegetatively - lavender doesn't come true from seed.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating lavender hedges economically
- Sharing favourite fragrant varieties
- Replacing plants lost to root rot or cold
- Expanding cottage garden plantings
- Container growing

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Standard method using current season's growth once it has firmed.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: November to March (late spring to early autumn)
- Time of day: Morning
- Wood condition: Firm at base, still slightly flexible

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Non-flowering shoots preferred
   - This season's growth from lower on the plant
   - Avoid long, leggy shoots
   - Healthy, typical growth

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut 8-10cm lengths
   - Strip leaves from lower half
   - Include a "heel" if possible (tear cutting from main stem)
   - Or cut just below a node

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Dip in rooting hormone powder
   - Medium strength
   - Tap off excess
   - Recommended for lavender

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Very well-drained mix (perlite, or 50/50 sand/perlite)
   - Insert 2-3cm deep
   - Space so leaves don't touch
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Bright, indirect light
   - Moderate humidity (cover loosely)
   - Good air circulation
   - Keep barely moist

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Ventilate daily
   - Don't overwater - lavender rots easily
   - Remove any failures promptly
   - Patience - 3-6 weeks to root

7. **Pot up rooted cuttings**
   - Very well-drained potting mix
   - Handle gently
   - Grow on before planting out

### Softwood Cuttings (Alternative - Spring)

**When to use:** Early season propagation using soft new growth.

**Process:**
1. Take 8cm tip cuttings of soft new growth (October)
2. Remove lower leaves
3. Use rooting hormone
4. Higher humidity needed
5. Faster rooting but higher rot risk
6. More attention required

### Layering (Alternative - Simple)

**When to use:** For a few plants with no equipment.

**Process:**
1. In spring, bend low branch to ground
2. Wound underside, apply hormone
3. Bury wounded section 5cm deep
4. Pin in place, keep moist
5. Roots in 3-6 months
6. Sever and transplant

### Seed (Species Only)

**When to use:** Growing species lavenders (not named cultivars).

**Process:**
1. Sow in spring, barely covered
2. Light helps germination
3. Cool, moist conditions
4. Germination 2-4 weeks
5. Variable results
6. 18-24 months to flowering size

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Moderate (18-24°C) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate (not too high) |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Extremely well-drained |
| **Air flow** | Good ventilation essential |

### Environment Setup

**Basic setup:**
Seed tray with very free-draining medium. Cover loosely - not sealed tight. Daily venting. Barely moist.

**Key difference from other herbs:**
Lavender needs less humidity than most cuttings. Too much moisture causes rot. Think "Mediterranean" - bright, airy, not wet.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Stem blackens from base, leaves grey and collapse.

**Causes:** Too wet, too humid, poor drainage, fungal infection.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, reduce humidity, increase ventilation.

**Prevention:** Free-draining medium, don't seal humidity cover, ventilate daily, water sparingly.

### Problem 2: Cuttings Dry and Shrivel

**Symptoms:** Leaves brown and crisp, stems desiccate.

**Causes:** Too dry, too much sun, too hot.

**Solution:** Move to shade, maintain light moisture.

**Prevention:** Balance is key - moist but not wet, bright but not scorching.

### Problem 3: Very Slow Rooting

**Symptoms:** No roots after 6+ weeks, cutting still alive.

**Causes:** Wrong wood type, too cold, variety difficult.

**Solution:** Wait longer, try again with better material.

**Prevention:** Choose semi-hardwood carefully, maintain warmth, use hormone.

## Pro Tips

1. **The heel cutting advantage** - Pulling a cutting away from the main stem leaves a "heel" of older tissue. This heels often root better than straight cuts. Try it with lavender.

2. **Don't pick the flowers** - If taking cuttings from plants in bloom, choose non-flowering shoots. If you must use flowering material, remove the flowers and expect lower success.

3. **English lavender is easier** - Lavandula angustifolia varieties root more readily than L. stoechas or L. dentata. Start with English types.

4. **The gravel surface trick** - Top-dress cutting medium with coarse sand or fine gravel. Keeps stems dry at soil level, reducing rot.

5. **Winter is killing time** - Young lavender is susceptible to wet winter conditions. Keep first-year plants protected, ideally under cover with minimal water.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy lavender produces the best cuttings:

- **Pruning:** Annual hard prune after flowering maintains compact growth with good cutting material.
- **Position:** Full sun, excellent drainage, alkaline soil preferred.
- **Age:** Plants become woody with age. Propagate from younger growth near the outside of plants.
- **Replace regularly:** Lavender plants decline after 5-10 years. Propagate replacements before old plants fail.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, firmed but not woody
- Healthy, aromatic grey-green foliage
- Compact, bushy growth
- No flower buds if possible
