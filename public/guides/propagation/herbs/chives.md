## Overview

Chives (Allium schoenoprasum) are among the simplest herbs to propagate. Their clumping, grass-like growth makes them natural candidates for division - literally pulling apart established clumps and replanting the pieces. Success is virtually guaranteed.

Division is so easy and effective that there's rarely reason to use other methods. You can divide a single pot of supermarket chives into multiple plants within minutes, each of which will grow into a full clump within months.

Seed is also straightforward if you need large numbers or don't have existing plants, but division is faster and maintains named varieties exactly.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Multiplying a single pot into many
- Edging herb beds or paths
- Container plantings
- Sharing with other gardeners
- Replacing declining clumps

## Propagation Methods

### Division (Recommended - Easiest)

**Overview:** Simply pull apart established clumps. Near-certain success.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: August to October (early spring) or March to April (autumn)
- Anytime: Really works any time of year; best avoided in high summer

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Lift the clump**
   - Water thoroughly day before
   - Dig around and lift entire clump
   - Or remove from pot

2. **Divide into sections**
   - Pull apart with hands - no tools needed
   - Each section can be small (10-20 bulbs) or larger
   - Roots and tops on each piece

3. **Trim and replant**
   - Trim leaves to 5-10cm
   - Replant at original depth
   - Water well

4. **Establish**
   - Keep moist for 2 weeks
   - New growth quickly follows
   - Ready to harvest in 4-6 weeks

### Seed (Alternative)

**When to use:** When starting from scratch or wanting large numbers.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: August to October (spring)

**Process:**
1. Sow in trays or pots, 5mm deep
2. Keep moist
3. Germination 14-21 days
4. Thin or transplant when 5cm tall
5. Slower than division but still straightforward

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Clump Dies Back to Nothing

**Symptoms:** All foliage dies; no new growth.

**Causes:** Chives are deciduous in cold areas - this is normal dormancy. Or: severe drought, root rot from waterlogging.

**Solution:** Wait for spring regrowth (if dormancy). Ensure drainage isn't causing rot.

**Prevention:** Understand that winter dieback is normal. Ensure good drainage.

### Problem 2: Thin, Weak Growth

**Symptoms:** Few, spindly leaves instead of dense tufts.

**Causes:** Too shady, needs division, poor soil.

**Solution:** Move to more sun, divide congested clumps, feed lightly.

**Prevention:** Full sun to light shade, divide every 2-3 years, annual compost.

### Problem 3: Flowers but Poor Leaf Production

**Symptoms:** Pretty purple flowers but leaves tough and sparse.

**Causes:** Flowering redirects energy from leaf production.

**Solution:** Remove flowers for culinary use, or accept trade-off.

**Prevention:** For kitchen chives, remove flower buds as they appear.

## Pro Tips

1. **Supermarket chives are a bargain** - A single pot of living chives from the supermarket can be divided into 6-10 portions, each growing into a full clump.

2. **Small divisions work** - Even a clump of 10-15 tiny bulbs will grow into a full plant. You can be aggressive with division.

3. **Trim at planting** - Cutting leaves back to 5-10cm at planting reduces water stress and encourages fresh growth.

4. **Garlic chives too** - The same division technique works for garlic chives (A. tuberosum), which have flat leaves and white flowers.

5. **Division rejuvenates** - Congested clumps become less productive. Division every 2-3 years keeps plants vigorous.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy chives produce abundant division material:

- **Position:** Full sun to light shade.
- **Watering:** Regular during active growth.
- **Feeding:** Annual compost or light fertiliser.
- **Flowers:** Remove for leaf production, or leave for ornamental value and seeds.

### Selecting Material

Choose clumps that display:

- Dense, healthy growth
- Good green colour
- No signs of disease
- Active growth (not dormant)
