## Overview

French tarragon (Artemisia dracunculus var. sativa) is the choice culinary herb with its distinctive anise flavour - and it presents a propagation quirk: it rarely sets viable seed. This means vegetative propagation (division or cuttings) is the only practical method.

Division is the easiest and most reliable approach. Established tarragon clumps spread via underground runners, producing multiple crowns that can be separated and replanted. Spring division takes minutes and has high success rates.

Cuttings are possible but trickier than division. The soft stems root reasonably well with proper care, but success rates are lower than division. Use cuttings when you need many plants or can't disturb the parent.

Note: Russian tarragon (A. dracunculus var. inodora) does set seed but lacks the flavour of French tarragon. Always propagate named French tarragon vegetatively.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Expanding your tarragon patch
- Sharing with other cooks
- Insurance against losing plants
- Container growing
- Establishing new herb gardens

## Propagation Methods

### Division (Recommended)

**Overview:** Simple, reliable, and maintains the root system. Nearly always succeeds.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: August to September (early spring)
- Alternative: March to April (autumn)
- Plant condition: As new shoots emerge, or after growth slows

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Prepare for division**
   - Water clump day before
   - Have new planting sites ready
   - Prepare containers if potting up

2. **Lift the clump**
   - Dig around entire clump
   - Lift carefully with roots intact
   - Shake off loose soil

3. **Identify divisions**
   - Look for separate crowns (shoot clusters)
   - Follow runners to individual plants
   - Each division needs shoots and roots

4. **Separate divisions**
   - Pull apart gently where possible
   - Cut runners with knife if needed
   - Keep as much root as possible with each division

5. **Replant immediately**
   - Plant at same depth as before
   - Water well
   - Keep moist until established

6. **Aftercare**
   - Protect from harsh sun for first week
   - Regular water until new growth appears
   - Feed lightly once established

### Softwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** When many plants needed, or when parent can't be disturbed.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October to December (spring to early summer)
- Time of day: Morning
- Wood condition: Soft, new growth

**Process:**
1. Take 8-10cm tip cuttings
2. Remove lower leaves
3. Dip in mild-medium rooting hormone
4. Insert in perlite or propagation mix
5. Cover for humidity
6. Keep moist, warm, bright
7. Roots in 3-4 weeks
8. Pot up carefully

### Root Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** Winter propagation when dormant.

**Process:**
1. Dig up some runners in late winter
2. Cut into 5cm sections
3. Plant horizontally 2cm deep
4. Keep moist
5. Shoots emerge in spring

| **Humidity** | Moderate (for cuttings) |

### Environment Setup

**For divisions:**
Plant directly in garden or pots. Keep watered until established.

**For cuttings:**
Enclosed propagation unit. Well-drained medium. Bright, indirect light. Daily venting.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Divisions Fail to Establish

**Symptoms:** New shoots appear then wilt; plant doesn't grow.

**Causes:** Not enough roots, planted too deep, too dry.

**Solution:** Keep well-watered, shade from hot sun, be patient.

**Prevention:** Ensure adequate roots on each division, plant at original depth, water consistently.

### Problem 2: Cuttings Rot at Base

**Symptoms:** Stem turns black from base, cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, medium stayed too moist.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, reduce watering.

**Prevention:** Well-drained medium, allow to dry slightly between waterings.

### Problem 3: Russian Tarragon Instead of French

**Symptoms:** Plants lack the distinctive anise flavour.

**Causes:** Grew from seed or obtained mislabeled plant.

**Solution:** Start over with genuine French tarragon from a trusted source.

**Prevention:** Always propagate vegetatively from a parent plant with confirmed flavour. Taste before propagating!

## Pro Tips

1. **Taste before you propagate** - French tarragon has a distinctive anise/liquorice flavour. Russian tarragon (often seed-grown) is bland. Always taste the parent plant before propagating.

2. **Spring division is easiest** - Dividing just as new shoots emerge lets you see exactly what you're separating and gives divisions the whole growing season to establish.

3. **Keep some crown attached** - Each division works best when it includes a portion of the crown (where shoots meet roots), not just an isolated runner.

4. **Potted protection** - Tarragon can be borderline hardy in cold areas. Keep divisions in pots for the first winter so they can be protected if needed.

5. **The refresh cycle** - Tarragon clumps benefit from division every 3-4 years even if you don't need more plants. It rejuvenates the patch.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy tarragon produces the best divisions and cuttings:

- **Position:** Full sun to light shade, well-drained soil.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding in spring.
- **Watering:** Regular during growth, reduce in winter.
- **Winter care:** In cold areas, mulch crowns for protection.

### Selecting Material

For division, choose clumps that display:

- Vigorous, aromatic growth
- Multiple healthy crowns
- No signs of rust or disease
- Strong anise flavour (taste it!)
