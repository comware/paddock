## Overview

Willows (Salix species) are legendary for their ease of propagation. They root so readily that a willow stake driven into moist ground will often sprout into a tree. This exceptional rooting ability has made willows the traditional subject for anyone learning to propagate plants from cuttings.

In fact, willow contains natural rooting hormones - primarily indolebutyric acid (IBA) and salicylic acid. This is why "willow water" (water in which willow stems have soaked) has been used for centuries to help root other plants. The willow's own cuttings benefit from these compounds, contributing to their remarkable strike rates.

All willow species propagate easily, from the weeping willows to shrubby forms to pollarded varieties grown for coloured stems. Whether you want a single specimen tree or hundreds of plants for a living fence, willow propagation delivers reliable results.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Garden specimen trees
- Living fences and windbreaks
- Erosion control on waterways
- Basketry and craft willow production
- Pollarded trees for coloured winter stems
- Fast shade establishment

## Propagation Methods

### Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** The classic method using dormant winter wood. Extraordinarily reliable with willow.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: June through August (winter dormancy)
- Time of day: Any time during dormant season
- Plant condition: Fully dormant, no active growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose healthy, one-year-old wood
   - Stems should be pencil thickness to finger thickness
   - Avoid old grey wood or very thin tips
   - Cuttings can be 20-100cm long depending on purpose

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut cleanly with sharp secateurs or pruning saw
   - Make bottom cut just below a bud (angled)
   - Make top cut just above a bud (straight) - this helps you remember which end is up
   - Standard length is 20-30cm for tree production

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Not required for willow
   - Will root without any treatment

4. **Plant cuttings**
   - Insert 2/3 of cutting into ground
   - In rows for production, or final position for trees
   - Firm soil around cutting
   - Space 15-30cm apart in nursery beds

5. **Provide environment**
   - Full sun to part shade
   - Moist soil essential (willows love water)
   - No protection needed - willows are hardy

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Keep soil moist
   - Weed around cuttings
   - No other care needed

7. **Check for success**
   - Buds swell and open in spring
   - Roots develop alongside shoot growth
   - Well-rooted by end of first summer

8. **Transplant or grow on**
   - Can transplant following winter
   - Or leave in place if already in final position

### Softwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** During growing season when hardwood is not available.

**Process:**
1. Take 15-20cm tip cuttings in spring/summer
2. Remove lower leaves
3. Insert in moist potting mix or water
4. Keep humid
5. Roots develop in 2-3 weeks
6. Grow on in pots or transplant

### Large Stakes (For Quick Establishment)

**When to use:** For instant living posts, erosion control, or mature-sized establishment.

**Process:**
1. Cut stakes 1-2m long, 3-10cm diameter
2. Insert 40-50cm into ground
3. In wet season or irrigate well
4. Stakes sprout and root simultaneously
5. Produces established tree faster than small cuttings

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** June-August (full dormancy)
- **Avoid:** Late spring when sap is flowing strongly
- **Signs the plant is ready:** Leaves dropped, buds dormant

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Cold to cool (winter cuttings are hardy) |
| **Humidity** | Not critical for hardwood |
| **Light** | Full sun to part shade |
| **Medium** | Garden soil, wet areas ideal |
| **Container** | Direct planting preferred |

### Environment Setup

**Field cutting:**
Simply push cuttings directly into moist ground. Willows prefer damp to wet conditions. Near water features, ponds, or drainage lines is ideal.

**Container propagation:**
Use deep pots (roots grow fast). Standard potting mix kept moist. Can root in pure water as well.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Dry Out

**Symptoms:** Bark shrivels, buds fail to open, cutting dies.

**Causes:** Cuttings taken from dry wood, insufficient soil moisture, hot exposed position.

**Solution:** Usually too late once dried. Start fresh with new cuttings in moister location.

**Prevention:** Take fresh cuttings, plant in moist soil, plant deeply, irrigate if needed.

### Problem 2: Stakes Push Out of Ground

**Symptoms:** Large stakes lift as roots push against soil.

**Causes:** Normal for very large cuttings in some soils.

**Solution:** Push back down, stake temporarily, or plant smaller cuttings.

**Prevention:** Use smaller cuttings, or angle large stakes slightly.

### Problem 3: Poor Growth After Rooting

**Symptoms:** Cutting roots but remains small and stunted.

**Causes:** Poor soil, competition from grass/weeds, insufficient water.

**Solution:** Weed around cutting, mulch, ensure adequate moisture.

**Prevention:** Site preparation, regular weeding, irrigation if needed.

## Pro Tips

1. **Willow water for other plants** - Soak willow twigs in water for 24-48 hours. Use this water for rooting cuttings of other species - it contains natural rooting hormones.

2. **Living fences** - Plant willow stakes 30cm apart in a line. As they grow, weave the stems together. Creates a living fence that strengthens over time.

3. **The bigger, the faster** - Unlike most cuttings, willow can be propagated from very large pieces. A 2m stake will establish faster than a 20cm cutting, creating a tree-sized plant in one season.

4. **Harvest for crafts** - Willows grown for basketry are cut to the ground (coppiced) annually. Each stump produces dozens of long, straight shoots for weaving.

5. **Coloured stems** - Some willows are grown for their colourful winter stems (coral, gold, black). Coppice annually for the most vivid colours, using the removed stems as cuttings.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Willows are vigorous and largely self-maintaining:

- **Pruning regime:** Coppice or pollard to produce straight cutting material. Heavy pruning tolerated well.
- **Feeding:** Generally not needed - willows thrive in most soils.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for willow sawfly, aphids, rust. Usually not serious.
- **Rotation:** Willows regenerate indefinitely from coppiced stools.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Desired form (weeping, upright, shrubby)
- Good health and vigour
- Correct variety characteristics (bark colour, leaf shape)
- Appropriate size for intended use
