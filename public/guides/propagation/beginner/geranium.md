# Geranium Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Best Method** | Softwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 85-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended but optional |

## Overview

Geraniums (Pelargonium species - not to be confused with hardy Geranium/Cranesbill) are classic cottage garden plants that propagate reliably from stem cuttings. While not quite as foolproof as mint or pothos, geraniums still achieve high success rates and are well within beginner capability.

The technique for geranium propagation is slightly different from most plants - cuttings are allowed to dry for a few hours before planting, and they prefer minimal moisture during rooting. This approach prevents the stem rot that is the main cause of failure. Once you understand this key difference, geranium propagation becomes straightforward.

Geraniums root best from late summer to autumn cuttings, which is also when most gardeners want to preserve their favourites before winter. The same cuttings that root now will flower abundantly next spring and summer.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Preserving favourite varieties over winter
- Filling garden beds for summer colour
- Container and window box plantings
- Gifts and plant swaps
- Building collections of different varieties

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** The standard method using actively growing stem tips. The key difference from most cuttings is allowing them to dry before planting.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer/autumn)
- Time of day: Morning, after dew has dried
- Plant condition: Healthy growth, not flowering heavily

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose healthy, non-flowering stem tips
   - Each cutting should be 8-10cm long
   - Stems should be firm but not woody
   - Avoid stems with flower buds

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut cleanly just below a node
   - Remove all but 2-3 leaves at top
   - Remove any flower buds or flowers
   - Strip any stipules (small leaf-like growths at nodes)

3. **Dry the cutting** (CRITICAL STEP)
   - Leave cuttings in shade for 1-4 hours
   - Cut end should form a dry "skin"
   - This prevents rot when planted
   - Skip this step and failure rates increase significantly

4. **Apply rooting hormone** (recommended)
   - Dip dried end in hormone powder
   - Tap off excess
   - Hormone helpful but not essential

5. **Plant cutting**
   - Use very well-draining mix (50/50 perlite and potting mix)
   - Make hole with pencil or dibber
   - Insert cutting 2-3cm deep
   - Firm gently - don't pack tightly

6. **Provide environment**
   - Bright light (some direct sun fine)
   - Normal room temperature or warmer
   - NO humidity dome - geraniums hate humidity
   - Good air circulation important

7. **Ongoing care**
   - Water sparingly - only when mix is nearly dry
   - Do not mist or cover
   - Avoid overwatering (main cause of failure)

8. **Check for roots**
   - Roots develop in 2-4 weeks
   - New growth at tip indicates rooting
   - Gently tug to test for resistance

9. **Pot up**
   - When roots fill container
   - Use well-draining potting mix
   - Move to final pot or overwinter position

### Semi-hardwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** When plants are less actively growing, or when softer growth isn't available.

**Process:**
Same as above but:
- Take slightly firmer, more mature stems
- Rooting may take 3-5 weeks instead of 2-4
- More tolerant of handling

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** Late February through April
- **Avoid:** Mid-summer heat, winter cold
- **Signs the plant is ready:** Healthy green growth, plants not heat-stressed

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 15-24°C |
| **Humidity** | LOW - no covering needed |
| **Light** | Bright, some direct sun OK |
| **Medium** | Very well-draining (perlite-heavy) |
| **Container** | Small pots or trays with excellent drainage |

### Environment Setup

**Important: No humidity dome**
Unlike most cuttings, geraniums do NOT want high humidity. This is the most common mistake. Leave uncovered with good air circulation.

**Simple setup:**
- Small pots or cell trays
- Mix of 50% potting mix + 50% perlite
- Bright windowsill or greenhouse shelf
- Water sparingly

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Stem Rots at Base (Black Leg)

**Symptoms:** Cutting turns black and mushy at soil level, collapses.

**Causes:** Cutting not dried before planting, too much moisture, poor drainage, high humidity.

**Solution:** Discard affected cuttings. Cannot be saved once rot begins.

**Prevention:** Always dry cuttings for 1-4 hours before planting. Use very well-draining mix. Water sparingly. Never cover with plastic.

### Problem 2: Wilting Despite Moist Soil

**Symptoms:** Cutting wilts and doesn't recover even when watered.

**Causes:** Root rot from overwatering, fungal infection.

**Solution:** Allow to dry out completely. May recover if caught early. Often too late.

**Prevention:** Let medium dry between waterings. Better too dry than too wet.

### Problem 3: No Root Development

**Symptoms:** Cutting stays alive but doesn't root after 4+ weeks.

**Causes:** Too cold, wrong part of plant, old woody growth.

**Solution:** Increase warmth, try fresh cuttings from younger growth.

**Prevention:** Use actively growing (but not too soft) stems, maintain warmth.

## Pro Tips

1. **The drying step is non-negotiable** - This is the single most important factor in geranium propagation success. Those 1-4 hours of drying prevent the rot that kills most failed cuttings.

2. **Remove flowers ruthlessly** - Any cutting with a flower bud puts energy into blooming rather than rooting. Remove all buds and flowers from both cuttings and mother plants you're taking from.

3. **Late summer is insurance** - Take cuttings in February-March as insurance against winter losses. Even if parent plants survive, you'll have vigorous young plants for spring.

4. **Overwinter dry** - Rooted cuttings should be kept on the dry side over winter. Water sparingly until spring growth begins.

5. **Scented-leaf varieties are easier** - Scented pelargoniums (lemon, rose, peppermint) often root more readily than zonals (common bedding geraniums).

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Keep mother plants healthy for the best cutting material:

- **Pruning regime:** Pinch regularly to encourage bushy growth. Don't let plants become leggy.
- **Feeding:** Moderate feeding during growing season. Too much nitrogen causes soft, rot-prone growth.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for rust, caterpillars, and whitefly. Treat promptly.
- **Rotation:** Replace plants every 3-4 years as they become woody.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Vigorous, healthy growth
- Good flower colour and form
- Compact, bushy habit
- No disease symptoms
