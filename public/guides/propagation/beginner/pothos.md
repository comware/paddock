# Pothos Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Best Method** | Stem cuttings in water |
| **Alternative Methods** | Stem cuttings in soil |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer (year-round indoors) |
| **Success Rate** | 90-98% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Pothos (Epipremnum aureum) is one of the easiest houseplants to propagate, making it a perfect starting point for beginners. This tropical vine roots readily in water, allowing you to watch the entire process unfold. A single healthy pothos can provide countless cuttings over its lifetime, making it ideal for filling your home with greenery or sharing with friends.

The key to pothos propagation lies in its nodes - the small bumps along the stem where leaves emerge. These nodes contain dormant root cells that activate when placed in water or moist medium. Each node can produce a new plant, so a long vine can yield many cuttings.

All pothos varieties propagate equally well, whether you're working with golden pothos, marble queen, neon, or any other cultivar. Variegated varieties will maintain their patterns when propagated from cuttings, unlike seed-grown plants.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Filling out sparse parent plants (root and replant in same pot)
- Creating new plants for other rooms
- Sharing with friends and family
- Building a collection of different varieties
- Trading with other plant enthusiasts

## Propagation Methods

### Stem Cuttings in Water (Recommended)

**Overview:** Water propagation is ideal for pothos because you can observe root development, and the plant roots extremely reliably in water alone.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: September through March (but works year-round indoors)
- Time of day: Any time
- Plant condition: Healthy vines with mature leaves

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose healthy stems with at least 2-3 nodes
   - Look for vines that aren't too old and woody
   - Each cutting should be 10-15cm long
   - Ensure at least one node will be submerged

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut just below a node using clean scissors
   - Remove leaves from the bottom 1-2 nodes
   - Leave 2-3 leaves at the top
   - Can take multiple cuttings from a single vine

3. **Place in water**
   - Use a clean glass jar or vase
   - Fill with room temperature water
   - Submerge at least one node fully
   - Keep remaining leaves above water

4. **Provide environment**
   - Bright indirect light
   - Room temperature (18-27°C)
   - Avoid direct sunlight on the jar (algae growth)

5. **Ongoing care**
   - Change water weekly (or when cloudy)
   - Top up as water evaporates
   - Remove any yellowing leaves

6. **Check for roots**
   - Roots usually appear within 1-2 weeks
   - Wait until roots are 5-8cm long
   - Multiple roots per cutting is ideal

7. **Pot up**
   - Plant in well-draining potting mix
   - Standard indoor plant mix works well
   - Water thoroughly after planting
   - Keep moist for first 2 weeks

### Stem Cuttings in Soil (Alternative)

**When to use:** When you prefer not to transfer water-rooted cuttings, or when rooting many cuttings at once.

**Process:**
1. Take cuttings as described above
2. Dip cut end in water (no hormone needed)
3. Insert into moist potting mix with node buried
4. Cover with plastic bag or place in propagator
5. Keep warm and maintain humidity
6. Roots develop in 3-4 weeks
7. Tug gently to test for root development

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** Spring through early autumn
- **Avoid:** No strict avoidance, but winter is slowest
- **Signs the plant is ready:** Healthy new growth, no stress

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 18-27°C |
| **Humidity** | Ambient (higher speeds rooting) |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Water or well-draining potting mix |
| **Container** | Glass jar (water) or small pot (soil) |

### Environment Setup

**Water propagation:**
Any clear glass container works. A narrow-necked vase keeps cuttings upright. Place in bright spot away from direct sun. Change water weekly.

**Soil propagation:**
Use a small pot with drainage. Standard potting mix with added perlite works well. Cover with clear plastic bag to maintain humidity. Vent daily to prevent mould.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Yellow Leaves on Cutting

**Symptoms:** Lower leaves yellow and may drop off while rooting.

**Causes:** Normal shedding as plant redirects energy to rooting, or too many leaves left on cutting.

**Solution:** Remove yellow leaves promptly. One or two leaves dropping is normal and not cause for concern.

**Prevention:** Leave only 2-3 healthy leaves per cutting. The plant can't support many leaves without roots.

### Problem 2: Root Rot in Water

**Symptoms:** Roots turn brown and mushy, stem becomes soft at water level.

**Causes:** Water not changed often enough, water level too high, debris in water.

**Solution:** Trim affected roots, let stem dry briefly, restart in fresh water. May not be salvageable if rot is extensive.

**Prevention:** Change water weekly, keep container clean, don't overfill.

### Problem 3: Failure to Root

**Symptoms:** Cutting stays alive but produces no roots after 3-4 weeks.

**Causes:** No node submerged, cutting from very old woody growth, too cold.

**Solution:** Ensure a node is in the water, try a cutting from younger growth, move to warmer location.

**Prevention:** Always include at least one node in water, select healthy non-woody stems.

## Pro Tips

1. **One node is enough** - A single node with one leaf can produce a new plant. This maximises the number of cuttings from one vine.

2. **Propagate in water, grow in water** - Pothos can live permanently in water. Just add a drop of liquid fertiliser monthly once established.

3. **Root and return** - Take cuttings from leggy vines, root them, and replant in the same pot to make the parent plant fuller.

4. **Watch for aerial roots** - Cuttings with existing aerial roots (the brown nubs on stems) root even faster.

5. **Group cuttings together** - When potting up, put 3-5 rooted cuttings in one pot for an instantly full plant.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Pothos are low-maintenance but benefit from regular care:

- **Pruning regime:** Trim long vines regularly. This encourages bushier growth and provides propagation material.
- **Feeding:** Monthly with balanced liquid fertiliser during growing season.
- **Health monitoring:** Check for mealybugs in leaf axils and along stems.
- **Rotation:** Plants can live indefinitely with proper care. Refresh by repotting every 2-3 years.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Healthy, vibrant foliage
- Good variegation (if variety is variegated)
- No signs of pests or disease
- Active new growth
