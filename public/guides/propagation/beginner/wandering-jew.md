# Wandering Jew Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|

| **Alternative Methods** | Stem cuttings in soil, layering |

| **Success Rate** | 95-100% typical |

## Overview

Wandering Jew (Tradescantia zebrina, T. pallida, and related species) is among the easiest of all plants to propagate. The stems root so readily that broken pieces left on moist soil will often establish themselves without any help. This remarkable vigour makes Tradescantia perfect for beginners wanting a guaranteed success.

The various species commonly called "Wandering Jew" or "Inch Plant" include purple-leaved, striped, and green varieties. All propagate identically and maintain their distinctive colours and patterns when grown from cuttings. The trailing habit of these plants means they produce abundant stems for propagation.

These plants are often used as groundcover outdoors in frost-free areas, or as hanging basket specimens. Their ability to root at every node means a single plant can quickly fill a pot, making it economical to create lush displays.

*Note: Some gardeners now prefer the names "Wandering Dude" or "Inch Plant" due to concerns about the traditional common name.*

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Hanging baskets and trailing displays
- Groundcover in frost-free gardens
- Quick-fill containers
- Easy gifts for new gardeners
- Teaching propagation to children

## Propagation Methods

### Stem Cuttings in Water (Recommended)

**Overview:** Water propagation is foolproof with Tradescantia. Roots appear within a week, and cuttings rarely fail.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: Any time (year-round indoors)
- Time of day: Any time
- Plant condition: Any healthy, non-damaged stems

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose any healthy stem sections
   - Each cutting should have 2-4 nodes
   - Length of 8-15cm is ideal
   - Even broken pieces work well

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut or snap stem below a node
   - Remove leaves from bottom 1-2 nodes
   - Leave 3-4 leaves at the top
   - No special preparation needed

3. **Place in water**
   - Use any clean container
   - Fill with room temperature water
   - Submerge at least one node
   - Keep leaves above water

4. **Provide environment**
   - Any light from low to bright
   - Room temperature
   - No special care needed

5. **Ongoing care**
   - Change water weekly
   - Otherwise, leave alone
   - Watch roots develop

6. **Check for roots**
   - Roots appear within 5-10 days
   - Can pot up when roots are 2-3cm
   - Longer roots establish faster

7. **Pot up**
   - Use standard potting mix
   - Plant at same depth
   - Water well
   - Keep moist initially

### Stem Cuttings in Soil (Alternative)

**When to use:** When propagating many cuttings at once, or for outdoor groundcover establishment.

**Process:**
1. Take cuttings as above
2. Push directly into moist potting mix
3. Bury 1-2 nodes
4. Keep moist
5. Roots develop in 1-2 weeks
6. New growth confirms success

This is even easier than water propagation when you don't need to monitor root development.

### Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** For the most effortless propagation method imaginable.

**Process:**
1. Place trailing stems in contact with soil
2. Pin down with wire or small stone
3. Keep soil moist
4. Roots develop at nodes touching soil
5. Sever from parent once rooted
6. Transplant or leave in place

This mimics how Tradescantia spreads naturally in the garden.

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 15-28°C |
| **Humidity** | Ambient (no special needs) |
| **Light** | Any (low to bright) |
| **Medium** | Water or any potting mix |
| **Container** | Any container |

### Environment Setup

**Water propagation:**
Literally any container with water works. Tradescantia is not fussy. A jam jar on a windowsill is perfect.

**Soil propagation:**
Any potting mix, garden soil, or even sand will work. Keep moist. No humidity dome needed.

**Layering:**
Just ensure soil contact and moisture. The plant does the rest.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Leggy, Sparse Growth After Potting

**Symptoms:** Rooted cutting grows but produces long stems with few leaves.

**Causes:** Insufficient light, not pinching to encourage branching.

**Solution:** Move to brighter location, pinch growing tips to encourage branching.

**Prevention:** Provide bright indirect light, pinch early and often.

### Problem 2: Loss of Variegation or Colour

**Symptoms:** Purple or striped leaves become green, patterns fade.

**Causes:** Insufficient light, over-fertilising.

**Solution:** Increase light exposure. Colour will return within weeks.

**Prevention:** Bright light maintains best colour. Go easy on fertiliser.

### Problem 3: Root Rot in Water

**Symptoms:** Roots turn brown and slimy, stem base becomes mushy.

**Causes:** Water not changed, debris in water, too warm.

**Solution:** Trim damaged parts, start fresh in clean water.

**Prevention:** Change water weekly, keep container clean.

## Pro Tips

1. **Plant multiple cuttings together** - Put 5-10 cuttings in one pot for an instantly full plant. They root and grow so fast you won't be waiting long.

2. **Pinch ruthlessly** - Tradescantia becomes leggy without regular pinching. Every pinched tip becomes another cutting. Pinch, propagate, repeat.

3. **The broken bits method** - When trimming your plant, simply drop the cuttings onto the soil surface of the pot. Many will root without any further action.

4. **Rejuvenate annually** - These plants get scraggly with age. Take fresh cuttings yearly and start new pots rather than trying to rehabilitate old, leggy plants.

5. **Use as living mulch** - In frost-free gardens, Tradescantia makes excellent groundcover. It roots wherever it touches soil, suppressing weeds and retaining moisture.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Tradescantia are low-maintenance but benefit from attention:

- **Pruning regime:** Pinch and trim regularly to maintain bushiness. Use trimmings for propagation.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding monthly during growing season. Heavy feeding causes leggy growth.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for spider mites in dry conditions. Increase humidity if needed.
- **Rotation:** Plants decline after 1-2 years. Propagate fresh plants rather than trying to rejuvenate old ones.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Healthy, colourful foliage
- Strong patterns and markings
- Compact, bushy growth
- No pests or disease
