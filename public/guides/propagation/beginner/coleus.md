# Coleus Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Beginner |
| **Best Method** | Softwood cuttings in water |
| **Alternative Methods** | Softwood cuttings in soil |
| **Time to Root** | 1-2 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 90-98% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Coleus (Plectranthus scutellarioides, formerly Solenostemon) is one of the most rewarding plants for beginner propagators. These vibrant foliage plants root incredibly fast, often producing visible roots within a week. The colourful leaves maintain their patterns when propagated from cuttings, making it easy to share your favourite varieties with friends.

The ease of coleus propagation is legendary among gardeners. A single plant can provide dozens of cuttings throughout the growing season, each one capable of becoming a full-sized plant within a couple of months. This makes coleus ideal for mass planting, market sales, or simply building a collection of different varieties.

Coleus are technically tender perennials but are often treated as annuals. Taking cuttings in autumn allows you to preserve your favourite plants over winter indoors, then multiply them again in spring for the next season.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Mass plantings for summer colour
- Bedding displays
- Container arrangements
- Overwintering favourite varieties
- Trading and sharing with other gardeners

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings in Water (Recommended)

**Overview:** Water propagation is perfect for coleus - roots develop quickly and visibly, providing instant feedback and high success rates.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: September through April
- Time of day: Morning, when stems are turgid
- Plant condition: Healthy, actively growing stems without flowers

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose healthy, non-flowering stem tips
   - Each cutting should be 8-12cm long
   - Look for thick, sturdy stems rather than thin, weak ones
   - Avoid stems that are flowering or about to flower

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut just below a node with clean scissors
   - Remove all leaves from the bottom half
   - Leave 4-6 leaves at the top
   - Pinch off any flower buds

3. **Place in water**
   - Use a clean glass jar or container
   - Fill with room-temperature water
   - Submerge at least 2 nodes
   - Keep leaves above water line

4. **Provide environment**
   - Bright indirect light (some direct morning sun is fine)
   - Room temperature (18-25°C)
   - Avoid cold windowsills

5. **Ongoing care**
   - Change water every 2-3 days
   - Remove any leaves that fall into water
   - Watch for root development

6. **Check for roots**
   - Roots typically appear within 5-10 days
   - Wait until roots are 3-5cm long
   - Multiple roots per cutting is ideal

7. **Pot up**
   - Use well-draining potting mix
   - Plant at same depth as in water
   - Water thoroughly
   - Keep moist for first week

### Softwood Cuttings in Soil (Alternative)

**When to use:** When propagating many cuttings at once, or when you prefer not to transition from water to soil.

**Process:**
1. Prepare cuttings as above
2. Dip cut end in water (hormone not needed but can speed rooting)
3. Insert into moist potting mix or propagation mix
4. Firm gently around cutting
5. Keep humid with plastic cover or propagator
6. Maintain moist soil
7. Roots develop in 2-3 weeks
8. New growth signals establishment

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best | Active growth, fast rooting |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Best | Fastest rooting, don't let wilt |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Good | Take cuttings to overwinter |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Possible | Slower, needs warmth indoors |

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** October through March (active growth period)
- **Avoid:** Taking cuttings from plants in full flower (pinch flowers first)
- **Signs the plant is ready:** Healthy new growth, no pest problems

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 18-27°C |
| **Humidity** | Moderate (higher in soil propagation) |
| **Light** | Bright indirect to part sun |
| **Medium** | Water or moist potting mix |
| **Container** | Glass jar or small pots |

### Environment Setup

**Water propagation:**
Any clean glass container works. Place in bright location with some indirect light. The colourful leaves make an attractive display while rooting. Change water regularly.

**Soil propagation:**
Use seed-raising mix or propagation blend. Keep consistently moist. Cover with plastic or use a propagator to maintain humidity. Vent daily to prevent mould.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cutting Wilts and Collapses

**Symptoms:** Cutting droops immediately after taking, leaves go limp.

**Causes:** Cutting taken in heat of day, too much leaf left on, stem damage.

**Solution:** If caught quickly, trim base, remove more leaves, place in water in shade. May recover.

**Prevention:** Take cuttings in morning, remove adequate leaves, keep in shade until in water.

### Problem 2: Stem Rots at Water Line

**Symptoms:** Stem becomes mushy and brown where it meets water, foul smell.

**Causes:** Water not changed often enough, too many leaves in water, poor water quality.

**Solution:** Trim rot, let dry briefly, restart in fresh water.

**Prevention:** Change water every 2 days, keep leaves above water, use clean containers.

### Problem 3: Leaves Lose Colour

**Symptoms:** Bright colours fade, leaves become greener than parent.

**Causes:** Insufficient light, over-fertilising after rooting.

**Solution:** Move to brighter location. Colour should intensify with more light.

**Prevention:** Provide bright indirect light. Coleus colour is light-dependent.

## Pro Tips

1. **Pinch to promote bushiness** - Pinch the growing tip of rooted cuttings to encourage branching. Each pinched tip can become another cutting.

2. **Remove flowers immediately** - Coleus that are allowed to flower put energy into seed production rather than growth. Pinch flowers as soon as they appear.

3. **Take insurance cuttings in autumn** - Before frost threatens, take cuttings of your favourite varieties to root indoors. These overwinter easily on a bright windowsill.

4. **Root in the colour** - Place cuttings where they get good light while rooting. This maintains the intense foliage colour even during propagation.

5. **Mass production is easy** - One healthy coleus can yield 10-20 cuttings. Root in bulk to create impressive bedding displays.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Coleus respond well to regular maintenance:

- **Pruning regime:** Pinch regularly to keep bushy. Each pinch yields potential cuttings. Remove all flower spikes.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding monthly during growing season. Too much nitrogen reduces colour intensity.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for mealybugs, aphids, and spider mites. Treat promptly.
- **Rotation:** Coleus become leggy with age. Propagate fresh stock annually and discard old plants.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Vibrant, healthy foliage
- Strong colour patterns
- Compact, bushy growth
- No signs of pests or disease
