## Overview

Sweet potatoes (Ipomoea batatas) are propagated vegetatively from "slips" - the sprouts that grow from tubers suspended in water or planted in soil. This makes them one of the most fascinating plants for beginners to propagate, as you can watch the entire process unfold from a grocery store sweet potato to a productive garden plant.

Unlike regular potatoes which are grown from cut tuber pieces, sweet potatoes are grown from the young shoots (slips) that emerge from the tuber. Each tuber can produce 15-50 slips, and each slip becomes a new plant capable of producing 1-3 kg of tubers. This makes sweet potato propagation extremely productive.

Sweet potato slips root easily in water and transplant without issue. The process is educational and rewarding, making it perfect for gardeners of all ages.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Vegetable garden production
- Ornamental edible landscaping (attractive foliage)
- Educational projects with children
- Container growing on patios
- Food security gardening

## Propagation Methods

### Growing Slips from Tubers (Recommended)

**Overview:** The traditional method - suspend a sweet potato in water and harvest the sprouts (slips) that grow from it.

**When to start:**
- Best months: August-September (late winter/early spring)
- Start 6-8 weeks before last frost
- Allows slips to be ready for spring planting

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select tuber**
   - Choose a healthy, organic sweet potato
   - Avoid supermarket potatoes treated with sprout inhibitors
   - Medium-sized tubers are ideal
   - No signs of damage or rot

2. **Set up water sprouting**
   - Insert toothpicks around tuber's middle
   - Suspend in jar with bottom third in water
   - Place in warm location (20-25°C)
   - Bright indirect light

3. **Wait for sprouts**
   - Change water weekly
   - Sprouts emerge in 1-4 weeks
   - Keep water level consistent
   - Warmth speeds sprouting

4. **Grow slips**
   - Allow sprouts to reach 15-20cm tall
   - Each sprout becomes one slip
   - Leaves will develop along the stem

5. **Harvest slips**
   - Twist or cut slips from tuber when 15-20cm
   - Each slip should have several leaves
   - One tuber produces many slips over several weeks

6. **Root slips**
   - Place slip base in water
   - Roots develop in 5-10 days
   - Wait for roots 5cm+ before planting

7. **Plant out**
   - After last frost, when soil is warm
   - Plant in garden or large container
   - Bury 2-3 nodes deep
   - Water well

### Soil Sprouting Method (Alternative)

**When to use:** When you want more slips faster, or when water sprouting is slow.

**Process:**
1. Lay tuber horizontally in tray of moist potting mix
2. Cover halfway with mix
3. Keep warm (20-25°C) and moist
4. Sprouts emerge in 2-4 weeks
5. Harvest slips when 15-20cm tall
6. Root in water before planting

### Vine Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** During growing season, to increase plantings from existing plants.

**Process:**
1. Take 20-30cm vine tip cuttings
2. Remove lower leaves
3. Root in water (7-10 days) or plant directly
4. Plant out when roots established
5. Works well in tropics where plants grow year-round

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best | Prime planting time after last frost |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Good | Can still plant early summer |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Harvest time | Dig tubers before frost |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Start slips | Begin sprouting late winter |

### Optimal Timing

- **Start sprouting tubers:** August-September (6-8 weeks before planting)
- **Plant slips outdoors:** October-November (after last frost)
- **Avoid:** Planting in cold soil (below 15°C)
- **Signs to plant:** Soil warm to touch, frost danger passed

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 20-30°C (warm is essential) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate |
| **Light** | Bright indirect for sprouting |
| **Medium** | Water for rooting slips |
| **Container** | Jar for tuber, smaller container for slips |

### Environment Setup

**Tuber sprouting:**
Use a jar or glass that supports the tuber with the bottom in water. A warm, bright spot like a sunny windowsill works well. Many gardeners use their kitchen.

**Slip rooting:**
Small jars or containers work well for rooting individual slips. Keep in bright location. Change water every few days.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Tuber Won't Sprout

**Symptoms:** Sweet potato sits in water for weeks with no growth.

**Causes:** Supermarket potato treated with sprout inhibitor, too cold, tuber too old.

**Solution:** Try a different tuber (organic or farmers market), increase temperature to 25°C+, be patient (can take 4+ weeks).

**Prevention:** Source organic or locally-grown tubers known not to be treated.

### Problem 2: Tuber Rots in Water

**Symptoms:** Tuber becomes soft, mushy, smelly while waiting for sprouts.

**Causes:** Water not changed, tuber had existing damage, too much tuber submerged.

**Solution:** Discard and start fresh with new tuber.

**Prevention:** Change water weekly, use healthy tubers, only submerge bottom third.

### Problem 3: Slips Die After Planting

**Symptoms:** Transplanted slips wilt and fail despite watering.

**Causes:** Cold soil, insufficient roots, sun shock, wind damage.

**Solution:** Provide shade cloth, water frequently, protect from wind.

**Prevention:** Wait until soil is warm (15°C+), ensure good root development, harden off before planting, plant on overcast day.

## Pro Tips

1. **Choose the right tuber** - Organic or farmers market sweet potatoes work best. Supermarket potatoes are often treated with sprout inhibitors. One healthy tuber can produce 20+ slips.

2. **Bottom heat speeds sprouting** - Place the jar on top of a refrigerator, heat mat, or other warm surface. Sweet potatoes need warmth (20°C+) to sprout.

3. **Stagger your harvest** - The tuber continues producing slips over several weeks. Harvest the largest ones first while smaller ones continue growing.

4. **Plant deep** - Bury slips up to the lowest leaves. Roots form along the buried stem, creating a larger root system for better tuber production.

5. **Ornamental varieties are edible too** - While bred for foliage, ornamental sweet potato vines produce edible tubers. They're smaller but still tasty.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Tubers

For annual slip production:

- **Storage:** Keep tubers in cool (12-15°C), dark, dry conditions over winter
- **Selection:** Save best-shaped, disease-free tubers from harvest
- **Rotation:** Use fresh tubers annually for strongest slips

### Selecting Tubers for Slip Production

Choose tubers that display:

- Firm, healthy flesh
- No signs of rot or disease
- Medium size (too large = fewer slips per kg)
- True-to-type characteristics
