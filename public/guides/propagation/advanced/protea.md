# Protea Propagation

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed |
| **Time to Root** | 8-16 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late winter to early spring |
| **Success Rate** | 30-60% at home |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Essential |

## Overview

Protea is one of the hardest things in this library to strike, and it is worth being honest about the numbers before you start. Research on *Protea obtusifolia* reached 75% rooting in ten weeks — with heated benches, controlled misting and a wounded base. At home, without that equipment, half a batch is a good result and a third is normal.

Three things separate a batch that works from one that does not, and none of them are optional:

- **Timing.** Late winter to early spring, August into September, once flowering has finished and the new growth has begun to firm. This is the opposite of the late-summer window most shrubs in this library want.
- **Bottom heat.** Proteaceae root slowly and warm roots are what shortens it. Around 22-24°C at the base, with the foliage kept cooler.
- **Wounding.** A shallow slice up one side of the base, 2-3cm, exposes more cambium and measurably improves both speed and percentage.

Protea also hates phosphorus. That matters at potting-on rather than at rooting, but a standard potting mix or a general fertiliser will kill a rooted cutting that survived everything else — use a low-phosphorus native mix.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Reproducing a named cultivar, which seed will not do
- Keeping a plant going that suits your soil and aspect, when many do not
- Cut-flower rows, where uniformity matters
- Replacing losses, which in protea are a normal part of growing them

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** The only method that reproduces a named plant. Slow, demanding, and worth it if you want more of a particular protea.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: August to September (late winter to early spring)
- Wood condition: This season's growth, firming but not hard. Not soft green tips, not old woody stems.

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Semi-hardwood, current season, from a healthy vigorous plant
   - 10-15cm lengths
   - Take them early in the morning and keep them cool and damp until prepared

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut cleanly below a node
   - Strip the lower half to two-thirds of leaves — do not tear them off, cut them
   - Keep 4-6 leaves at the top

3. **Wound the base**
   - A shallow slice 2-3cm up one side, removing a sliver of bark
   - Not decorative. This is one of the main levers on success rate.

4. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - IBA at semi-hardwood strength, roughly 3000-8000 ppm
   - A five-second dip at around 4000 ppm is a commonly cited starting point
   - Essential, not optional

5. **Insert cuttings**
   - Very free-draining, low-fertility mix — coarse sand and perlite, or a native propagating mix
   - Insert to about a third of their length
   - Space so no leaf touches another

6. **Provide environment**
   - **Bottom heat at 22-24°C**, foliage kept cooler
   - High humidity but excellent airflow — misting is better than a sealed cover
   - Bright, indirect light

7. **Ongoing care** (8-16 weeks)
   - Never let the mix stay wet; never let it dry out completely
   - Remove any dropped leaf or dead cutting the day you see it — botrytis moves through a tray fast
   - Do not tug to test before eight weeks

8. **Pot on carefully**
   - **Low-phosphorus native mix only**
   - Roots are brittle; disturb as little as possible
   - Harden off slowly

### Seed

**When to use:** For species rather than cultivars, or when cuttings have failed repeatedly.

Seed of many proteas germinates better after smoke treatment or with fluctuating day-night temperatures. It will not reproduce a named cultivar, and plants take considerably longer to flower, but germination is often less frustrating than striking cuttings.

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | **22-24°C at the base**, cooler foliage |
| **Humidity** | High, with strong airflow |
| **Light** | Bright, indirect |
| **Medium** | Coarse, free-draining, low fertility |
| **Bottom heat** | **Essential** |

### Environment Setup

This is the one plant here that genuinely wants equipment. A heat mat with a thermostat, an open tray rather than a sealed propagator, and somewhere air moves. A closed bag on a windowsill will produce botrytis, not roots.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Grey Mould Through the Tray

**Symptoms:** Fuzzy grey growth on leaves and stems; cuttings collapse in sequence.

**Causes:** Botrytis — still air, high humidity, dropped leaves left in place.

**Solution:** Remove every affected cutting immediately. Increase airflow.

**Prevention:** Space cuttings apart, cut rather than tear leaves off, clear debris daily, mist rather than seal.

### Problem 2: Cuttings Firm but Rootless After Four Months

**Symptoms:** Green, alive, no roots.

**Causes:** No bottom heat, or wood too mature.

**Solution:** Add heat and wait — some root in the following season.

**Prevention:** Bottom heat from the start, correct semi-hardwood stage, wound the base.

### Problem 3: Rooted Cutting Died After Potting On

**Symptoms:** Thrives in the tray, declines within weeks of potting.

**Causes:** Phosphorus toxicity from standard potting mix or fertiliser, or root disturbance.

**Solution:** None once the damage is done.

**Prevention:** Low-phosphorus native mix. Never a general-purpose fertiliser. Handle the root ball as little as possible.

## Pro Tips

1. **Take three times what you need** — A 30-50% strike is normal at home. Plan the batch around that, not around hope.

2. **Wound the base** — Of everything you can do without buying equipment, this has the clearest effect.

3. **Heat the bottom, cool the top** — Warm roots and cooler foliage is the combination. Warming the whole plant just pushes top growth it cannot support.

4. **Airflow beats humidity** — More protea batches die of botrytis than of drying out. Mist; do not seal.

5. **Never feed it phosphorus** — Not at rooting, not at potting, not ever. This kills more surviving cuttings than the rooting stage does.

6. **Cultivars need cuttings** — Seed gives you a protea, not *that* protea. If the plant has a cultivar name, cuttings are the only route.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

- **Position:** Full sun, sharp drainage, air movement. They fail in heavy wet soil.
- **Pruning:** Prune after flowering — this produces the flush you will take cuttings from next year.
- **Feeding:** Little, and **never phosphorus**. A low-phosphorus native fertiliser at most.
- **Watering:** Deep and infrequent once established. Wet feet kill them.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, firming but still flexible
- Healthy, undamaged, typical foliage
- No flower buds
- Vigorous parent — cuttings from a struggling plant strike poorly
