# Avocado Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Best Method** | Grafting (cleft or wedge graft) |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed (rootstock only), air layering |
| **Time to Root** | N/A (grafting), 3-8 weeks (seed) |
| **Best Season** | Spring to early summer (grafting) |
| **Success Rate** | 70-90% (grafting), varies (cuttings - rarely attempted) |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not typically used |

## Overview

Avocado propagation is unusual because although seeds germinate readily (anyone can grow an avocado pit!), producing a named variety that will fruit reliably requires grafting. Seed-grown trees take 7-15 years to fruit and produce unpredictable quality - usually inferior to the parent.

This disconnect between easy seed growth and complex vegetative propagation is what makes avocados interesting. Commercial orchards use grafted trees exclusively, combining proven varieties with vigorous seedling rootstock.

Avocados are also challenging because they don't root reliably from cuttings. The standard propagation route is: grow seedling rootstock → graft desired variety. Air layering can work but is less common.

For home gardeners, grafting avocados follows similar principles to citrus, though the techniques differ in detail. Success requires attention to timing, cleanliness, and aftercare.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating productive trees of named varieties
- Producing rootstock from seeds
- Understanding commercial avocado production
- Top-working existing trees to better varieties
- Preserving varieties from mature trees

## Propagation Methods

### Growing Rootstock from Seed (First Step)

**Overview:** Essential first step - you need something to graft onto.

**When to plant:**
- Best months: Year-round in frost-free areas
- Seeds are recalcitrant (don't store well) - plant fresh

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Obtain fresh seed**
   - Use seed from healthy, mature fruit
   - Seed must be fresh - doesn't store
   - Remove from fruit, wash clean
   - Don't let dry out

2. **Plant seed**
   - Plant 2-3cm deep, pointed end up
   - Large pot or nursery bag (at least 15L)
   - Well-drained potting mix
   - Keep moist, not waterlogged

3. **Alternative water method**
   - Pierce seed with three toothpicks
   - Suspend over water, base just touching
   - Change water weekly
   - Pot up once roots well-developed

4. **Growing on**
   - Germination 3-8 weeks
   - Single stem develops
   - Grow until pencil-thick (12-18 months)
   - Ready for grafting

### Cleft Grafting (Recommended)

**Overview:** Simple, reliable method for avocados. Good for beginners to grafting.

**When to graft:**
- Best months: September to December (spring to early summer)
- Plant condition: Rootstock actively growing, sap flowing
- Scion: Dormant or just breaking dormancy

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Prepare rootstock**
   - Healthy seedling, pencil-thick or larger
   - Cut off top at 15-20cm above soil
   - Straight, clean cut

2. **Split rootstock**
   - Make vertical cut down centre of stem
   - Split 3-4cm deep
   - Use grafting knife, keep open with chisel or screwdriver

3. **Prepare scion**
   - Select healthy terminal shoot with plump buds
   - 3-4 nodes, similar diameter to rootstock
   - Cut long wedge shape at base (both sides)
   - Wedge should match depth of split

4. **Insert scion**
   - Slide wedge into split
   - Align cambium on at least one side
   - Scion bark should align with rootstock bark

5. **Secure the graft**
   - Release split - pressure holds scion
   - Wrap firmly with grafting tape or parafilm
   - Cover all exposed cuts

6. **Protect the graft**
   - Cover with plastic bag (humidity tent)
   - Support bag away from scion
   - Or use grafting tube/sleeve
   - Shade from direct sun

7. **Aftercare**
   - Maintain humidity for 2-4 weeks
   - Gradually increase ventilation
   - Remove cover once scion growing strongly
   - Remove rootstock shoots below graft

### Wedge Grafting (Alternative)

**When to use:** Similar to cleft, slightly different technique.

**Process:**
1. Prepare rootstock with angled cuts (notch)
2. Match with wedge-shaped scion
3. Insert, align cambium
4. Wrap and protect as above

### Chip Budding (Alternative)

**When to use:** Efficient use of scion material.

**Process:**
1. Remove chip from rootstock (matching notch)
2. Cut identical chip with bud from scion
3. Fit precisely, tape firmly
4. Works when rootstock actively growing

### Approach Grafting (Alternative)

**When to use:** High success rate, good for valuable scions.

**Process:**
1. Grow rootstock and scion mother plant side by side
2. Make matching cuts on both stems
3. Bind together, both on own roots
4. Sever scion from mother when union healed
5. Essentially "training wheels" grafting

### Air Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** To create larger plants, or when grafting unsuccessful.

**Process:**
1. Select pencil-thick branch in spring
2. Ring-bark 2-3cm section completely
3. Scrape exposed wood, apply rooting hormone
4. Wrap with moist sphagnum moss
5. Cover with plastic, seal ends
6. Roots develop in 3-6 months
7. Sever and pot carefully

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best for grafting | Active growth, good healing |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Grafting possible | Watch for heat stress |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Air layering | Time to root before winter |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Avoid grafting | Growth too slow |

### Optimal Timing

- **Grafting:** September to December (spring to early summer)
- **Seed planting:** Year-round (fresh seed)
- **Air layering:** February to April
- **Avoid grafting:** Winter months when growth dormant

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (20-28°C) |
| **Humidity** | High around graft initially |
| **Light** | Bright indirect (shade graft) |
| **Medium** | Well-drained potting mix |
| **Frost** | Protect - avocados frost-tender |

### Environment Setup

**For grafted plants:**
Humidity tent over graft for first 2-4 weeks. Bright shade. Protect from extremes. Gradually harden off.

**For seedlings/rootstock:**
Warm position, frost-free. Good drainage essential (avocados hate wet feet). Consistent moisture.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Graft Doesn't Take

**Symptoms:** Scion dries out, no growth, union fails to heal.

**Causes:** Poor cambium alignment, grafted at wrong season, desiccation, infection.

**Solution:** Try again on same rootstock if healthy, or start new rootstock.

**Prevention:** Align cambium carefully, graft in spring, maintain humidity, work cleanly.

### Problem 2: Seedling Rootstock Dies

**Symptoms:** Leaves wilt, roots rot, stem collapses.

**Causes:** Overwatering, poor drainage, Phytophthora root rot (common in avocados).

**Solution:** Usually too late. Improve drainage for future plants.

**Prevention:** Well-drained mix, don't overwater, use disease-free containers and media.

### Problem 3: Grafted Tree Fails to Fruit

**Symptoms:** Good growth but no flowers or fruit after several years.

**Causes:** Tree too young, poor pollination (need A and B types), excessive nitrogen, wrong climate.

**Solution:** Wait (grafted trees should fruit in 2-4 years), ensure pollination partner, reduce nitrogen, check climate suitability.

**Prevention:** Understand variety requirements, plant appropriate cultivars for your climate.

## Pro Tips

1. **A + B pollination** - Most avocado varieties need a pollination partner. 'A' type flowers open female in morning, male in afternoon. 'B' types do the opposite. Plant both for best fruit set.

2. **Fresh seed only** - Avocado seeds are recalcitrant - they don't store. Plant fresh from fruit, don't let dry out. Seeds from refrigerated fruit may have reduced viability.

3. **Top-working mature trees** - Existing avocado trees can be grafted to better varieties. Multiple grafts on scaffold branches spread risk and maintain tree structure.

4. **Clonal rootstocks exist** - Commercial production increasingly uses clonal rootstocks (propagated vegetatively) for disease resistance. Dusa, Latas, and others resist Phytophthora.

5. **The pit trick** - That avocado you sprouted in water will likely never produce good fruit. Graft onto it! Your windowsill seedling can become rootstock for a productive variety.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Scion Sources

Healthy avocado trees provide the best grafting material:

- **Nutrition:** Balanced feeding, not excessive nitrogen.
- **Disease management:** Monitor for root rot, anthracnose, other fungal issues.
- **Irrigation:** Consistent moisture, excellent drainage.
- **Variety confirmation:** Know your variety - A or B type, fruit characteristics.

### Selecting Scion Wood

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's hardened growth
- Plump, healthy buds
- No disease symptoms
- From productive, confirmed variety
