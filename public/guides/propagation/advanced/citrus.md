# Citrus Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Best Method** | Grafting (T-budding) |
| **Alternative Methods** | Cuttings, air layering, seed (rootstock only) |
| **Time to Root** | 4-12 weeks (cuttings), 3-6 months (air layers) |
| **Best Season** | Spring to early summer (grafting), year-round (protected cuttings) |
| **Success Rate** | 80-95% (grafting), 30-50% (cuttings) |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Essential for cuttings |

## Overview

Citrus propagation is considered advanced because commercial success depends on grafting desired varieties onto disease-resistant rootstocks. While citrus can be grown from cuttings or seed, the industry standard is grafting for good reason: rootstocks provide vigour, disease resistance, and adaptability to different soil types that own-root trees lack.

For home gardeners, understanding why grafting dominates citrus propagation helps make informed choices. Cuttings are possible but slow and less reliable. Air layering works well but produces trees without rootstock benefits. Seed grows easily but won't produce true-to-type fruit for most varieties, and trees take 5-15 years to fruit.

The techniques here focus on T-budding (shield budding), the most common citrus grafting method, along with cutting propagation for those who want to try the simpler but less reliable approach.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating trees on appropriate rootstocks for your soil
- Preserving heritage or unusual varieties
- Multi-grafting several varieties onto one tree
- Replacing damaged sections of existing trees
- Understanding commercial citrus production

## Propagation Methods

### T-Budding / Shield Budding (Recommended)

**Overview:** Insert a single bud from the desired variety into rootstock bark. The most efficient use of scion material.

**When to graft:**
- Best months: September to December (bark slipping freely)
- Time of day: Morning, avoid heat of day
- Plant condition: Rootstock actively growing, bark peeling easily

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Prepare rootstock**
   - Grow seedling rootstock to pencil thickness (8-12mm diameter)
   - Water well day before grafting
   - Choose smooth section of stem 15-25cm above soil
   - Wipe clean with damp cloth

2. **Make T-cut in rootstock**
   - Make horizontal cut through bark only (not into wood)
   - Cut about 1/3 around stem circumference
   - Make vertical cut downward from centre of horizontal cut (2-3cm)
   - Twist knife gently to lift bark flaps

3. **Prepare bud stick**
   - Select current season's growth from desired variety
   - Mature wood with plump, healthy buds
   - Remove leaves, leaving short petiole stubs
   - Keep moist in damp cloth

4. **Cut shield bud**
   - Start cut 1cm below bud, slice shallowly under bud
   - Continue cut to 1cm above bud
   - Remove thin shield of bark with bud attached
   - Minimal wood behind bud is ideal

5. **Insert bud**
   - Slide shield into T-cut, bud facing upward
   - Push down until top of shield sits at horizontal cut
   - Bark flaps should cover shield edges
   - Bud must be exposed

6. **Wrap the graft**
   - Wrap with grafting tape or budding rubber
   - Start below graft, spiral upward
   - Leave bud exposed
   - Firm but not crushing

7. **Aftercare**
   - Keep wrapped for 3-4 weeks
   - Check for union - green, healthy bud indicates success
   - Brown, shrivelled bud means failure
   - Once united, remove wrapping

8. **Force the bud**
   - Cut rootstock above graft when bud begins growing
   - Or bend rootstock over to force bud growth
   - Remove rootstock shoots below graft
   - Train new growth upward

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** When rootstock benefits aren't needed, or for experimental purposes.

**Process:**
1. Take 15-20cm cuttings from current season's growth
2. Semi-mature wood (slightly firm, not soft tips)
3. Remove lower leaves, keep 3-4 at top
4. Wound base lightly (scrape bark)
5. Apply strong rooting hormone (IBA 3000-8000ppm)
6. Plant in heated propagation unit
7. Bottom heat essential (25-27°C)
8. High humidity, mist if possible
9. Rooting takes 8-12 weeks
10. Pot up carefully when roots established

### Air Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** To create larger plants quickly, preserving mature wood characteristics.

**Process:**
1. Select pencil-thick branch
2. Ring-bark a 2cm section (remove bark completely)
3. Apply rooting hormone to exposed wood
4. Wrap with moist sphagnum moss
5. Cover with plastic, seal both ends
6. Check moisture monthly
7. Roots visible through plastic in 3-6 months
8. Sever below roots, pot up carefully

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best for grafting | Bark slipping, active growth |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Good for grafting | Continue while bark slips |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Cuttings possible | Growth slowing |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Avoid grafting | Dormant, poor take |

### Optimal Timing

- **T-budding:** September to December (when bark peels easily)
- **Cuttings:** Year-round with bottom heat, best in warm months
- **Air layering:** Spring to early summer
- **Test bark slip:** Cut small T - if bark lifts easily, conditions are right

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (25-30°C for cuttings) |
| **Humidity** | High (80-90% for cuttings) |
| **Light** | Bright indirect (shade grafts initially) |
| **Medium** | Free-draining propagation mix |
| **Equipment** | Grafting knife, tape, bottom heat for cuttings |

### Environment Setup

**For grafting:**
Shade newly grafted plants for 1-2 weeks. Protect from extreme heat. Keep rootstock healthy and growing.

**For cuttings:**
Heated propagation unit essential. Misting system helpful. Bottom heat 25-27°C. Humidity 80%+. Well-drained medium.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Graft Fails to Take

**Symptoms:** Bud dries, shrivels, or turns brown. No growth after 4-6 weeks.

**Causes:** Poor contact between bud and rootstock, bark not slipping, contamination, drying out.

**Solution:** Try again in a different location if bark still slipping. Wait until next season if not.

**Prevention:** Graft when bark slips freely, work quickly, ensure cambium contact, wrap firmly.

### Problem 2: Cuttings Rot Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Base of cutting turns black and soft, leaves drop.

**Causes:** Excessive moisture, poor drainage, fungal infection, too cold.

**Solution:** Remove affected cuttings. Improve drainage and air circulation.

**Prevention:** Well-drained medium, bottom heat, fungicide dip, don't overwater.

### Problem 3: Rootstock Suckers Overtake Graft

**Symptoms:** Vigorous growth below graft point, grafted variety weak.

**Causes:** Rootstock more vigorous, suckers not removed promptly.

**Solution:** Remove all growth below graft point immediately.

**Prevention:** Check regularly, remove suckers while small, ensure grafted variety is growing well.

## Pro Tips

1. **Bark slip test is crucial** - Before attempting T-budding, make a test cut. If bark doesn't peel back easily with a gentle twist, wait for better conditions. Forcing dry bark guarantees failure.

2. **One-hand budding technique** - Hold bud stick and make shield cut with same hand, keeping other hand free to hold rootstock. Reduces handling and speeds work.

3. **Chip budding for off-season** - When bark won't slip, chip budding (removing a chip of rootstock and replacing with matching scion chip) works year-round.

4. **Rootstock choice matters enormously** - Trifoliata for cold tolerance, Citrange for disease resistance, Volkamer lemon for vigour. Match rootstock to your conditions.

5. **Multi-graft citrus trees** - Graft several varieties onto one rootstock for a "fruit salad" tree. Useful for small gardens wanting variety.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Scion Sources

Healthy mother trees provide the best grafting material:

- **Certification:** Source scion from certified disease-free trees if available.
- **Nutrition:** Balanced citrus fertiliser program. Healthy growth = better buds.
- **Pest control:** Manage scale, mites, leaf miner. Don't graft from stressed trees.
- **Variety confirmation:** Ensure mother tree is true-to-type before propagating.

### Selecting Scion Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, well-matured
- Plump, healthy buds
- No signs of disease or pest damage
- Typical growth for the variety
