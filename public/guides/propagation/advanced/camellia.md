# Camellia Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Grafting, air layering, seed |
| **Time to Root** | 8-16 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 50-80% (cuttings), 85-95% (grafting) |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Essential |

## Overview

Camellias are the aristocrats of the shade garden, and propagating them is correspondingly more demanding than easier shrubs. While camellias can be grown from cuttings, success rates are lower and rooting times longer than beginner species. Professional nurseries often use grafting for faster, more reliable results.

The key challenges with camellia cuttings are the extended rooting period (3-4 months typical) and sensitivity to environmental conditions. Bottom heat, high humidity, and the right cutting maturity all matter significantly. Get any factor wrong and success rates plummet.

Grafting camellias overcomes rooting difficulties by using vigorous rootstock species, but adds complexity through the need for grafting skills. For most home propagators, cuttings remain the accessible method, even if patience and attention to detail are required.

Understanding that camellia propagation is slow - plan for at least 18 months from cutting to landscape-ready plant - helps set realistic expectations.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Preserving heritage or rare varieties
- Sharing favourite named cultivars
- Converting wild species to garden hybrids (grafting)
- Replacing aged or damaged specimens
- Building collections economically

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Cuttings from current season's growth once it has firmed but not fully hardened.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)
- Time of day: Morning, when plants turgid
- Wood condition: Current season's growth, firm but not brittle

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth that has firmed
   - Shoot tips with 3-5 leaves
   - Avoid flowering shoots
   - Healthy, pest-free wood

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut 8-12cm lengths
   - Cut just below a node at base
   - Remove lower leaves, keep 2-3 at top
   - Reduce remaining leaves by half (cut across)
   - Wound base lightly (scrape one side)

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Dip in medium-strength hormone (IBA 1500-3000ppm)
   - Powder or liquid gel formula
   - Tap off excess
   - Essential for camellia success

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - 50/50 peat and perlite mix
   - Insert 3-4cm deep
   - Firm gently
   - Space so leaves don't touch

5. **Create humid environment**
   - Covered propagation unit or plastic tent
   - Bottom heat 20-24°C
   - High humidity (80%+)
   - Bright indirect light

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Mist regularly or maintain humidity
   - Ventilate briefly daily to prevent fungal issues
   - Check medium moisture - moist but not waterlogged
   - Remove any fallen leaves promptly
   - Patience - rooting takes 8-16 weeks

7. **Testing for roots**
   - Gentle tug resistance indicates rooting
   - New growth often appears before roots substantial
   - Don't disturb early - wait until clearly rooted

8. **Pot up rooted cuttings**
   - Use acid potting mix (camellia/azalea mix)
   - Handle roots gently
   - Keep in shade initially
   - Grow on for full season before planting

### Grafting (Alternative - Professional)

**When to use:** For difficult-to-root varieties, or faster results on vigorous rootstock.

**Common methods:**
- Cleft grafting in late winter
- Side-veneer grafting
- Approach grafting (both plants in pots)

**Process (cleft graft):**
1. Grow Camellia sasanqua seedlings as rootstock
2. In winter, cut rootstock at 10-15cm
3. Split centre 3cm deep
4. Prepare scion wedge to match
5. Insert, align cambium
6. Wrap firmly, seal exposed cuts
7. Keep in humid, shaded conditions
8. Union forms in 6-8 weeks

### Air Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** For larger plants from mature branches, or when other methods fail.

**Process:**
1. Select pencil-thick branch in spring
2. Ring-bark 2cm section completely
3. Apply rooting hormone to exposed wood
4. Wrap with moist sphagnum moss
5. Cover with plastic, seal both ends
6. Check moisture monthly
7. Roots visible in 4-6 months
8. Sever below roots, pot up carefully

### Seed (For Species or New Varieties)

**When to use:** For species camellias or hybridising. Named varieties don't come true from seed.

**Process:**
1. Collect seed in autumn as pods ripen
2. Plant immediately (don't let dry out)
3. Plant 2cm deep in acid mix
4. Keep cool and moist over winter
5. Germination in spring (may take 2 seasons)
6. First flowers 4-7 years

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Seed planting | New growth too soft for cuttings |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Possible | Very early summer cuttings |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Best | Ideal cutting season |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Grafting | Too cold for cuttings |

### Optimal Timing

- **Cuttings:** February to April (late summer to mid-autumn)
- **Grafting:** July to August (late winter)
- **Air layering:** September to November (spring)
- **Seed:** March to May (as pods ripen)

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Bottom heat 20-24°C |
| **Humidity** | High (80%+) |
| **Light** | Bright indirect, no direct sun |
| **Medium** | 50/50 peat/perlite, acidic |
| **Container** | Covered propagation unit |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation unit:**
Heated base essential. Enclosed for humidity. Brief daily venting. Misting system ideal but not essential with good enclosure.

**Growing on:**
Shade for first season. Acid potting mix. Don't let dry out. Protect from frost (young plants less hardy).

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Base of cutting turns black, leaves drop, cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, fungal infection, no ventilation.

**Solution:** Remove affected cuttings, improve drainage, increase ventilation.

**Prevention:** Well-drained medium, daily venting, don't overwater, fungicide drench.

### Problem 2: Cuttings Dry Out

**Symptoms:** Leaves wilt and dry despite moist medium, cutting desiccates.

**Causes:** Humidity too low, air leaks in propagation unit, bottom heat too high.

**Solution:** Improve seal on propagation unit, reduce temperature, mist more frequently.

**Prevention:** Maintain humidity 80%+, check unit for air leaks, reduce leaf area on cuttings.

### Problem 3: Very Slow or No Rooting

**Symptoms:** Cuttings survive but don't root after 4+ months.

**Causes:** Wrong wood maturity, temperature too low, insufficient hormone, difficult variety.

**Solution:** Wait longer (some camellias take 6 months), try different wood next time.

**Prevention:** Use correctly matured wood, ensure bottom heat working, use stronger hormone, research variety-specific requirements.

## Pro Tips

1. **The "feel test" for cutting readiness** - Ideal wood snaps cleanly when bent. Too soft and it bends without breaking. Too hard and it shatters. The clean snap indicates perfect semi-hardwood maturity.

2. **Reduce leaves but don't remove all** - Half-leaves (cut across the middle) maintain photosynthesis while reducing water loss. Removing all leaves dramatically reduces success.

3. **Bottom heat is non-negotiable** - Unlike easy rooters, camellias need that 20-24°C root zone warmth. Air temperature can be cooler. Heat mat or heated bench makes the difference.

4. **Sasanqua roots easier than japonica** - Camellia sasanqua varieties generally root more readily than C. japonica. Try sasanquas first to build confidence.

5. **Two-year timeline** - From cutting to landscape-ready plant: 6 months rooting, 12 months growing on, 6 months hardening. Set expectations accordingly.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy camellias produce the best propagation material:

- **Pruning:** Light annual pruning after flowering encourages cutting-suitable growth.
- **Feeding:** Acid-loving plant fertiliser in spring. Don't overfeed.
- **Mulching:** Acid mulch (pine bark, oak leaves) maintains soil pH.
- **Pest management:** Scale and mites weaken plants. Control before taking cuttings.

### Selecting Cutting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, semi-hardened
- No flowers or flower buds
- Healthy leaves, no pest damage
- Typical growth for the variety
