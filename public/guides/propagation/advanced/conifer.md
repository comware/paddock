# Conifer Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Best Method** | Hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Semi-hardwood cuttings, grafting |
| **Time to Root** | 12-24 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late autumn to winter |
| **Success Rate** | 40-70% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Essential |

## Overview

"Conifer" covers a great many plants that behave differently, so the first thing to establish is which one you have. As a rough guide: junipers, chamaecyparis, thuja and cypress root reasonably well from cuttings. Pines, spruces and firs largely do not — they are grafted commercially, and a home batch of pine cuttings is usually a waste of a winter.

For the ones that do root, the method is undemanding but the timescale is not. Cuttings taken in winter may show first roots in four to six weeks, but a root system solid enough to pot on often takes four to six months, and some junipers run to 16-24 weeks before there is anything worth lifting. From cutting to a plant you can put in the ground is commonly a year.

The single most useful thing to know is that **patience is the technique**. A conifer cutting that has done nothing after three months is usually not dead. Emptying the tray in frustration is the most common cause of failure.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Replacing a gap in a conifer hedge with a plant that matches
- Reproducing a named cultivar — form and colour are cultivar traits and seed will not carry them
- Building a screen cheaply, when a year of waiting is acceptable
- Keeping a dwarf or unusual form going when nurseries stop listing it

## Propagation Methods

### Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** The standard method for rooting-capable conifers. Slow, low-maintenance, and mostly a matter of leaving it alone.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: May to August (after the first hard frosts, before bud movement in late winter)
- Wood condition: Current season's growth, fully firmed

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Take from the tips of side shoots, not leaders — leader material often grows into an odd one-sided plant
   - 10-15cm lengths
   - Take from the lower half of the parent where growth is denser

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Pull the cutting off with a heel of older bark where you can — heel cuttings root better in conifers
   - Strip needles or scale leaves from the lower third
   - Stripping is itself the wound; no further cutting is needed

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Essential here. Conifers root slowly and unaided percentages are poor
   - Hardwood-strength formulation

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Free-draining, low-fertility mix — sand and perlite, or a gritty propagating mix
   - Insert a third of their length
   - Space so foliage does not touch

5. **Provide environment**
   - Cool, bright, sheltered — a cold frame or unheated greenhouse is ideal
   - Cover for humidity, but ventilate regularly
   - Bottom heat helps but is not required

6. **Ongoing care** (12-24 weeks, sometimes longer)
   - Keep just moist
   - Ventilate to keep fungal problems down
   - **Do not lift to check before three months**
   - Green foliage does not mean rooted; conifer cuttings stay green for months either way

7. **Pot on when genuinely rooted**
   - Resistance to a gentle tug, and preferably roots at the drainage holes
   - Ordinary potting mix, grow on in a sheltered spot for a season

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Summer)

**When to take material:** February to April, once the current flush has firmed.

**Process:** As above but with softer wood, higher humidity, and a shorter timeframe — 8-16 weeks. Faster, but more prone to rotting off.

### Grafting

For pines, spruces and firs, and for many dwarf cultivars, grafting onto seedling rootstock is the commercial method and effectively the only reliable one. It is a different skill with its own guide.

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Avoid | New growth too soft |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Poor | Heat stress in a closed frame |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Good | Semi-hardwood, late in the season |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Best | Hardwood, fully dormant |

### Optimal Timing

- **Hardwood cuttings:** May to August
- **Semi-hardwood cuttings:** February to April
- **Key factor:** After the first hard frosts, before buds swell in late winter

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Cool (10-18°C) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate to high, well ventilated |
| **Light** | Bright, indirect |
| **Medium** | Gritty, free-draining, low fertility |
| **Bottom heat** | Helpful, not essential |

### Environment Setup

A cold frame is close to ideal — humid, sheltered, and cold enough that nothing tries to grow before it has roots. The failure mode to design against is stagnant damp air, so ventilate on mild days.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Foliage Browns from the Base Up

**Symptoms:** Lower foliage discolours, then the cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Fungal rot in still, wet conditions.

**Solution:** Remove affected cuttings immediately; they infect neighbours.

**Prevention:** Space cuttings, ventilate, water less, keep debris off the surface.

### Problem 2: Nothing After Three Months

**Symptoms:** Cuttings green and firm, no roots.

**Causes:** Usually none. This is normal for conifers.

**Solution:** Wait. Many root in their fifth or sixth month.

**Prevention:** Expect it. Plan on a full year from cutting to plantable.

### Problem 3: Rooted Plant Grows Sideways

**Symptoms:** Plant grows at an angle or refuses to form a leader.

**Causes:** Cutting taken from a side branch — some conifers retain the growth habit of the shoot they came from.

**Solution:** Stake a shoot upright to train a leader; may take a season or two.

**Prevention:** For upright forms, take cuttings from upright shoots. For spreading forms it does not matter.

## Pro Tips

1. **Find out what you have first** — Juniper, thuja, chamaecyparis and cypress are worth trying. Pine, spruce and fir are not; graft those or buy them.

2. **Take a heel** — Pulling the cutting off with a sliver of older bark attached noticeably improves the strike.

3. **Leave it alone** — The commonest cause of failure is checking. Three months minimum before you touch anything.

4. **Match the shoot to the shape you want** — Some conifers keep the habit of the shoot they came from. Upright cutting for an upright plant.

5. **Take plenty** — At 40-70%, forty cuttings for a twenty-plant hedge is realistic.

6. **Green is not rooted** — Conifer cuttings hold their colour for months whether they root or not. Only tug resistance tells you anything.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

- **Position:** Full sun to part shade, free-draining soil.
- **Pruning:** Light trimming in late summer keeps growth dense and produces cutting material.
- **Feeding:** Light annual feed. Overfeeding makes soft growth that roots badly.
- **Watering:** Deep watering while establishing, drought-tolerant afterwards.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, fully firmed
- Dense, well-coloured, typical foliage
- Side shoots rather than leaders, unless training an upright
- No browning, no mite damage, no dieback
