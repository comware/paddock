# Guava Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy-Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Air layering, seed, grafting |
| **Time to Root** | 4-8 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 70-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Guavas (Psidium guajava and related species) are relatively easy to propagate from cuttings, making them accessible for home gardeners. Both common guava and strawberry guava (P. cattleianum) root readily with standard techniques.

Semi-hardwood cuttings taken during the growing season give good results. The process is straightforward - guavas aren't particularly fussy about conditions, though warmth accelerates rooting.

Seed propagation is also easy and produces reasonably uniform plants for common guava, though named cultivars should be propagated vegetatively. Air layering offers a high-success alternative that produces larger plants.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Multiplying productive fruiting trees
- Creating guava hedges or screens
- Preserving favorite varieties
- Sharing with other gardeners
- Container growing

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Reliable method with moderate care requirements.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: November to February (spring to summer)
- Wood condition: Current season's growth, slightly firmed

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth, semi-hardened
   - 10-15cm lengths
   - Pencil-thick or slightly thicker
   - Healthy, pest-free

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node at base
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep 2-3 leaves at top
   - May reduce large leaves by half

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Medium to strong formula
   - Recommended for faster, more reliable results

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 4-5cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Warm conditions (22-28°C ideal)
   - High humidity
   - Bright indirect light
   - Cover or use propagator

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Maintain humidity
   - Ventilate daily
   - Keep moist not wet
   - Roots in 4-8 weeks

7. **Pot up and grow on**
   - Well-drained potting mix
   - Protect from frost
   - Plant out when well-established

### Air Layering (Alternative - High Success)

**When to use:** For fewer plants with very high success rate.

**When to layer:**
- Best months: September to November (spring)

**Process:**
1. Select pencil to finger-thick branch
2. Ring-bark 2-3cm section
3. Apply rooting hormone
4. Wrap with moist sphagnum moss
5. Cover with plastic
6. Roots in 2-4 months
7. Sever and pot up

### Seed (Easy but Variable)

**When to use:** For rootstock, or when variety not critical.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: Spring (September to November)

**Process:**
1. Extract seeds from ripe fruit
2. Wash clean
3. Sow immediately (viability drops if dried)
4. Plant 5mm deep
5. Keep warm and moist
6. Germination 2-4 weeks
7. Variable fruit quality

### Grafting (Commercial)

**When to use:** For specific cultivars on disease-resistant rootstock.

**Process:**
1. Use seedling guava as rootstock
2. Side-veneer or cleft graft
3. Standard tropical fruit tree technique

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (22-28°C) |
| **Humidity** | High |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Well-drained |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation unit:**
Covered tray or propagator. Warm conditions essential. High humidity. Bottom heat helpful.

**Key factor:**
Guavas are tropical plants. Warmth accelerates rooting; cold conditions cause failure.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Base turns black, cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, too cold.

**Solution:** Improve drainage, increase warmth, reduce watering.

**Prevention:** Well-drained medium, warm conditions, don't overwater.

### Problem 2: Frost Damage to Young Plants

**Symptoms:** Leaves blacken and fall, stem damage.

**Causes:** Guavas are frost-tender.

**Solution:** Move to protection, may recover if roots survive.

**Prevention:** Keep first-year plants protected from frost.

### Problem 3: Seed-Grown Plants Fruit Poorly

**Symptoms:** Prolific tree but small, seedy, or flavorless fruit.

**Causes:** Seedling variation - normal for guava.

**Solution:** Use for rootstock or hedging; graft good variety onto it.

**Prevention:** Propagate named varieties vegetatively.

## Pro Tips

1. **Warmth is key** - Guavas are tropical plants that want warmth. Cold conditions dramatically slow rooting and can cause failure. Propagate in warm months only.

2. **Fresh seed works best** - Guava seed loses viability quickly when dried. For best germination, plant seed fresh from fruit.

3. **Strawberry guava roots easily** - Psidium cattleianum (strawberry guava) often roots more readily than common guava. A good species for building confidence.

4. **Container control** - Guavas can be invasive in warm climates. Container growing keeps them manageable and protects from frost.

5. **Air layering for certainty** - If cutting success is inconsistent, air layering gives very high success rates and larger plants.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy guavas produce the best propagation material:

- **Position:** Full sun, warm, protected from frost.
- **Pruning:** Light pruning produces good cutting wood.
- **Feeding:** Regular feeding during growing season.
- **Watering:** Regular water, especially during fruiting.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, semi-hardened
- Healthy, pest-free condition
- From productive, disease-free plants
- Typical growth for the variety
