# Olive Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Hardwood cuttings, suckers |
| **Time to Root** | 8-12 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 60-80% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Essential |

## Overview

Olives (Olea europaea) have been propagated from cuttings for thousands of years, making it one of humanity's oldest deliberately propagated crops. While not as easy as some fruit trees, olive propagation is certainly achievable with attention to technique.

Semi-hardwood cuttings taken in late summer root most reliably. Strong rooting hormone, good drainage, and bottom heat all improve success rates. Patience is required - olives root slowly compared to many garden plants.

Large hardwood cuttings (truncheons) can also work, particularly in Mediterranean climates where they're a traditional method. The enormous energy reserves in large cuttings can power root development.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Establishing olive groves
- Preserving specific varieties
- Container specimens
- Garden ornamentals
- Hedging and screening

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Most reliable method for home propagators.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)
- Wood condition: Current season's growth, well-matured

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth that has hardened
   - Pencil-thick, 15-20cm long
   - Healthy, pest-free wood
   - Not the very soft tips

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut just below a node at base
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep 2-3 pairs of leaves at top
   - Wound base slightly (light scraping)

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Strong formula (IBA 3000-5000ppm)
   - Essential for olives
   - Apply to wounded base

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Very well-drained mix (perlite works well)
   - Insert 5-8cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Bottom heat (20-25°C) very helpful
   - Moderate humidity
   - Bright light
   - Good ventilation

6. **Long-term care** (8-12 weeks)
   - Keep moist but not wet
   - Don't overwater - olives hate wet feet
   - Patience - rooting is slow

7. **Pot up when rooted**
   - Use well-drained potting mix
   - Handle roots carefully
   - Grow on for full season

### Hardwood Cuttings / Truncheons (Traditional)

**When to use:** Traditional Mediterranean method, works in warm climates.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: June to August (winter)
- Wood condition: Mature wood, 1-3 years old

**Process:**
1. Cut 30-100cm lengths of mature wood
2. Can be very large (arm-thick)
3. Plant 1/3 to 1/2 depth directly in ground
4. Well-drained position essential
5. Keep moist first season
6. Slow to establish but effective

### Suckers (When Available)

**When to use:** Some olive varieties sucker from roots.

**Process:**
1. Locate sucker arising from roots
2. Dig around to expose root connection
3. Cut with portion of root attached
4. Transplant immediately
5. Keep watered until established

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (20-25°C base) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate (not excessive) |
| **Light** | Bright |
| **Medium** | Excellent drainage essential |
| **Bottom heat** | Very helpful |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation unit:**
Heat mat for bottom heat. Well-drained medium (pure perlite works well). Good light. Moderate humidity - not too wet.

**Critical factor:**
Drainage. Olives come from Mediterranean climates with dry summers. Waterlogged conditions cause rot. Keep on the dry side.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Rot Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Base turns black and soft, cutting dies.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, fungal infection.

**Solution:** Improve drainage dramatically, water less frequently.

**Prevention:** Very well-drained medium, don't overwater, good ventilation.

### Problem 2: Very Slow Rooting

**Symptoms:** After 8 weeks, no roots despite healthy cutting.

**Causes:** Normal for olives - they're slow. Or: too cold, no hormone.

**Solution:** Be patient. Maintain conditions. Olives can take 12+ weeks.

**Prevention:** Bottom heat, strong rooting hormone, optimal timing.

### Problem 3: Rooted Cutting Dies When Planted Out

**Symptoms:** Successfully rooted cutting fails after transplanting.

**Causes:** Root damage, overwatering, frost damage to young plant.

**Solution:** Usually too late. Improve technique for next batch.

**Prevention:** Handle roots gently, plant in well-drained soil, protect from frost.

## Pro Tips

1. **Mist is wrong for olives** - Unlike many cuttings, olives don't want constant misting. They prefer drier conditions with bottom heat. Think Mediterranean climate.

2. **Strong hormone is essential** - Olives are reluctant rooters. Standard strength hormone gives poor results. Use the strongest available.

3. **Bottom heat transforms success** - A heat mat under your propagation setup significantly improves and accelerates rooting.

4. **Varietal differences** - Some olive varieties root more readily than others. 'Arbequina' and 'Koroneiki' are considered easier; 'Frantoio' more difficult.

5. **The thousand-year tree** - Olives are incredibly long-lived. A cutting rooted today could become a tree that lives for centuries.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Trees

Healthy olives produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Full sun, excellent drainage.
- **Pruning:** Annual pruning provides cutting material.
- **Feeding:** Low nitrogen - too much produces soft growth.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established; water young plants.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Well-matured current season's growth
- Healthy, typical leaves
- From productive, disease-free trees
- No signs of olive lace bug or scale
