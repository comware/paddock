# Gooseberry Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Layering, mound layering |
| **Time to Root** | 8-16 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Winter (dormant) |
| **Success Rate** | 60-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Gooseberry propagates the same way as its close relative the currant — dormant hardwood cuttings taken in winter and left in the ground for a year — but it is the slower and less certain of the two. Some cultivars are notoriously reluctant. Where a batch of blackcurrant cuttings might strike at ninety per cent and above, a gooseberry batch of the same size and treatment can come in well under that, and the difference is usually the variety rather than anything you did.

That is worth knowing before you count on a number. Take half as many again as you need.

The other thing to get right is the bud treatment, which is the opposite of blackcurrant. **Rub off all but the top three or four buds.** Gooseberry is grown on a short clear leg with an open goblet-shaped head — partly for the fruit, mostly so you can get a hand in among the thorns to pick. A cutting that keeps its lower buds throws suckers from below ground and turns into an impenetrable thicket.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Replacing an ageing bush from a variety you like
- Building a row cheaply, if you can wait a year
- Keeping a heritage variety going where nurseries no longer list it
- Replacing a bush lost to gooseberry sawfly or mildew

## Propagation Methods

### Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Straightforward but slow, and the strike rate depends on the variety.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: June to August (winter, fully dormant)
- Wood condition: This season's growth, ripened, pencil-thick

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - One-year-old wood, pencil thickness
   - 25-30cm lengths — longer than currant, because the buried portion is longer
   - Wear thick gloves and take your time; the thorns are the hard part of this job

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut the base straight below a bud
   - Slant the top cut above a bud, so you can tell which way up it goes
   - **Rub off all buds except the top three or four** — this is what gives the clear leg

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Recommended, not optional as with currant
   - Gooseberry is slower to root and needs the help

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Free-draining soil in a sheltered spot, or deep pots
   - Bury two-thirds, leaving the retained buds well clear of the soil
   - Firm hard

5. **Provide environment**
   - Outdoors, uncovered, unheated
   - Winter dormancy is the mechanism

6. **Ongoing care** (8-16 weeks and beyond)
   - Refirm after frost heave
   - Rub off any shoot that appears from below the soil line as soon as you see it
   - Keep weed-free and evenly moist through the first summer

7. **Lift the following winter**
   - A full year in place
   - Lift dormant, plant straight out

### Mound Layering (Reliable Alternative)

**When to use:** When cuttings have failed, or the variety is a known poor striker.

**Process:**
1. In winter, cut an established bush hard back to near ground level
2. When new shoots reach 15-20cm in spring, mound soil over their bases
3. Add more soil through the season, keeping the shoot tips exposed
4. The following winter, scrape back the soil — shoots will have rooted
5. Sever each rooted shoot and plant out

Slower to set up but close to certain, and it produces several plants from one bush.

### Simple Layering

Peg a low branch into soil in late winter, cover, sever a year later. Fine for one or two plants.

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Avoid | Buds moving; mound layering starts here |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Avoid | In full leaf |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Possible | After leaf fall |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Best | Dormant, wood ripe |

### Optimal Timing

- **Hardwood cuttings:** June to August
- **Mound layering:** Cut back in winter, mound in spring, lift the following winter
- **Key factor:** After leaf fall, before bud swell

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Ambient winter — no heat |
| **Humidity** | Not required |
| **Light** | Open position |
| **Medium** | Free-draining soil |
| **Bottom heat** | Not necessary |

### Environment Setup

An outdoor trench. Gooseberry dislikes waterlogging more than currant does, so pick the better-drained corner and add grit if the ground is heavy.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Half the Batch Did Nothing

**Symptoms:** Some strike, many stay inert and eventually shrivel.

**Causes:** Variety. Some gooseberries simply root poorly from cuttings.

**Solution:** Use mound layering for that variety instead.

**Prevention:** Take extras, use hormone, and do not assume a currant strike rate.

### Problem 2: Suckers from Below Ground

**Symptoms:** Shoots appearing from soil level around the new plant.

**Causes:** Buds left on the buried portion.

**Solution:** Rub them out at the base as they appear, repeatedly.

**Prevention:** Remove all but the top three or four buds before insertion.

### Problem 3: Rotted at the Base

**Symptoms:** Base soft and black when lifted.

**Causes:** Waterlogged ground over winter.

**Solution:** None for those cuttings.

**Prevention:** Free-draining position, grit in heavy soil, raised trench.

## Pro Tips

1. **Gloves, then patience** — Preparing thorny cuttings badly because you are hurrying is how you end up with torn bases that rot.

2. **Take them when you prune** — Winter pruning gives the right wood, and you are already wearing the gloves.

3. **Mound layering is the fallback that works** — If a variety refuses from cuttings twice, stop trying and mound layer it.

4. **The slant tells you which end is up** — A cutting inserted upside down will not root, and with buds rubbed off there is little else to go by.

5. **Do not lift to check** — A year in the ground, undisturbed. Testing breaks what you are testing for.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

- **Position:** Full sun to light shade, free-draining soil, good airflow to limit mildew.
- **Pruning:** Winter — open the centre, shorten side shoots, keep the leg clear.
- **Feeding:** Compost and a balanced feed in late winter; potash suits it.
- **Watering:** Even moisture as fruit swells; irregular watering splits berries.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- One year old, pencil thickness, well ripened
- Straight lengths with even spacing between buds
- No mildew — grey felted growth on shoot tips means choose another bush
- No sawfly damage history if you can avoid it
