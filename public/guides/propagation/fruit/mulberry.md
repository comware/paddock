# Mulberry Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy-Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Softwood cuttings, air layering, seed |
| **Time to Root** | 4-8 weeks (softwood), 3-6 months (hardwood) |
| **Best Season** | Winter (hardwood), summer (softwood) |
| **Success Rate** | 70-90% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Mulberries (Morus species) are surprisingly easy to propagate from cuttings, considering they're substantial trees. Both hardwood and softwood cuttings root reliably, with hardwood cuttings being the traditional method and softwood offering faster rooting.

The ease of mulberry propagation has made it a traditional farm tree, with cuttings passed between generations. Many heritage mulberry trees exist today as direct clones of trees planted centuries ago.

All named mulberry varieties must be propagated vegetatively. This includes favorite fruiting varieties like 'Hicks', 'Shahtoot', and 'Black English'. Seed-grown mulberries are variable and may take years to fruit.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Preserving heritage or favorite varieties
- Establishing productive fruiting trees
- Creating shade and wildlife habitat
- Sharing with other gardeners
- Growing poultry fodder

## Propagation Methods

### Hardwood Cuttings (Traditional Method)

**Overview:** Large dormant cuttings taken in winter. Simple and reliable.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: June to August (winter)
- Wood condition: Fully dormant, current season's hardened wood

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - One to two-year-old wood, pencil-thick or larger
   - Healthy, well-budded wood
   - Can use large cuttings (30-50cm or more)

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut lengths 20-50cm (larger roots faster)
   - Straight cut at base, angled cut at top
   - 3-6 buds per cutting

3. **Apply rooting hormone** (optional but helpful)
   - Dip base in hormone powder
   - Medium strength sufficient

4. **Plant cuttings**
   - Insert 2/3 of cutting into ground
   - Only 1-2 buds above soil level
   - Deep planting encourages strong roots
   - Firm well

5. **Wait for rooting**
   - Buds swell and leaf out in spring
   - Roots developing below ground
   - Don't disturb to check

6. **Transplant or grow on**
   - Leave for full growing season
   - Transplant following winter
   - Or grow in place if well-positioned

### Softwood Cuttings (Summer Method)

**When to use:** For faster rooting with more control.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: November to January (spring to midsummer)
- Wood condition: New growth, slightly firmed

**Process:**
1. Take 15-20cm tip cuttings
2. Remove lower leaves, keep 2-3 at top
3. Apply rooting hormone
4. Insert in propagation mix
5. High humidity, warm conditions
6. Roots in 4-8 weeks
7. Pot up when well-rooted

### Air Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** For specific branches, or when cuttings fail.

**Process:**
1. In spring, select pencil-thick branch
2. Ring-bark 2-3cm section
3. Apply rooting hormone
4. Wrap with moist sphagnum moss
5. Cover with plastic
6. Roots in 3-6 months
7. Sever and pot up

### Seed (Variable Results)

**When to use:** Only when specific variety not important.

**Process:**
1. Collect ripe fruit
2. Macerate and wash out seeds
3. Plant immediately (or dry and store briefly)
4. Germination in 2-4 weeks
5. Seedlings variable, may take years to fruit

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Cool (hardwood), warm (softwood) |
| **Humidity** | High for softwood |
| **Light** | Full sun once growing |
| **Medium** | Well-drained soil or mix |
| **Position** | Sheltered for establishment |

### Environment Setup

**For hardwood cuttings:**
Plant directly in garden or nursery bed. Deep planting, firm soil. Keep moist.

**For softwood cuttings:**
Covered propagation unit. High humidity. Bright indirect light. Bottom heat helpful.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Hardwood Cuttings Don't Sprout

**Symptoms:** No growth by late spring despite healthy-looking cutting.

**Causes:** Cutting dried out, planted too shallow, poor quality wood.

**Solution:** Check cutting is still firm. May still sprout late. Try again next winter if not.

**Prevention:** Plant deeply, keep moist, use healthy wood.

### Problem 2: Leaves Appear Then Wilt

**Symptoms:** Initial growth followed by wilting and death.

**Causes:** Roots not developed yet, cutting exhausted reserves.

**Solution:** Keep moist, shade from harsh sun. May still develop roots.

**Prevention:** Deep planting, don't disturb, patient watering.

### Problem 3: Rooted Plant Fruits Poorly

**Symptoms:** Tree grows well but produces few berries.

**Causes:** Wrong variety (non-fruiting), needs polliniser, tree too young.

**Solution:** Verify variety, add polliniser if needed, wait for maturity.

**Prevention:** Propagate from known fruiting trees, understand variety requirements.

## Pro Tips

1. **Bigger is often better** - Unlike many species, very large mulberry cuttings (even 1m+ stakes) root and establish faster than small cuttings. The energy reserves accelerate establishment.

2. **Deep planting works** - Burying 2/3 or more of a hardwood cutting encourages extensive root development along the buried portion.

3. **Fruiting variability** - Mulberries exist as male, female, or monoecious forms. Only females and some monoecious trees fruit. Propagate from known fruiters.

4. **The 'shahtoot' secret** - Pakistan/Shahtoot mulberries have sweeter, longer fruit. They propagate just as easily as common mulberries.

5. **Fast growing, fast fruiting** - Mulberries are among the fastest trees from cutting to fruit. Well-established cuttings may fruit in 2-3 years.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Trees

Healthy mulberries produce the best cutting material:

- **Pruning:** Annual pruning produces good cutting wood.
- **Feeding:** Moderate fertility - excess nitrogen = soft growth.
- **Watering:** Established trees are drought-tolerant; water young plants.
- **Selection:** Propagate from productive, fruiting trees.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Healthy, vigorous wood
- Well-developed buds
- No disease symptoms
- From trees with proven fruiting
