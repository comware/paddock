# Feijoa Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Semi-hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed, layering, grafting |
| **Time to Root** | 6-12 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late summer to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 50-70% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Essential |

## Overview

Feijoas (Acca sellowiana) are moderately challenging to propagate from cuttings, but success is certainly achievable with proper technique. The challenge lies in the extended rooting period and moderate success rates - taking more cuttings than needed compensates for losses.

Semi-hardwood cuttings taken in late summer offer the best results. Bottom heat and strong rooting hormone both improve outcomes. Patience is required as rooting takes 2-3 months.

Seed propagation is easy but produces variable offspring - fine for hedging but not for replicating specific fruiting varieties. All named cultivars must be propagated vegetatively to maintain their characteristics.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Multiplying productive fruiting varieties
- Creating feijoa hedges
- Sharing with other gardeners
- Replacing old plants
- Container growing

## Propagation Methods

### Semi-Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Standard method for propagating named varieties.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: February to April (late summer to autumn)
- Wood condition: Current season's growth, well-matured

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Current season's growth that has hardened
   - 10-15cm lengths
   - Pencil-thick
   - Healthy, typical growth

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut below a node at base
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Keep 2-3 pairs at top
   - May reduce remaining leaves by half

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Strong formula (IBA 3000-5000ppm)
   - Essential for feijoas
   - Apply to lower 2cm

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Well-drained propagation mix
   - Insert 4-5cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Bottom heat helpful (20-24°C)
   - Moderate to high humidity
   - Bright indirect light
   - Covered propagation unit

6. **Long-term care** (6-12 weeks)
   - Maintain consistent conditions
   - Keep moist but well-drained
   - Ventilate to prevent disease
   - Patience required

7. **Pot up when rooted**
   - Handle roots carefully
   - Well-drained potting mix
   - Grow on for full season

### Seed (For Hedging/Rootstock)

**When to use:** When specific variety not important, or for rootstock.

**When to sow:**
- Best months: March to May (autumn)

**Process:**
1. Extract seeds from ripe fruit
2. Rinse clean
3. Sow immediately or dry briefly
4. Plant 5mm deep
5. Keep moist, warm
6. Germination 3-6 weeks
7. Seedlings variable

### Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** For a few plants with high success rate.

**Process:**
1. In spring, select low, flexible branch
2. Wound underside, apply rooting hormone
3. Pin to ground and bury 5-10cm
4. Keep moist
5. Roots in 6-12 months
6. Sever and transplant

### Grafting (Alternative)

**When to use:** For difficult-to-root varieties or commercial production.

**Process:**
1. Use seedling feijoa as rootstock
2. Whip or cleft graft in late winter
3. Standard fruit tree grafting technique
4. High success with practice

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm (20-24°C base) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate to high |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Well-drained |
| **Bottom heat** | Very helpful |

### Environment Setup

**Propagation unit:**
Heated bench or heat mat. Enclosed for humidity. Well-drained medium. Extended occupation (2-3 months).

**Key factor:**
Feijoas are slow to root. Plan for cuttings to occupy propagation space for an extended period.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Low Success Rate

**Symptoms:** Many cuttings fail despite good conditions.

**Causes:** Feijoas are moderately difficult - 50-70% is normal.

**Solution:** Take 1.5-2x as many cuttings as plants needed.

**Prevention:** Accept moderate success rates, use strong hormone, bottom heat.

### Problem 2: Slow or No Rooting

**Symptoms:** After 8 weeks, little progress.

**Causes:** Normal for feijoas - they're slow. Or conditions not optimal.

**Solution:** Be patient. Feijoas can take 12 weeks. Maintain conditions.

**Prevention:** Bottom heat, strong hormone, optimal timing.

### Problem 3: Seedlings Don't Fruit Like Parent

**Symptoms:** Seed-grown plants produce different (often inferior) fruit.

**Causes:** Genetic variation - normal for seed propagation.

**Solution:** Use seedlings for hedging; propagate named varieties vegetatively.

**Prevention:** Only use seed when variety doesn't matter.

## Pro Tips

1. **Varieties matter** - Some feijoa varieties are easier to root than others. 'Mammoth', 'Apollo', and 'Gemini' are considered easier; others more difficult.

2. **Self-fertile vs cross-pollinators** - Some feijoas are self-fertile; others need pollinators. Propagate appropriately for your situation.

3. **Bottom heat transforms results** - Feijoas respond strongly to bottom heat. A heat mat under your propagation setup can double success rates.

4. **Seed for hedging** - Seed-raised feijoas make excellent hedges. The variation doesn't matter for screening, and plants are tough.

5. **Layering as backup** - If cuttings repeatedly fail for a particular plant, try layering. Higher success rate, just slower.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy feijoas produce the best cutting material:

- **Position:** Full sun, good drainage.
- **Pruning:** Light trim after fruiting provides cutting wood.
- **Feeding:** Annual balanced fertilizer.
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established; water young plants.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- Current season's growth, well-hardened
- Healthy, typical leaves
- From productive, disease-free plants
- No signs of scale or guava moth
