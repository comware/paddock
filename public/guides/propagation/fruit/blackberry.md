# Blackberry Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Tip layering |
| **Alternative Methods** | Root cuttings, root suckers |
| **Time to Root** | 2-4 months (tip layers) |

| **Success Rate** | 90-100% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Blackberries are remarkably easy to propagate - in fact, they're so eager to spread that the main challenge is often controlling them. Tip layering is the classic method: when blackberry cane tips touch the ground, they naturally root and form new plants.

This natural behavior makes blackberry propagation almost effortless. Simply bury cane tips in late summer, and by winter you'll have rooted plants ready to transplant. Root suckers and root cuttings are alternatives that also work well.

Thornless and other improved varieties should always be propagated vegetatively to maintain their characteristics. Wild blackberries will self-propagate with no encouragement whatsoever.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Establishing productive blackberry patches
- Sharing favorite varieties
- Creating hedgerows or barriers
- Replacing old, unproductive plants
- Starting thornless varieties

## Propagation Methods

### Tip Layering (Recommended)

**Overview:** The natural method - bury cane tips and they root themselves.

**When to layer:**
- Best months: February to March (late summer)
- Cane condition: Long primocanes touching or near ground

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select suitable canes**
   - Long, vigorous primocanes (current season's growth)
   - Tips naturally reaching toward ground
   - Healthy, typical growth

2. **Prepare the site**
   - Dig small hole 10-15cm deep where tip will reach
   - Loosen soil in hole
   - No amendments needed

3. **Bury the tip**
   - Bend cane so tip enters hole
   - Bury 10-15cm of cane tip
   - Firm soil around it
   - Water well

4. **Wait for rooting**
   - Roots form in autumn
   - Small shoot may emerge
   - Leave attached over winter

5. **Separate in winter**
   - Cut connecting cane 30cm from rooted tip
   - Dig new plant with roots
   - Transplant to final position

### Root Suckers (Alternative)

**Overview:** Many blackberry varieties produce suckers from spreading roots.

**When to dig:**
- Best months: June to August (winter)

**Process:**
1. Locate suckers emerging away from main plant
2. Dig around to expose root connection
3. Cut with portion of root attached
4. Transplant immediately
5. Water well

### Root Cuttings (Alternative)

**Overview:** Root pieces produce new plants when planted in winter.

**When to take:**
- Best months: June to July (winter)

**Process:**
1. Dig to expose pencil-thick roots
2. Cut 10-15cm sections
3. Plant horizontally 5cm deep
4. Keep moist
5. Shoots emerge in spring
6. Transplant following winter

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Tip Layer Doesn't Root

**Symptoms:** After several months, no roots when checked.

**Causes:** Not buried deep enough, dried out, cane pulled loose.

**Solution:** Try again with deeper burial, secure with pin or stone.

**Prevention:** Bury at least 10cm of cane, firm well, keep moist.

### Problem 2: Plant Spreads Aggressively

**Symptoms:** Suckers appearing everywhere, canes rooting uninvited.

**Causes:** This is normal blackberry behavior!

**Solution:** Regular removal of unwanted canes, root barriers.

**Prevention:** Plant in contained area, vigilant maintenance.

### Problem 3: Thorny Suckers from Thornless Variety

**Symptoms:** New growth from roots has thorns despite thornless parent.

**Causes:** Thornless grafted onto thorny rootstock (rare), or reversion.

**Solution:** Remove thorny suckers, propagate only from thornless canes (not suckers).

**Prevention:** Know your plant's origin, propagate from cane tips not root suckers.

## Pro Tips

1. **Tip layering is nearly foolproof** - If you bury a blackberry cane tip and keep it from drying out, it will almost certainly root. Success rates approach 100%.

2. **The trailing types layer best** - Trailing blackberries have naturally long, flexible canes that tip-layer readily. Erect varieties may need their canes bent down.

3. **Thornless means tip-propagate** - Some thornless varieties are thornless only in the top growth, with thorny roots. Propagate from cane tips to maintain thornlessness.

4. **Watch for invasiveness** - Blackberries can become weedy. Consider this before planting. Use thornless varieties in accessible areas.

5. **Remove old fruited canes** - After harvesting, remove two-year-old canes that have fruited. This keeps plants manageable and prevents disease.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy blackberries produce the best propagation material:

- **Position:** Full sun for best fruiting, will tolerate some shade.
- **Training:** Support canes on wires or trellis for easier management.
- **Pruning:** Remove fruited canes after harvest, tip prune primocanes.
- **Feeding:** Annual compost or balanced fertilizer.

### Selecting Material

Choose canes that display:

- Vigorous, healthy growth
- Typical characteristics for the variety
- No disease symptoms
- From productive, well-managed plants
