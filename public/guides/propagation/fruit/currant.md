# Currant Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Best Method** | Hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Layering, division |
| **Time to Root** | 8-12 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Winter (dormant) |
| **Success Rate** | 80-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Optional |

## Overview

Currants are among the easiest woody fruit to propagate. Dormant hardwood cuttings taken in the middle of winter, pushed into the ground and left alone, come away at a rate most cuttings never approach — sources put a good batch close to a hundred per cent take, and hormone lifts an untreated strike from around 70% to over 90%.

Nothing about the technique is demanding. What matters is one detail that differs between the currants, and it is the opposite of what you would do with almost any other cutting.

**Blackcurrant: leave every bud on.** Buds below the soil line send up strong basal shoots, and a blackcurrant crops best as a multi-stemmed stool constantly renewing itself from the base. Rubbing off the lower buds — the standard move for most hardwood cuttings — gives you a single-stemmed plant that will disappoint for years.

**Red and white currant: rub the lower buds off.** These crop on a permanent framework of older wood, so you want a short clear leg and a defined head, not a thicket.

Same genus, same cutting, same week of the year, opposite preparation.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Replacing ageing blackcurrant stools, which decline after 8-10 years
- Extending a row from a variety that has proven itself in your ground
- Replacing winter losses at no cost
- Swaps — currants are generous and cuttings travel well

## Propagation Methods

### Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** The standard commercial method, and one of the few kinds of cutting that asks for neither humidity nor heat.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: June to August (winter, fully dormant)
- Wood condition: This season's growth, pencil-thick, wood ripened and firm

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - One-year-old wood, roughly pencil thickness
   - Straight lengths, 20-25cm
   - Avoid thin whippy tips and anything older than a year

2. **Prepare cuttings**
   - Cut the base straight, just below a bud
   - Cut the top on a slant just above a bud — the slant is only there to tell you which way up it goes
   - **Blackcurrant:** leave all buds intact
   - **Red and white currant:** rub off all but the top three or four buds

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Optional; they root without it
   - Worth using if you want an even, high-percentage strike

4. **Insert cuttings**
   - Outdoors in a spare corner of free-draining soil, or in deep pots
   - Two-thirds buried, top third proud
   - Firm the soil hard against them

5. **Provide environment**
   - Open ground, no cover, no heat
   - Winter cold is doing the work

6. **Ongoing care** (8-12 weeks and beyond)
   - Refirm after hard frost — freeze-thaw lifts cuttings and leaves them loose
   - Keep weed-free
   - Leaves in spring do not mean roots; wait

7. **Lift the following winter**
   - A full year in place gives a well-rooted plant
   - Lift while dormant and plant straight out

### Layering (A Few Plants)

**When to use:** When you want two or three and would rather not wait a year.

**Process:** Peg a low branch into soil in late winter, cover, leave a full season, sever the following winter.

### Division (Blackcurrant Only)

Old blackcurrant stools can sometimes be split with roots attached. Quick, but hard on the parent — use it when replacing a stool you were going to remove anyway.

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Avoid | Buds breaking, cuttings dry out |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Avoid | In full leaf |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Possible | Once leaves have dropped |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Best | Fully dormant, wood ripe |

### Optimal Timing

- **Hardwood cuttings:** June to August
- **Layering:** Late winter
- **Key factor:** Take them after leaf fall and before bud swell. Mid-winter is the safe middle.

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Ambient winter — no heat |
| **Humidity** | Not required |
| **Light** | Open position |
| **Medium** | Free-draining soil |
| **Bottom heat** | Not necessary, and unhelpful |

### Environment Setup

A trench in a spare bed is all that is needed. Bottom heat is actively unhelpful — warming the base into growth before the plant has roots is how hardwood cuttings fail.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Lift Out of the Ground

**Symptoms:** Cuttings loose or leaning after frost.

**Causes:** Freeze-thaw heaving.

**Solution:** Press them back and firm hard.

**Prevention:** Firm well at insertion, check after every hard frost.

### Problem 2: Leafed Out in Spring, Then Died

**Symptoms:** Healthy leaves, then sudden collapse.

**Causes:** The cutting ran on stored energy and never rooted.

**Solution:** None once collapsed.

**Prevention:** Take pencil-thick ripened wood, not thin growth. Do not lift to inspect.

### Problem 3: Blackcurrant Grew a Single Stem

**Symptoms:** One stem, no basal shoots, poor cropping.

**Causes:** Lower buds removed.

**Solution:** Cut hard to ground level in winter to force basal shoots.

**Prevention:** Leave every bud on blackcurrant cuttings. See the overview.

## Pro Tips

1. **Take them when you prune** — Winter pruning produces exactly the material you want. Do both jobs at once.

2. **Slant the top cut** — Not for the plant's benefit. It is so you can tell top from bottom in a bundle, and a cutting planted upside down does nothing.

3. **Take more than you need** — They cost nothing but a cut, and spares let you pick the best.

4. **Do not check on them** — Pulling to test breaks the roots you are testing for. Leave them a full year.

5. **Know which currant you have** — The bud rule inverts between black and red. If unsure, black currants smell strongly of blackcurrant when a leaf is crushed.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

- **Position:** Full sun to light shade, moisture-retentive soil.
- **Pruning:** Blackcurrant — remove a third of old wood at ground level each winter. Red and white — spur prune to a framework.
- **Feeding:** Compost and a balanced feed in late winter.
- **Watering:** Keep moist through fruit swelling.

### Selecting Material

Choose material that displays:

- One year old, pencil thickness
- Ripened wood, not soft tips
- Plump healthy buds
- No big bud mite — swollen round buds mean the plant is infected; do not propagate from it
