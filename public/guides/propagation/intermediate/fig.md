# Fig Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Hardwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Layering, suckers |
| **Time to Root** | 4-8 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Winter (dormant) |
| **Success Rate** | 80-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended but not essential |

## Overview

Figs (Ficus carica) are among the most rewarding fruit trees to propagate. They root readily from hardwood cuttings, with success rates approaching those of willows. A single mature fig can provide enough cutting material to start an orchard, and rooted cuttings often fruit within 1-2 years.

The traditional method uses dormant hardwood cuttings in winter, which root over spring in a process that requires minimal attention. Figs can also be propagated through layering or by separating the suckers that many varieties produce.

All fig varieties propagate equally well from cuttings. Whether you're working with common varieties like 'Brown Turkey' or specialty cultivars, the technique remains the same.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Establishing fig hedges or orchards
- Sharing favourite varieties
- Creating espaliers and trained forms
- Container-growing on patios
- Preserving heritage varieties

## Propagation Methods

### Hardwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** The classic method using dormant winter wood. Highly reliable with figs.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: June to August (winter)
- Time of day: Any time during dormancy
- Plant condition: Fully dormant, no leaves

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose pencil-thick to thumb-thick wood
   - Healthy, one to two-year-old growth
   - Avoid old grey wood or very thin tips
   - Each cutting 15-25cm long

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut cleanly with sharp secateurs
   - Bottom cut just below a node (straight)
   - Top cut just above a node (angled)
   - 3-5 nodes per cutting is ideal

3. **Apply rooting hormone** (optional)
   - Dip bottom 2cm in hormone powder
   - Tap off excess
   - Helps but not essential for figs

4. **Plant cutting**
   - Insert 2/3 of cutting into ground or pot
   - Well-drained soil or potting mix
   - Only 1-2 nodes above soil level
   - Firm well

5. **Provide environment**
   - Sheltered, well-drained position
   - Part sun to full sun
   - Protect from severe frost

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Keep moist but not waterlogged
   - Weed around cuttings
   - Patience - rooting takes time

7. **Signs of success**
   - Buds swell and produce leaves in spring
   - Growth indicates roots developing
   - Don't disturb to check

8. **Grow on**
   - Leave in place for full growing season
   - Transplant following dormant season
   - Or grow in final position

### Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** For certain success with no risk of failure.

**Process:**
1. In spring, bend low branch to ground
2. Wound bark where it touches soil
3. Bury wounded section 10cm deep
4. Pin down with wire hoop
5. Leave for 6-12 months
6. Sever from parent when well-rooted
7. Transplant to permanent position

### Suckers (Alternative)

**When to use:** When parent tree produces root suckers.

**Process:**
1. Identify sucker growing from roots
2. Carefully dig around to find root attachment
3. Sever with portion of root
4. Transplant immediately
5. Keep moist until established

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Growth begins | Monitor for budding |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Active growth | Train young plants |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Possible | Late cuttings as leaves fall |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Best | Prime cutting season |

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** June to August (full dormancy)
- **Avoid:** Active growth periods, late spring
- **Signs to take cuttings:** Leaves completely fallen, buds dormant

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Cool initially, warmth speeds rooting |
| **Humidity** | Moderate |
| **Light** | Part sun to full sun |
| **Medium** | Well-drained soil or potting mix |
| **Container** | Deep pots or direct ground |

### Environment Setup

**Outdoor bed:**
Well-drained, sheltered position. Insert cuttings deeply. Mulch around but not touching cuttings.

**Pot propagation:**
Use deep pots (20cm+) with drainage. Potting mix with extra drainage material. Keep in protected spot.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cuttings Don't Sprout

**Symptoms:** No leaf development by late spring despite healthy-looking cutting.

**Causes:** Cutting dried out, planted too shallow, poor quality wood, too cold.

**Solution:** Be patient - figs can be slow. Check cutting is still firm. May take until early summer.

**Prevention:** Keep moist, plant deeply, use healthy wood, protect from extreme cold.

### Problem 2: Leaves Appear But Die Back

**Symptoms:** Initial leaves emerge but then wilt and die.

**Causes:** Roots not developed yet, cutting exhausted reserves, extreme heat.

**Solution:** Keep moist, shade from hot afternoon sun. May reshoot from lower buds.

**Prevention:** Plant early so roots develop before hot weather, don't let dry out.

### Problem 3: Cutting Rots at Base

**Symptoms:** Base turns soft and mushy, foul smell.

**Causes:** Waterlogged conditions, poor drainage, contaminated material.

**Solution:** Cannot be saved. Improve drainage for remaining cuttings.

**Prevention:** Well-drained site essential, don't overwater, healthy source material.

## Pro Tips

1. **Bigger cuttings, faster results** - Figs can be propagated from very large cuttings (truncheons). A 50cm+ cutting roots and establishes faster than a small one.

2. **Keep some milk in the stem** - Fig sap (latex) is distinctive. A healthy cutting will exude milky sap when cut. Dry wood won't propagate well.

3. **Fruit from cuttings is fast** - Unlike many fruit trees, fig cuttings may fruit in their first or second year after propagation. Don't expect heavy crops initially.

4. **Air-dry the cuts** - Leave cuttings in shade for a few hours before planting. This allows cut surfaces to begin sealing, reducing rot risk.

5. **Pot culture keeps them small** - Root restriction in pots makes figs fruit more heavily on smaller plants. Great for container growing.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Trees

Healthy fig trees provide the best cutting material:

- **Pruning regime:** Annual pruning provides abundant cutting material. Figs fruit on new wood.
- **Feeding:** Annual compost mulch, moderate feeding. Too much nitrogen = excess growth.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for rust, scale, and fruit fly. Only propagate healthy material.
- **Watering:** Regular deep watering during fruit development.

### Selecting Mother Trees

Choose mother trees that display:

- Desirable fruit quality (taste, size, colour)
- Good productivity
- Healthy, disease-free growth
- Appropriate vigour for your needs
