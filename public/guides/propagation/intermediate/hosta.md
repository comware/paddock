# Hosta Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Division |
| **Alternative Methods** | Tissue culture (commercial) |
| **Time to Root** | Instant (divisions already rooted) |
| **Best Season** | Early spring or autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 95-100% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Hostas are propagated almost exclusively by division - the simple process of splitting an established clump into smaller pieces. Each division is already a complete plant with roots and growing points, so success rates are extremely high. This makes hostas one of the most reliable perennials to propagate.

The main consideration with hosta division is timing. Dividing at the right moment - either as shoots emerge in spring or as growth slows in autumn - minimises stress and maximises success. Dividing during active summer growth is possible but harder on the plants.

All hosta varieties can be divided, from miniatures to giants. Slower-growing cultivars build up more gradually and shouldn't be divided as often, while vigorous growers can be split every few years.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Filling shady borders
- Creating groundcover masses
- Sharing favourite varieties
- Refreshing old, crowded clumps
- Building variety collections economically

## Propagation Methods

### Division (The Only Practical Method)

**Overview:** Hostas don't root from cuttings and seed doesn't come true. Division is the only reliable method.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: August-September (early spring) or April-May (autumn)
- Time of day: Morning, overcast days ideal
- Plant condition: As shoots emerge (spring) or as leaves begin to fade (autumn)

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Prepare for division**
   - Water plant well day before
   - Have new planting sites prepared
   - Gather sharp spade or knife
   - Have hose or watering can ready

2. **Lift the clump**
   - Dig around entire clump, cutting roots
   - Lever under and lift out
   - Shake or wash off excess soil
   - Assess natural division points

3. **Divide the clump**
   - For large clumps: Use sharp spade to cut through
   - For smaller clumps: Use knife or hands to separate
   - Each division needs: 2-3 growing points (eyes/shoots)
   - Some root attached to each division

4. **Assess divisions**
   - Trim any damaged roots
   - Remove dead or damaged leaves/stalks
   - Discard woody centre of old clumps
   - Keep outer, more vigorous portions

5. **Replant immediately**
   - Plant at same depth as before
   - Firm soil around roots
   - Water thoroughly
   - Mulch around but not over crowns

6. **Aftercare**
   - Keep moist for several weeks
   - Shade from harsh sun initially
   - Divisions will establish quickly

### Single-Eye Division (for valuable varieties)

**When to use:** To maximise plants from a rare or expensive cultivar.

**Process:**
1. Divide into smallest viable pieces (single eye with root)
2. Pot individually in good potting mix
3. Keep in shade, high humidity
4. Grow on until well-established
5. Plant out following season
6. More stressful but creates maximum plants

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best (early) | Divide as shoots emerge |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Avoid | Plants stressed by heat |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Good | Divide as growth slows |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Possible | Dormant division works |

### Optimal Timing

**Spring division:**
- Best window: As shoots just break ground (August-September)
- Can see growing points clearly
- Roots active and ready to establish
- Full growing season ahead

**Autumn division:**
- Best window: When leaves begin yellowing (April-May)
- Still warm enough for root establishment
- Less stress than summer
- Overwinter and emerge strong

### Environment Setup

**Garden planting:**
Prepare soil with compost. Dig hole wider than roots. Firm in, water well, mulch.

**Container growing:**
Good potting mix, keep moist. Protect from hot sun. Can grow on before planting out.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Divisions Wilt After Planting

**Symptoms:** Leaves go limp and flop despite moist soil.

**Causes:** Normal transplant shock, hot weather, too much sun, insufficient roots.

**Solution:** Shade from sun, increase watering, remove some leaves to reduce water demand.

**Prevention:** Divide in cool weather, replant immediately, shade for first week.

### Problem 2: Divisions Rot in Ground

**Symptoms:** Crown turns mushy, foul smell, plant dies.

**Causes:** Planted too deep, waterlogged soil, crown damaged during division.

**Solution:** Dig up, trim rot, replant shallower in better-drained spot. May not survive.

**Prevention:** Plant at same depth as before, ensure drainage, don't bury crown.

### Problem 3: Slow Recovery / Weak Growth

**Symptoms:** Division survives but remains small for years.

**Causes:** Division too small, wrong time of year, poor soil, too much competition.

**Solution:** Feed with balanced fertiliser, water well, remove competing plants.

**Prevention:** Make larger divisions, divide at optimal time, prepare soil well.

## Pro Tips

1. **Eyes up!** - When dividing, ensure you can see the growing "eyes" (pointed shoots). Plant with eyes just at or slightly below soil level, never buried deeply.

2. **The spade test** - Can you easily push a spade through your hosta clump? If you have to force it, the plant is ready to divide. If the spade slides through easily, wait another year.

3. **Ditch the centre** - The middle of an old hosta clump is often woody and tired. The best divisions come from the vigorous outer edges. Compost the centre.

4. **Smaller = slower** - Single-eye divisions can take 3-5 years to reach mature size. If you want established plants quickly, make fewer, larger divisions.

5. **Spring for speed, autumn for size** - Spring divisions grow faster their first season. Autumn divisions often emerge larger the following spring, having established roots over winter.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy hostas divide better:

- **Feeding:** Annual compost mulch, feed in spring with balanced fertiliser.
- **Watering:** Consistent moisture produces lush growth. Drought stress = poor divisions.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for slug damage, virus symptoms, crown rot.
- **Division frequency:** Every 3-5 years for vigorous growers; less often for slow growers.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- True-to-type characteristics
- Healthy, undamaged foliage
- No virus symptoms (streaky discolouration, puckering)
- Strong, vigorous growth
