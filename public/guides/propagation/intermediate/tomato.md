# Tomato Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Sucker cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Tip cuttings, seed |
| **Time to Root** | 1-2 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to summer |
| **Success Rate** | 85-95% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Tomatoes (Solanum lycopersicum) are remarkably easy to propagate from cuttings, a fact that many gardeners overlook. The suckers (side shoots) that form at leaf axils root readily in water or soil, producing clones of your favourite varieties in as little as two weeks. This technique can extend your harvest season, replace diseased plants, or multiply a prized variety.

The beauty of tomato propagation from suckers is that it produces mature plants quickly. A rooted sucker can be fruiting in 6-8 weeks, compared to 10-14 weeks from seed. This makes sucker propagation invaluable for extending the season or recovering from plant losses.

Since you're creating genetic clones, cuttings from hybrid varieties (unlike seeds from hybrids) will produce plants identical to the parent. This is one case where propagation outperforms seed saving.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Extending harvest season with later plantings
- Replacing disease-affected plants mid-season
- Multiplying rare or expensive varieties
- Sharing favourite varieties with friends
- Backup plants for succession planting

## Propagation Methods

### Sucker Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Suckers are the side shoots that develop where leaves meet the main stem. Normally removed to concentrate energy into fruit, they make perfect propagation material.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October through January
- Time of day: Morning
- Plant condition: Healthy, actively growing plants with well-developed suckers

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select suckers**
   - Choose suckers 10-15cm long
   - Should have several leaves but no flowers
   - Select from healthy, disease-free plants
   - Multiple suckers can be taken at once

2. **Remove sucker**
   - Snap cleanly at base by bending sideways
   - Or cut cleanly with clean scissors
   - Remove immediately in morning for best turgor

3. **Prepare cutting**
   - Remove lower leaves, leaving 3-4 at top
   - Remove any flower buds
   - Can trim very large leaves by half

4. **Root in water**
   - Place in jar of clean water
   - Submerge lower 5cm of stem
   - Keep in bright, warm location
   - Change water every 2-3 days

5. **Monitor rooting**
   - Roots appear within 5-10 days
   - Wait until roots are 3-5cm long
   - Strong root system is visible

6. **Pot up or plant out**
   - Can go directly into garden if weather is warm
   - Or pot up for further growth
   - Water well when planting
   - Provide shade for first few days

### Tip Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** When suckers aren't available, or when you want larger cuttings.

**Process:**
1. Take 15-20cm tip cuttings from main stems
2. Remove lower leaves and any flowers
3. Root in water or moist soil
4. Same timeline as suckers

### Volunteer Transplanting

**When to use:** When fallen tomato fruits have germinated naturally.

**Process:**
1. Carefully dig volunteer seedlings
2. Transplant to desired location
3. Keep moist until established
4. Note: Only works for open-pollinated varieties; hybrids won't breed true

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 20-27°C |
| **Humidity** | Moderate |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Water or moist potting mix |
| **Container** | Glass jar or small pots |

### Environment Setup

**Water rooting:**
Simple glass jar on windowsill. Change water regularly. Roots develop very quickly in warm conditions.

**Soil rooting:**
Insert cuttings directly into moist potting mix or garden soil. Keep shaded and moist. Roots develop in 10-14 days.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Cutting Wilts Immediately

**Symptoms:** Sucker goes limp within hours of cutting.

**Causes:** Cut in heat of day, too many leaves, not placed in water quickly.

**Solution:** Trim base, reduce leaves, place in water in shade. May recover if caught quickly.

**Prevention:** Cut in early morning, remove most leaves, have water ready immediately.

### Problem 2: Transplant Shock After Planting

**Symptoms:** Rooted cutting wilts when planted out, takes time to recover.

**Causes:** Sudden change from water to soil, harsh sun exposure, wind.

**Solution:** Shade for first week, water frequently, protect from wind.

**Prevention:** Harden off gradually, plant on overcast day, provide temporary shade.

### Problem 3: Disease Transfer from Parent

**Symptoms:** Rooted cutting shows same disease symptoms as parent plant.

**Causes:** Viral or systemic bacterial diseases carried in the cutting.

**Solution:** Destroy affected plants. Cannot cure viral diseases.

**Prevention:** Only take cuttings from completely healthy plants. Don't propagate from diseased stock.

## Pro Tips

1. **Suckers in a bucket** - When pruning suckers during routine care, drop them in a bucket of water instead of the compost. By the time you need extra plants, they'll be rooted and ready.

2. **Late season second crop** - In warm climates, root suckers in mid-summer for a second crop that fruits into autumn. New plants are often more productive than tired mid-season plants.

3. **Rescue mission** - If your main plants get disease, you can sometimes rescue the variety by taking cuttings from healthy-looking upper growth before disease spreads there.

4. **Larger suckers root faster** - While smaller suckers work, 15cm suckers with thick stems root faster and establish more quickly than small, thin ones.

5. **The "bend and snap" test** - Suckers are ready for cutting when they're thick enough to snap cleanly when bent. If they just bend limply, they're too young.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

For best sucker production:

- **Pruning regime:** For indeterminate varieties, regular suckering produces continuous material. Leave some suckers to harvest for propagation.
- **Feeding:** Well-fed, healthy plants produce robust suckers.
- **Health monitoring:** Only propagate from disease-free plants. Watch for wilts, viruses, and blights.
- **Rotation:** Tomatoes are seasonal. Plan propagation during the growing season.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Strong, healthy growth
- Prolific fruiting with desired characteristics
- No signs of disease (especially viral symptoms)
- True-to-type variety characteristics
