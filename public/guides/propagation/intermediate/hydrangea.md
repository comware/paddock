# Hydrangea Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Softwood cuttings |
| **Alternative Methods** | Hardwood cuttings, layering |
| **Time to Root** | 3-6 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Late spring to early summer |
| **Success Rate** | 70-85% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Recommended |

## Overview

Hydrangeas are among the most rewarding shrubs to propagate. Their large, showy flowers make them garden favourites, and the plants root readily from cuttings. A single mature hydrangea can provide enough cutting material to create dozens of new plants, making this an economical way to fill a border or share with friends.

The most reliable method is softwood cuttings taken in late spring or early summer when new growth is still tender. These root in 3-6 weeks under the right conditions. Hardwood cuttings taken in winter are an alternative that requires less attention but takes longer.

All hydrangea types propagate similarly, whether you're working with mophead (Hydrangea macrophylla), lacecap, oakleaf (H. quercifolia), or panicle hydrangeas (H. paniculata).

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Creating hedges and borders
- Sharing favourite varieties
- Replacing ageing plants
- Building a collection of different varieties
- Cost-effective mass plantings

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Cuttings from soft, new spring growth root fastest and most reliably.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October to December (late spring/early summer)
- Time of day: Morning
- Plant condition: New growth that's still flexible but not floppy

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose non-flowering shoots or remove flower buds
   - Stems should be soft, not woody
   - Each cutting 10-15cm long
   - Look for stems that flex without snapping

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut just below a node
   - Remove all but top pair of leaves
   - Cut remaining leaves in half to reduce water loss
   - Remove any flower buds

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Dip cut end in rooting hormone powder
   - Tap off excess
   - Medium-strength hormone works well

4. **Plant cutting**
   - Use well-draining propagation mix
   - Make hole with dibber
   - Insert cutting 4-5cm deep
   - Firm gently

5. **Provide environment**
   - Humidity essential - cover with clear plastic or use propagator
   - Bright but not direct sunlight
   - Temperature 18-22°C
   - Good air circulation when venting

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Keep medium moist, not waterlogged
   - Vent cover daily
   - Mist if humidity drops
   - Watch for fungal issues

7. **Check for roots**
   - Roots develop in 3-6 weeks
   - New growth at tip indicates success
   - Gentle tug test shows resistance

8. **Pot up**
   - When roots fill propagation container
   - Use quality potting mix
   - Grow on in shade for first season

### Hardwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** Winter propagation when you have more time and less urgency.

**Process:**
1. Take 15-20cm cuttings in winter (June-August)
2. Remove leaves (they'll have fallen anyway)
3. Dip in strong rooting hormone
4. Insert in sheltered outdoor bed or cold frame
5. Leave undisturbed until spring
6. Roots develop slowly over winter/spring
7. Pot up when new growth appears

### Layering (Alternative)

**When to use:** For guaranteed success with minimal effort.

**Process:**
1. In spring, bend a low branch to the ground
2. Wound the bark where it touches soil
3. Bury wounded section 5-10cm deep
4. Pin down with wire hoop
5. Leave attached for 6-12 months
6. Sever and transplant when well-rooted

| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Good | Early summer still works |

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** October to December for softwood
- **Avoid:** Mid-summer heat, drought stress
- **Signs the plant is ready:** New growth that's firm but still flexible

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 18-22°C |
| **Humidity** | 80%+ (covered) |
| **Light** | Bright indirect, no harsh sun |
| **Medium** | Free-draining propagation mix |
| **Container** | Pots with covers or propagator |

### Environment Setup

**Propagator or covered container:**
Place cuttings in pots or trays, cover with clear plastic dome or inverted plastic bag. Keep out of direct sun to prevent overheating. Vent daily.

**Cold frame (for hardwood):**
Insert hardwood cuttings directly into cold frame. The enclosed environment provides some protection while the slow rooting occurs over winter.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Leaves Rot Under Cover

**Symptoms:** Leaves develop grey mould, turn mushy.

**Causes:** Humidity too high without ventilation, leaves touching cover.

**Solution:** Remove affected leaves, increase ventilation, reposition cuttings.

**Prevention:** Vent cover daily, space cuttings so leaves don't touch cover, cut large leaves in half.

### Problem 2: Wilting Despite Humidity

**Symptoms:** Cuttings wilt even under cover in moist conditions.

**Causes:** Stem rot at base, too much water, cutting taken at wrong stage.

**Solution:** Check base for rot. If rotted, discard. If not, reduce watering.

**Prevention:** Well-draining medium, water sparingly, select appropriate growth stage.

### Problem 3: Slow or No Rooting

**Symptoms:** Cuttings stay alive but don't root after 6+ weeks.

**Causes:** Too cold, no rooting hormone, wrong growth stage (too soft or too hard).

**Solution:** Increase warmth, be patient - some take 8 weeks.

**Prevention:** Use rooting hormone, ensure warmth, select right maturity of growth.

## Pro Tips

1. **The "scratch test"** - The ideal softwood cutting will flex without snapping. If it snaps, it's right. If it just bends limply, it's too soft. If it's stiff, it's too woody.

2. **Non-flowering stems root better** - Stems that weren't going to flower put all their energy into rooting. If you can only find flowering stems, remove all flower buds completely.

3. **Cutting leaves in half really works** - Halving the leaves reduces water loss dramatically while still allowing photosynthesis. This simple step significantly improves success rates.

4. **Morning dew is your friend** - Take cuttings early in the morning when plants are fully turgid from overnight dew. This gives them the best start.

5. **Colour is variable** - The blue or pink colour of mophead hydrangeas depends on soil pH, not genetics. Your cutting will produce the same flowers, but colour may vary in different soils.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy mother plants produce the best cutting material:

- **Pruning regime:** Prune after flowering to encourage fresh growth for next year's cuttings.
- **Feeding:** Annual mulch with compost, feed in spring.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for powdery mildew, scale insects. Treat before taking cuttings.
- **Watering:** Hydrangeas hate drought. Consistent moisture produces healthy growth.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Strong, vigorous growth
- Desired flower colour and form
- Good disease resistance
- True-to-type characteristics
