# Pepper Propagation

| Attribute | Value |

| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |

## Overview

Peppers (Capsicum species) can be propagated from cuttings, allowing you to clone your favourite chilli or sweet pepper varieties. While not as fast-rooting as tomatoes, pepper cuttings produce genetic clones that fruit sooner than seed-grown plants. This technique is particularly valuable for exceptional varieties or superhot chillies that can be difficult to grow from seed.

Pepper propagation requires more patience than some other vegetables. Cuttings take 3-4 weeks to root and are more sensitive to conditions than tomato suckers. However, the reward is a head start on the season and guaranteed genetics of your best performers.

In warm climates, overwintered pepper plants can provide cutting material in early spring, giving you established plants months ahead of seed-sown crops.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Preserving productive varieties
- Multiplying expensive or rare chilli varieties
- Creating backup plants from overwintered specimens
- Getting a head start on the season
- Sharing favourite varieties with other growers

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings (Recommended)

**Overview:** Cuttings from soft, actively growing shoots root most readily. Success depends on warmth and humidity.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October through December
- Time of day: Morning
- Plant condition: Healthy, actively growing plants with soft new growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose healthy, non-flowering shoot tips
   - Each cutting should be 10-15cm long
   - Stems should be green and pliable, not woody
   - Avoid stems with flowers or fruit

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut cleanly just below a node
   - Remove all but 2-3 leaves at top
   - Remove any flowers or buds
   - Trim large leaves by half to reduce water loss

3. **Apply rooting hormone**
   - Dip cut end in rooting hormone powder or gel
   - Tap off excess
   - Use medium-strength hormone

4. **Plant cutting**
   - Use seed-raising mix or fine propagation blend
   - Insert cutting 3-4cm deep
   - Firm gently around stem

5. **Provide environment**
   - Warm (22-27°C) - critical for success
   - High humidity (cover with clear plastic or use propagator)
   - Bright indirect light
   - Bottom heat very helpful

6. **Ongoing care**
   - Keep medium moist but not waterlogged
   - Vent cover daily to prevent mould
   - Mist if humidity drops

7. **Check for roots**
   - Roots develop in 3-4 weeks
   - New growth is the best indicator
   - Gentle tug to test for resistance

8. **Harden off and pot up**
   - Gradually reduce humidity
   - Pot into standard potting mix
   - Keep warm until established

### Semi-hardwood Cuttings (Alternative)

**When to use:** Late season or from overwintered plants when soft growth isn't available.

**Process:**
Same as above but:
- Use slightly firmer, more mature stems
- Rooting may take 4-6 weeks
- Higher hormone concentration helpful
- Success rate slightly lower

- **Best window:** October to December
- **Avoid:** Cool conditions, diseased plants
- **Signs the plant is ready:** Healthy soft growth, no flowers on cutting material

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 22-27°C (warmth critical) |
| **Humidity** | 80%+ during rooting |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Fine seed-raising mix |
| **Container** | Small pots or cell trays with covers |

### Environment Setup

**Heated propagator:**
Ideal for peppers. Set bottom heat to 22-25°C. Clear lid maintains humidity. Vent daily.

**DIY setup:**
Small pots covered with clear plastic bags. Rubber band to seal. Place on heat mat or warm surface. Vent daily.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Stem Rots at Soil Level

**Symptoms:** Cutting base turns black, cutting collapses.

**Causes:** Too wet, poor drainage, too cold, contaminated medium.

**Solution:** Discard affected cuttings. Cannot be saved.

**Prevention:** Well-draining medium, water sparingly, maintain warmth, use clean materials.

### Problem 2: Leaves Drop Before Rooting

**Symptoms:** Cutting loses leaves progressively from bottom up.

**Causes:** Low humidity, transplant shock, too much light initially.

**Solution:** Increase humidity, reduce light, ensure cover is sealed. Some leaf drop is normal.

**Prevention:** High humidity from start, shade initially, don't overwater.

### Problem 3: Cuttings Don't Root

**Symptoms:** Cutting stays alive but doesn't develop roots after 4+ weeks.

**Causes:** Too cold, material too woody, no rooting hormone used.

**Solution:** Increase warmth significantly, try softer growth next time, use hormone.

**Prevention:** Ensure 22°C+ temperatures, select softest growth available, always use hormone.

## Pro Tips

1. **Overwinter to propagate** - Bring pepper plants indoors before frost, overwinter on a sunny windowsill, then take cuttings in early spring for a massive head start.

2. **Bottom heat is your friend** - A heat mat makes pepper propagation much more reliable. Peppers evolved in warm climates and need warmth to root.

3. **The "crutch" technique** - Support wobbly cuttings with small stakes or crossed toothpicks. Cuttings that move don't root well.

4. **Superhots are worth the effort** - Rare superhot varieties (Carolina Reaper, Trinidad Scorpion) can take years to develop and are difficult from seed. Cuttings preserve your hard work.

5. **Root before they flower** - Once pepper cuttings start flowering, energy goes to reproduction. Remove any flower buds and focus on root development.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Keep mother plants healthy through the off-season:

- **Overwintering:** Bring indoors before first frost. Reduce watering, keep in brightest spot.
- **Pruning regime:** Cut back by one-third when bringing indoors. Fresh growth in spring is ideal for cuttings.
- **Feeding:** Light feeding during active growth. Reduce in winter.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for aphids and whitefly indoors. Treat promptly.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Exceptional flavour or heat levels (for chillies)
- High productivity
- Disease resistance
- Desired fruit characteristics
