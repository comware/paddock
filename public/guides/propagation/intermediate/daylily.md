# Daylily Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Division |
| **Alternative Methods** | Proliferations, seed (for hybridising) |
| **Time to Root** | Instant (divisions already rooted) |
| **Best Season** | Early spring or after flowering |
| **Success Rate** | 95-100% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Daylilies (Hemerocallis) are among the most division-friendly perennials. These tough, forgiving plants can be divided almost any time of year and will survive, though timing does affect how quickly they establish. Division is also the only way to propagate named cultivars true-to-type.

The clumping growth habit of daylilies makes them natural candidates for division. A single fan (the unit of leaves from one crown) can grow into a large clump of multiple fans over several years. Dividing this clump yields many new plants, each capable of flowering the same year.

With thousands of named daylily cultivars available, division lets you build a collection economically and share favourites with other enthusiasts.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Building mass plantings
- Sharing favourite cultivars
- Controlling clump size
- Refreshing old, crowded plantings
- Trading with other collectors

## Propagation Methods

### Division (Recommended)

**Overview:** The standard method for all named cultivars. Simple, reliable, and productive.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: August-September (spring) or February-April (after flowering)
- Time of day: Morning or overcast days
- Plant condition: Active growth (spring) or post-flowering recovery (late summer/autumn)

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Prepare for division**
   - Water thoroughly day before
   - Prepare destination beds/pots
   - Have sharp knife or spade ready
   - Plan before digging

2. **Lift the clump**
   - Dig around entire clump
   - Lever under and lift
   - Shake off loose soil
   - Can rinse for clearer view

3. **Separate into divisions**
   - Identify individual fans (rosettes of leaves)
   - Pull apart where possible by hand
   - Use knife to cut through tough connections
   - Each division: 1 fan minimum, 2-3 fans ideal

4. **Prepare divisions**
   - Trim leaves to 15-20cm to reduce water loss
   - Trim roots to 10-15cm
   - Remove dead or damaged material
   - Single fans are viable but slower to establish

5. **Replant immediately**
   - Dig hole twice width of roots
   - Plant with crown at soil level
   - Fan out roots in hole
   - Firm soil and water deeply

6. **Aftercare**
   - Water regularly for 2-3 weeks
   - Mulch to conserve moisture
   - Light shade helpful in hot weather

### Proliferations (Alternative)

**When to use:** When small plants appear on flower scapes (some cultivars only).

**Process:**
1. Identify proliferations (small plantlets on flowering stem)
2. Wait until plantlet has developed roots
3. Cut scape section with plantlet
4. Pot up or plant directly
5. Keep moist until established

### Seed (For Hybridising Only)

**When to use:** Creating new varieties, not for reproducing named cultivars.

**Process:**
1. Collect seed pods before they open
2. Remove seeds from pods
3. Plant fresh or stratify (depends on dormancy)
4. Seedlings take 1-3 years to flower
5. Each seedling is genetically unique

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Best | Divide before heavy flowering |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Possible | Avoid during flowering, stressful in heat |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Good | After flowering finishes |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Possible | Dormant varieties, less ideal |

### Optimal Timing

**Spring:**
- Best window: As new fans emerge (August-September)
- Plants about to enter active growth
- Long establishment period ahead

**Post-flowering (late summer/autumn):**
- Best window: 2-4 weeks after flowering ends
- Energy returning to roots
- Still warm for root establishment

### Environment Setup

**Garden planting:**
Daylilies are adaptable. Average garden soil is fine. Good drainage prevents crown rot. Full sun for best flowering.

**Container growing:**
Standard potting mix. Keep moist. Useful for growing on small divisions before planting out.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Divisions Don't Flower First Year

**Symptoms:** Healthy foliage but no flowers in first season.

**Causes:** Normal for small divisions, stressful timing, too much nitrogen.

**Solution:** Patience - will flower following year. Not a problem.

**Prevention:** Larger divisions (3+ fans) usually flower the same year.

### Problem 2: Crown Rot After Planting

**Symptoms:** Centre of plant becomes mushy, foul smell, plant collapses.

**Causes:** Planted too deep, waterlogged soil, damaged crown.

**Solution:** Dig up, trim rot, replant shallower in better-drained location.

**Prevention:** Plant with crown at or slightly above soil level, ensure drainage.

### Problem 3: Divisions Dry Out

**Symptoms:** Leaves brown from tips, divisions fail to thrive.

**Causes:** Roots dried between lifting and planting, insufficient watering after.

**Solution:** Water deeply and consistently. May recover if roots still viable.

**Prevention:** Replant immediately after dividing, water well and consistently.

## Pro Tips

1. **Two-fork technique** - Insert two garden forks back-to-back into the clump and lever apart. Works well for large clumps without a knife.

2. **Leaf trim is essential** - Cutting leaves to 15cm dramatically reduces water demand, helping divisions establish without wilting.

3. **Single fans are viable** - Even single-fan divisions will grow. They just take 2-3 years to reach flowering size. Useful for maximising rare cultivars.

4. **Crown level is critical** - The crown (where leaves meet roots) must be at soil level. Too deep = rot. Too shallow = drying out.

5. **Don't be gentle** - Daylilies are tough. You can be quite aggressive with separation and they'll recover. Better to make clean breaks than tease apart messily.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Healthy clumps produce the best divisions:

- **Feeding:** Annual mulch with compost, balanced fertiliser in spring.
- **Watering:** Regular water during active growth and flowering.
- **Dead-heading:** Remove spent scapes to encourage vigour.
- **Division frequency:** Every 3-5 years prevents overcrowding and maintains flowering.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- True-to-type flowers
- Strong, healthy foliage
- Vigorous growth
- No disease symptoms
