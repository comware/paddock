# Iris Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Rhizome division |
| **Alternative Methods** | Seed (for hybridising) |

## Overview

Bearded irises (Iris germanica and hybrids) are propagated by dividing their thick, horizontal rhizomes. Unlike plants propagated by root division, iris rhizomes are modified stems that grow along or just under the soil surface. Each section with a fan of leaves and some roots can become a new plant.

Division is not just optional for irises - it's essential maintenance. Overcrowded clumps flower poorly and become susceptible to disease. Regular division every 3-4 years keeps plants healthy and productive while providing abundant material for new plantings.

The traditional timing for iris division is midsummer, several weeks after flowering ends. This allows plants to establish before winter while the rhizomes are actively growing.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Refreshing overgrown clumps
- Expanding iris borders
- Sharing favourite varieties
- Creating mass plantings
- Trading with other enthusiasts

## Propagation Methods

### Rhizome Division (The Standard Method)

**Overview:** Separate and replant sections of rhizome, each with leaves and roots.

**When to divide:**
- Best months: January-February (6-8 weeks after flowering)
- Time of day: Any time
- Plant condition: Post-flowering, active rhizome growth

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Prepare for division**
   - Mark plants for identification if needed
   - Have new planting areas prepared
   - Gather sharp knife, spade
   - Plan placement before digging

2. **Lift the clump**
   - Insert spade/fork around entire clump
   - Lever under and lift
   - Shake off loose soil
   - Assess structure of rhizomes

3. **Clean and assess**
   - Wash rhizomes to see clearly
   - Identify healthy vs damaged sections
   - Note natural division points
   - Discard soft, mushy, or borer-damaged pieces

4. **Divide the clump**
   - Use sharp knife to cut rhizomes
   - Each division needs: 1 rhizome section (5-10cm) + 1 fan of leaves + roots
   - Cut at natural constrictions between sections
   - Keep outer, younger sections; discard old centre

5. **Prepare divisions**
   - Trim leaves to 15cm fan shape (traditional "flag" cut)
   - Trim roots to 8-10cm
   - Inspect for borer damage - discard if found
   - Allow cuts to dry 24 hours before planting (optional but helpful)

6. **Plant divisions**
   - Dig shallow hole
   - Create ridge in centre
   - Drape rhizome over ridge, roots on either side
   - Rhizome top should be at or slightly above soil level
   - Face fan away from centre of planting

7. **Firm and water**
   - Firm soil around roots
   - Water to settle soil
   - Do not bury rhizome - should be visible

8. **Aftercare**
   - Water weekly until established
   - No mulch directly over rhizomes (causes rot)
   - Divisions establish before winter

### Seed (For Hybridising Only)

**When to use:** Creating new varieties, not for reproducing named cultivars.

**Process:**
1. Collect mature seed pods
2. Plant in autumn
3. Cold stratification occurs naturally over winter
4. Seedlings emerge in spring
5. First flowers in 2-3 years
6. Each seedling is genetically unique

## Timing & Seasonality

### Southern Hemisphere Calendar

| Season | Suitability | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Spring** (Sep-Nov) | Avoid | Plants are flowering or about to |
| **Summer** (Dec-Feb) | Best | 6-8 weeks after flowering ends |
| **Autumn** (Mar-May) | Possible | Later establishment, OK in mild climates |
| **Winter** (Jun-Aug) | Avoid | Dormant, poor root growth |

### Optimal Timing

- **Best window:** January-February (midsummer, post-flowering)
- **Why this timing:** Rhizomes actively growing, time to establish before winter
- **Avoid:** Spring (disturbs flowering), late autumn (poor establishment)

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | Warm for establishment |
| **Humidity** | Moderate |
| **Light** | Full sun essential |
| **Medium** | Well-drained, neutral to alkaline |
| **Position** | Rhizome visible above soil |

### Environment Setup

**Garden planting:**
Full sun essential for flowering. Well-drained soil (add gypite or grit if heavy clay). Raise beds if drainage poor. Rhizome top exposed to sun.

**Container growing:**
Well-drained potting mix. Wide, shallow pot suits rhizome habit. Full sun position.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Rhizome Rot After Planting

**Symptoms:** Rhizome becomes soft and mushy, foul smell, fan collapses.

**Causes:** Planted too deep, poor drainage, damaged rhizome.

**Solution:** Dig up, cut away rot, let dry, replant shallower or in better-drained soil.

**Prevention:** Never bury rhizomes, ensure excellent drainage, dry cuts before planting.

### Problem 2: No Flowers After Division

**Symptoms:** Healthy foliage but no flowers first season.

**Causes:** Divisions too small, transplant too late in season, too much shade.

**Solution:** Patience - should flower following year.

**Prevention:** Make larger divisions, divide in mid-summer, plant in full sun.

### Problem 3: Iris Borer Damage

**Symptoms:** Tunnels in rhizomes, mushy sections, pink caterpillars inside.

**Causes:** Iris borer larvae tunnel through rhizomes.

**Solution:** Cut away damaged sections, destroy larvae. May save plant if caught early.

**Prevention:** Good sanitation - remove dead leaves where eggs overwinter. Inspect when dividing.

## Pro Tips

1. **The sunbake is essential** - Iris rhizomes need sun exposure to initiate flowering. Never bury them. The top of the rhizome should be visible, baking in the sun.

2. **Point fans outward** - When planting, orient the fan of leaves away from the centre of your planting. This directs growth outward rather than into crowding.

3. **The "heel" technique** - Rock the division gently after planting. It should be stable. If it rocks loosely, plant deeper or firm more thoroughly.

4. **Discard the mother** - The original rhizome that produced this year's flower is exhausted. Keep the younger, outer rhizomes; discard the old centre.

5. **Less is more with water** - Once established, bearded irises are drought-tolerant. Overwatering, especially in winter, causes rot.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Clumps

Healthy irises produce the best divisions:

- **Feeding:** Low-nitrogen fertiliser in spring. Too much nitrogen = lush foliage, few flowers.
- **Dead-heading:** Remove spent flower stalks promptly.
- **Sanitation:** Clean up dead leaves in autumn - borer eggs overwinter in debris.
- **Division frequency:** Every 3-4 years to prevent overcrowding.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Strong, healthy fans with no leaf spots
- Firm, clean rhizomes
- Good flowering performance
- True-to-type flowers
