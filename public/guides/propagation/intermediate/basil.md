# Basil Propagation

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Intermediate |
| **Best Method** | Softwood cuttings in water |
| **Alternative Methods** | Softwood cuttings in soil, seed |
| **Time to Root** | 1-2 weeks |
| **Best Season** | Spring to autumn |
| **Success Rate** | 80-90% typical |
| **Rooting Hormone** | Not needed |

## Overview

Basil (Ocimum basilicum) roots readily from cuttings, allowing you to multiply a single supermarket plant into an endless supply. While technically easy, basil earns its intermediate rating because the cuttings are more prone to rot and wilting than truly beginner-friendly plants. Understanding how to prevent these issues makes the difference between success and frustration.

The advantage of propagating basil from cuttings rather than seed is speed. A rooted cutting can be producing harvestable leaves in 3-4 weeks, while seed-grown basil takes 8-10 weeks to reach the same stage. Cuttings also guarantee you get the exact variety you want, whether that's sweet basil, Thai basil, or a specialty cultivar.

Basil is a warm-weather crop, and warmth is essential for successful propagation. Cuttings taken in cool conditions are prone to blackening at the base (stem rot), the main cause of failure.

## Best Uses for Propagated Plants

- Continuous kitchen supply
- Bulk plantings for pesto making
- Preserving favourite varieties
- Market garden production
- Companion planting with tomatoes

## Propagation Methods

### Softwood Cuttings in Water (Recommended)

**Overview:** Water propagation allows you to monitor root development and produces high success rates when done correctly.

**When to take material:**
- Best months: October through March (warm weather)
- Time of day: Morning, when plants are turgid
- Plant condition: Healthy, actively growing, not flowering

**Step-by-step process:**

1. **Select material**
   - Choose healthy, non-flowering stem tips
   - Each cutting should be 10-12cm long
   - Stems should be green and firm
   - Avoid any stems with flower buds

2. **Prepare cutting**
   - Cut just below a node with clean scissors
   - Remove all leaves from lower half
   - Leave 4-6 leaves at top
   - Pinch off any flower buds

3. **Place in water**
   - Use clean glass or jar
   - Room temperature water
   - Submerge bottom 4-5cm of stem
   - Keep leaves above water

4. **Provide environment**
   - Warm location (20-27°C) - critical
   - Bright indirect light
   - Avoid cold windowsills

5. **Ongoing care**
   - Change water every 2 days (prevents rot)
   - Top up as needed
   - Remove any leaves that fall in

6. **Check for roots**
   - Roots appear in 7-14 days
   - Wait until roots are 3-5cm long
   - Multiple roots per cutting is ideal

7. **Pot up**
   - Rich, well-draining potting mix
   - Plant at same depth as in water
   - Water well, keep moist
   - Full sun once established

### Softwood Cuttings in Soil (Alternative)

**When to use:** When rooting many cuttings at once, or in warm, humid conditions.

**Process:**
1. Prepare cuttings as above
2. Insert into moist propagation mix
3. Cover with clear plastic to maintain humidity
4. Keep warm (20°C+ essential)
5. Vent daily to prevent mould
6. Roots develop in 2-3 weeks

## Rooting Environment

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 20-27°C (warmth critical) |
| **Humidity** | Moderate to high |
| **Light** | Bright indirect |
| **Medium** | Water or rich, moist potting mix |
| **Container** | Clean glass jar or small pots |

### Environment Setup

**Water propagation:**
Use a clean jar in a warm spot. Kitchen windowsill above sink often works well. Change water frequently - stagnant water causes rot.

**Soil propagation:**
Use seed-raising mix or fine potting blend. Keep moist but not waterlogged. Cover with clear plastic for humidity. Must be warm (20°C+) or failure is likely.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Black Stem Base (Stem Rot)

**Symptoms:** Cutting base turns black, stem collapses, cutting dies within days.

**Causes:** Too cold, water not changed, contaminated water, already infected cutting.

**Solution:** Discard affected cuttings. Start fresh with clean water and warm conditions.

**Prevention:** Ensure warmth (20°C+), change water every 2 days, use clean containers.

### Problem 2: Wilting Despite Wet Conditions

**Symptoms:** Cutting wilts even though base is in water or soil is moist.

**Causes:** Cutting taken during hot part of day, too many leaves, stem damage.

**Solution:** Reduce leaf count, increase humidity, shade from direct sun.

**Prevention:** Take cuttings in morning, remove most leaves, keep out of direct sun until rooted.

### Problem 3: Flowering Cuttings

**Symptoms:** Cutting tries to flower after rooting, grows poorly.

**Causes:** Cutting taken from flowering plant, long days triggering flower.

**Solution:** Pinch off all flower buds repeatedly. Eventually cutting will give up flowering.

**Prevention:** Take cuttings from non-flowering growth, pinch parent plant regularly.

## Pro Tips

1. **Supermarket basil is free plant material** - That pot of basil from the grocery store can yield 10+ cuttings. Take cuttings within a day or two of purchase when still healthy.

2. **Change the water obsessively** - Fresh water every other day prevents the bacterial growth that causes rot. This is the biggest factor in success vs failure.

3. **Warmth is non-negotiable** - Basil cuttings in temperatures below 18°C will almost certainly rot. Don't attempt propagation in cold conditions.

4. **Pinch for bushiness** - Once cuttings are established, pinch growing tips to encourage branching. Each pinch creates a bushier plant with more harvesting points.

5. **Make continuous batches** - Take new cuttings every few weeks through summer to ensure continuous supply. Basil is fast-growing but short-lived.

## Mother Plant Care

### Maintaining Stock Plants

Basil requires regular attention for continuous propagation material:

- **Pruning regime:** Harvest/pinch regularly to prevent flowering and encourage bushy growth.
- **Feeding:** Light, regular feeding with balanced fertiliser.
- **Health monitoring:** Watch for downy mildew (grey fuzz on leaf undersides), aphids, and slugs.
- **Rotation:** Basil is annual. Start new plants from cuttings continuously rather than trying to maintain old plants.

### Selecting Mother Plants

Choose mother plants that display:

- Healthy, dark green foliage
- Bushy growth habit
- No signs of flowering
- Characteristic fragrance for variety
