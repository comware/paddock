## Overview

Brussels sprouts microgreens deliver a nutty, mild brassica flavor that hints at the beloved roasted vegetable. The microgreens are far milder than mature Brussels sprouts - no strong sulfur notes, just a pleasant, slightly sweet taste.

Growing characteristics are similar to other brassicas - reliable germination, moderate growth rate, and susceptibility to damping off if overwatered. The seeds are slightly larger than some brassica varieties.

These microgreens are interesting for the story they tell - Brussels sprouts fans appreciate trying the microgreen version.

## Best Uses

- Salads (especially fall/winter themed)
- Garnish for roasted Brussels sprouts dishes
- Sandwich and burger topping
- Hearty grain bowls
- Pairing with bacon (classic Brussels sprouts combo)

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Brussels sprouts seeds
- Larger than typical brassica seeds
- Dark brown, round

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- 3+ years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** No

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Any - coco coir, soil, or mats.

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-35g per 10x20 tray

**Technique:** Even distribution, press gently, mist surface.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

Standard brassica protocol - cover, 18-22°C, daily misting.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** Cotyledons forming at 2-3cm

Moderate to bright light develops best flavor.

### 7. Watering

Bottom watering preferred. Keep moist but not wet.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing:** 8-12 days, height 5-7cm

**Yield:** 250-350g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Storage:** Refrigerate, 7-10 days shelf life.

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor or Patchy Germination

**Symptoms:** Uneven stand, bare patches, seeds visible on the surface still unsprouted.

**Causes:**
- Surface dried out during blackout - this seed is sown dry and has no soaked reserve to fall back on
- Seed sown too thickly, so the top layer never reaches the medium
- Medium too cold to start
- Old seed

**Solution:**
- Mist once or twice a day through blackout so the top few millimetres never dry
- Sow at 25-35g per 10x20 tray, spread evenly rather than heaped
- Keep the tray and hold 18-22°C
- Press seed gently into the medium so every seed makes contact

**Prevention:** Weigh the seed rather than eyeballing it - dense patches are the usual cause of an uneven stand.

### Problem 2: Mould, or Something That Looks Like It

**Symptoms:** White fuzz around the base of the stems, sometimes a musty smell.

**Causes:**
- Overwatering, or water sitting in the tray
- No air movement during or after blackout
- Sown too densely, so nothing dries between stems
- A long blackout - this crop sits covered for 3-4 days - gives mould more time to establish

**Solution:**

![Root hairs, fine and even on every stem, beside patchy cobwebby mould](/guides/diagrams/root-hairs-vs-mould.svg)

- **First, check whether it is actually mould.** Root hairs are extremely common on microgreens and are routinely mistaken for it. Root hairs are fine, even, radiate from the root, and vanish when misted with water. Mould is patchy, cobwebby, has a musty smell, and does not wash away.
- If it is root hairs, do nothing. They are a sign of a healthy root.
- If it is mould: get air moving immediately with a fan on low, stop bottom-watering until the surface dries, and remove affected patches.
- Sow at 25-35g per 10x20 tray rather than heavier

**Prevention:** Air movement from the moment the cover comes off, clean trays, and fresh medium every time.

### Problem 3: Leggy, Pale, Falling-Over Stems

**Symptoms:** Long thin stems, small pale leaves, the tray collapsing under its own weight.

**Causes:**
- Left in blackout past 3-4 days
- Light too weak, or too far from the tray, once uncovered
- Sown too densely, so seedlings stretch past each other
- Growing too warm

**Solution:**
- Uncover on schedule - 3-4 days for this crop, no longer
- Move the light closer or use a stronger one; pale growth means not enough, not too much
- Thin the sowing rate on the next tray
- Keep the growing area cooler once the tray is in light

**Prevention:** Set a reminder for the end of blackout. This crop harvests at 8-12 days, so a day of stretch is a meaningful part of its life.

---

### Problem 4: Harvest Window Missed

**Symptoms:** True leaves showing between the cotyledons, tougher stems, flavour stronger or coarser than expected.

**Causes:**
- Left past the harvest window - this is a 8-12 days crop and the window is short
- Judged by size rather than by stage, so the tray was left to bulk up
- Grown warm, which brings the true leaves on sooner

**Solution:**
- Harvest at the cotyledon stage, when the first pair of seed leaves is fully open and before true leaves appear
- Cut as soon as the tray is ready rather than waiting for more height - once true leaves arrive, flavour coarsens and the sweetness goes
- Refrigerate immediately after cutting; these fast brassicas wilt quickly at room temperature

**Prevention:** Diary the sow date and check daily from two days before the window opens. Fast crops are far easier to miss than slow ones.
