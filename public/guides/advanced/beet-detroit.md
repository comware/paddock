# Beet (Detroit Dark Red) Microgreens

## Quick Facts

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Days to Harvest** | 12-18 days |
| **Blackout Period** | 4-5 days |
| **Pre-soak Required** | Yes (8-12 hours) |
| **Seed Density** | 50-70g per 10x20 tray |
| **Expected Yield** | 5-7:1 ratio |
| **Flavor Profile** | Earthy, sweet beet flavor with stunning red stems |

## Overview

Detroit Dark Red beet microgreens produce beautiful red/pink stems with green cotyledons - a striking color combination. The flavor is distinctly beet - earthy and sweet - but more delicate than mature beets. The color adds dramatic visual interest to any dish.

Beet microgreens are classified as advanced due to their unique seed structure. Beet "seeds" are actually seed clusters (multigerm) containing 2-4 seeds each. This results in clumpy germination that can be challenging to manage. The slower growth and susceptibility to fungal issues add to the difficulty.

Both Detroit Dark Red and Bull's Blood varieties are popular, with Bull's Blood having darker, more purple coloring.

## Best Uses

- Salads (color contrast)
- Garnish for beet dishes
- Upscale plating
- Smoothies (color + nutrition)
- Sandwiches and grain bowls

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Detroit Dark Red beet variety
- Seeds are actually seed clusters (multigerm)
- Irregular, corky appearance

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Sealed container
- 3-4 years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes - essential for beet seeds

**Process:**
1. Measure 50-70g of seed clusters
2. Soak in room temperature water 8-12 hours
3. Change water once if possible (removes inhibitors)
4. Drain and rinse before planting
5. Seeds will soften but not dramatically change appearance

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Coco coir or soil - good drainage essential

**Setup:**
1. 2-3cm of medium
2. Pre-moisten well
3. Level surface

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 50-70g per 10x20 tray

**Technique:**
1. Spread soaked seed clusters evenly
2. Expect clumpy germination - seed clusters produce multiple plants
3. Press gently into medium
4. Mist surface
5. Do NOT cover with medium

**Note:** Each "seed" produces 2-4 plants, so adjust expectations for spacing.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-5 days

**Method:**
1. Cover with inverted tray
2. Light weight (1-2kg) helps
3. Keep at 18-22°C
4. Mist daily

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Seeds swell, root emergence begins
- Day 3-4: Multiple stems per cluster emerge
- Day 4-5: Red/pink coloring visible in stems

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When stems are 2-3cm with coloring visible

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate to bright light
- 12-16 hours per day
- Light intensity affects stem color depth

**What happens:**
- Red/pink coloring intensifies
- Cotyledons green up
- Clumpy growth pattern continues

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering strongly preferred

**Frequency:**
- Once or twice daily
- Keep moist but not wet
- Beets are prone to root rot if overwatered

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 12-18 days from seeding
- Height: 5-8cm
- Vibrant red stems
- Cotyledons fully developed

**Technique:**
1. Cut just above medium
2. Harvest clumps together - separating is tedious
3. Morning harvest for best color

**Yield:** 250-400g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Gentle washing - color can stain hands
2. Dry carefully
3. Handle in clumps rather than separating

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate immediately
- Shelf life: 7-10 days (good shelf life)
- Color holds well refrigerated

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Clumpy Growth

**Symptoms:** Multiple plants growing in tight clusters

**Causes:** Normal - beet seeds are multigerm

**Solution:** Harvest clumps together, or thin if severe

**Prevention:** Accept this characteristic, or seek monogerm varieties

### Problem 2: Root Rot

**Symptoms:** Brown, mushy roots, wilting despite wet soil

**Causes:** Overwatering, poor drainage

**Solution:** Reduce watering, improve drainage

**Prevention:** Bottom water only, well-draining medium

### Problem 3: Pale Color

**Symptoms:** Stems not as red as expected

**Causes:** Insufficient light, too warm

**Solution:** More light, cooler temperatures

**Prevention:** Bright light from start, moderate temps

### Problem 4: Mold in Clusters

**Symptoms:** Fungal growth between tightly packed stems

**Causes:** Moisture trapped in clusters, poor airflow

**Solution:** Improve airflow, reduce watering

**Prevention:** Good ventilation, conservative watering

## Pro Tips

1. **Embrace the Clumps** - Fighting clumpy growth is futile. Market the clusters as rustic, natural character.

2. **Color is King** - The red stems are the selling point. Maximize with good light.

3. **Beet Dish Pairing** - Perfect garnish for beet-based dishes. The story sells.

4. **Staining Warning** - Beet juice stains everything. Warn customers and handle carefully.

5. **Long Shelf Life** - Unlike basil, beet microgreens keep well. Good for farmers market.

6. **Compare Varieties** - Try both Detroit and Bull's Blood to see which works better for you.

- **Year-round crop:** No strong seasonal preference
