## Overview

Shiso (also called perilla) is a distinctive Japanese herb with a complex flavor profile that's difficult to describe - hints of mint, basil, anise, cinnamon, and even cumin combine into something unique. Both green and red (purple) varieties are available, with red shiso being particularly visually striking.

This is an advanced crop due to slow germination, temperature sensitivity, and lower yields. However, shiso microgreens command premium prices and have a devoted following in Japanese cuisine and among adventurous chefs.

The leaves have a beautiful ruffled texture even at the microgreen stage, and red shiso provides stunning color that holds well after harvest.

## Best Uses

- Sushi and sashimi (traditional)
- Japanese cuisine broadly
- Asian fusion
- Unique garnish
- High-end plating

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Green shiso or Red shiso (different varieties)
- Small, round seeds
- Note which color you're purchasing

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Fresh seeds important - shiso loses viability quickly
- Use within 1-2 years

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes - helps slow germination

**Process:**
1. Measure 20-30g seeds
2. Soak in cool water 12-24 hours
3. Some sources suggest cold stratification (refrigerator soak)
4. Drain and rinse before planting

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Fine coco coir or soil, well-draining

**Setup:**
1. 1-2cm of medium
2. Pre-moisten well
3. Level surface

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 20-30g per 10x20 tray

**Technique:**
1. Spread soaked seeds evenly
2. Seeds need light to germinate - do NOT cover
3. Press very gently into surface
4. Mist lightly

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-6 days (slow germinator)

**Method:**
1. Cover loosely - some light is actually beneficial for shiso
2. Keep at 18-22°C
3. Mist daily
4. Patience required - germination is slow and uneven

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-3: Slow to show activity
- Day 4-5: First signs of germination
- Day 5-6: Uneven emergence begins

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When germination is visible

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate light
- 12-16 hours per day
- Red shiso needs light to develop color

**What happens:**
- Very gradual growth
- Red variety develops purple/red coloring
- Distinctive ruffled leaves begin forming

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering preferred

**Frequency:**
- Once daily typically sufficient
- Keep moist but not wet
- Shiso is moderately prone to damping off

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 14-21 days from seeding (patient crop)
- Height: 4-6cm
- Distinctive shiso leaf shape visible
- Red variety has developed color

**Technique:**
1. Cut just above medium
2. Harvest progressively as plants mature
3. Handle gently - leaves are delicate

**Yield:** 80-150g from 10x20 tray (lower yield)

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Gentle handling essential
2. Minimal washing
3. Use quickly

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate at 4-8°C
- Shelf life: 4-6 days
- Red color holds well

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Very Poor Germination

**Symptoms:** Few seeds germinate

**Causes:** Old seeds, insufficient soak, covered too deeply

**Solution:** Fresh seeds, longer soak, surface sow only

**Prevention:** Use seeds within 1 year, soak 24 hours, never cover

### Problem 2: No Color (Red Variety)

**Symptoms:** Red shiso stays green

**Causes:** Insufficient light, wrong variety

**Solution:** More light exposure, verify variety

**Prevention:** Adequate light from start, verified red shiso seeds

### Problem 3: Slow Growth

**Symptoms:** Takes even longer than expected

**Causes:** Cool temperatures, insufficient light

**Solution:** Warmer conditions, more light

**Prevention:** Maintain 18-22°C, adequate lighting

## Pro Tips

1. **Fresh Seeds Critical** - Shiso seeds lose viability quickly. Buy from reputable source, use promptly.

2. **Japanese Restaurant Niche** - This is a specialty crop. Build relationships with Japanese restaurants.

3. **Red for Impact** - Red shiso provides stunning visual impact. Worth the lower yield for premium positioning.

4. **Price Accordingly** - Low yield + difficulty + specialty demand = premium pricing justified.

5. **True Leaves Add Value** - Shiso's distinctive ruffled leaves are part of the appeal. Let them develop slightly.

6. **Umami Partner** - Shiso pairs beautifully with soy sauce, rice, and fish - core Japanese flavors.
