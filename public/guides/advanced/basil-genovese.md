## Overview

Genovese basil microgreens deliver the classic sweet basil flavor prized in Italian cooking. The aroma is intense - you'll smell basil before you see it. These microgreens provide a concentrated basil experience in a tender, easy-to-use format.

Basil is classified as advanced due to its temperature sensitivity and tendency toward damping off. It's a warm-weather crop that sulks in cool conditions and develops fungal problems easily when overwatered. However, master the conditions and basil becomes very rewarding.

The seeds are mucilaginous - they form a gel coating when wet, which can aid moisture retention but also promotes fungal growth if overdone.

## Best Uses

- Caprese salads (primary use)
- Pizza and pasta garnish
- Bruschetta topping
- Italian dishes of all kinds
- Pesto (use larger quantities)

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Genovese or Italian Large Leaf variety
- Tiny, black/dark brown seeds
- Fresh seeds (basil loses viability faster than some)

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Sealed container
- Use within 2 years for best germination

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** No - avoid soaking

Basil seeds are mucilaginous. They develop a gel coating when wet that makes them clump together. Do NOT soak.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Fine coco coir or soil with excellent drainage

**Setup:**
1. 1-2cm of medium
2. Pre-moisten well - but not saturated
3. Very level surface - seeds are tiny

**Critical:** Good drainage is essential for basil.

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 20-30g per 10x20 tray (seeds are tiny)

**Technique:**
1. Sprinkle seeds evenly using shaker
2. Seeds will stick when misted
3. Do NOT cover with medium
4. Mist gently - gel coating forms immediately

**Note:** The mucilaginous coating helps seeds adhere to surface - this is normal.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-5 days

**Method:**
1. Cover loosely - airflow is important
2. No heavy weight - basil is delicate
3. Keep at 21-25°C - warmth is essential
4. Mist lightly daily - don't overdo it

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Seeds form gel, slight swelling
- Day 3-4: White roots visible, slow stem emergence
- Day 4-5: Pale stems with forming cotyledons

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When cotyledons are forming (day 4-5)

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate to bright light
- 12-16 hours per day
- Keep temperature warm (20-25°C)

**What happens:**
- Leaves green up and release basil aroma
- Growth accelerates in warmth
- Cotyledons expand and flatten

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering is essential

**Frequency:**
- Once daily usually sufficient
- Never let basil sit in water
- Better slightly dry than too wet

**Critical:** Basil is extremely prone to damping off. Water conservatively.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 12-18 days from seeding
- Height: 4-6cm
- Strong basil aroma when touched
- Cotyledons fully developed, may see first true leaves

**Technique:**
1. Cut just above medium
2. Harvest in morning for strongest aroma
3. Handle gently - basil bruises and blackens easily

**Yield:** 100-200g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Minimal handling - basil blackens when bruised
2. Don't wash unless necessary
3. Dry quickly if washed

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate carefully (basil is cold-sensitive)
- Best stored around 10-12°C if possible
- Shelf life: 3-5 days (shorter than many microgreens)
- Use quickly for best quality

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Damping Off

**Symptoms:** Seedlings collapse, stems thin and brown at base

**Causes:** Overwatering, poor drainage, cool temperatures

**Solution:** Reduce watering, improve drainage, increase temp

**Prevention:** Bottom water only, excellent drainage, keep warm

### Problem 2: Slow/No Germination

**Symptoms:** Seeds don't emerge or emerge very slowly

**Causes:** Too cold, old seeds, insufficient moisture

**Solution:** Increase temperature to 22-25°C, fresh seeds

**Prevention:** Warm location, use seeds within 2 years

### Problem 3: Black Spots on Leaves

**Symptoms:** Dark spots or edges on harvested greens

**Causes:** Cold damage, handling damage, age

**Solution:** Warmer storage, gentler handling

**Prevention:** Keep above 10°C, handle minimally

### Problem 4: Weak Aroma

**Symptoms:** Basil flavor not as strong as expected

**Causes:** Insufficient light, too cool, harvested too early

**Solution:** More light, warmer temps, let grow longer

**Prevention:** Optimal conditions from start

## Pro Tips

1. **Heat Lover** - Basil is tropical. Keep it warm. Cool temperatures cause problems and slow growth.

2. **No Soaking** - The mucilaginous seeds become a clumpy mess if soaked. Plant dry.

3. **Smell Test** - If your basil doesn't smell strongly, conditions aren't optimal. Adjust!

4. **Quick to Market** - Short shelf life means frequent small plantings rather than large batches.

5. **Italian Restaurants** - Build relationships with Italian restaurants. They use basil constantly.

6. **Multiple Varieties** - Once you master Genovese, try Thai and Lemon basil for variety.

## Scheduling Recommendations

- **Continuous supply:** Plant every 5-7 days
- **Summer is easier** - Warmer ambient temps help
- **Winter challenge** - May need heat mats in cool climates
