# Cilantro (Coriander) Microgreens

## Overview

Cilantro microgreens deliver the distinctive cilantro flavor that's essential to Mexican, Asian, and Middle Eastern cuisines. The microgreens have a milder, more delicate taste than mature cilantro while maintaining the characteristic citrusy, slightly soapy (to some) flavor.

This is classified as an advanced crop due to slow, uneven germination and susceptibility to fungal issues. The seeds are actually fruits containing two seeds each, which is why germination seems irregular. Splitting the seeds before soaking can improve germination.

Cilantro microgreens are particularly valuable because mature cilantro bolts quickly in warm weather, making microgreens a year-round alternative for that essential cilantro flavor.

## Best Uses

- Tacos, burritos, Mexican cuisine (essential)
- Vietnamese pho and banh mi
- Thai dishes
- Indian chutneys and curries
- Fresh salsas and guacamole

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Whole coriander seeds (cilantro = leaves, coriander = seeds)
- Round, ribbed, tan/brown seeds
- Split seeds germinate faster and more evenly

**Seed preparation:**
- Gently crush or split seeds before soaking
- This breaks the two-seed fruit into halves
- Dramatically improves germination rate

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Sealed container
- 2-3 years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** Yes - essential for cilantro

**Process:**
1. Split seeds gently (rolling pin or mortar)
2. Measure 40-50g
3. Soak in room temperature water 12-24 hours
4. Change water halfway through if possible
5. Drain and rinse thoroughly
6. Seeds may have slight sliminess - normal

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Fine coco coir or soil - good drainage essential

**Setup:**
1. 2-3cm of moistened medium
2. Level surface carefully
3. Pre-moisten but don't saturate

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 40-50g per 10x20 tray

**Technique:**
1. Spread pre-soaked, split seeds evenly
2. Seeds should be close but not piled
3. Press gently into medium
4. Mist surface lightly
5. Do not cover with medium

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 4-6 days (longer than most microgreens)

**Method:**
1. Cover with dome or inverted tray
2. Light weight optional
3. Keep at 18-22°C - temperature is critical
4. Mist daily but don't overwater
5. Check for mold - cilantro is susceptible

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Seeds swell, some splitting visible
- Day 3-4: White roots emerge, slow stem growth
- Day 5-6: Pale stems visible, cotyledons forming

**Note:** Germination will be uneven - this is normal for cilantro.

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When majority of stems are 2-3cm

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate light is sufficient
- 12-14 hours per day
- Avoid intense heat from lights

**What happens:**
- Gradual greening (slower than brassicas)
- Distinctive cilantro leaf shape develops
- Growth rate increases

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering strongly preferred

**Frequency:**
- Once or twice daily
- Keep moist but never wet
- Cilantro is very prone to damping off

**Critical:** This is where most cilantro crops fail. Err on the side of slightly dry rather than too wet.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 14-21 days from seeding (patient crop)
- Height: 5-8cm
- First true leaves showing cilantro leaf shape
- Aroma is strong when brushed

**Technique:**
1. Cut just above medium
2. Harvest progressively as plants reach size
3. Morning harvest for best flavor

**Yield:** 150-250g from 10x20 tray (lower yield but premium pricing)

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Gentle washing
2. Dry carefully - cilantro bruises easily
3. Handle minimally

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate immediately
- Shelf life: 5-7 days
- Store dry - moisture accelerates decay

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Poor/Uneven Germination

**Symptoms:** Patchy growth, many ungerminated seeds

**Causes:** Seeds not split, insufficient soak, old seeds

**Solution:** Split seeds next time, longer soak

**Prevention:** Always split seeds, soak 12-24 hours, fresh seeds

### Problem 2: Damping Off

**Symptoms:** Seedlings collapse, mushy stems, mold

**Causes:** Overwatering, poor drainage, poor airflow

**Solution:** Reduce watering, improve airflow, may need to restart

**Prevention:** Bottom water only, excellent drainage, good ventilation

### Problem 3: Very Slow Growth

**Symptoms:** Growth much slower than expected

**Causes:** Too cold, insufficient light, poor quality seeds

**Solution:** Increase temperature (within range), more light

**Prevention:** Maintain optimal temps, adequate lighting

### Problem 4: Yellow/Pale Leaves

**Symptoms:** Greens not vibrant green

**Causes:** Insufficient light, nutrient deficiency

**Solution:** More light exposure, ensure quality medium

**Prevention:** Adequate light from start of light phase

## Pro Tips

1. **Split the Seeds** - This single step dramatically improves germination. Don't skip it!

2. **Patience Required** - Cilantro takes 2-3x longer than radish. Plan your schedule accordingly.

3. **Air is Your Friend** - Good air circulation prevents the fungal issues cilantro is prone to.

4. **Premium Pricing** - The difficulty justifies charging more. Restaurants need year-round cilantro.

5. **True Leaf Bonus** - Unlike many microgreens, cilantro's true leaves are actually desirable - they have the distinctive cilantro shape.

6. **Summer Alternative** - When mature cilantro bolts in heat, microgreens become the only cilantro option.

## Scheduling Recommendations

- **Continuous supply:** Plant every 7-10 days (longer cycle)
- **Higher failure rate** - Plant backup trays, especially while learning
- **Year-round value** - Especially important in summer when mature cilantro bolts

## Market Value

Cilantro microgreens command premium prices due to:
- Difficulty of growing
- Strong restaurant demand
- Year-round availability (unlike mature cilantro)
- Essential flavor with no substitute
