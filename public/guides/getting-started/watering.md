# Watering Guide

Proper watering is the difference between thriving microgreens and failed trays. Learn when, how much, and which method to use.

---

## The Golden Rule

**Consistent moisture, never waterlogged.**

Microgreens need:
- Roots that stay moist (not dry, not drowning)
- Leaves that stay dry (reduces disease)
- Good drainage (no standing water in medium)

---

## Watering Methods

### 1. Top Misting (Blackout Phase)

**When to use:** During germination and blackout phase

**How to do it:**
1. Use a spray bottle with fine mist setting
2. Spray evenly across the entire surface
3. Aim for seeds/medium, not pooling water
4. Mist until surface glistens, not soaked

**Frequency:** Once daily, or when surface looks dry

**Amount:** Light mist - 50-100ml per tray typically

**Why misting works for blackout:**
- Seeds need moisture to germinate
- Fine mist won't displace seeds
- Maintains humidity in covered environment
- Easy to apply without removing weight

---

### 2. Bottom Watering (Light Phase)

**When to use:** After moving trays to light (primary method)

**How to do it:**
1. Place growing tray (with holes) inside solid tray
2. Add water to solid tray - about 1-2cm depth
3. Wait 15-30 minutes
4. Check that medium has absorbed water
5. **Drain any remaining water**

**Frequency:** 1-2 times daily depending on conditions

**Amount:** Enough to wet the medium through absorption

**Why bottom watering is better:**
- Keeps leaves dry (prevents fungal disease)
- Encourages roots to grow downward
- More even moisture distribution
- Harder to overwater
- Cleaner greens at harvest

---

### 3. Direct Pouring (Emergency Only)

**When to use:** If medium has dried out completely

**How to do it:**
1. Pour water slowly along the edge of the tray
2. Let it seep across rather than flooding center
3. Drain excess immediately

**Warning:** This can displace seeds and splash onto leaves. Use only when necessary.

## Watering Schedule

### By Growth Phase

| Phase | Method | Frequency | Signs to Water |
|-------|--------|-----------|----------------|
| **Day 1 (Seeding)** | Pre-moisten medium | Once | Before adding seeds |
| **Blackout (Days 2-5)** | Top misting | 1x daily | Surface looks dry |
| **Light (Days 5-14)** | Bottom watering | 1-2x daily | Medium surface feels dry |

### By Environment

| Conditions | Adjustment |
|------------|------------|
| **Hot (>25°C)** | Water 2x daily, monitor closely |
| **Dry (<40% humidity)** | Water more frequently |
| **Cool (<18°C)** | Water less, medium stays moist longer |
| **Humid (>60%)** | Reduce watering, watch for mold |

### By Variety

| Variety Type | Water Needs |
|--------------|-------------|
| Large seeds (Sunflower, Pea) | Moderate - medium dries slower |
| Fast growers (Radish) | More frequent - rapid consumption |
| Small seeds (Broccoli, Kale) | Careful - delicate roots |
| Mucilaginous (Basil, Chia) | Less - hold moisture in gel coating |

## How to Check If Watering Is Needed

### Visual Signs

**Needs water:**
- Surface of medium looks lighter/tan colored
- Medium pulling away from tray edges
- Greens starting to droop

**Well watered:**
- Medium appears dark and evenly moist
- Greens standing upright
- No standing water visible

### Touch Test

1. Touch the surface of the growing medium
2. **Dry** = time to water
3. **Moist** = wait and check later
4. **Wet/soggy** = too much water, improve drainage

### Lift Test

1. Pick up the tray
2. **Light** = needs water
3. **Heavy** = well watered
4. With practice, you'll know the difference immediately

## Signs of Watering Problems

### Underwatering

**Symptoms:**
- Wilting leaves that don't recover
- Dry, cracked medium surface
- Stunted growth
- Yellowing from dehydration

**Fix:**
- Water immediately using bottom watering
- Check more frequently
- Consider humidifying the environment

### Overwatering

**Symptoms:**
- Yellowing at the base of stems
- Soft, mushy stems
- Mold or fungal growth
- Sour or musty smell
- Standing water in tray

**Fix:**
- Reduce watering frequency
- Improve drainage (tilt tray to drain)
- Add airflow with a small fan
- If severe, start over with new tray

## Water Quality

### Tap Water

**Usually fine for microgreens.** If you notice issues:
- Let water sit overnight (chlorine evaporates)
- Use a simple filter
- Check if water is very hard (mineral buildup)

### Filtered or RO Water

**Benefits:**
- No chlorine
- No mineral deposits on leaves
- Consistent quality

**When to use:** If tap water causes problems, or for selling/market quality

### Temperature

- **Room temperature** water is best
- Cold water can shock roots
- Warm water may encourage pathogens
- Around 18-22°C (65-72°F) is ideal

## Watering Tools

### Spray Bottles
**Best for:** Blackout phase, small operations
- Look for fine mist capability
- 500ml-1L size is convenient
- Adjustable nozzle helpful

### Watering Can
**Best for:** Filling bottom trays, multiple trays
- Long, narrow spout for control
- 2-5L capacity for convenience

### Automated Systems
**Best for:** Larger operations, consistent schedules
- Drip irrigation
- Flood tables
- Misting systems with timers

## Common Watering Mistakes

### 1. Watering on a Fixed Schedule

**Problem:** "I water every day at 8am no matter what"

**Better:** Check the actual moisture level. Conditions change daily.

### 2. Watering from Above in Light Phase

**Problem:** Getting leaves wet invites disease

**Better:** Use bottom watering once greens have emerged

### 3. Not Draining Excess Water

**Problem:** Roots sitting in water rot

**Better:** Always drain solid tray after bottom watering

### 4. Using Ice Cold Water

**Problem:** Shocks the plant, slows growth

**Better:** Room temperature water

### 5. Misting Too Much During Blackout

**Problem:** Creates puddles, seeds float and clump

**Better:** Light, even mist that doesn't pool
