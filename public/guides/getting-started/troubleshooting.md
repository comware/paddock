# Troubleshooting Guide

Common problems and how to fix them. Don't worry - even experienced growers encounter these issues.

---

## Quick Diagnosis

| Symptom | Most Likely Cause | Quick Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| Nothing sprouted | Old seeds, too dry, too cold | Fresh seeds, moisture, warmth |
| White fuzz/mold | Too wet, no airflow | Reduce water, add fan |
| Leggy/falling over | Not enough light or weight | More light, weight in blackout |
| Yellow greens | Still in blackout, low light | Move to light, increase duration |
| Brown/slimy stems | Overwatering, disease | Start over, sanitize trays |
| Hulls stuck on leaves | Dry conditions | Mist and brush gently |
| Uneven growth | Uneven seeds or light | Spread evenly, rotate tray |
| Bitter taste | Harvested too late | Harvest earlier |

## Problem 1: Poor or No Germination

**What you see:** Few or no seeds sprouting after 3-4 days

### Possible Causes

1. **Old or dead seeds**
   - Seeds lose viability over time
   - Improper storage accelerates decline
   - Some batches have poor germination rates

2. **Temperature too low**
   - Below 15°C slows germination significantly
   - Seeds may rot before sprouting

3. **Too dry**
   - Seeds need consistent moisture to germinate
   - Surface dried out during blackout

4. **Too wet**
   - Seeds drowned and rotted
   - Fermentation occurred

5. **Seeds not pre-soaked (when required)**
   - Large seeds with hard shells need hydration

### Solutions

- **Test germination first:** Soak 10 seeds, place on wet paper towel in plastic bag. If fewer than 7 sprout in 3 days, seed quality is poor.
- **Check temperature:** Use thermometer. Move to warmer location if below 18°C.
- **Maintain moisture:** Check daily during blackout, mist as needed.
- **Don't overwater:** Medium should be moist, not dripping wet.
- **Pre-soak large seeds:** 8-12 hours for sunflower, pea, buckwheat.

## Problem 2: Mold or Fungus

**What you see:** White fuzzy growth, slimy patches, or musty smell

### Possible Causes

1. **Overwatering**
   - Wet conditions favor fungal growth
   - Standing water creates anaerobic conditions

2. **Poor air circulation**
   - Stagnant air allows spores to settle
   - High humidity compounds the issue

3. **Contaminated supplies**
   - Dirty trays carry spores
   - Unsterile medium harbors pathogens

4. **Too high seed density**
   - No air movement between seedlings
   - Creates humid microclimate

5. **High ambient humidity (>70%)**
   - Environmental moisture promotes mold

### Solutions

**If caught early:**
- Remove affected areas immediately
- Reduce watering
- Add a small fan for airflow
- Spray remaining greens with 3% hydrogen peroxide solution
- May be salvageable if problem is localized

**If widespread:**
- Discard the tray (compost it)
- Do NOT harvest for eating
- Sanitize all equipment before next use

**Prevention:**
- Use clean, sanitized trays
- Avoid overwatering
- Ensure air circulation (small fan)
- Don't overcrowd seeds
- Use sterile growing medium

### Root Hairs vs. Mold

**Don't confuse them!**

| Root Hairs | Mold |
|------------|------|
| Fine white fuzz on roots | Fuzzy growth on stems/leaves |
| Concentrated near medium surface | Spreads across surface |
| Looks like cotton around roots | May have colored spots |
| No smell | Musty or sour smell |
| Normal and healthy! | Problem - address immediately |

## Problem 3: Leggy, Weak Stems

**What you see:** Tall, thin, pale stems that fall over

### Possible Causes

1. **Insufficient light**
   - Seedlings stretch toward light source
   - Not enough intensity or duration

2. **Light source too far away**
   - Plants stretch to reach it
   - Weak stem development

3. **No weight during blackout**
   - Seedlings didn't develop strength
   - Missing the resistance training

4. **Temperature too high**
   - Promotes rapid, weak growth
   - Stems elongate without strengthening

### Solutions

- **During blackout:** Always use 1-2kg weight on top
- **Light phase:** Position lights 15-30cm above trays
- **Duration:** 12-16 hours of light daily
- **Intensity:** Use appropriate grow lights or strong natural light
- **Temperature:** Keep below 24°C during growth
- **If already leggy:** Increase light, they may strengthen somewhat

## Problem 4: Yellowing Greens

**What you see:** Pale yellow or lime green instead of vibrant green

### Possible Causes

1. **Still needs to green up**
   - Normal immediately after blackout
   - Takes 24-48 hours to turn green

2. **Insufficient light**
   - Chlorophyll needs light to develop
   - May need more intensity or duration

3. **Overwatering**
   - Root damage reduces nutrient uptake
   - Causes yellowing from base up

4. **Temperature stress**
   - Too cold slows chlorophyll production
   - Too hot can cause chlorosis

### Solutions

- **Wait:** If just removed from blackout, give it 24-48 hours
- **Increase light:** More hours, closer position, stronger source
- **Check roots:** If brown/slimy, overwatering is the issue
- **Adjust temperature:** 18-22°C is optimal

## Problem 5: Seed Hulls Not Shedding

**What you see:** Seed coats stuck on cotyledons (leaves)

### Possible Causes

1. **Low humidity during germination**
   - Hulls dried and stuck tight
   - More common in dry environments

2. **Insufficient pre-soaking**
   - Hull didn't soften enough
   - Especially affects large seeds

3. **Poor seed quality**
   - Some batches have tougher hulls
   - Older seeds may have dried hulls

### Solutions

- **Mist heavily** and wait 30 minutes, then gently brush
- **Run your hand** across the tops - loose hulls stick to your palm
- **Pick off by hand** for market-quality appearance
- **Shake harvested greens** - many hulls fall off
- **Prevention:** Thorough pre-soaking, maintain humidity

## Problem 6: Uneven Growth

**What you see:** Some areas tall and full, others sparse or short

### Possible Causes

1. **Uneven seed distribution**
   - Clumps in some areas, gaps in others
   - Rushed seeding process

2. **Uneven watering**
   - Some areas too dry, others too wet
   - Improper misting technique

3. **Uneven light**
   - One side closer to window/light
   - Shadows from obstructions

4. **Temperature variations**
   - Drafts, heat sources
   - Edge effects near walls

### Solutions

- **Seeding:** Take time to spread evenly, single layer
- **Watering:** Ensure even coverage, check all corners
- **Light:** Rotate trays 180° daily
- **Placement:** Away from vents, radiators, drafty areas

## Problem 7: Damping Off (Seedling Collapse)

**What you see:** Seedlings suddenly fall over, stems pinched at base

### Possible Causes

1. **Fungal infection**
   - Pythium, Fusarium, or Rhizoctonia fungi
   - Lives in soil or on seed surface

2. **Overwatering + poor drainage**
   - Creates ideal fungal conditions
   - Roots can't get oxygen

3. **Contaminated equipment**
   - Fungal spores on dirty trays
   - Reusing contaminated medium

### Solutions

**Unfortunately, there's no saving a tray with damping off.**

- Discard the entire tray
- Sanitize equipment with hydrogen peroxide or bleach solution
- Start fresh with sterile medium
- Improve drainage
- Reduce watering frequency
- Add air circulation

## Problem 8: Slow Growth

**What you see:** Greens taking much longer than expected

### Possible Causes

1. **Temperature too low**
   - Growth slows dramatically below 18°C
   - Check actual temperature, not room setting

2. **Insufficient light**
   - Plants don't photosynthesize efficiently
   - Growth slows to a crawl

3. **Underwatering**
   - Stressed plants grow slowly
   - Check moisture levels

4. **Old seeds**
   - Lower vigor even if they germinate
   - Weaker seedlings from the start

### Solutions

- **Temperature:** Maintain 18-24°C consistently
- **Light:** 12-16 hours of good light daily
- **Water:** Consistent moisture, don't let it dry out
- **Seeds:** Use fresh seeds from reliable sources

## Problem 9: Bitter or Off-Flavors

**What you see:** Greens taste unpleasantly bitter or strange

### Possible Causes

1. **Harvested too late**
   - Older greens become bitter
   - True leaves well-developed

2. **Heat stress**
   - High temperatures increase bitterness
   - Especially affects brassicas

3. **Light stress**
   - Too much direct, intense light
   - Can increase bitter compounds

4. **Variety characteristic**
   - Some varieties are naturally bitter
   - Mustards, arugula have stronger flavors

### Solutions

- **Harvest earlier:** When cotyledons just fully open
- **Control temperature:** Keep below 24°C
- **Moderate light:** Bright but not scorching
- **Try different varieties:** If you dislike bitterness, choose mild varieties (sunflower, pea)

---

## Problem 10: Short Shelf Life

**What you see:** Greens wilting or spoiling within days

### Possible Causes

1. **Harvested wet**
   - Excess moisture promotes decay
   - Didn't dry properly after washing

2. **Not refrigerated**
   - Room temperature accelerates spoilage
   - Greens respire and deteriorate

3. **Bruised during handling**
   - Damaged tissue breaks down quickly
   - Cut roughly instead of cleanly

4. **Harvested too late**
   - Older greens don't last as long
   - Cellular structure weakening

### Solutions

- **Harvest in morning:** Greens most hydrated
- **Cut cleanly:** Sharp scissors, single motion
- **Dry thoroughly:** Salad spinner or towels
- **Store cold:** 2-4°C (refrigerator)
- **Paper towel lining:** Absorbs excess moisture
- **Don't wash until using:** Moisture accelerates decay

## When to Start Over

Sometimes the best solution is a fresh start. Consider starting over when:

- Mold covers more than 25% of tray
- Damping off has started
- Severe root rot (slimy, smelly)
- Germination rate below 50%
- Contamination suspected

**Don't feel bad about starting over.** It's part of learning, and a fresh tray with proper technique will outperform a struggling one.

## Preventing Problems

### Hygiene
- Sanitize trays between uses
- Use fresh, sterile growing medium
- Clean hands before handling seeds
- Store seeds properly

### Environment
- Maintain 18-24°C
- Keep humidity 40-60%
- Ensure air circulation
- Provide adequate light

### Technique
- Pre-soak large seeds
- Use correct seeding density
- Apply weight during blackout
- Bottom water during light phase
