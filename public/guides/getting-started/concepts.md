# Core Concepts

Understanding these fundamental terms will help you succeed with microgreens.

---

## Pre-Soak

**What it is:** Hydrating seeds by soaking them in water before planting.

**Why it matters:**
- Softens hard seed coats for faster germination
- Ensures even moisture absorption across all seeds
- Improves germination rate and consistency
- Reduces time to harvest

**How long to soak:**

| Seed Type | Examples | Soak Duration |
|-----------|----------|---------------|
| Large seeds | Sunflower, Pea, Fava | 8-12 hours |
| Medium seeds | Buckwheat, Beet | 6-8 hours |
| Small seeds | Radish | 4-6 hours (optional) |
| Tiny seeds | Broccoli, Basil, Amaranth | Not required |

**How to do it:**
1. Place seeds in a bowl or jar
2. Cover with 3x volume of room temperature water
3. Leave overnight or for recommended duration
4. Drain and rinse before planting

**Warning:** Don't over-soak! Seeds left in water more than 24 hours can ferment and become unusable.

---

## Blackout Phase

**What it is:** The initial dark period after planting, typically 2-5 days.

**Why it matters:**
- Seeds don't need light to germinate
- Darkness prevents premature greening
- Weight applied during blackout strengthens stems
- Mimics natural soil covering

**Where to put trays during blackout:**
- Inside a closet or cabinet
- Under a shelf with cloth draped over
- Stacked with another tray on top (inverted)
- Any consistently dark spot at room temperature (18-24°C)

**The weight is essential:**
- Place 1-2kg on top of the cover tray
- Use books, bricks, or a water-filled container
- This pressure makes seedlings push harder
- Result: thicker, stronger stems

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Seeds swell, tiny white roots emerge
- Day 2-3: Pale/yellow shoots push upward
- Day 3-5: Shoots reach 3-5cm, pushing against weight

**When to end blackout:** When shoots are 3-5cm tall and pushing hard against the cover tray.

---

## Light Phase

**What it is:** The growth period after blackout when trays are exposed to light.

**Why it matters:**
- Photosynthesis begins - this is when greens turn green
- Cotyledons (seed leaves) fully open
- Growth rate accelerates
- Flavor develops

**Light requirements:**
- Natural light: Bright indirect or 4-6 hours direct sunlight
- Artificial: Full-spectrum LED grow lights
- Duration: 12-16 hours per day
- Distance: 15-30cm from light source

**What happens:**
- Yellow shoots turn vibrant green within 24-48 hours
- Cotyledons spread open
- Stems continue strengthening
- Seed hulls naturally shed

---

## Bottom Watering

**What it is:** Adding water to a tray underneath the growing tray, allowing the medium to wick moisture upward.

**Why it matters:**
- Keeps leaves dry (prevents disease)
- Encourages roots to grow downward
- More even moisture distribution
- Harder to overwater

**How to do it:**
1. Place growing tray (with holes) inside solid tray
2. Add 1-2cm water to solid tray
3. Wait 15-30 minutes for absorption
4. Drain any remaining water

**When to use:**
- During light phase (primary method)
- Can also use during blackout if medium is drying

**Alternative - Top watering/misting:**
- Use spray bottle for gentle misting
- Preferred during blackout phase
- Avoid heavy streams that displace seeds

---

## Germination

**What it is:** The process of a seed sprouting and beginning to grow.

**The stages:**
1. **Imbibition:** Seed absorbs water, swells
2. **Activation:** Internal enzymes activate
3. **Radicle emergence:** First root breaks through
4. **Shoot emergence:** Stem pushes upward
5. **Cotyledon opening:** Seed leaves unfold

**Optimal conditions:**
- Temperature: 18-24°C (65-75°F)
- Moisture: Consistently moist, not waterlogged
- Oxygen: Seeds need air exchange
- Light: Not needed until shoots emerge

---

## Cotyledons vs True Leaves

**Cotyledons (seed leaves):**
- First leaves to emerge
- Contain stored nutrients from seed
- Often different shape than adult plant leaves
- What you eat when harvesting microgreens

**True leaves:**
- Second set of leaves (appear after cotyledons)
- Look like miniature versions of adult plant
- Indicate the plant is photosynthesizing well
- Harvest before or just as true leaves appear

**Harvest timing:**
- Most microgreens: Harvest when cotyledons fully open
- Some varieties: Wait until first true leaves for more substance
- Too late: True leaves well-developed, flavor becomes stronger/bitter

## Yield Ratio

**What it is:** The weight of harvested greens divided by the weight of seeds planted.

**How to calculate:**
```
Yield Ratio = Harvest Weight / Seed Weight
```

**Example:** 80g seeds → 640g harvest = 8:1 ratio

**Typical ratios by variety:**

| Variety | Expected Ratio |
|---------|----------------|
| Sunflower | 6-8:1 |
| Pea Shoots | 5-7:1 |
| Radish | 6-10:1 |
| Broccoli | 8-12:1 |
| Wheatgrass | 6-8:1 |

**Why it matters:**
- Tracks your growing efficiency
- Helps calculate seed costs
- Indicates if conditions were optimal
- Improves over time with experience

## Growing Medium

**What it is:** The material seeds are planted in/on for root support and moisture.

**Common options:**

| Medium | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Coco coir** | Sterile, sustainable, good drainage | Needs rehydrating |
| **Potting soil** | Nutrient-rich, readily available | Can harbor pests/disease |
| **Hemp/jute mats** | Clean harvest, no debris | Single use, less moisture buffer |
| **Perlite mix** | Great drainage | Lightweight, can displace |

**Recommendation for beginners:** Coco coir - forgiving, consistent results.

**Depth:** 2-3cm (about 1 inch) for most microgreens.

---

## Seed Density

**What it is:** How much seed to spread per tray area.

**Why it matters:**
- Too sparse: Wasted tray space, lower yield
- Too dense: Poor airflow, mold risk, weak stems
- Just right: Seeds touching but not piled

**General guide for 10x20 tray:**

| Seed Size | Density |
|-----------|---------|
| Large (sunflower, pea) | 80-120g |
| Medium (radish, buckwheat) | 25-40g |
| Small (broccoli, kale) | 10-15g |
| Tiny (basil, amaranth) | 5-8g |

**Visual check:** Seeds should form an even single layer, touching but not overlapping.
