# Tray Setup Guide

Everything you need to know about trays, dimensions, seeding density, and planting depth.

---

## Standard Tray Dimensions

The **10x20 tray** is the industry standard for microgreens.

### Measurements

| Dimension | Imperial | Metric |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| Length | 20 inches | 50 cm |
| Width | 10 inches | 25 cm |
| Depth | 1-2.5 inches | 2.5-6 cm |
| Growing Area | 200 sq inches | 1,290 sq cm |

### Tray Types

**Growing trays (with holes):**
- Drainage holes in bottom
- Seeds and medium go here
- Allows excess water to drain

**Solid trays (no holes):**
- Water reservoir for bottom watering
- Growing tray nests inside
- Catches runoff, prevents mess

**Tip:** Buy both types - you need solid trays for bottom watering during the light phase.

## Growing Medium Setup

### Recommended Depth

**2-3cm (about 1 inch)** of growing medium for most microgreens.

| Depth | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| Too shallow (<1cm) | Dries out quickly, roots exposed |
| Optimal (2-3cm) | Good moisture buffer, strong root zone |
| Too deep (>4cm) | Wastes medium, stays too wet |

### Preparation Steps

1. **Hydrate medium** (if using coco coir bricks)
   - Add warm water to compressed coir
   - Break apart as it expands
   - Wring out excess - should be moist, not dripping

2. **Fill tray evenly**
   - Add medium to desired depth
   - Spread across entire surface
   - Level gently with hand or straight edge

3. **Don't compact**
   - Roots need air pockets
   - Lightly pat surface, don't press hard
   - Surface should be level but fluffy

4. **Pre-moisten**
   - Spray surface until evenly damp
   - Wait a moment for water to absorb
   - Add more if needed

## Seeding Density

Getting the right amount of seeds per tray is crucial for healthy growth.

### Density by Seed Size

| Seed Size | Examples | Per 10x20 Tray |
|-----------|----------|----------------|
| **Large** | Sunflower, Pea, Fava, Corn | 80-120g |
| **Medium** | Buckwheat, Radish, Beet, Chard | 25-40g |
| **Small** | Broccoli, Kale, Cabbage, Kohlrabi | 10-15g |
| **Tiny** | Basil, Amaranth, Celery, Carrot | 5-8g |

### Visual Guide

**Correct density:**
- Seeds touch each other
- Single layer (not piled)
- Even distribution
- No large gaps

**Too sparse:**
- Visible medium between seeds
- Lower yield per tray
- Wasted growing space

**Too dense:**
- Seeds piled on top of each other
- Poor air circulation
- Mold risk increases
- Weak, spindly growth

### Measuring Tips

1. **Use a scale** - Most reliable method
2. **Tare your container** - Zero out the bowl weight
3. **Be consistent** - Same density each time
4. **Adjust by variety** - Check individual guide for exact recommendations

## Planting Depth

**Key principle: Microgreen seeds are NOT buried.**

### Correct Method

1. Seeds sit **on top** of the growing medium
2. **Gently press** into surface for good contact
3. **Do not cover** with additional medium

### Why Surface Planting?

- Microgreens are harvested young (7-14 days)
- Seeds contain all nutrients needed
- Light reaches cotyledons quickly after blackout
- Easy to see germination progress
- Clean harvest with no buried stems

### The Blackout "Cover"

Instead of burying seeds, we use **blackout phase**:
- Cover tray blocks light
- Weight provides pressure
- Simulates being underground
- Seeds push against resistance = stronger stems

### Exceptions

A few varieties benefit from a **light covering** of vermiculite or fine coco:
- Cilantro (helps retain moisture around seed)
- Some herb varieties

This covering should be:
- Very thin (<3mm)
- Light and loose
- Only used when variety guide specifies

## Seeding Technique

### For Large Seeds (Sunflower, Pea)

1. Pour seeds onto prepared tray
2. Use fingers to spread across surface
3. Fill in gaps by hand
4. Press gently for soil contact
5. Mist surface

### For Medium Seeds (Radish, Buckwheat)

1. Pour seeds into center of tray
2. Use hand to spread in circular motion
3. Work toward edges
4. Shake tray gently to settle
5. Press lightly, mist

### For Small/Tiny Seeds (Broccoli, Basil)

1. Mix seeds with dry sand for even distribution (optional)
2. Sprinkle across surface slowly
3. Use shaker container for control
4. Do NOT press hard (seeds are delicate)
5. Gentle mist only

**Tip for tiny seeds:** Fold a piece of paper, pour seeds into the crease, and tap gently while moving across the tray.

## Tray Placement

### During Blackout

**Location requirements:**
- Dark (minimal light leakage)
- Room temperature (18-24°C / 65-75°F)
- Away from direct heat sources
- Accessible for daily checking

**Good spots:**
- Inside a cabinet
- Closet with door closed
- Under a shelf with dark cloth cover
- Basement (if temp is suitable)

**Stacking:**
- Multiple trays can be stacked
- Bottom tray: seeded, growing
- Top tray: inverted, with weight
- Or: multiple seeded trays, single weighted cover on top

### During Light Phase

**Location requirements:**
- Strong light (natural or artificial)
- Good air circulation
- Stable temperature
- Easy watering access

**Natural light:**
- South-facing window (northern hemisphere)
- Rotate trays daily
- Supplement in cloudy weather

**Grow lights:**
- 15-30cm above trays
- Timer for 12-16 hours daily
- Shelving rack maximizes space
