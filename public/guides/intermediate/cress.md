## Overview

Cress (also called garden cress or peppergrass) is one of the oldest cultivated microgreens, with a history of use dating back centuries. The flavor is distinctively peppery - similar to watercress but grown without water cultivation.

Cress microgreens have small, delicate leaves with a refined appearance. The traditional British "egg and cress" sandwich showcases their classic pairing with eggs. The flavor is bright and peppery without the intense heat of mustard.

Cress seeds are mucilaginous, meaning they develop a gel-like coating when wet. This makes them easy to grow on simple media like paper towels or cotton wool - a common elementary school science project.

## Best Uses

- Egg dishes (classic pairing)
- British tea sandwiches
- Garnish for soups
- Salad accent
- Simple appetizers

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Garden cress (Lepidium sativum)
- Tiny, reddish-brown seeds
- Very small - a little goes a long way

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Sealed container
- 2-3 years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** No - and avoid soaking

Cress seeds are mucilaginous. Soaking creates a gel-like mass that's difficult to spread. Plant dry.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium options:**
- Coco coir or fine soil (recommended for commercial)
- Paper towels work for home growing
- Hemp mats or cotton

**Setup:**
1. Very thin layer of fine medium (1cm)
2. Pre-moisten well
3. Level surface critical - seeds are tiny

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 15-25g per 10x20 tray (very small seeds)

**Technique:**
1. Sprinkle seeds evenly - use shaker
2. Seeds should form thin, even layer
3. Do NOT cover with medium
4. Mist very gently - seeds will stick to surface

**Note:** The mucilaginous coating means seeds adhere to surface when misted.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 2-3 days

**Method:**
1. Cover loosely - don't crush delicate sprouts
2. No weight needed
3. Keep at 15-20°C (cooler is fine)
4. Mist gently daily

**What to expect:**
- Day 1: Seeds gel and adhere to surface
- Day 2: White roots and stems visible
- Day 3: Small cotyledons forming

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When cotyledons are just forming

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate light is sufficient
- 12-14 hours per day
- Tolerates lower light than many varieties

**What happens:**
- Cotyledons green and expand
- Delicate leaves develop
- Steady but not aggressive growth

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering or very gentle misting

**Frequency:**
- Once or twice daily
- Keep moist but not wet
- Cress is delicate - overwatering damages roots

**Critical:** Very gentle watering. Cress is more fragile than radish or mustard.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 7-10 days from seeding
- Height: 3-5cm
- Cotyledons fully open
- Before true leaves for most tender texture

**Technique:**
1. Cut carefully just above medium
2. Very sharp scissors - stems are delicate
3. Harvest gently to avoid bruising

**Yield:** 100-200g from 10x20 tray (lower yield but premium pricing)

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Very gentle handling required
2. Minimal washing - pat dry
3. Best used immediately or within days

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate at 2-4°C
- Shelf life: 4-6 days (shorter than hardier greens)
- Use quickly for best quality

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Clumpy Seed Distribution

**Symptoms:** Seeds germinate in clusters

**Causes:** Uneven spreading, seeds stuck together

**Solution:** Thin carefully if severe

**Prevention:** Use shaker, spread while dry before misting

### Problem 2: Damping Off

**Symptoms:** Seedlings collapse, stems brown at base

**Causes:** Overwatering, poor drainage, too warm

**Solution:** Improve airflow, reduce watering

**Prevention:** Light watering, good drainage, cooler temps

### Problem 3: Short Shelf Life

**Symptoms:** Wilts quickly after harvest

**Causes:** Natural - cress is delicate

**Solution:** Use quickly, refrigerate immediately

**Prevention:** Harvest to order when possible

### Problem 4: Bitter Flavor

**Symptoms:** More bitter than expected

**Causes:** Heat stress, too old, underwatered

**Solution:** Grow cooler, harvest earlier

**Prevention:** Cool growing conditions, timely harvest

## Pro Tips

1. **Classic Pairings** - Market the egg-and-cress connection. Classic British tea sandwiches are a niche but devoted market.

2. **Lower Yield, Higher Price** - Cress yields less than radish but can command premium pricing for its refined flavor.

3. **School Project Upgrade** - Many people grew cress in school. Market the nostalgia with a professional product.

4. **Cool Grower** - Cress prefers cool conditions. Good for unheated spaces in mild weather.

5. **Quick Turnover** - Short shelf life means frequent small plantings rather than large batches.

---

## Scheduling Recommendations

- **Continuous supply:** Plant every 3-4 days (shorter cycle)
- **Grow to order** - Short shelf life makes this ideal for restaurant accounts
- **Cool weather crop** - Better in spring/fall than summer heat

## Historical Note

Cress has been cultivated since ancient times and was grown aboard British naval ships to prevent scurvy. It remains a traditional crop in British and European cuisine.
