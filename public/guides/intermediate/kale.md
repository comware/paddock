## Overview

Kale microgreens deliver the nutritional benefits of kale in a more tender, milder form. The microgreen version lacks the fibrous texture and strong bitterness of mature kale leaves, making it more palatable for those who don't enjoy full-grown kale.

Multiple kale varieties can be grown as microgreens - curly kale, Red Russian, Lacinato (dinosaur), and others. Each produces slightly different leaf shapes and colors at the microgreen stage. Red Russian is particularly attractive with purple-tinged stems.

Kale microgreens are rich in vitamins K, A, and C, plus minerals and beneficial plant compounds. They're a superfood in miniature form.

## Best Uses

- Smoothies (very popular)
- Salad mixes
- Garnish for upscale dishes
- Sandwich addition
- Juicing blends

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Any kale variety works for microgreens
- Red Russian for color, curly for texture
- Small, round, dark seeds

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location
- Sealed container
- 3+ years viability

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** No

Optional short soak (4-6 hours) can slightly speed germination.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium:** Coco coir or fine soil preferred. Mats work but may produce smaller yield.

**Setup:** 1-2cm moistened medium, level surface.

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-35g per 10x20 tray

**Technique:**
1. Even distribution using shaker
2. Light misting to settle
3. No covering with medium
4. Very light press optional

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

**Method:**
1. Cover with inverted tray
2. Light weight (1-2kg)
3. Keep at 18-22°C
4. Mist daily

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Root emergence
- Day 2-3: White stems visible
- Day 3-4: Cotyledons forming, ready for light

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** Cotyledons forming, stems 2-3cm

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate to bright light
- 12-16 hours per day
- Red Russian develops best color with strong light

**What happens:**
- Greening within 24-48 hours
- Purple/red coloring develops (variety dependent)
- Cotyledons expand

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering preferred

**Frequency:**
- 1-2x daily in light phase
- Keep consistently moist
- Avoid waterlogging

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 8-12 days from seeding
- Height: 5-8cm
- Cotyledons fully developed
- Before true leaves emerge

**Technique:**
1. Cut just above medium
2. Harvest in morning for best freshness
3. Some seed hulls may cling - brush off gently

**Yield:** 200-350g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Gentle wash if needed
2. Dry thoroughly
3. Handle carefully - more delicate than radish

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate at 2-4°C
- Shelf life: 7-10 days
- Keeps well when properly stored

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Damping Off

**Symptoms:** Collapsed seedlings, brown stems at base

**Causes:** Overwatering, poor drainage, poor airflow

**Solution:** Improve airflow, reduce watering, remove affected plants

**Prevention:** Good ventilation, don't overwater, moderate temps

### Problem 2: Pale or Yellow Color

**Symptoms:** Leaves not deep green, yellowish tinge

**Causes:** Insufficient light, nutrient deficiency in medium

**Solution:** Move to brighter light, ensure quality medium

**Prevention:** Adequate light from start of light phase

### Problem 3: Slow Growth

**Symptoms:** Takes longer than expected to reach harvest size

**Causes:** Too cold, insufficient light, poor quality seeds

**Solution:** Increase temperature (within range), more light, fresh seeds

**Prevention:** Optimal conditions, quality seeds

## Pro Tips

1. **Try Different Varieties** - Red Russian, curly, and Lacinato each offer unique appearances. Mix for visual interest.

2. **Smoothie Favorite** - Kale microgreens are milder than mature kale, making them ideal for smoothies.

3. **Light for Color** - Red Russian needs bright light to develop purple coloring. Low light produces mostly green.

4. **Slower Than Radish** - Don't expect radish-like speed. Kale takes its time but is reliable.

5. **Winter Hardy** - Kale tolerates cooler growing temps well - good for unheated spaces in mild climates.
