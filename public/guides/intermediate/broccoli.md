# Broccoli Microgreens

| Attribute | Value |

| **Pre-soak Required** | No |

## Overview

Broccoli microgreens are one of the most studied and nutritionally celebrated microgreens available. Research has shown they contain 10-100x the concentration of sulforaphane compared to mature broccoli heads. Sulforaphane is a powerful antioxidant linked to numerous health benefits.

The flavor is milder than mature broccoli - a pleasant, slightly sweet taste with subtle cabbage-family notes. The small, round cotyledons are bright green and visually appealing. This variety is extremely popular in health-conscious markets.

While classified as intermediate due to seed cost and slightly slower growth than radish, broccoli microgreens are quite forgiving and adapt well to various growing conditions.

## Best Uses

- Salads and grain bowls
- Smoothies (popular for sulforaphane content)
- Sandwich topping
- Garnish for soups
- Health shots and juicing

## Step-by-Step Growing Guide

### 1. Seed Selection & Storage

**What to look for:**
- Specifically labeled for microgreen/sprouting use
- Small, round, dark brown seeds
- Uniform size for even germination

**Storage:**
- Cool, dry location (15-20°C)
- Sealed container away from light
- Use within 2-3 years for best germination

### 2. Pre-Soaking

**Required:** No - small seeds germinate well without soaking.

Optional: A 4-6 hour soak can speed germination by 12-24 hours.

### 3. Tray Preparation

**Medium options:**
- Coco coir (recommended)
- Fine-textured soil
- Hemp mats work well for clean harvest

**Setup:**
1. Fill tray with 1-2cm of moistened medium
2. Level surface carefully - small seeds need even surface
3. Pre-moisten thoroughly but avoid pooling

### 4. Seeding

**Density:** 25-35g per 10x20 tray (seeds are small)

**Technique:**
1. Sprinkle seeds evenly - use shaker for distribution
2. Seeds should be close but not piled
3. Do not cover with medium
4. Mist gently - don't displace seeds
5. Press very lightly if desired

**Note:** Broccoli seeds are mucilaginous - they get slightly sticky when wet.

### 5. Blackout Phase

**Duration:** 3-4 days

**Method:**
1. Cover with inverted tray or dome
2. Light weight optional (1-2kg)
3. Keep at 18-22°C
4. Mist daily - maintain moisture

**What to expect:**
- Day 1-2: Seeds swell, white radicles emerge
- Day 2-3: Pale stems push up, seed coats lifting
- Day 3-4: Cotyledons forming, 2-3cm height

### 6. Light Phase

**When to move:** When cotyledons are forming and starting to open (day 3-4)

**Light requirements:**
- Moderate to bright light
- 12-16 hours per day
- Consistent lighting produces best color

**What happens:**
- Rapid greening within 24-48 hours
- Cotyledons expand and flatten
- Growth rate increases

### 7. Watering

**Method:** Bottom watering strongly preferred

**Frequency:**
- Blackout: Mist once daily
- Light phase: Bottom water 1-2x daily
- Keep moist but never waterlogged

**Critical:** Brassicas are prone to damping off. Avoid overwatering and ensure good drainage.

### 8. Harvest

**Timing indicators:**
- 8-12 days from seeding
- Height: 5-8cm
- Cotyledons fully open and deep green
- Before true leaves emerge (first set of serrated leaves)

**Technique:**
1. Cut just above medium with sharp scissors
2. Harvest in morning for best crunch
3. Cut what you need - remaining greens continue growing briefly

**Yield:** 200-350g from 10x20 tray

### 9. Post-Harvest

**Handling:**
1. Minimal washing if grown cleanly
2. Dry thoroughly before storage
3. Remove any seed hulls clinging to leaves

**Storage:**
- Refrigerate immediately (2-4°C)
- Breathable container or bag with paper towel
- Shelf life: 7-10 days
- Maintains sulforaphane content well when cold

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Damping Off

**Symptoms:** Seedlings collapse, thin brown stems at base, fuzzy mold

**Causes:** Overwatering, poor drainage, poor airflow, too warm

**Solution:** Remove affected plants, increase airflow, let surface dry slightly

**Prevention:** Don't overwater, ensure good drainage and air circulation

### Problem 2: Uneven Germination

**Symptoms:** Patchy growth, some areas dense, others sparse

**Causes:** Uneven seed distribution, uneven moisture, old seeds

**Solution:** Not much for current tray - note for next planting

**Prevention:** Use shaker for even distribution, mist evenly, fresh seeds

### Problem 3: Leggy/Stretched Growth

**Symptoms:** Tall thin stems, small cotyledons, pale color

**Causes:** Insufficient light, too warm

**Solution:** Move closer to light, reduce temperature

**Prevention:** Adequate light from day 1 of light phase, cooler temps

### Problem 4: Seed Hulls Stuck on Cotyledons

**Symptoms:** Brown seed coats remain attached to leaves

**Causes:** Insufficient humidity during blackout, planted too shallow

**Solution:** Mist heavily to loosen, gently brush off at harvest

**Prevention:** Maintain humidity during blackout, light weight on cover

## Pro Tips

1. **Sulforaphane Activation** - Chewing or blending activates the enzyme that creates sulforaphane. Don't cook if you want maximum benefit.

2. **Stacking Helps** - Use weighted cover during blackout. This helps push off seed hulls as cotyledons emerge.

3. **Cool Growing** - Broccoli prefers cooler temps than some varieties. 16-18°C produces stockier, more robust greens.

4. **Mix with Radish** - Broccoli and radish planted together provide color contrast and flavor variety.

5. **Premium Pricing** - Broccoli microgreens command premium prices due to health benefits. Market the sulforaphane content.

## Scheduling Recommendations

- **Continuous supply:** Plant every 4-5 days
- **Slower in winter** - May take 2-3 extra days in cooler conditions
- **Good year-round crop** - Tolerates range of conditions

## Companion Planting

Grows well with:
- **Kale** - Same family, similar timing
- **Red Cabbage** - Color contrast, same care
- **Radish** - Faster, can be mixed in final days
